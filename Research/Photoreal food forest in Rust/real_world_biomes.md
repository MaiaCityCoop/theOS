# Pluggable real-world biomes from open data, and the ecological and growth simulation a food-forest game needs

Legend used in every "Cited Findings" list: **[F]** = documented fact (licence text, specification, dated number published by the data owner or measured in a paper); **[C]** = vendor or author claim that nobody independent has measured; "(via search summary)" = I saw the statement quoted in search results but did not open the page myself. Everything under "Inferences" is my own reasoning. All pages were retrieved on 2026-11-19. Research condition: the shared web-search budget ran out after 46 searches, so the last third of the work used direct fetches of known primary URLs only; what could not be verified is listed under "Gaps" and never presented as fact.

## 1. Open global and regional datasets: resolution, licence, format, access

### Takeaway
A commercially usable global stack exists at 10–30 m, with climate at about 1 km and soil at 250 m (Copernicus GLO-30, ESA WorldCover, ETH and Meta canopy height, CHELSA, Köppen-Geiger, SoilGrids, HydroRIVERS, RESOLVE ecoregions), and real 0.5–2 m bare-earth elevation exists only country by country (USA, Switzerland, France, Spain). Three datasets named in the brief are closed to a commercial game without a separate deal: WorldClim 2.1, FABDEM and HWSD v2. MERIT Hydro is commercial only under its share-alike ODbL option, and OpenStreetMap and most Overture themes are ODbL as well.

### Cited Findings

#### Elevation, global
- [F] Copernicus DEM is a digital surface model ("represents the surface of the Earth", so it includes buildings and vegetation). GLO-90 has worldwide coverage at 90 m; GLO-30 Public has "limited worldwide coverage at 30 meters" because "a small subset of tiles covering specific countries are not yet released to the public". Access: S3 buckets `copernicus-dem-30m` and `copernicus-dem-90m` (eu-central-1), "No AWS account required", STAC endpoints; no scheduled updates — [AWS Open Data Registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/copernicus-dem/)

### Inferences
- Commercial-safe default stack (my selection from the findings above): Copernicus GLO-30/GLO-90, ESA WorldCover, ETH 10 m and Meta 1 m canopy height, CHELSA (CC0) plus Köppen-Geiger V2 (CC BY, includes monthly temperature and precipitation), ERA5-Land or TerraClimate for year-to-year weather, SoilGrids, HydroRIVERS/HydroLAKES, RESOLVE ecoregions, sPlotOpen, GBIF records filtered to CC0 and CC BY, GLiM. All of them need a credits screen; none forbids commercial use.
- Avoid or license separately: WorldClim 2.1, FABDEM, HWSD v2, MERIT Hydro under CC BY-NC. The SmartImposter plan lists WorldClim as "CC BY"; that is wrong according to WorldClim's own page, so that stack must not be copied unchecked.
- Share-alike risk: anything ODbL (OpenStreetMap, Overture base/buildings/transportation, MERIT Hydro's commercial option) may force a baked tile database derived from it to be published under ODbL. Whether a game's baked package is a "derived database" or a "produced work" is a legal question I cannot settle; the low-risk route is to keep ODbL data out of the natural-landscape bake (rivers from HydroRIVERS, land cover from WorldCover).
- GLO-30 is a surface model: in forest it measures roughly the canopy top. For a game that needs a forest floor, the bare ground must be estimated (for example by subtracting canopy height in WorldCover tree cells) or taken from national lidar; FABDEM does exactly this but is non-commercial.
- Earth Engine is convenient for exploration, but its free tier is limited to research, education and non-profit use, so the production pipeline should pull from the AWS buckets, WCS endpoints and owner downloads directly.
- Species composition data are thin: 95,104 sPlotOpen plots over about 149 million km² of land is one plot per roughly 1,570 km² on average, and GBIF is presence-only with strong observer bias. A per-site species palette will be a plausible list, never a measured community.

### Gaps
- EUNIS habitat maps, national forest inventories and potential natural vegetation maps: not verified. The one paper I tried (Hengl et al. 2018, PeerJ) returned HTTP 403. From background knowledge only: US FIA is public domain and EEA products are usually CC BY 4.0; both need checking.
- Italy's national 1 m lidar licence, the full-resolution GLiM vector licence, the exact AW3D30 and Copernicus credit wording, and the resolution of TerraClimate (I believe about 4 km) and HWSD (about 1 km) were not confirmed on primary pages.
- Whether a newer Meta canopy height release ("v2") exists: the AWS registry and the GitHub repository show only v1 and DINOv2-based models.
- Other national lidar programmes (England, Netherlands, Nordic countries, Austria, Germany's states, New Zealand, Australia) were not researched; Cesium's source list (question 2) shows that England 1–2 m (Environment Agency), Australia 5 m (Geoscience Australia) and New Zealand 8 m (LINZ) exist, but their licences are unverified.

## 2. Terrain detail from 10–30 m down to centimetres; what Flight Simulator 2024, Google 3D Tiles, Cesium and SmartImposter do

### Takeaway
No product has metre-scale measured terrain worldwide; every one that looks convincing at ground level synthesises the detail, either by constrained erosion (SmartImposter, Schott et al. 2024) or by machine-classified ground types driving procedural material sets (Flight Simulator 2024). Learned diffusion terrain from 2025–2026 generates new plausible terrain at 30–90 m and is not a super-resolver for real elevation data; Google's Photorealistic 3D Tiles may not be cached, stored, used offline or turned into terrain models, so they cannot feed a baked biome package.

### Cited Findings

#### SmartImposter (github.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter), from its README and docs/EARTH_PLAN.md
- [C] What it is: "A hardware ray-traced renderer of forests and of the whole Earth, written from scratch in Rust + Vulkan", using `VK_KHR_ray_tracing_pipeline` with no rasteriser; distant plants are parallax octahedral impostors; NVIDIA DLSS Ray Reconstruction or an in-house TAA produces the image. Code is MIT; Quixel assets and the DLSS SDK are not included. Developed on an RTX 5080 (16 GB) — [README](https://github.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter)
- [C] `--site lat,lon` mode: "the same forest scene on real terrain and land cover"; elevation is Copernicus GLO-30, land cover is ESA WorldCover 2021 v200, and drainage comes from a priority-flood pass over the real 30 m heights — [README](https://github.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter)
- [C] `--globe` mode: Earth at 1:1 streamed from orbit to standing height. Elevation is NOAA ETOPO 2022 (60″) with GLO-30 blended in where tiles exist, converted to a half-metre int16 `.dem` format "about 4.5x smaller than GeoTIFF". WorldCover is sampled per terrain vertex to pick ground scans, mark lakes and rivers and drive tree and understory placement. RESOLVE Ecoregions 2017 (846 ecoregions, 14 biomes, 3.7 km grid) gives the biome per place. HydroRIVERS and HydroLAKES are listed as planned. Species are Quixel assets chosen by name from a per-biome table in `globe/forest.rs`; there is no external species database, and the README names no canopy-height, climate or soil dataset in use — [README](https://github.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter)
- [C] Data volumes it reports (download / on disk): ETOPO 60″ 466 MB / 0.9 GB; GLO-30 about 543 GB / about 120 GB; WorldCover 124 GB / 124 GB; vectors 1.5 GB / 5.3 GB. The planning document instead quotes "~26,000 1°×1° tiles, ~90–100 GB compressed (third-party estimate)" for GLO-30, so the two files disagree — [README](https://github.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter); [EARTH_PLAN](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter/main/docs/EARTH_PLAN.md)
- [C] Erosion terrain (generated-forest mode, a 144 km landscape in eight biomes): tectonic uplift followed by river erosion with the stream-power law solved implicitly (citing Braun & Willett 2013 and Cordonnier et al. 2016); every iteration runs a priority-flood pass (Barnes et al. 2014) to route water to the sea and accumulate drainage area; cells draining more than 12 km² become smoothed meandering polylines whose width follows catchment size; the mesh has 3 m cells near the start growing to 200 m cells at ±60 km and carries Earth's curvature; results are cached. The README does not say whether erosion runs on the CPU or GPU — [README](https://github.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter)
- [C] Forest succession: "seed-dispersal and succession simulation" with no named model. Founder pairs on a 60 m lattice; 10 generations described as "~150 years"; six ecological roles (shade-tolerant climax trees, wet-ground trees, edge pioneers, clonal groves, an understory tree, others); each species has a lifespan and an age of first seeding; windthrow gaps reset patches; a seedling fails under a crown two generations older within 5.5 m. Output about 6 million trees in an 8 km radius in about 23 s on first run, then cached; shrubs, ferns and grass are scattered on jittered grids from canopy cover — [README](https://github.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter)
- [C] Planned detail synthesis: "erosion and relief amplification of the 30 m DEM down to about 1 m, constrained to match the 30 m data", channels and banks carved along vector rivers, river width from discharge, streams below 10 km² from its own drainage extraction; cube-sphere quadtree of 65×65-vertex tiles, level 17 about 76 m tiles with about 1.2 m vertex spacing, 1,000–2,000 active tiles. Per-tile plant generation: "6M trees over 200 km² in ~20 s on the CPU; a 1 km² tile (~20–60k trees) takes a few ms" — [EARTH_PLAN](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter/main/docs/EARTH_PLAN.md)
- [C] Its measured cost (RTX 5080, 2560×1440, median of 300 frames): generated forest with about 6.2 million plants uses 4.2 GB of GPU memory at 9.9–10.5 ms per frame with DLSS-RR Quality and 15.5–20.5 ms with DLAA; globe views use 7.1–10.8 GB at 5.2–18.1 ms. Stated limits: no wind, repeating grass patches, mostly European and Mediterranean species with stand-ins elsewhere, only trunks collide — [README](https://github.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter)

#### Erosion simulation
- [F] "Terrain Amplification using Multi-scale Erosion" (Schott, Galin, Guérin, Peytavie, Paris; ACM TOG 43(4), SIGGRAPH 2024, DOI 10.1145/3658200) amplifies "a low-resolution input terrain into a high-resolution, hydrologically consistent terrain" using "a fast and accurate approximation of different simulations, including thermal, stream power erosion and deposition performed at different scales" — [project page](https://h-schott.github.io/p/mserosion); [co-author page](https://aparis69.github.io/public_html/projects/schott2024_Erosion.html)
- [F] The release code is MIT-licensed, builds with CMake on Windows or Linux, "Requires at least OpenGl 4.3 for compute shaders", and recommends "erosion -> thermal -> deposition", noting that "Alternating this sequence with the x2 upsampling gives great amplification results"; a single-scale demo runs on Shadertoy — [GitHub](https://github.com/H-Schott/MultiScaleErosion)

#### Learned terrain models, 2025–2026
- [F] Terrain Diffusion / InfiniteDiffusion (Goslin; arXiv 2512.08309, first version 9 December 2025, revised 3 May 2026; the repository title says SIGGRAPH 2026): a hierarchy of diffusion models distilled to consistency models, with seed consistency and constant-time random access, presented as a learned successor to Perlin noise. Training data per the paper: 90 m MERIT DEM for land, ETOPO1 for bathymetry, WorldClim for climate. Measured on an RTX 3090 Ti: 0.66 ± 0.18 s per additional 512×512 tile (one tile covers 46 km at 90 m), 1.72 s to first tile, peak video memory 2,214 MB (1,846 MB at half precision); FID-50k 14.78 for the seamless two-step setting against 186.70 for Perlin blending. It also outputs climate fields (mean temperature, temperature variation, annual precipitation, seasonality). The paper notes hydrology failures become more frequent at fewer blending steps — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08309); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08309)
- [F] Terrain Diffusion repository: code under MIT; no licence stated for the weights; 30 m and 90 m per pixel checkpoints; an NVIDIA GPU is "strongly recommended", CPU is "much slower" and "Mac is CPU-only"; integrations are a Minecraft Fabric mod, a Python API and GeoTIFF export — [GitHub](https://github.com/xandergos/terrain-diffusion)
- [F] MESA (arXiv 2504.07210, CVPR 2025 workshop): Stable Diffusion 2.1 fine-tuned on about 1.3 million 10 × 10 km samples pairing Copernicus GLO-30 (30 m) with Sentinel-2 imagery (10 m), generating co-registered image and elevation from text; trained on 8 A100 GPUs, 50 sampling steps; evaluation is qualitative only; the Major TOM Core-DEM dataset is released openly on Hugging Face — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07210); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07210v2)
- [F] One search for a lidar-trained diffusion model that super-resolves real 30 m elevation to 1–3 m with open weights found none; the nearest hit is a 2025 multi-modal elevation super-resolution network with an open benchmark dataset, which is not a diffusion model — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1569843225005126)

#### Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024
- [C] Asobo's CEO: "The amount of detail between 2020 and 2024 is about 4,000 times more, whether that's texture resolution or terrain mesh." The system was trained "by looking at satellite photos" to recognise "maybe 40 types of ground", estimates mixtures per location, and blends hand-made "sets of trees, plants, rocks, and types of minerals" accordingly (TechRadar, 19 November 2024) — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/gaming/consoles-pc/microsoft-flight-simulator-2024-ceo-on-how-the-team-achieves-about-4-000-times-more-detail-than-its-predecessor)
- [C] "enhanced 3D ground textures and procedurally generated vegetation across nearly 30 distinct biomes" (24 June 2024); other outlets say 27 — [flightsim.to](https://flightsim.to/news/fsexpo-2024-key-takeaways-for-microsoft-flight-simulator-2024)
- [C] 2023 preview: machine learning "based on low-altitude training", "3D out of 2D" ground at 50 cm where photogrammetry is missing (Kilimanjaro example), more data "streamed on demand" — [FSElite, 23 June 2023](https://fselite.net/content/microsoft-seminar-at-fsexpo/)
- [F] A third-party vegetation package built on the simulator's own definitions ships 358 XML files: 242 TreeSpecies, 34 PlantTypes, 61 GrassTypes, 7 CropSpecies and 8 RockTypes, and covers "848 / 848" ecoregions with 16 tuned biome categories — [Orbx product page](https://orbxdirect.com/product/burningbluedesign-global-vegetation)

#### Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles and Cesium
- [F] Google Map Tiles API policy: "you must not pre-fetch, index, store, or cache any Content except under the limited conditions stated in the terms"; offline use is prohibited; no bulk download or extraction; no image analysis, machine interpretation, object detection or geodata extraction; no 3D or terrain models built from the content; overlaid 3D objects are allowed only if they "aren't extracted, traced, or otherwise derived by hand or machine"; Google logo and per-tile data attributions must be shown — [Google Map Tiles API policies](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/policies)
- [F] Cesium Native is Apache-2.0 C++ ("free for both commercial and non-commercial use") for 3D Tiles streaming, quantized-mesh terrain, glTF and raster overlays; official engine integrations are Unreal, Unity, Omniverse and O3DE, Godot is a community project, and Bevy is not listed — [GitHub](https://github.com/CesiumGS/cesium-native)

### Inferences
- For a baked package the realistic open path is: real 30 m (or national lidar) heights, hydrological conditioning by priority-flood, river channels carved along HydroRIVERS with width from the discharge attribute, then five to six rounds of "erode, thermal, deposit, upsample ×2" to go from 30 m to about 1 m, constrained so a blur of the result matches the source. Both reference implementations (SmartImposter in Rust, Schott et al. in OpenGL compute) are MIT-licensed, so an agent team can port the method to wgpu compute shaders without licence friction.
- Centimetre detail cannot be stored and does not need to be: below about 1 m it has to come from tiling materials, displacement and scattered meshes (rocks, roots, litter) chosen by slope, curvature, wetness and rock class. GLiM's average scale of 1:3,750,000 only supports a regional rock-type and colour palette, not placement of individual outcrops.
- Learned terrain models are a poor fit for this project today: they invent terrain instead of refining a real place, need about 2 GB of video memory and a desktop GPU for sub-second tiles, and Terrain Diffusion's weights were trained on WorldClim (non-commercial) and MERIT DEM (non-commercial or share-alike), which is an unresolved licence question for a commercial product.
- SmartImposter cannot be a base for phones: it requires hardware ray tracing, was measured only on an RTX 5080 and uses 4–11 GB of GPU memory. What transfers is its data pipeline, tile format idea, erosion method and cheap succession model.
- Flight Simulator 2024 shows the ceiling of the "classify ground type, then blend authored material sets" approach with a large team and cloud streaming, and its forums show that ecoregion-driven species assignment still produces visible mistakes. A small team should expect to hand-tune each shipped biome.
- Cesium is a visualisation stack for streaming globes; nothing in it helps bake an offline phone package, and there is no Rust or Bevy integration listed.

### Gaps
- Timings, grid sizes and amplification factors of Schott et al. 2024 are in the paper PDF, which I could not open (the host blocks automated access).
- Classic references for fast GPU hydraulic and thermal erosion (the 2007 "virtual pipes" shallow-water method, later stream-power GPU solvers and analytical erosion from 2023–2024) and for procedural rock, cliff and riverscape modelling were not verified this session; I know them only from background knowledge.
- The widely repeated figure of about 28,000 hand-labelled training tiles for Flight Simulator 2024 appeared only in a search summary; I could not trace it to one article. A community note about a "15 cm" procedural terrain tier is likewise unverified, and I found no official technical documentation of the simulator's biome rules (the SDK page I tried returned 404).
- Licences of the Terrain Diffusion and MESA weights are not stated on the pages I read.

## 3. Plant placement from data: graphics methods and ecology's individual-based forest models

### Takeaway
The graphics literature has settled on a two-stage pattern: derive a plausible layout offline from terrain-driven conditions, real canopy height where available and cheap competition or succession rules, then realise it at run time from density maps. Ecology's individual-based models are validated but slow and parameter-hungry; the cohort and canopy-layer abstraction (the perfect plasticity approximation) is the cheapest model with published validation.

### Cited Findings
- [F] Deussen et al. 1998 (SIGGRAPH): a pipeline of terrain editing, plant distribution "by hand", "by ecosystem simulation" or both, procedural plant models, and "approximate instancing" that replaces similar plants and organs by representative instances so scenes with "thousands of plants" and "billions of primitives" can be rendered — [Stanford paper page](http://www-graphics.stanford.edu/papers/ecosys/)
- [C] Makowski et al. 2019, "Synthetic Silviculture" (ACM TOG 38(4)): multi-scale growth model built on reusable branch modules; "interactive design of plant ecosystems of up to 500K plants"; parameter space covers "nine distinct plant ecologies"; each plant is a 3D mesh. A second description of the same work cites 90,000 plants — [project page](https://storage.googleapis.com/pirk.io/projects/synthetic_silviculture/index.html); [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/synthetic-silviculture-multi-scale-modeling-of-plant-ecosystems/)
- [F] Kapp et al. 2020, "Data-driven Authoring of Large-scale Ecosystems" (ACM TOG 39(6), article 217): machine learning predicts a canopy height model for unseen terrain; canopy trees are fitted to it by a constrained iterative process that meets a target species distribution; the understorey comes from distributions derived from biome-specific undergrowth simulations. Trained and validated on elevation, canopy height and biome data for 380 km² of Sonoma County, California; generates 1 km × 1 km ecosystems with about a million plants in under five seconds (via search summary of the abstract) — [HAL record](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03007259); [author PDF](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/egalin/Articles/2020-ecolearn.pdf)
- [C] Pałubicki et al. 2022, "Ecoclimates" (ACM TOG 41(4), article 155): couples vegetation, soil and atmosphere; "we model tree growth interactively in response to gradients of water, temperature and light"; reproduces forest edge effects, the Foehn effect and spatial vegetation patterning — [project page](https://storage.googleapis.com/pirk.io/projects/ecoclimates/index.html)
- [F] Horizon Zero Dawn (van Muijden, GDC 2017): the placement system builds 2D density maps from artist-authored logic and world-data maps, then discretises them on the GPU with a dither-based step into object positions while the player moves. Published numbers: world data "~4MB/km2", "500+ asset types", "100.000+ objects in scene", "~250µs avg busy load on GPU" (via search summary of the slides) — [slides](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf); [talk abstract](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/gpu-based-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn)
- [C] SmartImposter's succession rules (question 2) are the simplest working example I found: lattice founders, generations of about 15 years, six roles, a 5.5 m shading radius, windthrow resets; about 6 million trees in about 20–23 s on a desktop CPU — [README](https://github.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter)
- [F] Machine learning for vegetation layout, 2024–2026: one extended search found no ACM TOG paper on diffusion-based ecosystem layout. Nearest results: "Greenscapes" (arXiv 2610.40007, 29 October 2026; Bartkowiak, Pałubicki, Pirk, Benes, Michels et al.), where a conditioned vision-language model draws a top-down vegetation plan for single urban lots and CMA-ES then optimises plant scale and asset choice against shade and acoustic surrogates; and "Lay-Your-Scene" (ICCV 2025), a general natural-scene layout generator using a diffusion transformer — [Greenscapes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40007); [Lay-Your-Scene](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04718)
- [F] Related 2025 work on single plants: "Light Distribution Models for Tree Growth Simulation" (Computer Graphics Forum 2025) compares four light models for a functional-structural plant model — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cgf.15268)
- [F] Perfect plasticity approximation (Purves, Lichstein, Strigul, Pacala; PNAS 2008): "all crown area above a critical height Z* receives full sunlight" and the rest is shaded; the model tracks cohorts ("a group of trees with identical properties") instead of individuals; per species it needs canopy and understory growth and mortality rates plus height and crown allometry. Parameterised from short-term (15 years or less) inventory data for the eight commonest species on four soil types in Michigan, Wisconsin and Minnesota, then run for 100 years without tuning: basal area of the oldest plots matched "to within a few percent"; it failed to predict the dominance of Thuja occidentalis on wet soils. Derived from the spatial simulator SORTIE; simulations are "orders of magnitude faster" — [PNAS via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2579370/)
- [F] iLand: "simulates each tree over 4m tall as a unique individual", while "trees under 4m tall are grouped into cohorts"; hierarchy of environment, "1-hectare resource unit" and individual; "simulates the entire water cycle on a daily basis"; C++ with Qt, open source, multi-threaded; designed for "the simulation of millions of individual trees over large landscapes" — [iLand book](https://iland-model.org/iland-book/intro.html); [how iLand works](https://iland-model.org/2.2/wiki/how-it-works.html)
- [F] iLand at scale: the 2012 paper reports computing time rising linearly with landscape extent; a later overview covers landscapes of 10^5 hectares and 50 peer-reviewed studies; a 2024 Ecological Modelling paper names high computational demand as a limitation (via search summary; exact context not checked) — [2012 paper record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:ecomod:v:231:y:2012:i:c:p:87-100); [overview](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/the-individual-based-forest-landscape-and-disturbance-model-iland/); [2024 paper](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1749373/1749373.pdf)
- [F] FORMIND: "an individual-based vegetation model" working at the hectare scale on 20 m × 20 m patches (the size of a treefall gap); hundreds of hectares over centuries on an annual step; species are grouped into plant functional types partly "to reduce computing time"; described as "computationally intensive even when few PFTs are included" (via search summary) — [FORMIND](https://formind.org/model/); [Forests 2018](https://doi.org/10.3390/f9080460)
- [F] LANDIS-II: simulates forests "at decadal to multi-century time scales" over "hundreds to millions of hectares"; all components free and open source — [LANDIS-II](https://www.landis-ii.org/)
- [F] 3-PG (Landsberg & Waring 1997): stand-level, monthly time step, growth from intercepted radiation with efficiency reduced by nutrition, soil drought, vapour pressure deficit and stand age; the r3PG package (Fortran core, GPL-3.0) includes 3-PGmix for mixed, uneven-aged and deciduous stands — [3-PG site](https://3pg.forestry.ubc.ca/); [r3PG](https://github.com/trotsiuk/r3PG)

### Inferences
- Cost at game scale, from the published numbers: generating a layout is cheap (about a million plants per km² in under five seconds in Kapp's system; a 1 km² tile of 20–60 thousand trees in a few milliseconds by SmartImposter's claim), so wild vegetation can be baked offline or even generated per tile at load time. Running a validated process model live is not cheap: iLand and FORMIND are run for research on workstations and clusters over hours, and their own authors flag the cost.
- The split that follows: wild landscape = baked or seed-generated layout constrained by real canopy height and land cover; the player's plot (hundreds to a few thousand plants) = a per-plant model stepped once per game day, which is trivial compute on a phone.
- For shading among layers the perfect plasticity approximation is the right abstraction for a food forest: sort plants by height, give full light to crown area above the closure height, and attenuate below it. It maps directly onto the canopy / sub-canopy / shrub / herb layers and needs only six or seven numbers per species.
- Nobody has published a learned model that lays out natural vegetation for a real site; the 2026 state of the art applies language-vision models to small urban lots. For this project, rules plus real canopy-height data are both cheaper and better documented.

### Gaps
- EcoBrush (Gain, Long, Cordonnier, Cani; Eurographics 2017) could not be opened on any host (HTTP 403 or bot wall). From background knowledge only: it indexes a database of small sandbox ecosystem simulations by terrain conditions (slope, temperature, moisture, sunlight) and synthesises matching plant distributions under interactive brushes.
- JABOWA/FORET gap models and SORTIE-ND: primary pages were unreachable (certificate error, 404). From background knowledge only: gap models simulate each tree on patches of roughly 0.01–0.1 ha with yearly diameter growth reduced by light, temperature and soil multipliers; SORTIE tracks every tree's position and computes light per tree, which is the costly part the perfect plasticity approximation removes.
- iLand's light-grid and height-grid cell sizes and any published runtime per simulated year were not found on the pages I could read.
- Because the search budget ran out, coverage of 2024–2026 machine-learning work on vegetation layout rests on one search.

## 4. Biome package definition: minimal baked data, size per km², how other products structure pluggable regions

### Takeaway
A site package can be tens to a few hundred kilobytes per km² if it stores only the measured 10–30 m rasters, vectors, climate curves and a species palette with rules, and regenerates metre-scale terrain and plant positions deterministically from seeds; storing fully baked 1 m maps costs megabytes per km² (Horizon Zero Dawn: about 4 MB/km² for its placement maps alone). The only shipped precedents for real-place regions are streamed globes (Flight Simulator, Cesium, Google), not downloadable packages.

### Cited Findings
- [F] Horizon Zero Dawn keeps its world data as a "Collection of 2D maps", "Streamed in sections", "~4MB/km2", and derives all vegetation, rocks and effects from them at run time instead of storing instances (via search summary of the slides) — [GDC 2017 slides](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf)
- [C] SmartImposter's planned baker stores per tile: height (int16 or f16 with min/max), land cover (u8), canopy height (u8), biome/ecoregion id (u16), two climate indices (u8 each) and clipped river, lake and coast vectors, compressed with zstd into one pack file per cube face and level range with an index; everything finer (1 m relief, materials, plants) is regenerated deterministically with "seed = hash(tile id)" and a margin from neighbouring tiles to hide seams. It publishes no bytes-per-tile figure — [EARTH_PLAN](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter/main/docs/EARTH_PLAN.md)
- [C] SmartImposter's species scheme: "4–8 archetype species per biome" with several age variants, tinted and scaled per ecoregion; 50–60 assets in total; rainforest canopy and mangroves are named as the least accurate biomes — [EARTH_PLAN](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter/main/docs/EARTH_PLAN.md)
- [F] Flight Simulator 2024 structures vegetation as XML definition families (BiomeRules, TreeSpecies, PlantType, GrassType, CropSpecies, RockType) keyed to 848 ecoregions; a third-party package supplies 242 tree species, 61 grass types, 34 plant types, 7 crop species with 7 lifecycle stages, and 8 rock types — [Orbx product page](https://orbxdirect.com/product/burningbluedesign-global-vegetation)
- [C] In Flight Simulator 2024 the ecoregion map is served as a raster from the cloud and third parties can place biome polygons from shapefiles but cannot ship new biome rules (forum reports, 2024–2026) — [FSDeveloper thread](https://www.fsdeveloper.com/forum/threads/biome-corrections.459459/)
- [F] Cesium's model for pluggable terrain is a tileset in the open quantized-mesh 1.0 format with optional water-mask and vertex-normal extensions, streamed from a paid service — [Cesium World Terrain](https://cesium.com/platform/cesium-ion/content/cesium-world-terrain/)
- [F] Kapp et al. need only an elevation map and a density sketch as inputs to populate 1 km² with about a million plants in under five seconds, which shows that plant instances need not be stored (via search summary) — [HAL record](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03007259)
- [F] Terrain Diffusion demonstrates the same contract for terrain: a seed plus coordinates gives the same tile every time with "constant-time random access" and about 10 MB of cache — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08309)

### Inferences
- Proposed minimal package per site (my definition): (1) height tiles at source resolution (30 m global, 1–2 m where lidar exists) plus a coarse 90 m ring for the horizon; (2) land-cover and canopy-height rasters at 10 m; (3) water network as vectors with width and discharge, lake polygons with level; (4) climate curves: twelve monthly values each for mean, minimum and maximum temperature, precipitation, potential evapotranspiration and radiation, plus Köppen class, per roughly 1 km cell; (5) soil layers at 250 m: sand, silt, clay, pH, organic carbon and depth; (6) rock class and a ground colour palette; (7) a species palette: 30–150 species with layer, successional role, relative abundance, and niche ranges for temperature, moisture, light, soil and slope; (8) generator parameters and seeds; (9) a licence and credits manifest listing every source dataset.
- Size arithmetic (mine, not measured). Height at 30 m is about 1,100 samples per km², roughly 2 kB as int16; at 10 m about 20 kB; at 1 m 2 MB raw per km². Land cover and canopy height at 10 m are 10 kB each before compression. Climate, soil, rock and vectors together stay below about 5 kB per km². So a seed-regenerated package is on the order of 30–100 kB per km² globally and 0.5–2 MB per km² where real 1 m lidar is kept; a fully baked variant with 1 m height and four material channels is 3–8 MB per km², in line with Horizon Zero Dawn's figure. A 16 km × 16 km region (256 km²) is therefore about 8–26 MB regenerated, or 0.8–2 GB fully baked.
- Storing plant instances is the thing to avoid: a million plants per km² at about 7 bytes each is 7 MB per km². Store rules and density maps, store explicit positions only for player-planted and story-critical plants.
- Shared asset libraries (plant meshes or generator parameters, ground materials, rocks) should live outside site packages, keyed by species and material ids, so a new region is data only.
- There is no shipped example of an offline, downloadable real-place nature package for phones that I could find; the precedents all stream from a server. Expect to design the format from first principles.

### Gaps
- No public per-km² storage budget other than Horizon Zero Dawn's was found. Farming Simulator's map format (height map and density-map resolution) and official Flight Simulator SDK documentation could not be opened, so their structures are not described here.
- Phone memory budgets for streaming such packages were out of scope and not researched.

## 5. Food-forest domain model, open plant datasets and their licences, simple validated growth models, time compression

### Takeaway
The domain model is well defined (seven layers plus fungi, guilds, succession), but open plant-use data that a commercial game may ship is the binding constraint: Plants For A Future, Useful Tropical Plants and parts of Practical Plants are non-commercial, Permapeople is share-alike with commercial API access only by agreement, Ecocrop is discontinued with no stated licence, and ICRAF's database is all rights reserved. The validated minimum for growth is thermal time for phenology, Beer–Lambert light interception with radiation-use efficiency for biomass, a bucket water balance for stress and allometry for size; published models built from those parts need 13 to 21 parameters per species, and none is validated for a mixed multi-layer food forest.

### Cited Findings

#### Domain model
- [F] Forest gardening is "a low-maintenance, sustainable, plant-based food production and agroforestry system based on woodland ecosystems"; the commonly listed seven layers are canopy trees, understory trees, shrub layer, herbaceous plants, ground cover plants, vines and root zone. Robert Hart's model garden was a 0.12-acre (500 m²) orchard; a Kerala plot he cited held 23 coconut palms, 12 cloves, 56 bananas, 49 pineapples and 30 pepper vines on 0.12 ha; Beacon Food Forest in Seattle covers seven acres — [Wikipedia: Forest gardening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_gardening)
- [F] Companion examples with long practice: the Milpa (maize, beans, squash, chilis) and the Three Sisters (corn, bean, squash) — [Wikipedia: Forest gardening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_gardening)
- [F] Syntropic farming is "an organic, permaculture agroforestry system developed by Ernst Götsch in Brazil" that mirrors secondary succession through "Dense planting mixing perennial and annual crops", "Rapid cutting and composting of fast growing pioneer species" and greater water retention; also called successional agroforestry systems — [Wikipedia: Syntropic agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntropic_agriculture)

#### Datasets and licences
- [F] Plants For A Future: the database "is covered by a Creative Commons licence: (attribution required, non-commercial, share-alike)" and "cannot be used for commercial purposes". The paid Commercial User edition (180 USD) is for use in an office and "cannot be used in or as a commercial product for sale or distribution". Site text is copyright "Plants for a Future (1995-2026)"; images are attribution, non-commercial, no-derivatives — [PFAF database page](https://pfaf.org/user/cmspage.aspx?pageid=71); [PFAF copyright page](https://pfaf.org/user/cmspage.aspx?pageid=136)
- [F] Permapeople: "all data available through this API is licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0"; "We do not currently allow commercial projects free access to the API" and commercial users must contact them. Endpoints for plants, search, varieties and companions; example fields: edible, edible parts, layer, growth, water requirement, light requirement, USDA hardiness zone, soil type. The site lists "over 8500 plant profiles" and a free planner to "Place plants, draw shapes, and design guilds" — [API documentation](https://permapeople.org/knowledgebase/api-docs.html); [Permapeople](https://permapeople.org/)
- [F] FAO Ecocrop: 2,568 plant species, developed in 1999, "discontinued around 2015", now incorporated into FAO's GAEZ platform; the page states no licence — [FAO](https://www.fao.org/land-water/resources/tools/databases/ecocrop/en)

- [F] FAO's statistical database terms default to CC BY 4.0 "unless specified otherwise"; I could not confirm they cover Ecocrop (via search summary) — [FAO database terms](https://www.fao.org/contact-us/terms/db-terms-of-use)

- [F] ICRAF Agroforestree Database 4.0: 670 agroforestry tree species with ecology, propagation, management, uses and pests; browse only, no download; "© 2009 World Agroforestry Centre. All Rights Reserved." — [World Agroforestry](https://apps.worldagroforestry.org/treedb/)
- [F] Useful Tropical Plants (Ken Fern): 13,362 species; "licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 3.0 Unported License" — [Useful Tropical Plants](https://tropical.theferns.info/)
- [F] Trefle: "indexes about 450,000 plants (495,000 records with subspecies, varieties, hybrids and forms)" from USDA, Kew, IPNI, Tropicos and others; database "published under CC-BY-4.0" while "each upstream source keeps its own licence"; platform code AGPL-3.0; free tier 60 requests per minute, bulk export for institutions by arrangement; volunteer-run with one core maintainer — [Trefle](https://trefle.io/about)

#### Growth models
- [F] Growing degree days: daily value = max((Tmax + Tmin)/2 − Tbase, 0), summed over the season; common base temperatures 4.5 °C (wheat, barley, lettuce), 8 °C (potato, sunflower), 10 °C (maize, tomato, grapes, rice); maxima usually capped at 30 °C; examples: maize 800–2,700 to maturity, dry beans 1,100–1,300, lilac flowers at 80–110, black locust at 140–160 — [Wikipedia: Growing degree-day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growing_degree-day)
- [F] SIMPLE crop model (Zhao et al. 2019): 13 parameters define a crop (four of them cultivar traits); development by thermal time; growth from intercepted radiation via the Beer–Lambert law and radiation-use efficiency, reduced by stress; calibrated for 14 annual crops from 25 field experiments; sensitivity analysis ranks radiation-use efficiency and base temperature highest; comparable models need 22 (EPIC) and 29 (AquaCrop) parameters (via search summary) — [HAL record](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02619656); [TUM record](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/a-simple-crop-model/)
- [F] Yield-SAFE (van der Werf et al., Ecological Engineering 29:419–433, 2007): an agroforestry model built "with as few equations and parameters as possible"; seven state equations; a later identification study counts 21 parameters (6 per tree species, 9 per crop species, 6 per soil); calibrated on poplar agroforestry stands in the United Kingdom (via search summary) — [Cranfield repository](https://dspace.lib.cranfield.ac.uk/handle/1826/2727); [preprint](https://core.ac.uk/download/140013.pdf)
- [F] 3-PG and the perfect plasticity approximation (question 3) are the simplest validated tree and stand models found: monthly light-use-efficiency growth with stress modifiers, and height-ranked canopy layering with six or seven rates per species — [3-PG site](https://3pg.forestry.ubc.ca/); [PNAS via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2579370/)
- [F] WOFOST, LINGRA and LINTUL3 are implemented in the Python Crop Simulation Environment, whose "source code is open and licensed under the European Union Public License" — [PCSE documentation](https://pcse.readthedocs.io/en/stable/)
- [F] FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 56 (Allen et al. 1998) is the standard water-balance recipe: reference evapotranspiration by Penman-Monteith, single or dual crop coefficients, a water stress coefficient and a soil water balance — [FAO-56](https://www.fao.org/4/x0490e/x0490e00.htm)

#### Time compression
- [F] Stardew Valley "progresses through four 28-day seasons", a 112-day year; it had sold over 50 million copies by February 2026 — [Wikipedia: Stardew Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stardew_Valley)
- [F] Farming Simulator 22 "features a seasonal cycle" (the page gives no compression ratio) — [Wikipedia: Farming Simulator 22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farming_Simulator_22)

### Inferences
- A species record for the game needs: accepted name and family; layer (canopy, sub-canopy, shrub, herb, ground cover, root, vine, fungus); successional role and lifespan; climate envelope (hardiness, minimum and optimum temperature, rainfall range); light and soil tolerances; nitrogen fixation; pollination needs; edible parts and yield; years to first harvest; companion and antagonist links; and 10–20 growth parameters. No single open dataset supplies this under commercial terms.
- A defensible commercial data plan: USDA PLANTS, Trefle (checking upstream sources per field), TRY public data, the Ecocrop climate envelopes after written confirmation from FAO, and GBIF CC0/CC BY occurrences crossed with CHELSA to derive climate envelopes for species the tables miss; then negotiate with Plants For A Future and Permapeople for edibility ratings, uses and companion links, or pay curators to compile them from primary literature. Budget for licensing or curation; do not plan on scraping.
- "Thousands of species" is feasible as names, layers and climate envelopes, and unrealistic as individually calibrated growth models. Published parameter sets exist for dozens of crops and a few dozen timber trees. The workable design is 20–40 functional types (for example nitrogen-fixing pioneer tree, standard fruit tree, cane fruit, perennial herb, annual vegetable, vine, mushroom log) with calibrated defaults, plus per-species overrides for the handful of numbers the datasets provide (height, growth rate, hardiness, years to bearing).
- Minimal per-plant daily model I would specify, each term traceable to a cited model: development stage from accumulated degree days; light fraction from canopy layering (full light above the closure height, Beer–Lambert attenuation exp(−k·LAI) through the layers above); potential growth = radiation × intercepted fraction × radiation-use efficiency; multiplied by temperature, water (bucket balance per soil cell with crop coefficient and stress factor) and nitrogen factors between 0 and 1; biomass allocated to height, crown and fruit by allometric curves; guild effects as small modifiers on the nitrogen, water and pollination factors. This is honest only as a game model: the components are validated separately for monocultures, even-aged stands and poplar-with-arable-crop systems, not for dense polycultures.
- Companion planting effects have far weaker quantitative evidence than the growth physics; I found no open dataset of measured companion effects, only qualitative links (Permapeople's companions endpoint). Present guild bonuses as design rules, not as simulation truth.
- Time compression is a design choice with one hard constraint: fruit trees take years. With a Stardew-style 112-day year and 20 real seconds per day a year lasts about 37 minutes and a five-year apple tree about three hours of play; mixing annuals that pay back in minutes with trees that pay back over sessions, plus offline progression, is how the tension is usually resolved.

## 6. Existing games and tools that model ecological restoration or plant growth

### Takeaway
None of the games I could verify simulates real species with real growth physics: they use tile rules and scores (Terra Nil), server-side populations with climate feedback (Eco), requirement checklists for stylised species (Equilinox), fictional plants as puzzles (Botany Manor) or a few dozen species with canals and terrain shaping (Wildmender). The closest thing to real-species ecological data in a shipped product is Flight Simulator 2024's ecoregion-driven vegetation definitions, which are cosmetic.

### Cited Findings
- [F] Terra Nil (Free Lives, Unity, published by Devolver Digital; Windows and mobile via Netflix on 28 March 2023): a "reverse city-builder" in three phases. Wind turbines on stone tiles power toxin scrubbers that prepare soil for irrigators; upgraded buildings create wetlands, wildflower meadows and dense forests, which bring deer, birds, fish and bears; finally the player recycles all buildings and leaves. Metacritic 79 (PC) and 84 (iOS); Rock Paper Shotgun criticised late stages as "chasing numbers"; The Guardian found that its simplicity carried the message — [Wikipedia: Terra Nil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_Nil)
- [F] Eco (Strange Loop Games, Unity, early access February 2018): species can go extinct if players "harvest or hunt too much of them"; pollution from machines can raise sea levels and felling trees reduces how much air pollution is absorbed; players write and vote on laws. Began as a middle-school teaching tool with over a million US dollars from the US Department of Education and a Kickstarter that raised more than 200,000 dollars — [Wikipedia: Eco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eco_(2018_video_game))
- [C] Eco's designer: the game has "an ecosystem that determines the temperature, soil moisture, and rate of climate change based on atmospheric CO2" (via search summary) — [Strange Loop Games](https://strangeloopgames.com/user-generated-societies)
- [C] Equilinox (ThinMatrix): "over 120 species of plants and animals"; "Every species has its own requirements, needs, and behaviors"; new species come from "genetically modifying and evolving your species" — [Equilinox](https://equilinox.com/)
- [F] Botany Manor (Balloon Studios, 2024): a puzzle game about "magical flora" whose plants "respond to certain behaviors, such as light or temperature"; the player sets conditions from clues until each plant blooms; Metacritic 84; one review wished "plants could have had more utility in the game" — [Wikipedia: Botany Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany_Manor)
- [C] Wildmender (Muse Games, 28 September 2023): desert gardening survival in a "Procedurally generated world"; players "dig canals", "shape the terrain" and grow "Dozens of plant species", reclaiming desert "Acre by acre", with up to four players; 85% positive of 1,817 Steam reviews — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1599330/Wildmender/)
- [F] Farming Simulator 22 added "a seasonal cycle" and "production chains for harvested crops and livestock products" — [Wikipedia: Farming Simulator 22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farming_Simulator_22)
- [F] A discrete growth-state scheme in a shipped product: the Flight Simulator 2024 vegetation package defines 7 crop species with 7 lifecycle stages each (young, adult, mature, harvested, dormant, stubble, regrowth), and staggers leaf-out, blossom and autumn colour by species — [Orbx product page](https://orbxdirect.com/product/burningbluedesign-global-vegetation)
- [F] Permaculture design software: Permapeople combines a database of "over 8500 plant profiles" with a free planner for placing plants and designing guilds, journals and a seed swap; it models no growth — [Permapeople](https://permapeople.org/)

### Inferences
- Where they all simplify: space is a tile or a score, species are a short authored list, and growth is a timer or a state machine with conditions as gates. None computes light competition between neighbouring plants, a soil water balance or succession among real species. A game that did those three things for real species, even with the simple models of question 5, would be new.
- The commercial lesson from Terra Nil's reviews is that restoration reads well when the feedback is visible on the land and reads badly when it turns into number chasing; the ecological model should surface as visible plant response, not as meters.
- Eco shows that a persistent world-level ecosystem with climate feedback is workable server-side, at the price of abstracting plants into populations. That fits a wild-landscape layer, not the hand-tended plot.
- Discrete growth states (seven in the Flight Simulator package) are the established way to bound art cost. For procedurally generated plants the equivalent is sampling the generator at a handful of developmental ages and blending, with phenology (leaf-out, blossom, fruit, colour, dormancy) as a separate seasonal channel.
