# One fitted cage retires the voxel skin

**The body should become what every shipping body examined is: one closed control cage of four-sided faces, about 10,000 to 14,000 of them, with a fixed layout and texture coordinates, made smooth once at build time by Catmull-Clark subdivision and drawn at its limit surface. The voxel iso-surface stops being the mesh.** The anatomy is kept and changes its role: the field of bones, 206 muscles, fat and skin becomes what a 3D scan is in a studio, the raw shape that a clean cage is wrapped onto, in the same web workers that build the body today. The skin stays on linear blending, as in every engine checked, but with weights measured through the body's volume, twist and helper joints, a shoulder girdle that moves with the arm, and correctives the project trains on its own muscle model, all computed once per frame in a compute pass. Shading stays in the one forward shader and switches to real millimetres: scattering driven by curvature (red light spreads about 1.2 mm in skin, green and blue a quarter of that or less), two GGX highlight lobes at a reflectance of 0.028, light through thin parts from a per-vertex thickness, occlusion tinted by the skin's own colour, and baked tiling detail in place of per-pixel noise, behind a screen curve that does not shift hue. By operation count that fits about a millisecond, but **no source gives a browser timing for any of it and nothing was measured**, so a measuring harness is the first task. Under the rule the cage is written in code and the cost falls on the face, hands and feet; if the rule is lifted, the MakeHuman body (13,380 vertices, CC0) is the lowest-risk template, Anny (Apache-2.0 code over the same CC0 assets), Meta's MHR and NVIDIA's SOMA-X (both Apache-2.0, both distilled from scans) may be shipped, and the SMPL family may not. Today's body, at about 180,000 triangles, is already denser than a MetaHuman at its finest level, so the gap is the representation, not the triangle count; and perception research puts shape before material, which fixes the order of work: shape, then binding, then light, with hair and eyes rising alongside.

## Every shipping body is one quad cage; none meshes an implicit surface

### A cage of 10,000 to 30,000 vertices, a limit surface, and maps

Production pipelines agree to a degree that leaves little to choose: a coarse control mesh of four-sided faces, the cage, is rounded by Catmull-Clark subdivision, in which each round quarters every face and moves the vertices towards a smooth limit surface. Pixar's own documentation says "Subdivision surfaces are commonly used for final rendering of character shapes", with an example character of **30,000 polygons at the cage and 500,000 at the second level** ([OpenSubdiv, intro.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/intro.rst), read). Epic's guide budgets for a MetaHuman are 24,000 vertices for the head and 30,500 for the body at the finest level, falling to 7,600, 3,350 and 1,507 for the body ([Epic, LOD specifications](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/metahuman/platform-support-and-lod-specifications-for-metahumans), extract). Reallusion's base body is 14,046 polygons and its subdivided exports are 56,184 and 224,736, exactly four and sixteen times the base, which is the signature of Catmull-Clark subdivision of an all-quad mesh ([Reallusion manual](https://manual.reallusion.com/Character-Creator-4/Content/ENU/4.0/05_Character/CC3-Plus-Characters.htm); [subdivision export](https://www.reallusion.com/character-creator/subdivision-export.html), extracts). The body models of research sit in the same band: SMPL-X 10,475 vertices ([smplx README](https://github.com/vchoutas/smplx), read), GHUM 10,168 ([GHUM README](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/ghum), read), the MakeHuman body 13,380 vertices and 13,378 quads ([base.obj](https://github.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman/blob/master/makehuman/data/3dobjs/base.obj), measured), NVIDIA's SOMA-X 18,056 ([model card](https://github.com/NVlabs/SOMA-X/blob/main/docs/model_card.md), read), Meta's MHR in seven levels from 73,639 down to 595 ([MHR paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15586v3), extract). All of them share one construction: a fixed template mesh designed by an artist, a linear space of vertex offsets for a person's shape, and linear blend skinning with pose correctives.

Three consequences follow for this project. First, **density is not the problem**. A closed quad mesh makes about two triangles a vertex, so a MetaHuman's finest level is roughly 109,000 triangles for the whole person (computed from the extract above), fewer than today's body. The industry's smoothness comes from the representation. A control cage is coarse and regular, its limit surface is smooth by construction, and both the position and the normal of every vertex can be computed exactly on that limit, where plain refinement gives only "approximations to those of the limit surface" ([OpenSubdiv, subdivision_surfaces.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/subdivision_surfaces.rst), read). Second, fine relief is never geometry in real time: it is a normal map, 2K to 8K in MetaHuman's case ([Epic, materials and textures](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-materials-and-textures), extract), and maps need texture coordinates, which the body does not have. Third, the body is **one mesh**. MakeHuman's body group, SMPL-X and SOMA-X each cover head, trunk, limbs, hands and feet in a single closed surface, and vary density inside it with edge loops, a five-sided junction being used "to expand 3 edge loops into 5" ([OpenSubdiv, mod_notes.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/mod_notes.rst), read). MetaHuman is the exception, with head and body joined only through shared neck joints ([MetaHuman-DNA-Calibration](https://github.com/EpicGames/MetaHuman-DNA-Calibration), read), and it pays for the split with a neck seam that users hide by copying normals across the border ([Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/custom-metahumans-world-normals-mismatching-head-vs-body/2367243), extract). Overlapping pieces that share no border, which is how the body is laid today, cannot be lit continuously; that is the researchers' inference, and it matches the picture.

### Meshing anatomy straight to skin stopped in 1997

What the body does now has a name and a date. Wilhelms and Van Gelder's "Anatomically based modeling" of 1997 states that "skin is generated by voxelizing the underlying components, filtering, and extracting a polygonal isosurface" ([technical report](https://tr.soe.ucsc.edu/sites/default/files/technical-reports/UCSC-CRL-97-10.pdf), extract). The literature after it moved the other way. It takes a good skin as given and grows or fits anatomy inside it: Kadleček and colleagues call reconstructing anatomy from the surface alone "inherently ill-posed" ([project page](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~ladislav/kadlecek16reconstructing/kadlecek16reconstructing.html), extract), and the 2024 HIT model "learns to infer the internal tissues of the human body from its surface" ([HIT README](https://github.com/MarilynKeller/HIT), read). Even Wētā FX, which does simulate muscle for film, describes that route as "setup intensive" ([BodyOpt paper](https://www.wetafx.co.nz/assets/Uploads/PDFs/siggraph2023_bodyopt.pdf), extract). One extended search for work from 2024 to 2026 that reaches a photoreal surface from code-defined anatomy alone found nothing, which is absence of evidence from a single search, not proof.

The lumps and dents are the method's expected result. The implicit-surface literature names the mechanism: "a straightforward implementation produces a bulge where two line primitives touch" ([Bourke](https://paulbourke.net/geometry/implicitsurf/), extract), and a 2013 SIGGRAPH paper lists bulging among the standing problems of implicit blending that it sets out to solve ([Gourmel et al.](https://core.ac.uk/download/50538578.pdf), extract). A real body's surface is not the union of its muscles. It is muscles, fat pads that fill the valleys between them, and a skin of nearly constant thickness stretched over the result and tethered at creases and bony points; a blur of the voxel field removes the valleys only by also removing form. The best published evidence for what a scan-free model can reach is Anny, whose mean error against adult scans is **2.5 mm against 1.9 mm for SMPL-X**, which is trained on such scans ([Anny paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03589v2), extract). But Anny's surface was sculpted by artists and only calibrated with statistics, and its authors warn that its "phenotypes are based on preconceptions of artists" ([Anny tutorial](https://github.com/naver/anny/blob/main/tutorials/shape_parameterization.py), read). The honest ceiling under the rule is therefore an anatomically right, smooth figure. The part of believability that anatomy tables do not hold, how fat sits over muscle, the face, the hands and the feet, comes from artists or scans in every shipping system found.

### Under the rule, the anatomy becomes the scan

The standard step from a raw shape to a production body is template wrapping: a clean mesh with the wanted layout, texture coordinates and skin weights is deformed onto the raw surface, and the raw surface is discarded. It is how scans become film doubles ([Gnomon Workshop](https://accenture.thegnomonworkshop.com/blog/3d-scan-and-retopology-for-production/), extract), how SMPL and GHUM were built from "artist designed ... rigged quad-meshes" ([GHUM README](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/ghum), read), how SOMA-X converts between models with a mesh "wrapped onto the native mesh's surface" ([SOMA-X data assets](https://github.com/NVlabs/SOMA-X/blob/main/docs/data_assets.md), read), and how Anny builds its other layouts ([Anny changelog](https://github.com/naver/anny/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md), read). The textbook method pulls the template to the target through a sequence of decreasing stiffness ([Amberg et al. 2007](https://shapemodelling.cs.unibas.ch/gravis-literature/publications/2007/CVPR07_Amberg.pdf), extract). Automatic quad remeshers are not the answer for a deforming body: Disney Research's verdict that they "are not yet able to achieve the quality of manually created remeshings" still stands in current guides ([Disney Research](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/2013/07/21/sketch-based-generation-and-editing-of-quad-meshes/), extract), and their irregular points land elsewhere each time the shape changes, which defeats shared texture coordinates and weights across many people.

The project's field is a better target than any scan. A scan is a noisy triangle soup with holes; the field is closed, noise-free, and answers at any point with a distance and a direction. Wrapping becomes a short loop in the worker: move each cage sample along the field's gradient by its distance value, hold the cage together with a stiffness that is lowered over the rounds, and pin the samples that belong to landmarks the skeleton already knows, so that edge loops land on elbows, knees and knuckles. **No source documents wrapping a template onto a distance field in a character pipeline; that this is easier than wrapping onto a scan is the researchers' inference from how the methods work**, and it is the report's central technical bet.

The cage itself is written in code: rings of 8 to 16 faces round the trunk and limbs (Pixar: "6 spans are enough to create accurate circular shapes"), with planned five-sided junctions where the body divides ([OpenSubdiv, mod_notes.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/mod_notes.rst), read). The published recipe for growing such a cage from a skeleton, B-Mesh, has a known weakness at branches, and Blender's version of it draws the comment "you'd need to retopologize it" for animation ([B-Mesh](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/CGF.v29i7pp2169-2177); [Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/derailed-thread-are-b-spheres-the-skin-modifier-still-being-worked-on/521382), extracts); a layout specified by hand for the one species in question avoids that. The worker then applies one or two rounds of Catmull-Clark, moves every vertex to its limit position, computes its limit normal, and carries texture coordinates and weights through the same rounds. Levels of detail are the levels of the same cage: the first round for close range (about 80,000 to 112,000 triangles), the cage itself snapped to the limit for mid range (20,000 to 28,000), reduced cages for phones and crowds. SOMA-X does exactly this, its low mesh being "a strict vertex-index subset of the mid-LOD mesh" ([SOMA-X data assets](https://github.com/NVlabs/SOMA-X/blob/main/docs/data_assets.md), read). The dents between muscles disappear because a cage of this density cannot represent them, and whatever true relief the cage cannot hold is baked into a normal map by sampling the field along the limit normal. Subdividing on the GPU every frame buys nothing here, and WebGPU has no tessellation stage in any case ([WebGPU specification](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/blob/main/spec/index.bs), read). Until the cage stands, three repairs inside today's mesh reduce the artefacts without curing them: shading normals taken from the field's gradient in place of averaged face normals, vertices projected onto the field's zero level and then smoothed without shrinkage, and one extraction from one field in place of overlapping pieces. They leave the mesh without texture coordinates, edge loops or levels, and the first is recalled practice that no source confirmed in this session.

## Linear blending stays; weights, extra joints and home-made correctives carry the quality

### Weights measured through the body, not across the air

Fastening each vertex to its four nearest bones by distance is the same class as the lowest tier Unreal offers, "Direct Distance"; the other tier is "Geodesic Voxel" ([Unreal, skeleton editing](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/skeleton-editing-in-unreal-engine), extract). An armpit that passes through the trunk, and a crease at the loins, are what straight-line distance produces, because the inner arm is close to the ribs through the air. Two production methods exist to stop exactly that. Bone heat, as Blender implements it from Baran and Popović's 2007 paper, solves a heat equation over the surface and counts a bone only if a ray from the vertex to the bone hits no triangle, with weights under 0.025 pruned ([Blender, meshlaplacian.cc](https://github.com/blender/blender/blob/main/source/blender/editors/armature/meshlaplacian.cc), read); its known failures are meshes that are not closed or come in several pieces ([Blender issue 103409](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/103409), extract), one more argument for a single cage. Geodesic voxel binding measures distance through the interior of a voxelised body, at a default resolution of 256 in both Maya and Unreal ([Maya geomBind](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2022/ENU/Maya-Tech-Docs/CommandsPython/geomBind.html); [Unreal BindSkeletonToMesh](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/node-reference/Dataflow/BindSkeletonToMesh), extracts), and the project already owns a voxel grid. Either runs in a worker in seconds to tens of seconds by the researchers' estimate and is cached with the body. The smoothest published weights, bounded biharmonic, need the volume cut into tetrahedra, which "can take multiple hours" ([arXiv 2406.00238](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.00238v2), extract), and are not worth it here.

Eight influences should be computed and stored, and four derived for phones by keeping the largest: three.js skins with exactly four ([Skinning.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/nodes/accessors/Skinning.js), read), Godot with four or eight ([mesh_storage.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/servers/rendering/renderer_rd/storage_rd/mesh_storage.cpp), read), Unreal with four or eight by default ([Unreal, rendering paths](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/skeletal-mesh-rendering-paths-in-unreal-engine), extract). The neural riggers of 2025 and 2026 learn from thousands of rigged third-party models and target arbitrary shapes; a body grown from known anatomy knows more than they do. One further option fits this project unusually well. EA's open Dem Bones (BSD 3-Clause) solves "skinning weights from shapes and skeleton poses" ([dem-bones](https://github.com/electronicarts/dem-bones), read), and this project can generate its own example shapes.

### Linear blending, with centres of rotation as the one cheap upgrade

Linear blend skinning moves each vertex by a weighted mix of its bones' movements. Every engine checked ships it as the default and repairs its faults with extra joints and correctives: three.js and Godot by their source ([Skinning.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/nodes/accessors/Skinning.js); [Godot skeleton.glsl](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/servers/rendering/renderer_rd/shaders/skeleton.glsl), read), Unreal and Unity by their documentation ([Unreal Deformer Graph](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/deformer-graph-in-unreal-engine); [Unity MeshDeformation](https://docs.unity3d.com/2023.1/Documentation/ScriptReference/MeshDeformation.GPUBatched.html), extracts). Dual quaternions are not the answer. three.js closed its dual-quaternion pull request unmerged on 7 May 2026 with the words "Dual quaternions skinning (DQS) is no replacement for linear blending skinning (LBS)" ([PR 25902](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/pull/25902), read), and the method trades the collapsing elbow for a bulge ([Kavan and Sorkine 2012](https://www-old.cs.utah.edu/~ladislav/kavan12elasticity/kavan12elasticity.pdf), extract). The cheapest real improvement to the function itself is Disney Research's optimised centres of rotation: one precomputed point per vertex, and a shader that blends the four bone rotations as quaternions and takes the translation from the ordinary blend at that point ([reference shader](https://github.com/AvatarWorld/OptimisedCentresOfRotationSkinning/blob/master/shader/skeletons.glsl), read), which its authors say "significantly reduces the artifacts of LBS and DQS while maintaining real-time performance" ([Disney Research](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/?p=39548), extract). The brief's premise that direct delta mush shipped in Unreal and Unity could not be confirmed: it was found only in libigl and in student or community projects, and EA's own follow-up calls its storage and run-time cost "relatively high" ([EA SEED](https://www.ea.com/seed/news/ddm-compression-with-continuous-examples), extract). None of these functions handles skin touching skin.

### Extra joints first, then correctives trained on the project's own muscles

Shipping bodies get their quality from what is layered on the blend, and they spend it in the places this body breaks. MetaHuman adds corrective joints at torso, shoulders, elbows, hands, fingers, thighs, calves and feet, and runs body correctives only at its finest level ([Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/what-skeleton-does-metahuman-use/741546/2); [MetaHuman body rig](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-stylized-character-body-rig-editing), extracts). Tekken 8 reserves its most capable drivers for shoulders, trapezius, hips and upper femur ([CGWorld, CEDEC 2024](https://cgworld.jp/article/202409-cedec01-tekken.html), extract), and God of War Ragnarök reached its skin with joints alone, laid along the fibres of each muscle ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1029203/Joint-Based-Skin-Deformation-in), extract). Two twist joints per limb segment is the usual figure ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/1834910), extract): the upper arm and the thigh need them, while the forearm already has the anatomical version, a radius that turns about the ulna. A square shoulder with the arm raised is not a skinning fault at all. The scapula supplies roughly 60 of the 180 degrees of a raised arm ([shoulder biomechanics](https://wikimsk.org/wiki/Shoulder_Biomechanics), extract), so the clavicle and scapula must be driven from the humerus before any weight is tuned. The notes list region-by-region cures for armpit, hip, elbow, knee and neck; those are recalled practice, not verified against a source, and belong in the intent as hypotheses to test.

Pose correctives are where the project's anatomy pays back. The industry's current form is a small learned basis of shapes. Unreal's Neural Morph Model is trained on 5,000 to 20,000 poses and costs under 100 microseconds on the CPU and under 50 on the GPU per character on consoles, in about 6 MB for a muscular body ([Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/experimenting-with-daz-characters-and-the-ml-deformer/732897); [80 Level](https://80.lv/articles/unreal-engine-5-1-s-updated-ml-deformer-explained); [80 Level, sample update](https://80.lv/articles/unreal-engine-5-s-ml-deformer-sample-gets-updated), extracts), and its training data comes from an offline flesh simulation, not from scans ([Chaos Flesh quickstart](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/chaos-flesh-quickstart), extract). Wētā's BodyOpt, used on Avatar: The Way of Water, parametrises the learned skin by the **length changes of muscle fibres** ([BodyOpt paper](https://www.wetafx.co.nz/assets/Uploads/PDFs/siggraph2023_bodyopt.pdf), extract), which is precisely the signal 206 strands already produce. The fitting route is therefore an offline solve in the workers, sampled over the range of motion, that relaxes the skin at its thickness over muscle and bone; a compression of those shapes to a small basis; and at run time a few dozen weights driven by joint rotations and strand shortening. It needs no scans and stays inside the rule. The SMPL formulation shows how to feed it, with bounded rotation features applied before skinning ([smplx, lbs.py](https://github.com/vchoutas/smplx/blob/main/smplx/lbs.py), read), though a dense SMPL-style basis would be about 875 MB on this mesh by the researchers' arithmetic, so the basis must be sparse or compressed. A first step that already cannot tear is one smooth shape per muscle group, its weight spread over the surface by diffusion, scaled by that group's shortening. The rule behind it is general: skin tears when a vertex follows a single muscle, so muscles must only ever drive weights of smooth shapes.

### Once per frame, in a compute pass

three.js skins in the vertex stage and so repeats the work for the picture, each shadow cascade and any depth pass; its own maintainers moved their crowd example to compute because "skinning is otherwise recalculated for every pass" ([PR 33644](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/pull/33644), read). Version 0.187 ships the pattern ready: a compute pass writes position and normal to a storage buffer, and a plain mesh reads it back ([webgpu_skinning_instancing_individual](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/examples/webgpu_skinning_instancing_individual.html), read). For the hero body the pass adds correctives and muscle bulge in rest space, skins, rebuilds normals from the deformed positions, and writes buffers that every pass reads; last frame's buffer is the previous position, which matters because a hand-written position gets no motion vector for free and stock morph targets produce none ([Morph.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/nodes/accessors/Morph.js), read). The stock morph targets cannot carry the correctives: at 90,000 vertices one target with normals is about 2.9 MB of 32-bit float texture and every vertex loops over every target, so a hundred would be about 290 MB (computed from the read source). The correctives belong in the project's own sparse buffers. One variant is worth keeping open from the start. If the worker also emits a stencil table, each dense vertex as a short weighted list of cage vertices, the same pass can skin the cage and then apply the stencils, which puts weights and correctives on some 11,000 to 14,000 cage vertices instead of the dense mesh and lets a phone evaluate one round less from the same cage. That is the browser note's inference, with OpenSubdiv's own GPU evaluators recalled as working this way; skinning the dense mesh is the simpler first form and the two should be measured against each other. The researchers estimate skinning, a sparse corrective pass and a normal rebuild at well under 0.2 ms each on an M1-class GPU, **and state that no measured WebGPU skinning number exists in any source found**. Phones keep four influences, centres of rotation at most, a handful of correctives and no smoothing passes, which is MetaHuman's own rule; the compute route's fallback to WebGL 2 is reported as not working for the instanced example, so the old path must be kept for it. Deformation is judged on a range-of-motion set generated from published joint limits, each joint alone and then the known hard combinations, with numbers per pose (volume per segment, depth of self-penetration, stretch, drift of skin at bony landmarks, popping between neighbouring poses) scored against the offline solve as the internal ground truth.

## The whole skin model fits the forward shader when it works in millimetres

### Scattering is a red effect about a millimetre wide

Two families of skin scattering ship. One blurs the lit picture in screen space with a diffusion profile, which needs the diffuse light in its own render target and an extra pass; the other bends the diffuse term inside the lighting function by the surface's curvature. For a forward renderer the second is the established choice: it "can all be achieved within the local context of a single pixel shader", and "it was for this reason that we shipped with a variant of this technique in both The Order: 1886 and Lone Echo, both of which use a forward renderer" ([MJP, introduction to real-time SSS](https://github.com/TheRealMJP/TheRealMJP.github.io/blob/master/posts/sss-intro/index.html), read).

The numbers explain why the present shader reads as wax. The measured skin profile that Jimenez's and Godot's code both hard-code gives a spread (standard deviation) of **1.18 mm in red, 0.26 mm in green and 0.16 mm in blue** (computed from [SeparableSSS.h](https://github.com/iryoku/separable-sss/blob/master/SeparableSSS.h), read); Unity's built-in skin profile says the same with scattering distances of 0.75, 0.32 and 0.20 mm ([SkinDiffusionProfile.asset](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/0efc28ea742b1966b9dd9cc4489e58b986a13faf/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition/Runtime/RenderPipelineResources/SkinDiffusionProfile.asset), read). Set against that, a pixel of a frame drawn at 1,440 lines with a 50 degree field of view covers 0.29 mm at 0.45 m, 0.65 mm at 1 m and 2.6 mm at 4 m (computed; the project's real field of view and inside resolution should replace these assumptions). A diffusion blur therefore does visible work only in close-up, and Unity skips it outright "when the size of this radius is smaller than a pixel" ([HDRP, diffusion profile reference](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition/Documentation~/diffusion-profile-reference.md), read). The softening of the edge of shade depends on curvature in the same way. Expressed as the sharpness of the red lobe, the square of the surface's radius over the profile's variance, it is 6.5 on an ear rim of 3 mm radius, 46 on a finger and 72 on a nose tip, but 1,460 on an upper arm and 16,000 on a torso, and above a few hundred the result cannot be told from plain Lambert shading (computed from the spherical-Gaussian relation in [MJP, SG subsurface](https://github.com/TheRealMJP/TheRealMJP.github.io/blob/master/posts/sss-sg/index.html), read; the threshold is the researchers' judgement). **A fixed wrap applied to the whole body over-scatters exactly the large parts, and that is the waxy look.**

So the wrapped diffuse is replaced by a curvature-driven term, either the pre-integrated lookup or its texture-free spherical-Gaussian form, whose code is public in the posts above. Curvature must be baked per vertex at build time, because curvature from screen derivatives gives "a discontinuity at every triangle edge" and the stated fix is to "precompute it and store it in a texture or per-vertex" (same posts, read). Fine normal detail is softened per colour channel, red most, with the sharp normal kept for the highlight. Light through thin parts is a separate term in every engine: for Burley's profile it is `¼·A·(exp(−s·t) + 3·exp(−s·t/3))` with thickness t in millimetres ([Unity DiffusionProfile.hlsl](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition/Runtime/Material/DiffusionProfile/DiffusionProfile.hlsl), read), which in red passes about 42 % at 2 mm, 14 % at 5 mm and 3 % at 10 mm (computed). That is why ears, nostril wings and finger webs glow red and nothing thicker does; a per-vertex thickness baked beside the existing occlusion feeds it at the cost of two exponentials.

### Two lobes at 0.028, and occlusion that is not grey

The industry's highlight on skin is two lobes of GGX, the standard shape of a rough surface's highlight, sharing one Fresnel term at a head-on reflectance of 0.028, which is an index of refraction of 1.4. Unreal's defaults are lobes at 0.75 and 1.30 times the authored roughness mixed 15 to 85, a broad sheen with a small sharp core ([ShadingModels.ush, UE 4.26.2 mirror](https://github.com/OpenHUTB/engine/blob/2e52492477198332b8d01f6abfb1319f7b5a36f7/Engine/Shaders/Private/ShadingModels.ush), read); Unity's profile carries two smoothness multipliers and the comment "1.4 for skin (mean ~0.028)" ([DiffusionProfileSettings.cs](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition/Runtime/Material/DiffusionProfile/DiffusionProfileSettings.cs), read). The second lobe costs one more distribution term, because visibility and Fresnel are shared. The body's present highlight, two Blinn-Phong lobes by the notes' reading of the shader, should give way to this. A grazing-angle fuzz lobe stands in for the fine hair that gives real skin its soft rim, the effect Koenderink and Pont traced to a thin layer of sparse scatterers ([DOI record](https://unpaywall.org/10.1007%2Fs00138-002-0089-7), extract); three.js already holds a suitable sheen lobe in its physical model ([PhysicalLightingModel.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/nodes/functions/PhysicalLightingModel.js), read). Micro-normals must not sparkle under the upscaler, and Filament's source gives the standard cure, widening roughness by the screen-space variance of the normal ([surface_lighting.fs](https://github.com/google/filament/blob/414602be0e255ac0045d684d7dd5299d760d0de5/shaders/src/surface_lighting.fs), read).

Shade on skin is not grey. The multi-bounce occlusion fit of Jimenez and colleagues, nine multiply-adds as coded in Filament ([surface_ambient_occlusion.fs](https://github.com/google/filament/blob/main/shaders/src/surface_ambient_occlusion.fs), read), leaves medium skin at half visibility with (0.73, 0.65, 0.61) of its light where plain occlusion leaves 0.5 (computed): lighter and shifted to red. Shadow edges carry a red fringe a millimetre or two wide, light "diffusing its way over into the shadowed pixels" that is "very noticeable" ([MJP, introduction to real-time SSS](https://github.com/TheRealMJP/TheRealMJP.github.io/blob/master/posts/sss-intro/index.html), read), which a per-channel remap of the shadow factor reproduces in the forward shader; the exact curve is the researchers' own. Occlusion baked per vertex is wrong wherever limbs move, at the armpit, the inner thigh and the arm against the trunk; three.js 0.187 has a ready screen-space contact-shadow node for one directional light ([SSSNode.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/examples/jsm/tsl/display/SSSNode.js), read), and analytic capsules fastened to the bones are the usual alternative, though that practice is recalled, not sourced.

### Colour from two pigments, detail from baked tiles

Skin colour is two absorbers over a scattering base: melanin, which sets the tone, and haemoglobin, which removes green. The CC0 Physically Based database gives measured linear albedos from (0.847, 0.638, 0.552) for the lightest skin through (0.623, 0.433, 0.343) to (0.090, 0.050, 0.020) for the darkest, and blood coefficients in which oxygenated blood absorbs green about 27 times more strongly than red ([materials.json](https://github.com/AntonPalmqvist/physically-based-api/blob/main/deploy/v2/materials.json), read; ratio computed). An open skin-optics package supplies the spectra, melanin's absorption falling as `6.6e10 · λ^−3.33` ([skinoptics](https://github.com/victorportog/skinoptics/blob/524426e7c4bd5026fbb116f3d9ffbbb22ea9d75c/skinoptics/absorption_coefficient.py), read). A small table of albedo by melanin and haemoglobin amount, computed at load from those published formulas, gives the shader two scalars per pixel and keeps hue shifts physically ordered: more melanin goes brown and never grey, more blood goes pink and never orange. This is the researchers' design, within the rule because it uses published numbers only. The body-wide pattern has measured support, all of it as extracts: the elbow has the lowest lightness and the highest redness among arm and leg sites ([MDPI 2024](https://mdpi.com/2077-0383/13/9/2500)), the forehead the highest redness index and the back of the forearm the highest melanin index ([Université de Liège](https://orbi.uliege.be/handle/2268/88002)), buttock and inner upper arm are the pale unexposed sites ([Schmalwieser et al. 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43630-024-00533-6)). One flat peach colour is, on this evidence, a first-order error: in Zell's experiments the colour texture was the dominant factor within material ([TU Dortmund](https://ls7-gv.cs.tu-dortmund.de/publications/2015-stylize.page/), extract).

Real micro-relief lies far below a pixel at every distance in scope, so its job is to shape and break the highlight, not to be seen as bumps. Evaluating 3D noise per pixel has no mip chain; under a jittered temporal upscaler that is the classic source of shimmer, and by operation count it is probably the body's largest present cost. The repair stays inside the rule: bake the project's own procedural functions once at load into tiling textures with mip maps, and, once the cage has texture coordinates, bake albedo, normal, roughness, thickness and curvature in UV space. three.js 0.187 shows the method in its new `LightMapper`, which draws the mesh with its texture coordinates as screen positions and fills the borders afterwards ([LightMapper.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/examples/jsm/lighting/LightMapper.js), read). The cell sizes and tension-line stretch the notes propose for a pore and furrow generator are their own estimates; the follicle spacings behind them are recalled, not read.

### The screen curve comes first

Skin is a mid-value, low-saturation orange, the colour most easily pushed to yellow, grey or orange by the display transform. Khronos PBR Neutral reproduces the input exactly, less a constant 0.04, for all colours between 0.08 and 0.8, and its specification states "there are no hue shifts" ([Khronos ToneMapping](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/ToneMapping/blob/main/PBR_Neutral/README.md), read); three.js 0.187 carries it as the TSL function `neutral` ([ToneMappingFunctions.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/nodes/display/ToneMappingFunctions.js), read). The game's own curve is `1 − exp(−1.25 · exposure · c)` on each channel separately, then a 2.2 gamma (`TheGame/src/world/light.js`, line 59, first-hand). A curve applied per channel compresses the largest channel most. For the database's second-lightest skin the ratio of red to blue is 2.30 going into the curve and, before the gamma step, 1.79 coming out at a light level of 1 and 1.39 at a level of 2.5 (computed for this report with exposure taken as 1, which is an assumption): sunlit skin loses its colour and drifts towards yellow-white. Every albedo and profile constant in this report is linear and will be judged through that curve, so trying a hue-preserving curve on the body is the first experiment in shading. It is also a change to the whole picture, and so Samuel's decision.

The four classic failure looks each have a checkable cause, assembled by the researchers from the mechanisms cited above:

| Look | Usual causes | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Waxy | A wrap or scatter radius too large for the scene's units; all three channels scattering alike; shadows too soft and bright | Profile in real millimetres, softening driven by curvature, green and blue near Lambert, a crisp micro-normal for the highlight |
| Plastic | One narrow lobe; reflectance of 0.04 or more; uniform roughness; no grazing sheen | Two GGX lobes at 0.028, roughness varying by region, occlusion on the highlight, a fuzz lobe |
| Grey, dead | Grey occlusion and shadows; no red at the edge of shade; one flat albedo | Multi-bounce occlusion, red fringe, haemoglobin variation, a warm bounce from the ground in the sky's light (the game's `skyLight` already has one, first-hand) |
| Orange | Over-saturated albedo; red wrapping everywhere; a curve that skews bright reds | Measured albedo, physical radius, a hue-preserving curve |

### About one millisecond: plausible by operation count, unmeasured

No timing was measured on any GPU and no source read gives a per-component cost, so the budget below is the researchers' ordering by arithmetic. A frame at 1,440 lines is 3.7 million pixels; a standing body covers a few per cent of them at 2 to 4 m and up to all of them in a close-up, which is the worst case.

| Component | Added cost per skin pixel | Extra pass | First to drop on a phone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-bounce occlusion, highlight occlusion, variance-to-roughness | About 30 operations together | No | Never |
| Curvature-driven diffuse (spherical Gaussian or lookup) | About 60 operations, or one texture read | No | Keep; plain Lambert beyond about 3 m |
| Second highlight lobe | One distribution term | No | Late |
| Baked detail normal | One read (3D tile) or three (triplanar) | No | Fades by mip map with distance |
| Light through thin parts | Two exponentials, only where thickness is small | No | Late; it is the one scattering effect visible at 4 m |
| Fuzz lobe; per-channel softened normals | One cheap lobe; two more diffuse evaluations | No | Early |
| Screen-space contact shadows | A march of several depth reads | Yes | Early |
| Screen-space diffusion blur (separable, 2 × 11 taps) | 44 texture reads | Yes, plus a diffuse-only target | First |

A full-screen separable diffusion pass is about 162 million texture reads a frame in close-up, which the notes put near 2 ms on an M1 from a recalled fill rate; it does not fit, and at 2 to 4 m, where it would be cheap, it is invisible. The forward model above is what the sources imply, with a diffusion pass as an optional close-up refinement to judge last. If it is ever built, Babylon.js holds the one complete open reference for the web, a port of Unity's Burley pass with a WGSL twin ([subSurfaceScattering.fragment.fx](https://github.com/BabylonJS/Babylon.js/blob/master/packages/dev/core/src/Shaders/subSurfaceScattering.fragment.fx), read), and its documentation warns that "smaller GPUs won't necessarily have the resources to run this effect" ([Babylon.js PBR documentation](https://github.com/BabylonJS/Documentation/blob/master/content/features/featuresDeepDive/materials/using/masterPBR.md), read). Replacing the per-pixel noise stack with texture reads should pay for most of the additions; that too is an estimate.

## three.js r187 supplies every building block and no skin

Version 0.187 (released 30 October 2026) contains no skin shading of any kind, and two of its names mislead. `MeshSSSNodeMaterial` is the 2011 back-light approximation of Barré-Brisebois, four lines added to each direct light, with no blur and no profile ([MeshSSSNodeMaterial.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/materials/nodes/MeshSSSNodeMaterial.js), read); and the addon `SSSNode` is "Screen-Space Shadows", not subsurface scattering ([SSSNode.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/examples/jsm/tsl/display/SSSNode.js), read). Among 621 example names none shades skin ([files.json](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/examples/files.json), read). Because the body lights through its own function and writes its fragment node by hand, the built-in material would give it nothing anyway; the skin model goes into the project's own function.

| Need | What r187 offers (read in its source) | What it lacks, or the trap |
|---|---|---|
| Skinning | Linear blend, four influences; `computeSkinning`; an example of individually posed instances skinned in one compute pass | More influences, centres of rotation and correctives are custom code; dual quaternions closed unmerged; `BatchedMesh` has no skinning |
| Shape correctives | Morph targets in a float texture array | One texel fetch per target per vertex, a loop over all targets, at most 256 layers by default, no motion vectors |
| Subdivision | Nothing in core | The one official example imports `three-subdivide`: triangles only (Loop), last released August 2023. A WebAssembly port of OpenSubdiv exists since January 2026, about 25 ms for 126,000 output triangles by its author's own figures, little used ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/@nasedkinpv%2fopensubdiv-wasm), read). Write the few hundred lines in the worker |
| Baking maps from code | `rtt`, UV-space drawing as in `LightMapper`, compute into `StorageTexture`, and `unwrapUVs`, new in r187 | No run-time texture compressor, so generated maps stay uncompressed unless an encoder is added |
| A diffuse-light buffer | `pass().setMRT(mrt({...}))`, bilateral and depth-aware blurs | **A hand-written fragment node is not merged into the pass's target layout**: it must itself return the `mrt` structure, and so must every other hand-written material drawn in that pass ([NodeMaterial.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/materials/nodes/NodeMaterial.js#L585-L598), read) |
| Occlusion and contact | GTAO, a cheaper self-denoised SSAO, contact shadows | Contact shadows read a three.js `DirectionalLight` only; no curvature node |
| Upscaling and screen curve | TRAA, TAAU, FSR1; `neutral`, `agx`, `acesFilmic` | TRAA and TAAU need a `velocity` output, which a custom-skinned body must supply itself |
| Timing | `timestamp-query` where the device has it, an Inspector addon | Not on every iPhone; Safari needs Instruments |

Four limits of the platform shape the design. A device gets only WebGPU's default limits unless more are requested, and a requested limit the adapter lacks makes device creation fail: 65,536 bytes per uniform binding (1,024 bones, ample), 32 bytes of colour attachments per sample, textures of 8,192 pixels ([WebGPU specification](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/blob/main/spec/index.bs); [WebGPUBackend.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/renderers/webgpu/WebGPUBackend.js#L70-L97), read). Large generated shaders have failed pipeline creation in Chrome and Safari with "the combined byte size of all variables ... exceeds 8192 bytes"; the reported case is fixed in r187, but a very large hand-written fragment function with unrolled noise sits near the same ceiling, another reason to move noise into textures ([issue 34672](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/34672), read). 32-bit float textures cannot be filtered on roughly 44 % of sampled iOS browsers ([web3dsurvey](https://web3dsurvey.com/webgpu/features/float32-filterable/platform/iOS), extract), so baked maps should be 8-bit or half float. And three.js's known WebGPU slowness is per object, not per triangle, with 20,000 separate meshes at about 15 frames a second against 60 in the old renderer on an M1 Pro, in an issue that is still open ([issue 30560](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/30560), read), so the body and its levels should stay a handful of draws with shared node graphs.

WebGPU itself is on by default in Safari 26 on macOS, iOS and iPadOS, in Chrome on desktop since 113 and on Android 12 and later since 121, and in Firefox on Windows and Apple Silicon Macs ([gpuweb implementation status](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/Implementation-Status), read). On Apple GPUs WebKit always exposes the ASTC and ETC2 compressed formats and exposes BC and 32-bit float filtering only where Metal reports them ([WebKit, HardwareCapabilities.mm](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebGPU/WebGPU/HardwareCapabilities.mm), read). Phones are bounded by memory before speed. WebKit bug reports describe texture-heavy pages on iOS being killed and reloaded with no error to catch, and no byte budget is documented ([WebKit bug 300782](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=300782), extract). The arithmetic is plain: one 4K RGBA8 map with its mip chain is 89.5 MB and a 2K map 22.4 MB, while five 2K maps packed into three textures are about 56 MB uncompressed and 14 MB compressed at 8 bits a pixel (computed). The one published tiered budget found, VRChat's, allows a mobile avatar 10 to 40 MB of textures and 7,500 to 20,000 triangles ([VRChat creator docs](https://github.com/vrchat-community/creator-docs/blob/main/Docs/docs/avatars/avatar-performance-ranking-system.md), read). A phone level is therefore a coarse cage level, four influences, 1K maps and the short end of the shading table. Two iOS-only faults on record, one in three.js during `compileAsync()` and one WebKit crash with instancing, do not reproduce on a Mac ([issue 35069](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/35069), read; [WebKit bug 302711](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=302711), extract), so the phone needs its own soak test.

One finding sets expectations. **No open, locally rendered WebGPU digital human with real skin scattering was found for 2025 or 2026.** The realistic humans shown in browsers are streamed video, as MetaHuman is through Pixel Streaming ([HackerNoon](https://sia.hackernoon.com/pixel-streaming-vs-webgpu-which-one-is-best-for-your-immersive-experience), extract), and among web engines only Babylon.js ships true screen-space scattering. The project would be setting the reference, not following one.

## Shape decides realism first, and every feature must rise together

### What the experiments say

The perception research is consistent, with the caveat that almost all of it studied faces and all of it reached this report as extracts. Zell and colleagues found that "shape is the key attribute for perceived realism" and that "realistic materials do not increase realism of stylized shapes" ([TU Dortmund](https://ls7-gv.cs.tu-dortmund.de/publications/2015-stylize.page/), extract). MacDorman and colleagues found wrong proportions more disturbing on photoreal faces than on others ([IU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/e8fb6458-ddad-4067-b877-1207d720ba0c), extract). A review of the uncanny-valley hypotheses found "good support" for one account only, perceptual mismatch, meaning features of unequal human-likeness such as human skin with computer-modelled eyes ([Kätsyri et al. 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00390/full), extract), and a study of 548 people found that lowering the consistency of realism made humans look eerier and colder ([MacDorman and Chattopadhyay 2016](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/d82b9253-19d7-49a4-87f6-88ea71f80c08), extract). Among shading cues, shadow softness and surface smoothness changed whether an image was taken for a photograph ([Rademacher et al. 2001](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/EGWR.EGWR01.235-248), extract), and Jensen's original scattering paper describes the surface-only face as "more like hard plastic than human skin" ([Jensen et al. 2001](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs667/2005sp/readings/jensen01.pdf), extract).

Two design rules follow, and they are stronger than any single technique in this report. Shape is repaired before shading, because a photoreal shader on a body whose form is still off is predicted to look worse, not better. And features rise together: a body with fine skin and no hair, brows or lashes, or with a mask for a face, is the mismatch case. No study isolating hair was found, so its weight is inferred; hair and eye rendering were outside this research and need their own.

### The numbers and landmarks a lean man's surface needs

What separates a body from a mannequin, in the practitioners' own words, is bone showing where it lies under the skin (without landmarks "the anatomy becomes very soft"), fat and skin bridging the muscles, and asymmetry ([The Rookies](https://blog.therookies.co/2024/01/21/a-rookies-guide-to-sculpting-an-anatomy-study/), extract). A real body has at least three softnesses, sharp creases, firm ridges of bone and soft swells of muscle and fat, and a single blend radius makes clay; landmarks and creases have to be added after the soft blend, not through it. Small errors are large in shading: a bump 1 mm high over 3 cm tilts the normal by about 6 degrees (computed), enough to bend a highlight at any distance where the region covers more than a few pixels, so "smooth" means continuity of normals over centimetres far more than fine tessellation.

Published tissue numbers bound the layer over the muscles. Ultrasound of ten men found whole-body mean subcutaneous fat of about 2 to 6.5 mm ([Störchle et al. 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-34213-0), extract; range computed from its calibration factor), and skin itself measured from about 1.2 mm on the inner forearm to 2.5 mm over the shoulder blade in two ultrasound studies ([PubMed pointer to Laurent et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=Laurent+echographic+measurement+skin+thickness+intradermal+2007), extract; the page of the second study was not identified). For the Body, 1.80 m at about 14 % fat, the researchers' cross-check, which leans on recalled assumptions about internal fat and density, gives a mean fat layer near 4 mm: thickest at lower belly, flank and buttock, near zero over shin, clavicle, sternum, kneecap and the backs of hand and foot. That distribution is indicative, not a sourced table. The ANSUR II survey is public and usable under the rule, but its mean man is 175.6 cm and 85.5 kg ([Zhou et al. 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317634404_Anthropometry_model_generation_based_on_ANSUR_II_database), extract), a body-mass index of 27.7 and roughly a standard deviation heavier than the Body. None of its values was read from the report or the file, because the hosts were blocked. The correct use is a small script that filters the public table ([Penn State Open Lab](https://www.openlab.psu.edu/ansur2/), extract) to men of the Body's stature and build and takes that subgroup's girths.

### References that may be kept, and a protocol to judge against them

One freely licensed colour reference fits the need, with a caveat about how it is known. Mikael Häggström's 2011 studio photographs of a nude man and woman, front and back, about 4,089 by 4,334 pixels, are on Wikimedia Commons under CC0, with the models' consent stated on the page; **this rests on mirrors of the Commons page, which could not itself be opened** ([mirror](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Anterior_view_of_human_female_and_male,_without_labels.jpg); [original, not opened](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Anterior_view_of_human_female_and_male,_without_labels.jpg), extract). The model is recalled as of ordinary build under soft frontal light, good for proportion, landmarks and colour zones and weak for muscle definition. The Cleveland Museum of Art offers seven open-access prints of a standing male model from the circle of Eakins, about 1883, in black and white ([CMA](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/159763), extract). The Visible Human male, 1,871 colour cross-sections at 1 mm spacing, has needed no licence since 2019 ([NLM](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/research/visible/visible_human.html), extract), and is the practical source for the outline of a thigh, calf or forearm at any level; he was a cadaver frozen on his back, so back and calves are flattened. Several other standing male nudes exist on Commons by file name, but their licences were not read and they should not enter the repository until they are. The scan datasets should be treated as off limits even for looking, since tuning a commercial product against them is arguably commercial use: THuman2.0 is "non-commercial research purposes only" ([repository](https://github.com/ytrock/THuman2.0-Dataset), extract) and 3DBodyTex needs an institution's signature ([3DBodyTex.v2](https://cvi2.uni.lu/3dbodytexv2/), extract). The strongest reference a small team can have is its own photographs of a consenting person beside a grey ball and a colour chart, which is not third-party material and is the only way to get matched light.

Realism is judged by eye against photographs under known light, and by blind tests; automatic metrics are proxies. For photoreal avatars nine of ten subjective quality dimensions correlate only weakly with PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS and FID ([Cutler et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.09066v3), extract), and all the pixel metrics need a reference of the same person in the same pose and light, which a generated body does not have. The method is cheap to copy. Fix cameras as presets at 4 m, 1 m and 0.5 m, with three light setups: large and soft to match the Häggström photographs, one hard light raking at about 80 degrees to show every lump, and an environment with a grey ball, chrome ball and colour chart rendered beside the body, the kit of film look-development ([Cave Academy](https://caveacademy.com/wiki/post-production-assets/surfacing/surfacing-training/creating-a-neutral-look-development-environment/), extract). Render the body plain grey first and compare it with a desaturated photograph, which separates shape errors from shading errors in the order Zell's result demands. Overlay silhouettes at equal stature, flip between render and photograph in place, and render stripe reflections to find lumps of a millimetre. Run a short blind "photo or render?" test on people who do not know the project, remembering that untrained observers called photographs correctly 92 % of the time but renders only 60 %, with a bias toward answering "photograph" ([Holmes, Banks and Farid 2016](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/780736), extract), so the reasons people give matter more than the score. The sound use of a metric here is regression: NVIDIA's FLIP between today's and yesterday's frame of the same view ([FLIP paper](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/node/3260/FLIP_Paper.pdf), extract).

## The recommended architecture and its order of work

The architecture is the industry's, with the project's anatomy in the place where studios put scans and simulations. Each layer has a form under the rule, a form if the rule is lifted, and a phone level.

| Layer | Adopt | Under the rule | If the rule is lifted | Phone level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shape source | The anatomy's distance field, with fat and skin as an envelope that bridges the muscles | Unchanged; calibrated with published tables | Unchanged: the shape stays the project's own | Same data |
| Mesh | One closed quad cage of 10,000 to 14,000 faces with texture coordinates, wrapped onto the field; Catmull-Clark to the limit surface in the worker | Cage laid out in code | MakeHuman's CC0 body as the layout, or Anny, SOMA-X or MHR as whole bodies | The cage itself or a reduced cage |
| Detail | Normal, roughness, thickness and curvature baked from the field and from the project's own procedural functions | As stated | Same | 1K maps, 8-bit or half float |
| Binding | Geodesic-voxel or bone-heat weights, eight stored; twist and helper joints; scapula and clavicle driven by the arm | As stated | Template weights as a starting point | Four influences |
| Deformation | Linear blend with centres of rotation, sparse correctives and muscle-group bulge shapes, normals rebuilt, in one compute pass a frame | Correctives trained on the project's own offline solve | Same | Vertex-stage blend, few correctives |
| Light | Forward skin model in millimetres: curvature-driven diffuse, two GGX lobes at 0.028, thin-part transmission, multi-bounce occlusion, red shadow fringe, fuzz lobe | Albedo from published melanin and haemoglobin spectra | Same | The "never drop" rows of the budget table |
| Display | A hue-preserving screen curve, tried on the body first | Samuel's decision for the whole game | Same | Same |

The order of work follows the perception result, shape first, and puts a measurement before every claim of cost.

| Step | Work | Why here | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A reference rig and a timing harness: fixed cameras and three light setups, a plain grey render, GPU timestamps in Chrome | No timing exists in any source; shape must be judged without shading | Today's body is recorded in the standard views with its cost in milliseconds |
| 2 | One cage: layout in code, wrapped onto the field with landmarks pinned, subdivided to the limit with exact normals and texture coordinates, levels from the same cage | Removes dents, seams and faceting at once, and unlocks maps | Stripe reflections run unbroken under raking light; no seam; fewer triangles than today |

| 5 | Light: the screen-curve trial, pigment-based albedo with body zones, the forward skin model, baked detail in place of per-pixel noise | Judged only on a right shape | Side-by-side with the CC0 photographs under matched soft light; cost measured against the 1 ms |
| 6 | Correctives: muscle-group bulge shapes first, then the offline solve compressed to a basis driven by joint rotations and strand shortening; contact at armpit, groin and knee pit | Needs steps 2 and 4 | Real-time path within a set mean and maximum error of the offline solve |

The working tree already holds untracked first drafts of two pieces of step 2, `TheGame/characters/kit/skin/cage.js` and `subdiv.js` (first-hand; they were seen, not reviewed for this report).

What the recommendation stands on differs by part, and should be said plainly. The geometry verdict rests on the firmest ground: Pixar's documentation, the licences and the MakeHuman file were read or measured, while Epic's, Reallusion's and Daz's numbers are extracts that point the same way. The licence table is read at source except the Blender and MetaHuman rows; the Max Planck licence pages themselves were not opened, only the statements and licence files in the models' GitHub repositories, and SMPL's own licence was not read at all. The skinning verdict rests on source code for what engines ship and on extracts for every paper and every studio's rig; its per-region cures are recalled. The shading formulas and constants are read from shipping code, with Unreal's from mirrors of old versions and the papers behind them unopened. The browser findings are read from three.js 0.187.0, WebKit and the specification. The perception, anatomy, measurement and photograph findings are extracts throughout. **Every millisecond is an estimate, the wrap onto a distance field is an inference, and the frame time on a phone is unknown.**

Three decisions are Samuel's. The first is the rule, and it has three positions, not two: keep it whole; lift it for layout only, taking the CC0 MakeHuman body as edge flow and texture coordinates while every millimetre of shape stays the project's; or lift it fully and ship Anny, SOMA-X or MHR. The evidence favours starting under the rule, since the trunk and limbs are within reach of a code-written cage, and holding the layout-only position in reserve for the face, hands and feet, where the notes expect the code-only route to cost most and where anatomy under the skin determines least of what is seen. The second is the screen curve, which touches the whole picture. The third is scope: whether hair and eyes enter this intent or the next, given that a fine skin without them is predicted to read as eerier, not better.

## Conclusion

The research turns the project's premise around without discarding it. The anatomy was built to be the body's surface, and the industry's answer is that anatomy is never the surface; it is the target a surface is fitted to and the simulator a deformation is trained on. In both of those roles the project's own code is not a handicap but an advantage that studios pay for: a distance field is a cleaner target than any scan, and 206 strands that report their shortening are the very signal Wētā parametrises its film characters by. What the rule costs is narrower than it looked. It is the sculptor's knowledge of where edge loops go on a face, a hand and a foot, and of how fat sits over muscle, and since 2020 the first of those has been in the public domain, as a layout that can be taken without taking anyone's shape.

Two things remain unknown, and both can only be found on Samuel's own devices: what the forward skin model and the compute pass cost in a browser, and how much of either a phone will carry before memory, not speed, stops it. Nothing found in the open shows a body of this class rendered locally in a browser, so the measurements the first step produces will be the first of their kind. Three outside facts would change the plan: reading the blocked sources directly, above all Epic's documentation and the Commons licence pages, which could correct numbers here; a three.js release that adds a skin or subdivision node; and a direct look at MHR's and SOMA-X's assets, should the rule ever be lifted in full.
