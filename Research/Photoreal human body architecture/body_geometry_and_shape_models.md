# Photorealistic human body geometry: representation, shape models, open meshes and procedural routes (state of November 2026)

How each claim is known is marked on every line:

- **[read]** read at the source in this session (only GitHub was reachable for direct reading, so "the source" is the repository's own README, licence file or documentation file).
- **[measured]** counted by me on the file downloaded from the source repository.
- **[extract]** taken from a web-search extract of the linked page; the page itself could not be opened (see the access note in the Gaps of the first section). Wording and numbers should be re-checked at the link before they are quoted in public.

"Offline" means a build step (fine for a body that is built in web workers and cached). "Per frame" means a cost paid on every rendered frame.

## Production practice: how MetaHuman, Unity, Reallusion, Daz and VFX houses represent a body

### Takeaway
Every production pipeline found represents the body as an artist-designed, quad-dominant control mesh of roughly 10 000 to 30 000 vertices for the whole figure, with fixed topology shared by all characters, smoothed by Catmull-Clark subdivision and carrying fine detail in displacement or normal maps of 4K to 8K; none meshes an implicit surface for the final asset. The project's current 180 000 triangles are already more than a MetaHuman at its highest level of detail, so the visible gap is topology, smooth normals and map-based detail, not triangle count.

#### Film and VFX
- **[read]** Pixar: "Subdivision surfaces are commonly used for final rendering of character shapes for a smooth and controllable limit surfaces." Its example character is "30,000 polygons" at the cage, "500,000 polygons" at the second level of subdivision — [OpenSubdiv documentation, intro.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/intro.rst)
- **[read]** Pixar's topology rules for a subdivision cage: "They consist primarily of regular faces (quads for Catmull-Clark, tris for Loop)", "They contain few extraordinary vertices", "They efficiently describe the intended shape", and are manifold. "In most situations, 6 spans are enough to create accurate circular shapes." — [OpenSubdiv documentation, mod_notes.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/mod_notes.rst)
- **[read]** Why edge loops matter, in Pixar's words: "areas around the fingers of a hand require more CVs than the comparatively simpler region around the palm. It is important to handle the topology around these transitions efficiently", done with a valence-5 vertex "to expand 3 edge loops into 5". Non-quads "are often encountered in highly deforming areas with curvature saddle-points (ex: arm-torso connection). The strategic placement of a pentagon in one of these critical spots ensures that the surface remains smooth, while allowing for complex topology to flow around." — [OpenSubdiv documentation, mod_notes.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/mod_notes.rst)
- **[extract]** ILM, Davy Jones (2006, old but the pattern is unchanged): characters "went through the pipeline as subdivision surfaces" and "the already dense meshes were displaced with 32bit displacement maps from Zbrush" — [befores & afters, 2020](https://beforesandafters.com/2020/08/19/retro-renderman-ilms-stunning-davy-jones/)

### Inferences
- A closed quad mesh triangulates to about two triangles per vertex, so MetaHuman LOD 0 (24 000 head + 30 500 body vertices) is roughly 110 000 triangles for the whole person. The project's 180 000 triangles exceed that, so adding triangles will not fix "weird shapes and dents"; the industry's smoothness comes from the representation (a low-frequency cage whose limit surface is smooth by construction), not from density.
- Reallusion's subdivided counts are exactly 4 and 16 times the base (14 046 x 4 = 56 184; x 16 = 224 736), which is the signature of Catmull-Clark subdivision of an all-quad mesh. Character Creator 5's "switch between game-ready and film-quality mesh" is therefore the same cage at different subdivision levels.
- MetaHuman body LODs fall by about a factor of four per step at the top (30 500 to 7 600), which matches "one subdivision level less"; the lower steps are authored or decimated reductions.
- The working range for a production body cage (head, trunk, limbs, hands, feet in one figure) is about 10 000 to 30 000 vertices: SMPL-X 10 475, GHUM 10 168, MakeHuman 13 380, Reallusion 14 046 polygons, SOMA-X 18 056, MHR LOD 1 18 439, Genesis 9 about 25 182, MetaHuman body 30 500 plus head 24 000 (figures cited in this and the next section).
- Fine relief (pores, wrinkles, tendons, veins at close range) is never geometry in real time; it is a normal map, plus displacement in film. Because the project's body has no UVs, it cannot carry such maps today; UVs are a precondition for the industry look at 0.45 m.
- Two of the examined pipelines deliberately have no single mesh (MetaHuman head and body) and pay for it with a known neck-seam problem (see the last section).

## Statistical and parametric body models: what each is, size, data, licence, UVs and rig

### Takeaway
The scan-learned academic models (SMPL, SMPL-X, STAR, SUPR, SKEL, OSSO, and HIT because it needs SMPL) are all restricted to non-commercial use and cannot ship in a commercial game; three models released since November 2025 are permissively licensed and usable: Anny (Apache-2.0 code, CC0 MakeHuman assets, sculpted by artists, no scans), Meta's MHR (Apache-2.0, artist-rigged, seven LODs) and NVIDIA's SOMA-X (Apache-2.0, "ready for commercial use", learned from commercially licensed scans). All parametric models share one construction: a fixed artist-designed template mesh, plus a linear space of vertex offsets (blend shapes) for identity, plus linear blend skinning with pose correctives.

- A CC0 mesh can also be used only as a topology, UV and skin-weight template that is re-shaped onto the project's own anatomy (see the section on implicit surfaces); then the shape remains the project's own while the edge flow comes from a public-domain asset.

## Purely procedural generation of a body surface without scan data: what has been published and how far it goes

### Takeaway
Generating skin by voxelising bones, muscles and fat, filtering, and extracting an iso-surface is a 1997 research technique (Wilhelms and Van Gelder), which is what the project does today; the literature after it moved the other way, taking a good skin surface as given and fitting or growing anatomy inside it, and no publication was found that reaches a photoreal body surface from code-defined anatomy alone. Under the "own code only" rule the realistic ceiling is an anatomically plausible, smooth figure; the believable surface in every shipping system comes from an artist-sculpted or scanned template.

### Cited Findings
- **[extract]** Wilhelms and Van Gelder, "Anatomically based modeling" (SIGGRAPH 1997, old): "skin is generated by voxelizing the underlying components, filtering, and extracting a polygonal isosurface", and the skin position is then adjusted toward equilibrium with an elastic membrane model; each skin vertex is anchored to the nearest point on its underlying component — [Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Anatomically-based-modeling-Wilhelms-Gelder/6a109bbdf9ed42da1342dd074274dd31b256d2f9); [technical report UCSC-CRL-97-10](https://tr.soe.ucsc.edu/sites/default/files/technical-reports/UCSC-CRL-97-10.pdf)
- **[extract]** Scheepers, Parent, Carlson and May, "Anatomy-based modeling of the human musculature" (SIGGRAPH 1997, old) developed anatomy-based models of skeletal muscles that flesh out a skeleton, with ellipsoids for muscle bellies — [SIGGRAPH history archive](https://history.siggraph.org/learning/anatomy-based-modeling-of-the-human-musculature-by-scheepers-parent-carlson-and-may/)
- **[extract]** A later upper-limb study that applied this approach reported that the shape near the joints becomes degenerate and that local skin deformation from muscle deformation was not supported (paraphrase of the extract) — [Anatomically-based modeling and animation of human upper limbs](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228918461_Anatomically-based_modeling_and_animation_of_human_upper_limbs)
- **[extract]** Aubel and Thalmann's layered body represents muscles and organs by simple implicit surfaces under a skin that may not penetrate the inner layers — [Realistic Deformation of Human Body Shapes](https://files01.core.ac.uk/download/pdf/147926068.pdf)
- **[extract]** Dicko et al., "Anatomy Transfer" (SIGGRAPH Asia 2013): it "transfers a reference anatomical model from an input template to an arbitrary target character, only defined by its boundary representation (skin)"; "The fat distribution of the target character needs to be specified"; later papers note that its harmonic warp and per-bone affine transforms "might distort muscles and bones in an implausible way" — [Anatomy Transfer project page](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~ladislav/alihamadi13anatomy/alihamadi13anatomy.html); [Inside Humans, 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2021.694244/full)
- **[extract]** Saito, Zhou and Kavan, "Computational Bodybuilding" (SIGGRAPH 2015): creates "a wide range of human body shapes from a single input 3D anatomy template" by simulating "growth of skeletal muscles and subcutaneous fat using physics-based models which combine growth and elasticity" — [project page](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~ladislav/saito15computational/saito15computational.html)
- **[extract]** Kadleček et al., "Reconstructing Personalized Anatomical Models for Physics-based Body Animation" (SIGGRAPH Asia 2016): starts from "a template anatomical model of an average male" and solves an optimisation so the model matches 3D scans; the authors call reconstructing anatomy from surface scans alone "inherently ill-posed" — [project page](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~ladislav/kadlecek16reconstructing/kadlecek16reconstructing.html)
- **[extract]** Wētā FX on its own anatomical route (2023): muscle simulation "can produce great results" but "is also setup intensive and requires intrinsic knowledge of anatomy"; BodyOpt was built so that anatomical simulation can "be applied on a large scale without the need for extensive post-simulation corrections" — [BodyOpt paper](https://www.wetafx.co.nz/assets/Uploads/PDFs/siggraph2023_bodyopt.pdf); [SIGGRAPH history entry](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=227395)
- **[extract]** Ziva VFX (muscle, fat and skin simulation on an anatomical model) was discontinued by Unity in April 2024 and licensed to DNEG; AdonisFX 2.0 (2026), with separate muscle, fat and skin solvers, is described as a possible replacement and is rental-only — [CG Channel, April 2024](https://www.cgchannel.com/2024/04/unity-discontinues-the-ziva-products/); [CG Channel, March 2026](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/03/is-adonisfx-2-0-the-new-ziva-vfx/)
- **[read]** HIT (2024) goes from surface to tissue, not the reverse: it "learns to infer the internal tissues of the human body from its surface" — [MarilynKeller/HIT](https://github.com/MarilynKeller/HIT)
- **[extract]** Convolution surfaces around a skeleton "failed to reconstruct prescribed radii" and were "unable to model large shapes with fine details"; SCALe-invariant Integral Surfaces (Zanni, Bernhardt, Quiblier, Cani, 2013) fix the radius and scale problems; follow-up work says of blending that "the way they merge shapes is difficult to control", and related work notes that "the lack of parameterization makes the interaction with implicit shapes very difficult" — [SCALIS, Computer Graphics Forum 2013](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v32i8pp219-232); [HAL record](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00863523)
- **[extract]** B-Mesh (Ji, Liu, Wang, Computer Graphics Forum 2010): the user gives a skeleton with key balls at its nodes and the system produces a quad-dominant mesh whose "edge flow" runs along the skeleton; the meshes are intended as base meshes for sculpting and skeletal animation — [Eurographics Digital Library](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/CGF.v29i7pp2169-2177)
- **[extract]** Blender's Skin modifier (an implementation of the B-Mesh idea) is "a quick way to generate base meshes for sculpting and/or smooth organic shapes with arbitrary topology"; "The output is mostly quads, although some triangles will appear around intersections." A Blender Artists user: "It does not produce good topology in the sense of good edgeflow for animation – you'd need to retopologize it for that." — [Blender manual: Skin Modifier](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/3.5/modeling/modifiers/generate/skin.html); [Blender Artists thread](https://blenderartists.org/t/derailed-thread-are-b-spheres-the-skin-modifier-still-being-worked-on/521382)
- **[extract]** Anny shows what a scan-free model can reach when the surface is sculpted by artists and only calibrated with published statistics: 2.5 mm mean error against adult scans — [arXiv 2511.03589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03589v2)
- **[read]** Anny's authors on the limits of that route: "Phenotypes are based on preconceptions of artists regarding particular human traits." — [Anny tutorial](https://github.com/naver/anny/blob/main/tutorials/shape_parameterization.py)

### Inferences
- What works under "our own code only, published numbers allowed":
  - Keep the anatomy (skeleton, 206 muscles, fat tables) as the source of shape, since that is the project's distinctive asset, but stop treating the raw iso-surface as the final mesh. Every published system that began this way either stayed at 1997 quality or used the anatomy for deformation under a separately modelled skin.
  - Write a quad control cage in code (rings of 8 to 16 spans around limbs and trunk, following Pixar's "6 spans are enough to create accurate circular shapes", with planned 5-valence vertices at shoulder, hip, finger and toe roots), fit it to the implicit anatomy, and render its subdivision limit surface. B-Mesh and the Skin modifier are published recipes for generating such a cage from a skeleton; their stated weakness is edge flow at branches, which a hand-specified layout for one known species (a human) avoids.
  - Published tables (skinfold thicknesses, anthropometric girths, WHO growth data as Anny uses) remain legitimate inputs for calibrating the shape.
- What needs third-party data: the part of believability that anatomy tables do not contain, namely the sculpted superficial forms (how fat pads sit over muscle, the face, hands and feet, the knee and elbow in the rest pose). In every shipping system this comes from artists (MakeHuman, MetaHuman topology, Reallusion, Daz) or scans (SMPL, MHR, SOMA-X, MetaHuman's shape database).
- "Weird shapes and dents" are the expected result of the current method: a union of 206 separately swept muscles has valleys between neighbours that real bodies fill with fascia and fat, and a voxel blur removes them only by also removing form. The literature's answers are (a) an explicit smooth envelope layer around the muscles (Saito et al. are reported to show "that a layer that envelopes muscles yields more convincing muscle growth simulations", via [Inside Humans, 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2021.694244/full)) and (b) a low-degree-of-freedom surface (a cage or a template) that cannot represent high-frequency dents at all.
- The hardest regions for a code-only approach are face, hands and feet, where anatomy under the skin determines little of the visible form at 0.45 m; these are the first places where a CC0 template would pay off if the rule is lifted.

### Gaps
- The original 1997 papers, Saito 2015 and Kadleček 2016 could not be opened, so their own limitation sections are not quoted; the limitations above come from later papers describing them.

## From an implicit surface to a clean mesh: artefacts, cures, and whether the project's implicit anatomy can play the role of the scan

### Takeaway
The standard industry step from a raw shape to a production body is template wrapping (non-rigid registration): a clean, pre-authored quad topology with UVs and skin weights is deformed onto the raw surface, and the raw surface is then discarded; automatic quad remeshers are used for static or sculpting meshes, not for deforming hero characters. The project's signed-distance anatomy is an unusually good "scan" for this step, because it is watertight, noise-free and offers exact distances and gradients, so wrapping a cage onto it is an offline build step that fits the existing web-worker pipeline.

### Cited Findings

#### Artefacts of grid extraction
- **[extract]** Marching cubes places vertices on cube edges and shows artefacts near features that are not aligned with the grid; its poor triangles "can be fixed easily by edge collapses" — [DTU: Isosurface Polygonization notes](https://people.compute.dtu.dk/janba/gallery/polygonization.html); [Dual Contouring of Signed Distance Data, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00157)
- **[extract]** Surface nets put one vertex in each cell, at the cell centre or the average of the edge crossings; implementers who do this "have given up on using the gradient information"; "it at least has simplicity going for it" — [Boris the Brave: Dual Contouring Tutorial](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2018/04/15/dual-contouring-tutorial/)
- **[extract]** Dual contouring places the cell vertex using surface normals, reproduces sharp features, and "can sometimes generate a 3d surface that intersects itself" — [Boris the Brave: Dual Contouring Tutorial](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2018/04/15/dual-contouring-tutorial/)
- **[extract]** A 2026 paper refines dual-contouring vertex positions using the signed-distance samples themselves instead of exact normals — [Dual Contouring of Signed Distance Data, arXiv 2604.00157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00157)
- **[extract]** Normal errors are what the eye sees: a neural dual contouring paper uses the percentage of inaccurate normals as a quality metric because it correlates with surface quality — [Neural Dual Contouring, arXiv 2202.01999](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01999)

#### Smoothing and remeshing
- **[extract]** Taubin smoothing alternates a positive-lambda Laplacian step with a negative-mu step so that the mesh is low-pass filtered without the shrinkage of plain Laplacian smoothing; typical defaults are lambda 0.5 to 0.63 with mu slightly larger in magnitude — [PyTorch3D mesh filtering source](https://pytorch3d.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/pytorch3d/ops/mesh_filtering.html); [Lawlor's Taubin notes](https://www.cs.uaf.edu/~olawlor/projects/2003/taubin/index.html)
- **[read]** Instant Meshes ("Instant Field-Aligned Meshes", 2015): solve "for an orientation field" and then "a position field", then export; interactive brush tools can steer both fields — [wjakob/instant-meshes README](https://github.com/wjakob/instant-meshes)
- **[read]** QuadriFlow (Symposium on Geometry Processing 2018): "We take a manifold triangle mesh `input.obj` and generate a manifold quad mesh `output.obj`"; "By default, `quadriflow` does not explicitly detect and preserve the sharp edges in the model" — [hjwdzh/QuadriFlow README](https://github.com/hjwdzh/QuadriFlow)
- **[read]** QuadWild ("Reliable Feature-Line Driven Quad-Remeshing", ACM Transactions on Graphics, SIGGRAPH 2021) produces "isometric, pure-quad, conforming meshes, making it an ideal candidate for CAD models", driven by sharp feature lines — [nicopietroni/quadwild README](https://github.com/nicopietroni/quadwild)
- **[extract]** Disney Research (2013, still echoed by 2025 guides): "existing automatic quad remeshing algorithms are not yet able to achieve the quality of manually created remeshings", and artists want "explicit control over the edge flows and the singularities of the quad mesh" for animation — [Disney Research: Sketch-Based Generation and Editing of Quad Meshes](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/2013/07/21/sketch-based-generation-and-editing-of-quad-meshes/)
- **[extract]** Current practitioner guidance: manual retopology "for characters and deforming objects where edge flow around joints and facial features is critical"; automatic remeshing for static props, print models or quick LODs — [Meshy glossary: retopology](https://www.meshy.ai/3d-glossary/retopology)

#### Template wrapping and non-rigid registration
- **[extract]** Wrap (R3DS, now Faceform Wrap) takes "a base mesh with the desired topology" and uses "an optimization approach to automatically fit the base mesh to the surface of the scan"; scans processed this way need "almost no cleanup" according to the vendor; a ZBrush plug-in (ZWrap) does the same — [RWTH AR-for-EU book: 3D Scanning and Animation](https://codereality.dbis.rwth-aachen.de/ar-for-eu-book/chapter/digitalContent/3dscanning-animation/); [Faceform Wrap](https://russian3dscanner.com)
- **[extract]** Amberg, Romdhani and Vetter, "Optimal Step Nonrigid ICP Algorithms for Surface Registration" (CVPR 2007, old and still the textbook method): the template is pulled to the target through a sequence of decreasing stiffness weights; at each step correspondences are found from the displaced template vertices and the optimal deformation for that stiffness is solved exactly; it handles holes in the scan — [paper PDF](https://shapemodelling.cs.unibas.ch/gravis-literature/publications/2007/CVPR07_Amberg.pdf); [use for body scans, arXiv 1911.00694](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.00694)
- **[extract]** SCAPE, BlendSCAPE and SMPL were each built on an artist-generated template mesh and an algorithm that registers that template to real human scans (paraphrase of the extract) — [arXiv 1911.00694](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.00694)
- **[read]** GHUM started from "initial, artist designed, gender neutral rigged quad-meshes" registered to scans — [GHUM README](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/ghum)
- **[read]** SOMA-X converts between body models with wrapped meshes: for each other model it ships "`SOMA_wrap.obj` -- the **SOMA-topology** mesh wrapped onto the native mesh's surface", from which a barycentric transfer is precomputed; "a Laplacian blend at the boundary smooths the transition" where the wrap leaves a region uncovered — [SOMA-X data assets](https://github.com/NVlabs/SOMA-X/blob/main/docs/data_assets.md)
- **[read]** Anny builds its alternative topologies "by retopology": target vertices are projected onto the source surface, and midline vertices "onto the intersection of the source mesh with its symmetry plane, rather than onto the closest surface point, which was ambiguous over concave midline regions" — [Anny CHANGELOG](https://github.com/naver/anny/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)
- **[extract]** Epic's "From Custom Mesh" and Body Conform tools conform the fixed MetaHuman topology to an arbitrary input mesh — [Epic: From Custom Mesh tool](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-creator-from-custom-mesh-tool-in-unreal-engine)

### Inferences
- Yes, the implicit anatomy can play the role of the scan, and it is an easier target than a scan. A photogrammetry scan is an unordered, noisy, holed triangle soup; the project's field gives, at any point, the signed distance and (by finite differences or analytically) the gradient. The registration loop becomes: for each cage or limit-surface sample, step along the gradient by the distance value until the sample sits on the zero level; regularise with a stiffness term (Laplacian or as-rigid-as-possible) that is lowered over the iterations, as in Amberg et al.; pin landmarks (joint centres and bony landmarks the skeleton already knows) so that edge loops land on elbows, knees, knuckles and the like. Landmark pinning corresponds to Wrap's point pairs.
- Because the fitted mesh is low-frequency by construction, the dents between muscles are filtered by the representation, with no blur of the field needed. Detail that the cage cannot hold can be baked into a normal or displacement map by sampling the field (distance from the limit surface to the zero level along the normal), which requires UVs on the cage.
- Immediate, cheap improvements that stay inside the current architecture (all offline):
  - Shade with normals taken from the gradient of the signed-distance field at each vertex instead of averaged face normals. This removes most of the lumpy look of surface nets at 6 mm cells, because the shading no longer inherits the grid.
  - Project surface-net vertices onto the zero level with a few gradient steps, then apply a few Taubin iterations to regularise triangles without shrinking.
  - Extract one watertight surface from one field instead of overlapping pieces; where finer cells are needed, use an octree or adaptive grid in the same extraction so borders are shared.
  These reduce artefacts but leave the mesh without UVs, edge loops or an LOD chain.
- Automatic quad remeshing of the iso-surface (Instant Meshes, QuadriFlow) is feasible offline and permissively licensed, but its singularity placement changes whenever the shape changes, which defeats shared UVs, shared skin weights and morph-compatible LODs across many people. A fixed cage wrapped onto each generated body keeps one topology for all people.
- Under the rule, the cage is written in code by the project. If the rule is lifted, the MakeHuman CC0 body (13 380 vertices, quads, UVs, rig weights) is a ready template to wrap onto the project's field; the result's shape is the project's own.

| Technique | Offline build or per frame | Own code only? |
|---|---|---|
| Gradient normals from the field | Offline (stored per vertex), then skinned like any normal | Yes |
| Projection of vertices onto the zero level, Taubin smoothing | Offline | Yes |
| Isotropic or field-aligned quad remeshing | Offline | Yes if re-implemented; BSD-style code exists |
| Template wrapping onto the field (non-rigid registration) | Offline | Yes with a code-written cage; or with a CC0 template |
| Baking field detail into normal or displacement maps | Offline bake, per-frame cost is one texture fetch | Yes, needs UVs |

### Gaps
- Botsch and Kobbelt's isotropic remeshing ("A Remeshing Approach to Multiresolution Modeling", 2004) and bilateral mesh denoising were not confirmed by any source in this session. [recalled] The remeshing loop is: split long edges, collapse short edges, flip edges to equalise valence, tangentially relax, reproject onto the original surface.
- The statement that gradient normals cure lumpy shading on coarse grids is standard practice [recalled] and was returned only as the search tool's synthesis, not as a quotation from a source.
- No source was found that documents wrapping a template directly onto a signed-distance field in a character pipeline; the claim that it is easier than wrapping onto a scan is my inference from how the methods work.
- As-rigid-as-possible regularisation (Sorkine and Alexa, 2007) was not looked up. [recalled]

## Subdivision surfaces in real time: once on the CPU versus on the GPU, limit normals, cage size, adaptive tessellation on the web

### Takeaway
For a web target the practical choice is to subdivide the cage once at build time, store limit positions and limit normals in a static mesh, and skin that mesh as usual, with lower LODs being the lower subdivision levels of the same cage; per-frame GPU subdivision needs compute shaders (WebGPU has no tessellation stage) and buys nothing for a figure whose densest level is already affordable. A good body cage is about 10 000 to 25 000 vertices, giving about 80 000 to 200 000 triangles after one level.

### Cited Findings
- **[read]** OpenSubdiv "is optimized for drawing deforming surfaces with static topology at interactive framerates"; "Maya and Pixar's proprietary Presto animation system can take 100ms to subdivide a character of 30,000 polygons to the second level of subdivision (500,000 polygons). Being able to perform the same operation in less than 3ms allows the user to interact with the smooth, accurate limit surface at all times." — [OpenSubdiv intro.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/intro.rst)
- **[read]** Why the control cage must not be drawn directly: "The polygonal control hull is an approximation that is offset from the true limit surface." — [OpenSubdiv intro.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/intro.rst)
- **[read]** Refined points are not on the surface and their normals are approximations: "Subdivision will refine a mesh of arbitrary topology, but the resulting points will not lie on the limit surface and any normal vectors contrived from and associated with these points will only be approximations to those of the limit surface." — [OpenSubdiv subdivision_surfaces.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/subdivision_surfaces.rst)
- **[read]** The cure: "The recursive nature of subdivision does give rise to formulae that allow a point on the limit surface to be computed that corresponds to each point of the cage. This process is often referred to as "snapping" or "pushing" the points of the cage onto the limit surface." Normals can likewise be evaluated "explicitly for arbitrary locations on the surface just as we do for position." — [OpenSubdiv subdivision_surfaces.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/subdivision_surfaces.rst)
- **[read]** Each uniform subdivision step multiplies the control points by "roughly 4x" — [OpenSubdiv subdivision_surfaces.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/subdivision_surfaces.rst)
- **[read]** In regular regions (all quads, valence 4) the Catmull-Clark limit surface is a set of bicubic B-spline patches; around extraordinary vertices it "cannot be represented as simply", which is why feature-adaptive subdivision isolates them — [OpenSubdiv subdivision_surfaces.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/subdivision_surfaces.rst)
- **[read]** "Catmull-Clark is more widely used and suited to quad-dominant meshes, while Loop is preferred for (and requires) purely triangulated meshes." — [OpenSubdiv subdivision_surfaces.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/subdivision_surfaces.rst)
- **[read]** High-valence vertices "incur fairly large performance hits" and OpenSubdiv's GPU shaders cap valence at about 27; with triangles around a pole "The Catmull-Clark scheme can produce "wavy" surfaces" — [OpenSubdiv mod_notes.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/mod_notes.rst)
- **[read]** OpenSubdiv's GPU back ends are OpenGL 4.1+, Metal 3+ and vendor compute APIs; no web back end is listed — [OpenSubdiv README](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv)
- **[extract]** WebGPU's pipeline stages are vertex, fragment and compute; tessellation "is not support in WebGPU, as least not in v1" (Babylon.js maintainer); mesh shaders may come later as an extension — [Babylon.js forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/will-webgpu-make-tessellation-techniques-available-in-babylon-js/38624); [MDN: WebGPU API](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/WebGPU_API)
- **[extract]** Compute-shader Catmull-Clark without hardware tessellation exists: Dupuy and Vanhoey, "A Halfedge Refinement Rule for Parallel Catmull-Clark Subdivision" (2021); Dupuy and Deliot's compute-based adaptive triangulation shown in Unity (SIGGRAPH 2022 Advances in Real-Time Rendering); "Edge-Friend" (High-Performance Graphics 2023), "simple to implement in a single compute shader kernel" — [Eurographics Digital Library, cgf.14381](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14381); [SIGGRAPH 2022 course slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/LebCatmullClark.pdf); [Edge-Friend paper](https://diglib7.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.1111/cgf14863/v42i8_01_14863.pdf)
- **[read]** three.js has had no built-in subdivision modifier since r125; the third-party three-subdivide implements Loop subdivision on BufferGeometry under the MIT licence, with an option to limit by triangle count — [stevinz/three-subdivide](https://github.com/stevinz/three-subdivide)
- **[read]** A nested LOD chain from subdivision levels is used in practice: SOMA-X's low mesh (4 505 vertices) "is a strict vertex-index subset of the mid-LOD mesh" (18 056 vertices) — [SOMA-X data assets](https://github.com/NVlabs/SOMA-X/blob/main/docs/data_assets.md)

### Inferences
- Recommended real-time form for this project (all subdivision offline, nothing per frame beyond ordinary skinning):
  1. Author or fit a quad cage of about 10 000 to 14 000 quads for the whole body.
  2. In the build worker, apply one or two Catmull-Clark levels, then move every vertex to its limit position and compute its limit normal (closed-form limit masks; this is the "snapping" OpenSubdiv describes). The result is a static mesh with exact smooth normals and no stair-steps.
  3. Carry UVs and skin weights through the same subdivision weights so that the dense mesh is skinned exactly like the cage would be.
  4. LODs: level 1 limit mesh for close range (about 40 000 to 56 000 quads, 80 000 to 112 000 triangles), the cage snapped to the limit surface for mid range (about 20 000 to 28 000 triangles), and decimated or hand-reduced cages for phones and crowds. Compare MetaHuman body LODs 30 500, 7 600, 3 350, 1 507 vertices and MHR's seven levels from 73 639 to 595.
- With normals from the limit surface, a level-1 mesh at 100 000 triangles will look smoother than the present 180 000-triangle iso-surface, because smoothness is carried by exact normals and regular quads, and silhouettes at 0.45 m are helped by the limit positions.
- Per-frame GPU subdivision (compute shader) would only matter if the cage itself were deformed by something other than skinning each frame (for example simulated muscle bulging at cage level) and the dense mesh had to follow. Even then the cheaper web solution is to skin the dense mesh and apply corrective blend shapes. It also has no WebGL 2 fallback, since WebGL 2 lacks compute shaders [recalled].
- Adaptive tessellation is of low relevance here: one hero figure at 100 000 to 200 000 triangles is not the bottleneck at 1440p on an Apple M1-class GPU compared with skin shading, and distance LODs handle crowds.
- Loop subdivision of the current triangle soup would not help: subdivision smooths a coarse, well-shaped cage; applied to a dense iso-surface it only multiplies triangles around the same dents.

### Gaps
- No measurement of skinned-vertex throughput for three.js 0.187 on an Apple M1 was looked up; the triangle budgets above are reasoned from the production LOD tables, not from a benchmark.
- The closed-form Catmull-Clark limit position and tangent masks were not re-read at a source in this session [recalled]; they are standard and are implemented in OpenSubdiv's far-field evaluation.
- The SIGGRAPH 2026 Advances in Real-Time Rendering course lists a talk titled "Adaptive Tessellation and Subdivision" ([slides link returned by search](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2026/content/Adaptive%20Tessellation%20and%20Subdivision_7.pdf)); its content could not be opened and may update the compute-based state of the art.

## One mesh or several: how seams between head, body, hands and feet are avoided

### Takeaway
Body models and character systems that look seamless use a single watertight topology for head, trunk, limbs, hands and feet, and vary vertex density inside that one mesh with edge-loop transitions rather than by overlapping finer pieces; where a pipeline does split head from body (MetaHuman), it shares the border vertices and joints and still has to copy normals across the border to hide the seam.

### Cited Findings
- **[measured]** MakeHuman's `body` group is one connected quad mesh of 13 380 vertices covering head, trunk, limbs, hands and feet; eyes, teeth, tongue and lashes are separate helper groups — [makehuman base.obj](https://github.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman/blob/master/makehuman/data/3dobjs/base.obj)
- **[read]** SMPL-X is "a unified body model with shape parameters trained jointly for the face, hands and body" in one mesh of 10 475 vertices — [vchoutas/smplx README](https://github.com/vchoutas/smplx)
- **[read]** SUPR's authors train one whole-body model and only then "separate the model into a full suite" of head, hand and foot part models, noting that part models built in isolation "fail to capture the head and hands full range of motion" — [ahmedosman/SUPR](https://github.com/ahmedosman/SUPR)
- **[read]** SOMA-X has one skin mesh per LOD (`c_skin_mid`, `c_skin_lo`, `c_skin_xlo`); body parts are "Semantic segments (vertex id lists)" on that mesh, and each vertex has a mirror index across the midline — [SOMA-X data assets](https://github.com/NVlabs/SOMA-X/blob/main/docs/data_assets.md)
- **[read]** MHR's identity vector covers the whole figure in one model: 20 components for the body, 20 for the head, 5 for the hands — [facebookresearch/MHR README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/MHR)
- **[extract]** Reallusion's CC3 Base+ counts the head (4 758 polygons) as part of the 14 046-polygon full body — [Reallusion manual: CC3 Base+ Characters](https://manual.reallusion.com/Character-Creator-4/Content/ENU/4.0/05_Character/CC3-Plus-Characters.htm)
- **[read]** How density varies inside a single subdivision mesh: "areas around the fingers of a hand require more CVs than the comparatively simpler region around the palm"; an extraordinary vertex is used "to expand 3 edge loops into 5" — [OpenSubdiv mod_notes.rst](https://github.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenSubdiv/blob/master/documentation/mod_notes.rst)
- **[read]** MetaHuman is the exception: the head connects to the body through the joints "neck_01, neck_02, FACIAL_C_FacialRoot" — [MetaHuman-DNA-Calibration README](https://github.com/EpicGames/MetaHuman-DNA-Calibration)
- **[extract]** The cost of the split: Epic warns that when head and body are supplied separately, large differences in proportions give a poor neck seam; forum users fix visible seams by copying custom normals from the body onto only the boundary edges of the head, and by aligning material settings (the head material enables bent normals, the body does not) — [Epic: From Custom Mesh tool](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-creator-from-custom-mesh-tool-in-unreal-engine); [forum: normals mismatching head vs body](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/custom-metahumans-world-normals-mismatching-head-vs-body/2367243); [forum: UV seam skin tone inconsistency](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/metahuman-uv-seam-skin-tone-inconsistency-with-static-lighting-settings/2652500)
- **[read]** When two topologies must meet (SOMA-X transferring another model's shape where the wrap does not cover the head), "a Laplacian blend at the boundary smooths the transition" — [SOMA-X data assets](https://github.com/NVlabs/SOMA-X/blob/main/docs/data_assets.md)

### Inferences
- The project's present layout (trunk, legs and head in one extraction, arms in another, and finer overlapping grids for head, hands and feet) is the opposite of industry practice and is a direct source of visible artefacts: overlapping pieces have different vertex positions, different averaged normals and no shared border, so lighting cannot be continuous across them.
- The fix under the "own code only" rule is a single cage whose density rises toward face, hands and feet through planned edge-loop transitions. One mesh then gives identical positions and normals at what used to be borders, by construction. Finer anatomical detail in head, hands and feet still comes from the 2 mm fields, sampled when fitting the denser regions of the cage and when baking normal maps.
- If rendering needs separate draw calls (different skin shading for the face, or streaming the head at a higher LOD than the body, as MetaHuman does with two head LODs per body LOD), the split should be made after subdivision along an edge loop, duplicating the loop's vertices with identical positions, normals and skin weights on both sides. That is the only reliable way to keep the split invisible, and LOD changes on either side must keep that loop's vertices fixed.
- UV seams are a separate matter from geometric seams: a single watertight mesh still has UV cuts, which is normal and is hidden by baking maps with edge padding and by placing cuts along the inner arm, inner leg and back of the head [recalled as general practice].

### Gaps

- No authoritative written source was found for the general rule "duplicate the border loop with identical normals and weights"; it is inferred from the MetaHuman forum fixes and from how skinned meshes shade.
- How Daz Genesis 9 and Reallusion treat eyes, mouth interior and nails (separate meshes parented to the body) was not researched in detail.
