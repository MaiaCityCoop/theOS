## 1. For each asset kind: is it supported, since which version, experimental or production, by what mechanism, with what stated limits?

### Takeaway
Nanite began in 5.0 as one mechanism for one kind (rigid, opaque triangle meshes) and reached today's coverage by adding a separate mechanism per kind over nine releases: a programmable rasteriser for masked, two-sided and vertex-animated materials (5.1), spline deformation (5.3, production in 5.4), run-time tessellation (5.4), skinning (5.5), and assemblies plus voxels for foliage (5.7). In November 2026 only rigid meshes, masked and two-sided materials, spline meshes and heightfield landscape carry no warning label; tessellation, static displacement, Nanite Foliage (assemblies, voxels, wind) are still marked Experimental, vertex-animated foliage is "Beta", morph targets are unsupported, and translucency exists only behind a console variable that is off by default.

### Inferences
- **Nanite did not become generic through one algorithm.** The cluster hierarchy with a pixel-error cut is the one shared idea; every kind beyond the rigid opaque mesh got its own code path (programmable rasteriser bins, skinning, spline deformation, patch tessellation, voxel rasteriser, assembly transforms in culling) and, in 5.9, its own culling class. The order in which kinds arrived (materials first, then deformation, then aggregates last) is the order of difficulty Epic found.
- **Time to maturity is long.** Masked materials took one release to arrive and three more of optimisation (20 % in 5.3, 30–50 % in 5.4, raster sorting in 5.5). Spline meshes took two releases to production. Tessellation is Experimental five releases after its introduction. Skinned meshes are two to four releases in without a clear production label. Foliage voxels are Experimental three releases in.
- **Epic's coverage still depends on per-asset switches.** Preserve Area or Voxelize per mesh, Max Edge Length Factor per mesh for deformed meshes, Explicit Tangents per asset, Max World Position Offset Displacement per material, Enable Tessellation per material, a wind-data asset per skeleton. "Nothing written per asset" is not what Nanite delivers; it delivers "no LOD authored per asset".
- For the four asset families named in the brief: rocks → the production path; flat-faced tents and buildings → the production path, with the faceted-normal and low-poly cautions of section 5; skinned creatures → supported since 5.5, with animation detail (not geometry) reduced by distance and no morph targets; trees of tubes and alpha-cut cards → the one family Epic says to re-author (geometry instead of cards) and for which its far-distance answer is still Experimental.

### Gaps
- The exact release in which Nanite skeletal meshes stopped being called experimental is not stated anywhere read. The 5.5/5.6 page calls them experimental; the 5.7 notes say "Skinning (Experimental)"; the 5.7–5.9 Working page has no warning; no release note announces a change of status.
- Nanite translucency: no page describes how it is rasterised, shaded or limited, and no release note read introduces `r.Nanite.AllowTranslucency`. The only statements are one sentence on the 5.9 transparency page and one bug-fix line in RN 5.8 ("Fix Invalid Material log spam for translucent materials on Nanite meshes"). The version in which the variable first appeared is unknown.
- Whether Nanite is supported on iOS or through the 5.9 WebGPU target was not stated on any page read.

## 2. What remains unsupported or discouraged, and why: Epic's wording on aggregate geometry, thin geometry, translucency; Preserve Area and Voxelize, and which is recommended now

### Takeaway
Epic's definition of aggregate geometry and its two consequences (it cannot be simplified as hard as a surface, and it defeats occlusion culling) are word for word the same in the 5.9 documentation as in 5.0. The documented, stable advice is still "geometry instead of masked cards" plus Preserve Area on every foliage mesh; the engine's own API calls Preserve Area the "Legacy foliage technique" and Voxelize the technique that preserves "the perceived volume", but Voxelize sits behind an Experimental project setting and a warning that its options may not work.

### Cited Findings

**Aggregate geometry**
- [RD] Definition: "Aggregate geometry is geometry that has many disjointed parts that become a volume in the distance, such as hair, leaves on trees, and grass. This type of geometry breaks Nanite's level of detail and occlusion culling techniques." — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content); the same sentences are in [Main 5.0](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine?application_version=5.0)
- [RD] Why level of detail breaks: "Nanite is inherently a hierarchical level of detail structure that relies on being able to simplify small triangles into larger triangles and choosing the coarser one when it determines the difference is smaller than can be perceived. For continuous surfaces, this works well, but not for aggregate geometry that from a distance appears more like a partially opaque cloud than a solid surface. As such, Nanite is more likely to determine it cannot reduce aggregate geometry nearly as aggressively as it would typical solid surfaces, thus resulting in drawing more triangles for the same number of pixels covered." — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)
- [RD] Why culling breaks: "Although it is very fine-grained, its granularity is not per-pixel. Geometry that is filled with holes — and worse yet, layers upon layers of geometry filled with holes — causes excessive overdraw because many depth layers need to build up before that area on the screen will block anything behind it. One way to think about this is to consider an 8x8 pixel region on screen and how many depth layers need to be drawn before every pixel is filled." — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)
- [RD] The scope of the failure: for such content "scaling with pixels — and not scene complexity — no longer applies", "but this doesn't mean it should not be used at all for this content, or that it will not render faster than the traditional rendering pipeline." — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)
- [RD] The same diagnosis in the 2021 talk: "Riddled with holes sounds like a content problem but actually describes most aggregate geometry cases like leaves and grass. Overdraw is one of many reasons Nanite doesn't perform as well with those." And as future work: "Visibility prefiltering means expressing partial coverage"; "Triangle mesh stops making sense"; "Best solution is a hybrid": "Superpixel features as mesh", "Subpixel features expressed volumetrically"; "How to render partial coverage volumetric data? Stochastic? Ray march? Point scatter? OIT?"; "Subpixel partial coverage and prefiltering is especially important for aggregate geometry like leaves and grass. Solving that case is still an open question." — [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)

**Preserve Area**
- [RD] The fullest description: "For assets like trees with default Nanite settings you might find that the canopies tend to thin out with distance. These cases are a particular form of Aggregate Geometry where each disjoint part (a leaf or grass blade) has open edges at its boundary. ... When Nanite simplifies the geometry in the distance by reducing the number of triangles, it eventually needs to start removing some of these disjoint elements completely. Without Nanite having more information the result will look thinned out because there was major surface area loss. Preserve Area will redistribute that lost area to the remaining triangles by dilating out the open boundary edges. Dilation for symmetric shapes like leaves has the same effect as scaling them up. In non-symmetric cases like ribbons, for example, blades of grass, it has the effect of thickening them." — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)
- [RD] Recommendation on that page: "We recommend using Preserve Area for all foliage meshes, but not for meshes that are not intended to be used as scene foliage." In the settings table of the 5.9 page: "This setting should be enabled on all foliage meshes and nothing else." — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content); [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)
- [RD] The section that gives this advice is labelled: "Foliage using Nanite is considered Beta and is being actively researched and developed. This section provides some guidance on using foliage geometry with Nanite, but is not the Nanite Foliage feature set." — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)

**Voxelize**
- [RD] The engine enum, unchanged between 5.7 and 5.8: `None` — "Do not attempt to preserve the object's shape in the distance."; `PreserveArea` — "Try to maintain the same surface area at all distances (Legacy foliage technique)."; `Voxelize` — "Simplify triangles to voxels in the distance to preserve the perceived volume of the object." — [API, ENaniteShapePreservation (5.8)](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/ENaniteShapePreservation?application_version=5.8)
- [RD] How Epic now describes Preserve Area on the Foliage page: "Previous Nanite techniques for preventing leaves from losing their shape in the distance, worked well enough by adding a surface area back into surviving triangles when the simplification process would eliminate them. It was a mitigation for foliage geometry becoming sparse when rendering simplified clusters in the distance." — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] Enabling: Project Settings > Rendering > Nanite Foliage (editor restart), then per static mesh "Locate **Shape Preservation** and use the dropdown to select **Voxelize**", with the warning: "Some properties below this dropdown become available to edit when **Voxelize** is set. These are not intended for most, if anyone, to adjust for their projects. Some are still in development and don't actually work as intended yet, if at all. These settings are likely to change or be removed in future versions of Unreal Engine as Nanite Foliage matures." — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] The settings table on the 5.9 Technical page still lists a "Preserve Area" property and has no row for Shape Preservation or Voxelize. — [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)

**The older foliage advice (still on the 5.7–5.9 page)**
- [RD] "Use geometry instead of masked cards." "Masked materials are fairly expensive compared to Opaque ones. The fastest results are likely obtained by not using them at all." "The traditional card approach (many elements are represented with a single card) with Nanite can be slower than non-Nanite. Do not expect that enabling Nanite on card-based foliage will always be a performance improvement." "Masked-out pixels cost nearly as much as drawn pixels." "Geometry foliage has shown to be faster with Nanite than card approaches, both Nanite cards and non-Nanite cards. It also looks better." "When using World Position Offset (WPO), more vertices equal higher cost." — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)
- [RD] "We are still experimenting and learning ourselves what are the best approaches. So far, we have seen that foliage using Nanite should be authored differently than before". — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)
- [RD] The Foliage page's list of what "doesn't work well": "**Alpha Masking:** Nanite doesn't work well with it since it introduces a lot of overdraw in addition to having to execute a potentially expensive mask function. Using alpha masking also means that foliage would continue to be rendered as flat cards". "**Full Nanite Triangle Representation:** This would cause sub-optimal cluster culling and poor simplification in the distance. Also, each foliage type would require large amounts of disk space". "**World Position Offset (WPO):** ... adds per vertex calculations and forces sub-optimal cluster bounds since there's no way to know how a material will move a vertex". — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)

**Closely stacked surfaces**
- [RD] "when there is stacked geometry that is close together near the top-most surface, Nanite is not able to determine which is on top or bottom, causing both to be drawn simultaneously." "Imprecision like this scales with screen size and distance, so while 10 centimeters may separate layers and look fine while close to the surface, farther distances away can cause the difference in distance to be smaller than a pixel, resulting in overdraw." Geometry "buried well below the visible surface" is culled "fairly cheaply". — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)

**Thin and sub-pixel geometry**
- [RD] Epic's treatment of thin geometry is in the anti-aliasing documentation, not the Nanite pages: temporal super resolution's history rejection "is overly aggressive on sub-pixel geometry — thin structures like Nanite foliage, hair, and fine mesh edges that only partially cover a pixel. The result is flickering. This happens because the partially-covered pixel's color oscillates between foreground and background as the jitter pattern shifts." Thin Geometry Detection estimates per-pixel coverage over time and relaxes rejection; it "is automatically enabled when Nanite foliage is turned on". — [Thin Geometry Detection with Temporal Super Resolution](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/thin-geometry-detection-with-temporal-super-resolution) (5.8, 5.9; updated 29 June 2026)
- [RD] Its limits: "**Spatially uniform coverage loss.** When all pixels in the local neighborhood lose coverage under the same jitter offset, there is no local contrast to distinguish thin geometry from a scene change. This can happen with dense parallel lines". "As a content guideline, avoid geometry with dense, uniformly sub-pixel parallel lines." "Thin geometry detection is not free. It adds compute passes". — [same page](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/thin-geometry-detection-with-temporal-super-resolution)
- [RD] On voxels and thin features in the two talks: "Features which vanish as signed distance fields when they are thinner than a voxel / Or leak attributes from one side to the other when they are less than a few voxels" ([Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)); "features thinner than the width of a voxel at that level of detail are not expressible as an SDF. Voxels would have to express some sort of coverage information to handle this." ([Deck 2022](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf))

**Faceted meshes and low-poly meshes**
- [RD] "low amounts of vertex sharing in a mesh can become significantly more expensive both in rendering performance and data size. Ideally, the number of vertices for a mesh should be less than the number of triangles it has. If the ratio is 2:1 or higher, then there is likely a problem ... Having a ratio of 3:1 means the mesh is completely faceted". "ratios higher than 2:1 fall down some slow rendering paths. Intentional use in hard surface modeling shouldn't cause any issues, and there is no reason not to use them. However, accidental 100% facet very dense meshes are far more expensive than intended." — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)

**Translucency**
- [RD] See the table in section 1. No page read gives Epic's reason for the lack of translucency, and the 2021 deck does not contain the word. What the deck does state is the design it conflicts with: the visibility buffer slide reads "Write geometry data to screen — Depth : InstanceID : TriangleID", one entry per pixel. — [Main](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine); [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Which is recommended now" has two answers, and they do not agree. The pages a developer reads for a shipping project recommend Preserve Area on all foliage. The engine's API labels that technique legacy. The replacement is Experimental and Epic warns that its controls may not work. A reader should treat Voxelize as Epic's direction and Preserve Area as Epic's shipped answer.
- Epic's own text shows the 2021 "future work" slide becoming the 2025 feature almost line by line: mesh above a pixel, volume below it, a filtered normal distribution, stochastic rendering. The four years between them is the measure of how hard the aggregate case was for the team that knew the system best.
- Epic's discouragement of alpha-cut cards is about Nanite's cost model (a masked-out pixel costs as much as a drawn one, and cards hide many elements behind one quad), not about image quality alone. An engine whose trees are thousands of alpha-cut leaf cards starts from the representation Epic's documentation says to replace.
- Thin geometry is handled after rasterisation, by the temporal filter, and only with an accumulated coverage history. Without a temporal filter, sub-pixel-thin triangles flicker by Epic's own account.

### Gaps
- No Epic page gives numbers for Preserve Area (how far edges dilate, measured coverage against the original).
- The Nanite pages have no section named for thin geometry (wires, fences, hair); only the temporal super resolution page addresses it.
- Hair: named in the definition of aggregate geometry; Unreal renders hair with a separate strand system that was not read.

## 3. Nanite Voxels: how a cluster becomes voxels, what a voxel stores, how it is shaded, its status and known defects

### Takeaway
The switch is decided when the mesh is built, cluster by cluster, by comparing errors: a simplified cluster is stored as voxels wherever that gives lower error than more triangle simplification. A voxel cluster holds at most 128 bricks of 4×4×4 voxels; each voxel keeps coverage, material and a distribution of normals sampled by tracing rays through it. At run time the usual Nanite selection draws them at about one pixel each, front to back, and shades them by picking one normal at random per pixel from the stored distribution. It has been Experimental since 5.7; neither the 5.8 notes nor the 5.9 Preview notes change that.

### Cited Findings

**What they are and when triangles hand over**
- [RD] "**Nanite Voxels** are near pixel-sized aggregate voxels that preserve triangle details, animation, and material properties based on camera distance. Triangles seamlessly switch to voxels to solve the problem of rendering dense and dynamic aggregate triangle meshes. These voxels are imperceptible to the eye because of their size on screen." — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] "As the camera pulls away, once Nanite clusters become small enough they seamlessly transition to voxels. This allows trees to retain their volumetric look at distances without resorting to billboards or LODs in the distance." — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] **The rule**: "Nanite Voxels can preserve the general silhouette of aggregate geometry (disconnected pieces) during simplification at distance. When simplifying clusters of these voxel-enabled meshes, Nanite Builder voxelizes them into clusters of, at most, 128 4x4x4 voxel bricks. The simplification error of the voxel representation is the deciding factor for if the simplify makes the switch to voxels from triangles — it will switch to voxels if doing so would result in lower error than using triangles." — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] The 5.7 notes' one-line description: "pixel sized voxels that are able to retain details, animation and material characteristics based on the distance from the camera, all at a fraction of the original cost". — [RN 5.7](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-7-release-notes?application_version=5.7)

**Drawing**
- [RD] "At runtime, voxel clusters are binned separately from triangle clusters and rasterized with a special shader permutation. The clusters are sorted into depth buckets, and rasterized from front to back to gain some benefits of early Z testing, with the end result generally performing better in the distance over rasterized triangles." — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] The ancestor of the brick format, from Karis's 2022 account of an abandoned prototype: "Bricks are scattered to the screen. The simplest form of this is rasterizing a cube per brick. Within a brick I do a short ray cast through the brick data." "A uint64 expresses a 4x4x4 brick's occupancy." "a ray can be marched through the brick using DDA. Checking a voxel bit at a time. Only a single uint64 read is needed for the entire ray march of the brick". Timings on that prototype: point rendering "2ms on a PS4 Pro vs 9-10ms for 2 level brick tracing". — [Deck 2022](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf)

**What is stored and how it is built**
- [RD] "Many surfaces with different normals can be contained inside the region covered by a single voxel. So instead of storing a single normal per voxel, a distribution of normals is stored instead. This is similar in concept to how material roughness controls the distribution of microfacets used for shading." — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] "This requires extra data to store the statistics for the distribution. Currently that is stored in the vertex color alpha channel meaning it will override whatever was there. This may move to separate data independent from vertex color in the future." — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] The build settings structure (identical in 5.7 and 5.8) names the voxel controls: `ShapePreservation`; `NumRays` — "Number of rays traced per voxel to sample the surface"; `bVoxelNDF`, `bVoxelOpacity`, `bSeparable`, `RayBackUp`, `VoxelLevel` (these five have no description). — [API, FMeshNaniteSettings (5.8)](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/FMeshNaniteSettings?application_version=5.8); [5.7 version](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/FMeshNaniteSettings?application_version=5.7)

**Shading**
- [RD] "When writing to the GBuffer, a normal is chosen stochastically per pixel from this distribution. In a future release the distribution will be accounted for directly in the shading like roughness is. This will reduce noise and was already in use in the Witcher 4 demo." — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage) (page updated 18 June 2026, i.e. after the 5.8 release)
- [RD] The idea was on the 2021 future-work slide: "Normal distribution could be filtered — Similar to normal map prefiltering to roughness — SGGX (volumetric) — Needs to apply to diffuse as well — Fed to BxDF"; and its first line: "Can't prefilter BxDF — Material assignment is unknown". — [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)

**Status**
- [RD] 5.7: section title "Nanite Foliage and Skinning (Experimental)". — [RN 5.7](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-7-release-notes?application_version=5.7)
- [RD] 5.8: the release notes contain no entry on Nanite Voxels and no change of status for Nanite Foliage. A search of the whole page for "voxel" finds two lines, neither naming Nanite: one on hair ("groom's group index voxelization") and one unlabelled line in the general rendering fixes ("Fixed voxel quantization causing voxel resolution to drop/increase, having side effect (running out of voxel pages)"), which sits among hair fixes. — [RN 5.8](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes?application_version=5.8)
- [RD] 5.9 Preview: the only foliage entry is the wind plugin; its page, written for 5.9, tells the reader to "enable **Nanite Foliage (Experimental)**, which sets `r.Nanite.Foliage`". The Foliage page, which applies to 5.7, 5.8 and 5.9, carries the readiness value `ue_experimental`. "Nanite Foliage is not enabled by default for projects." — [RN 5.9 Preview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-9-release-notes); [Dynamic Wind for Nanite Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/dynamic-wind-for-nanite-foliage-in-unreal-engine); [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] The procedural tree tool that produces these assets: "PVE is experimental. Substantial architectural changes can occur between releases." Its output is "a Nanite asset that uses the latest Nanite Foliage technology, including instancing, GPU-based skeletal-driven animation, and voxel representation in the distance." — [Procedural Vegetation Editor](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/procedural-vegetation-editor-in-unreal-engine) (5.8, 5.9; updated 4 August 2026)
- [RD] No talk on Nanite Foliage or Nanite Voxels is in the SIGGRAPH "Advances in Real-Time Rendering" course of 2025 or 2026; Epic's 2025 entry is MegaLights. — [2025 course page](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/index.html); [2026 course page](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2026/index.html)

**Known defects and limits, in Epic's own pages**
- [RD] Noise: one random normal per pixel until the distribution is "accounted for directly in the shading" ("This will reduce noise"). — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] The vertex colour alpha channel is overwritten. — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] Voxel build options "don't actually work as intended yet, if at all". — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] Sub-pixel foliage flickers under temporal super resolution without Thin Geometry Detection (section 2). — [Thin Geometry Detection](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/thin-geometry-detection-with-temporal-super-resolution)
- [SE] Reported by users on Epic's forum, carried over from the earlier round and not re-read: holes in voxelised meshes (with a "Separable" flag and a voxelisation bias as counter-measures), poor ray-traced shadows with voxelised foliage, wrong two-sided sign once foliage turns to voxels, voxels not helping near-to-mid overdraw. — earlier notes, `/home/user/theOS/research_notes/Smooth automatic level of detail/aggregates_and_far_field.md`, section 3 (sources there: forums.unrealengine.com threads)
- [SE] One search digest claimed that the 5.8 release notes list fixes for "GPU crash in Nanite Voxel rasterizer" and for voxel foliage not rendering on some platforms. The 5.8 release-notes page read directly contains neither line; if they exist they are in a hotfix list elsewhere. Treat as unconfirmed. — search digest, attributed by the tool to [the 5.8 forum announcement](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/unreal-engine-5-8-released/2729274)

### Inferences
- **The rule is an error comparison, not a distance or a tag on the asset's parts.** That is what makes it automatic: connected surfaces keep simplifying as triangles because triangles keep the lower error; disconnected leaves go to voxels early because removing them costs more error than resampling them. The same mesh can hold both, cluster by cluster.
- But the rule is **not applied to every mesh**: it runs only on meshes whose Shape Preservation is set to Voxelize. Epic keeps a person in the loop to mark a mesh as foliage, exactly as with Preserve Area.
- Arithmetic from the stated limit: 128 bricks × 64 voxels = at most 8,192 voxels in a cluster.
- A voxel is a **statistical** sample: `NumRays` rays per voxel, with opacity and a normal distribution as outputs (the option names `bVoxelOpacity`, `bVoxelNDF` say as much, though Epic gives them no description). That is prefiltering of visibility and normals, not of the material: the 2021 slide's objection ("Can't prefilter BxDF — Material assignment is unknown") still holds, and the shipped shading is a random pick, with the proper filtered shading promised for "a future release" in a page updated after 5.8.
- The published quality claim is perceptual and unmeasured: "imperceptible to the eye because of their size on screen". No error figure, test image or comparison against the full-detail tree is given anywhere in the documentation.

### Gaps
- Not documented by Epic: voxel memory per tree, the screen size at which typical clusters turn into voxels, GPU time of the voxel pass, how material attributes are stored per voxel, how animation is applied to voxel clusters, how voxels are ray traced or shadowed.
- "Near pixel-sized" is the only statement of size on screen. The "LOD is chosen such that voxels are <= 1 pixel wide" wording in the earlier notes comes from a mirrored social-media post by Karis and could not be re-read. [RC] It is consistent with `r.Nanite.MaxPixelsPerEdge` = 1 applying to voxel clusters as to triangle clusters; not confirmed.
- The Unreal Fest Stockholm 2025 talk, the only long technical account, is a video and could not be read.

## 4. Nanite Assemblies and Nanite Skinning: what they are, how one very large asset is made of repeated parts, how wind works, the figures

### Takeaway
An assembly is one Nanite mesh whose cluster hierarchy points at the clusters of shared part meshes instead of copying them; the part transforms are applied during culling, and at the coarse end the parts are merged into the base mesh so that the whole asset still ends in one cluster. This cut the largest Witcher-demo tree from 3.5 GB to about 29 MB on disk. Skinning moves trees with bones instead of a vertex shader so that cluster bounds stay tight and everything stays on the fixed-function rasteriser; a hundred thousand bones update in about 0.1 ms.

### Cited Findings

**Assemblies**
- [RD] "**Nanite Assemblies** ... designed to efficiently handle the rendering of complex, repeatable geometry, such as branches and fronds of a tree. Assemblies work with both static and skeletal mesh assets, allowing for the 'micro-instancing' of small, highly detailed parts. They can contain up to 65k instances of other meshes. These instances are called the 'parts' of the assembly. This approach saves a significant amount of disk space and streaming space, with a small performance trade-off during cluster culling." — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] Mechanism: "Nanite assemblies work by taking the clusters of the part meshes and encodes them into the final mesh's hierarchy without duplicating the geometry of the parts during build time. At runtime, Nanite handles the part instances by calculating their final transform on-demand during cluster culling as it descends the hierarchy." — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] Structure: "a collection of **Parts** meshes ... instanced across a single piece of foliage. These instances transform their position, rotation, and scale within the assembly's local space. You can combine these parts with a non-instanced base mesh, such as a tree trunk". For skeletal meshes "these parts can be attached to one or more bones, with individual weights". — [Assemblies](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-assemblies)
- [RD] Detail across distance: "at close distances to the camera, the individual parts of an assembly render as their Nanite Instances, preserving all their geometric detail. As the objects become smaller on screen, these parts are simplified, such that Nanite's rendering system intelligently reduces the complexity until the entire assembly is represented by a single, simplified cluster." — [Assemblies](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-assemblies)
- [RD] Why not ordinary instancing: "Standard instancing has a significant memory overhead in GPU scene buffers ... with each visible instance requiring at least one root cluster be drawn. Instancing thousands of branches on thousands of trees would mean rasterizing millions of clusters in even the simplest of situations". "Assemblies have a much lighter weight overhead per instance, because they are simply a large array of matrices." "during build time, root clusters of assembly parts are combined to create the lowest-resolution levels of the assembly so that are far distances, assemblies simplify all the way down to a single cluster, which is similar to having its own internal hierarchical level of detail (HLOD)". "At farther distances, these simplified levels of individual part instances are merged together, with their geometry getting treated as part of the base mesh — the parts lose any identity of being an instance. For this reason, per-instance data isn't exposed in material graphs for assembly parts." — [Assemblies](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-assemblies)
- [RD] Figures: "the UASSET disk space of the biggest tree in the demonstration went from being **3.5 gigabytes** (Gb) to approximately **29 megabytes** (Mb). The streaming memory of just one of the trees in one particular view was measured to go down from approximately **36 Mb** to around **2.7 Mb**. This kind of saving makes it possible to render 500k instances of dozens of tree variants in a scene, with each tree being highly detailed and having between one and ten million polygons each." "Without them, there wouldn't be enough disk space or streaming memory to render a large-world forest". — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage); same figures on [Assemblies](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-assemblies)
- [RD] The example tree on the Foliage page: "41 million triangles; 12 unique assembly parts; 2160 assembly part instances; 850 skeletal bones". — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] Platforms without Nanite: "the triangles of the parts are transformed and merged together into a single mesh. From there, it is simplified to generate a fallback mesh". — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] "Known Limitations": "You cannot create assemblies of other assemblies. The system currently supports a single layer of instancing." "Geometry is not duplicated between assemblies that reference the same part meshes. Each assembly stores its own copy of the part data." "For skeletal meshes, you cannot pose individual parts — they will only render in their bind pose." And: "At this time, the vertices of skeletal assembly parts are **not skinned**, so they only animate rigidly". — [Assemblies](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-assemblies)
- [RD] Authoring: four routes, all "experimental plugins": Nanite Assembly Editor Utilities (Blueprint builders for static and skeletal assemblies), a PCG interop for static assemblies, the Procedural Vegetation Editor, and USD import with the schemas `NaniteAssemblyRootAPI`, `NaniteAssemblyExternalRefAPI`, `NaniteAssemblySkelBindingAPI`. In data, an assembly is two lists: "Parts — The list of assembly parts" and "Nodes — The list of assembly part instance nodes". — [Assemblies](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-assemblies); [API, FNaniteAssemblyData (5.8)](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/FNaniteAssemblyData?application_version=5.8)
- [RD] 5.8 fixes that show where it broke: "Added some new Nanite Assembly build errors when the data exceeds certain internal limits (would previously crash)"; "Fixed an issue where small Nanite Assembly parts could disappear"; "Fixed a bug where it will not simplify a Nanite Assembly static mesh fallback if there's no geometry in the base (non-part) mesh"; crashes with parts with no bone influences or different vertex formats. — [RN 5.8](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes?application_version=5.8)
- [RD] The wish in 2021: "Hierarchical instancing desired for the future — Instances of instances of instances". — [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)

**Skinning and wind**
- [RD] Why not a vertex shader: "Because the WPO for a given vertex can be any arbitrary offset, artists are required to set the **Max WPO Displacement** on any of materials for the worst-case in order to help pad the culling bounds ... However, this can result in overly conservative bounds that contribute to high overdraw cost. Because WPO also changes vertex shader logic, each material is binned into separate dispatches ... With many materials, these programmable raster bins can be a performance issue. **Nanite Skinning** resolves this because more accurate bounds can be calculated from skinning matrices and everything can use the fixed function rasterizer." — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] Figures: "there are one-hundred thousand bones updating, which takes about 0.1 milliseconds on the GPU". "Animation stops being applied automatically on trees that are below a certain screen size, with wind being able to affect trees very far in the distance". — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] Performance tips: "Virtual Shadow Map (VSM) performance requires you to disable animation in the distance." "Instanced meshes that are disabled switch to an unskinned raster bin and render like a fixed function static mesh (in bind pose)." "Use **Animation Min Screen Size** on the component ... If left at **0**, the global default value is used, which is 1/10th (0.1) of the object's screen size" (`r.Skinning.DefaultAnimationMinScreenSize`). — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] Bone weights are a build setting: `BoneWeightPrecision` — "Blend Weight Precision in bits. -1 is auto. 0 is rigid." — [API, FMeshNaniteSettings (5.8)](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/FMeshNaniteSettings?application_version=5.8)
- [RD] The wind plugin as documented for 5.7–5.8 (on the Foliage page): experimental; bones are classified into simulation groups by data imported from JSON; "Only has support for global wind direction"; "No collision with players or objects". — [Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [RD] The wind plugin as documented for 5.9 Preview: "simulating the bones of skeletal plant meshes on the GPU, instead of offsetting vertices in the material. Each bone's mass and wind-catching area come from the mesh itself". Two motions per bone, "Sway" and "Flutter". Two tiers: "Plants that only feel the global wind share a small set of simulations, one per wind direction. When a force source, push capsule or external velocity volume reaches a plant, that plant is **promoted** to its own simulation, which starts from the shared motion so there's no visible pop." A budget of individually simulated instances per mesh (project default 256). "Dynamic Wind needs the Shader Model 6 (SM6) feature level. Below it, plants don't move." Limits: "Each bone simulates on its own as a spring. There's no full constraint solver between bones." Still "an Experimental plugin". — [Dynamic Wind for Nanite Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/dynamic-wind-for-nanite-foliage-in-unreal-engine) (5.9; updated 9 November 2026)
- [RD] The tree generator reduces bones for real time: "The default skeleton is generally too complex for real-time animation, and this node reduces its complexity." — [Procedural Vegetation Editor](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/procedural-vegetation-editor-in-unreal-engine)
- [RD] Skinned characters at scale (5.8): the experimental MetaHuman Crowd plugin gives "seamless transitions between high-fidelity individual actors and lower-fidelity Instanced Skeletal Meshes based on camera distance", "rendered using Nanite or dynamic LOD systems". — [RN 5.8](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes?application_version=5.8)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic on Epic's figures: disk 3.5 GB → 29 MB is about 120 times smaller; streaming memory 36 MB → 2.7 MB is about 13 times smaller. The first measures how repetitive a tree is; the second measures what a view actually needs. The example tree's 2,160 part instances over 12 unique parts is an average of 180 uses per part.
- An assembly solves three things at once for a repeated-part asset: storage (parts stored once per assembly), the instance floor (no root cluster per twig; the tree ends in one cluster) and far simplification across parts (root clusters of parts are merged and simplified together). The third is the important one for quality: without it, each twig would bottom out separately.
- It is deliberately limited: one level, no sharing between assemblies, no per-part data, rigid parts. A procedural generator that already knows its tree is "trunk plus N copies of a few twig meshes" has the assembly structure for free; Epic needs USD schemas and editor tools to recover it from authored content.
- Wind by bones rather than by a vertex shader is chosen for culling and batching reasons, not for looks: bounds are computable from matrices, and all trees share one rasteriser path. The cost is a skeleton per tree (850 bones in the example) and an import step for wind data.

### Gaps
- No figure for the per-instance cost of an assembly part at run time beyond "a small performance trade-off during cluster culling".
- No documented limit on bones per mesh. The only numbers are 850 bones in one tree and 100,000 bones in about 0.1 ms.
- "Animation banks" (instanced skinned animation) are named but not explained on any page read.

## 5. Quality: how the error threshold relates to visible change, the controls that set quality, the fallback mesh, known artefacts and the advice given

### Takeaway
Nanite's quality rule is one number: draw the level whose estimated error projects to less than about one pixel (`r.Nanite.MaxPixelsPerEdge` = 1, "the triangle edge length that the Nanite runtime targets"), and rely on temporal anti-aliasing to hide the switch. Epic states the result as "Loss of quality is rare or non-existent", admits the error estimate "isn't perfect and can fail", and lists a set of artefacts that sit outside the rule: cracks at tessellation seams and landscape borders, pop-in from streaming, over-simplification of deformed meshes, blinking geometry on buffer overflow, flicker of sub-pixel geometry.

### Cited Findings

**The claim and the principle**
- [RD] Benefits list: "Level of Detail (LOD) is automatically handled and no longer requires manual setup for individual mesh's LODs"; "Loss of quality is rare or non-existent, especially with LOD transitions". — [Main](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine)
- [RD] "move the camera close enough and Nanite will draw the original source triangles that were imported." — [Main](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine)
- [RD] "regardless of the geometric complexity of the source data in the scene, the number of triangles Nanite attempts to actually draw to the screen is consistent and proportional to the number of pixels. Nanite follows the design principle that there is no use in drawing far more triangles than there are pixels." — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)
- [RD] The selection rule in the documentation's words: "choosing the coarser one when it determines the difference is smaller than can be perceived". — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)

**Why switching is not seen (2021 talk)**
- [RD] Slide "Seamless LOD": "Binary choice either parent or child — Won't this visibly pop? — Need smooth transitions? — Geomorphing — Cross fading — If < 1 pixel error, they are imperceptibly different — Temporal AA sees any difference as aliasing". Speaker notes: "if we only draw clusters that are less than 1 pixel of error they are imperceptibly different and temporal antialiasing smoothes out any change. TAA is built to blend subpixel differences over time. It does our work for us so long as the error is subpixel. This is the reason why getting an accurate error estimate is so important." — [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)
- [RD] On the error estimate: "This estimated error is later projected on screen to a number of pixels of error to determine which LOD to select so is foundational to both quality and efficiency." "probably a man year worth of effort has gone into this problem". "In some ways it is an impossible task because at this stage we don't know what material will be applied to this mesh. For example we don't know how shiny it is to know the exact impact of normal error / Or how much UV error is apparent based on the texture". "What we have definitely isn't perfect and can fail although it's rare to be noticable." Position and attribute errors are mixed "as if they were the same units but with weights ... There is no theoretical foundation for this." — [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)
- [RD] "Projection to screen does not account for surface angle — Similar to if mipmaps only were purely a function of distance". — [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)

**Global controls (console variables, with defaults as documented for about 5.5)**
- [RD] `r.Nanite.MaxPixelsPerEdge` = 1 — "The triangle edge length that the Nanite runtime targets, measured in pixels." `r.Nanite.MinPixelsPerEdgeHW` = 32 — "The triangle edge length in pixels at which Nanite starts using the hardware rasterizer." `r.Nanite.DicingRate` = 2 — "Size of the micropolygons that Nanite tessellation will dice to, measured in pixels." — [CVars](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-console-variables-reference)
- [RD] Detail tied to the upscaler: `r.Nanite.ViewMeshLODBias.Enable` = 1 — "Whether LOD offset to apply for rasterized Nanite meshes for the main viewport should be based off TSR's ScreenPercentage"; `.Min` = −2; `.Offset` = 0. — [CVars](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-console-variables-reference)
- [RD] A frame-time governor: `r.Nanite.PrimaryRaster.TimeBudgetMs` = 0 — "Frame's time budget for Nanite primary raster in milliseconds"; `r.Nanite.PrimaryRaster.PixelsPerEdgeScaling` = 30 — "Lower limit percentage to scale the Nanite primary raster MaxPixelsPerEdge value when over budget"; the same pair for shadows (`r.Nanite.ShadowRaster.*`, scaling default 100). — [CVars](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-console-variables-reference)
- [RD] Shadows: `r.Shadow.NaniteLODBias` = 1 — "LOD bias for nanite geometry in shadows. 0 = full detail. >0 = reduced detail." In the talk: "Nanite picks the LOD matching 1 pixel error. In the case of shadows this means the pixels of the mip level it is rasterizing to"; "We actually apply a Nanite LOD bias for shadow rendering ... Defaults to 2 pixels of error"; the mismatch with the main view "can cause incorrect self shadowing. We address that discrepancy with a short screen space trace". — [CVars](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-console-variables-reference); [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)
- [RD] Shadow advice: "Nanite geometry always renders to the Virtual Shadow Map regardless of distance, since this is the most performant option and provides the highest quality." "it is recommended to enable Nanite on all supported geometry, including low-poly meshes." "Non-Nanite geometry is much more expensive to render into VSMs than Nanite geometry." Deforming geometry "always invalidates cached pages every frame". — [Virtual Shadow Maps (5.5/5.6 version)](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/virtual-shadow-maps-in-unreal-engine?application_version=5.5)
- [RD] 5.8 additions for shadows: tessellation can be switched off in shadow maps (`r.Shadow.Virtual.Nanite.AllowTessellationDirectional`, `...Local`); an experimental "Prefiltered distant" mode "for drawing distant shadow casters into the clipmap at much lower resolution, while using prefiltering and temporal reprojection to produce a smooth result that often matched ground truth better than SMRT". — [RN 5.8](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes?application_version=5.8)

**Per-mesh controls ("Nanite Settings")**
- [RD] The table: Enable Nanite Support; Preserve Area; Explicit Tangents ("increases storage by roughly 10% but can be preferable when implicit tangents are not precise enough"); Lerp UVs ("If disabled, error from UVs is no longer accounted for when Nanite selects the LOD to render because error due to arbitrary vertex attributes that aren't interpolatable can't be generally reasoned about"); Position Precision; Normal Precision; Minimum Residency (Root Geometry) ("for some meshes, this can mitigate streaming pop-in issues"); Keep Triangle Percent; Trim Relative Error ("All detail in the source mesh with less visual impact than this relative error amount is removed. The relative error does not have a unit size and is relative to the size of the mesh. By default, Nanite stores all of the original source mesh's triangles."); Fallback Target (Auto, Fallback Triangle Percent, Fallback Relative Error); Displacement maps; Max Edge Length Factor. — [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)
- [RD] Units, from the API: `PositionPrecision` — "Step size is 2^(-PositionPrecision) cm. MIN_int32 is auto."; `NormalPrecision` — "Normal Precision in bits. -1 is auto."; `TangentPrecision` — "Tangent Precision in bits. -1 is auto."; `TargetMinimumResidencyInKB`; `KeepPercentTriangles` — "1.0 = no reduction". — [API, FMeshNaniteSettings (5.8)](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/FMeshNaniteSettings?application_version=5.8)
- [RD] Vertex precision and cracks: "Nanite quantizes mesh vertex positions ... The quantization step size is a power of two". "Lossy compression is particularly challenging when working with modular mesh pieces or other meshes that have shared boundaries, especially when those boundaries need to align perfectly to not introduce holes or cracks". "quantization happens in unnormalized object coordinates centered around the mesh origin. This ensures that the quantization never causes cracks when the mesh uses the same precision setting, and the translation between the mesh center is a multiple of that precision." — [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)
- [RD] Max Edge Length Factor: "The default Max Edge Length Factor is 0. This indicates that the edge length will not be considered for this mesh. Values greater than 0 represent a desired distance between any two connected vertices in screen space. More specifically, this distance is represented as a multiple of the smallest desired Nanite triangle edge (as configured by r.Nanite.MaxPixelsPerEdge)." It exists because "simplifications that are unnoticeable at a given distance when not deformed can become obvious when stretched along a spline curve"; the table says it is for "meshes that are intended to be deformed, such as animation using world position offset, and spline meshes". — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content); [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)
- [RD] Tangents: "Per-vertex tangents are not stored ... Instead, tangent space is implicitly derived in the pixel shader. ... This difference in tangent space using this approach could cause discontinuities at edges. However, this particular issue has not shown to be significant". — [Main](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine)
- [RD] Trimming is for size, not speed: "it's important to trim data to reduce disk size (in other words, download size), not specifically for improving performance". From the 5.0 notes: "the chest piece of one of the statues was able to lose 88% of its triangles (and disk size) without any noticeable differences". — [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details); [RN 5.0](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5.0-release-notes?application_version=5.0)

**The fallback mesh**
- [RD] "Many parts of Unreal Engine need access to the vertex buffer provided by traditionally-rendered meshes. When you enable Nanite for a static mesh, it generates a coarse representation, the fallback mesh, of the highly detailed mesh. The fallback mesh is used when Nanite rendering is not supported. It is also used when it wouldn't be ideal to use the full-detail mesh, like when a complex collision is needed, using lightmaps for baked lighting, and for hardware ray tracing reflections with Lumen." — [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)
- [RD] Its two targets: Fallback Triangle Percent (0–100) and Fallback Relative Error ("Any triangles whose removal incurs less relative error than this amount are removed, with detail of less visual impact removed first"). A hand-made LOD chain can replace it ("Set the Minimum LOD to 1 ... This causes the Nanite-generated fallback mesh to be ignored"). Generation can follow the platform: `ENaniteGenerateFallback` = `PlatformDefault` or `Enabled`. — [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details); [API, ENaniteGenerateFallback (5.8)](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/ENaniteGenerateFallback?application_version=5.8)
- [RD] The warning about running without Nanite: "Nanite efficiently handles very large numbers of instances, but if Nanite is disabled, there could be an overwhelming number of draw calls"; in the Valley of the Ancients sample "disabling Nanite would cause there to be tens of thousands of regular draw calls". `r.Nanite.ProxyRenderMode` 0 draws fallbacks, 1 draws nothing. — [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)
- [RD] Ray tracing uses the fallback by default; `r.RayTracing.Nanite.Mode 1` (experimental since 5.1) "preserves all detail while using significantly less GPU memory than zero-error Fallback Meshes"; 5.9 documents mode 2, "cluster acceleration structures" in a fixed pool (512 MB default) whose detail is lowered automatically "so the scene still fits", down to a scale of 0.3. — [Main](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine); [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)

**Artefacts Epic names**
- [RD] Tessellation cracks: "There is currently no solution for crack-free displacement. This means that UV seams, hard edge normals, or any vertex attribute affecting the displacement that isn't smooth will cause cracks." A 5.8 fix: cracks "on cluster boundaries when `r.Nanite.MaxPixelsPerEdge` is set to a value higher than 1" with landscape displacement fade. — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content); [RN 5.8](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes?application_version=5.8)
- [RD] Landscape seams: "the borders between Landscape Proxy Actors have vertices that might not be evenly distributed on each side of the border. At large distances this can introduce seam artifacts, which appear as tiny holes through the landscape. These are usually small enough to be corrected by temporal anti-aliasing." The fix is a skirt, so that neighbours "criss-cross each other". — [Using Nanite with Landscapes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-nanite-with-landscapes-in-unreal-engine)
- [RD] Streaming pop-in: Minimum Residency "can mitigate streaming pop-in issues"; for geometry collections "Increasing the value can avoid pop-in when a Geometry Collection is first shown" (5.7). With the streaming show flag off, "Nanite meshes are only rendered at the quality level that is always resident in memory". "If the pool is not large enough to fit all the data needed for a view, cache thrashing can occur where streaming never settles even for a static view." — [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details); [RN 5.7](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-7-release-notes?application_version=5.7)
- [RD] Buffer overflow: "There is no mechanism for dynamically resizing either of these buffers, or automatically scaling down quality on overflow, which can result in rendering artifacts from them being too small for scene complexity, and typically manifesting as missing or blinking geometry." — [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)
- [RD] Vertex animation outside its bounds: the mesh "is never able to render outside of its bounds, preventing visual artifacts due to 'popping'", but clamped offsets "could appear squished or flattened". — [Material Properties](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-material-properties)
- [RD] Imposters: "There is definitely a quality loss that can be noticed in some cases meaning switching to it can sometimes be noticed as a pop. Usually that only happens when many of the same mesh are next to one another like repeating wall sections". — [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)
- [RD] Low-poly meshes: a 5.6 fix for a "regression of LOD generation (when Nanite is enabled) that would allow meshes with low poly counts to simplify too much in generated LODs and destroy their silhouettes"; the shadow page notes that "Low poly geometry with high curvature and smooth normals can exhibit artifacts" (the shadow terminator), and that "With Nanite adding more polygons is straightforward and generally inexpensive". — [RN 5.6](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-6-release-notes?application_version=5.6); [Virtual Shadow Maps (5.5)](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/virtual-shadow-maps-in-unreal-engine?application_version=5.5)
- [RD] Displacement does not change shading: "You need to supply a corresponding normal map or derive a normal from the displacement derivatives." — [Working](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)

**Data size (the price of the detail)**
- [RD] "using the Unreal Engine 5 sample Valley of the Ancients, Nanite meshes average 14.4 bytes per input triangle. This means an average one million triangle Nanite mesh will be ~13.8 megabytes (MB) on disk." A 1,545,338-triangle mesh: 148.95 MB as a static mesh with 4 LODs, 19.64 MB as Nanite ("7.6x smaller"); a 4k normal map is 21.85 MB. — [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)
- [RD] From the talk: "433M Input triangles, 882M Nanite triangles"; raw 25.90 GB; memory format 7.67 GB; compressed disk format 4.61 GB; "5.6 bytes per Nanite triangle"; "11.4 bytes per input triangle"; "1M triangles = ~10.9MB on disk". — [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)

**Cost (for scale of the quality claim)**
- [RD] "Average render resolution of 1400p temporally upsampled to 4K. ~2.5 milliseconds (ms) to cull and rasterize all Nanite meshes ... ~2ms to evaluate materials for all Nanite meshes" on PlayStation 5; "approximately 4.5ms combined"; "Numbers like these should be expected from content that doesn't suffer from the aforementioned performance pitfalls". — [Main 5.5](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine?application_version=5.5) (the section is no longer on the 5.7–5.9 pages)
- [RD] The frame behind those figures: main pass 896,322 instances before culling, 3,668 after; 1,536,794 candidate clusters; 184,828 visible clusters rasterised in software and 6,686 in hardware; with the post pass, 199,420 clusters, 25,041,711 triangles, 19,851,262 vertices; "Nanite on the other hand rasterizes 25M triangles ... That 25M is consistent throughout the demo, regardless of how complex the scene is." Average resolution "~2496x1404 upsampled to 4k". — [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The quality guarantee is conditional on three things Epic states separately:** the error is under a pixel; a temporal filter blends what remains; the error estimate is right. The talk concedes the third "can fail", and the first is routinely relaxed in practice (shadows at two pixels by default, a detail bias tied to the upscaler's screen percentage, a time-budget governor that may scale pixels-per-edge down to 30 %). The published 4.5 ms figure was measured at about 1400p internal resolution, not at 4K.
- **"Max pixels per edge" is a target edge length, not a bound on visible error.** One pixel of edge at 1404p internal resolution upsampled to 4K is about 1.5 output pixels. An engine that renders at the screen's own 4K resolution with no temporal upsampling has roughly 2.4 times the pixels to satisfy and no temporal blend to lean on.
- "Higher quality than hand-made levels" is claimed by Epic in one specific sense: the finest level is the source mesh itself ("draw the original source triangles that were imported") and the in-between levels are finer-grained than any hand chain. It is not claimed for aggregates (section 2), for deformed meshes without the edge-length setting, or for displaced surfaces at seams.
- The artefacts Epic lists are all at the **edges of the one-mesh assumption**: between meshes (landscape proxies, modular pieces with different precision), between the rest shape and the deformed shape (splines, vertex animation), between what is resident and what is wanted (streaming), and below the pixel (thin geometry). Inside one rigid, connected mesh, Epic reports none.
- Shadows are a second view with its own detail choice. Epic accepts a mismatch and hides it with a short screen-space trace; "shadows thin differently from the tree" is a known class of problem, not solved by the level-of-detail rule itself.

### Gaps
- No Epic page states the pixel error threshold as a user-facing guarantee, gives a measured image difference, or says what happens to the estimate for alpha-cut cards (the error metric is geometric; a card's visible shape is in its texture).
- The current default of `r.Nanite.MaxPixelsPerEdge` in 5.8/5.9 was not confirmed; the console-variable page was last updated in March 2025.
- No Epic statement was found on how well the rule holds without temporal anti-aliasing. MSAA is listed as unsupported.

## 6. Scale: instance limits, streaming, memory budgets, and what happens to instances smaller than a pixel

### Takeaway
The cluster hierarchy ends at one root cluster of 128 triangles, so a tiny instance still costs a culling test and a cluster; "At that point cost stops scaling with resolution." Epic's answers are layered and mostly outside the hierarchy: a hard cap of 16 million instances, an instance-culling hierarchy (Beta in 5.3, reworked in 5.6 and 5.9), small imposters whose building was switched off by default in 5.1, merged proxies from the world-streaming system for the far distance, and since 5.7 assemblies, which remove the per-part instance altogether.

### Cited Findings
- [RD] "The maximum number of instances that can be present in the scene is hard-locked to 16 million instances, which includes all instances that are streamed in, not just those enabled for use with Nanite. Only instances streamed in are counted towards this total." — [Main](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine)
- [RD] The floor: "What happens when an entire mesh only covers a few pixels? The cluster hierarchy only goes so far. It ends at 1 root cluster of 128 triangles. At that point cost stops scaling with resolution. It stops scaling all together. That's really small though, we could just cull instances when they get tiny. Except not if they are a structural building blocks, say a wall section of a building seen from very far away. Culling small instances could mean entire buildings vanish." — [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)
- [RD] "Obviously need to merge at some point — Even if rendering scales sublinearly, memory doesn't — Instance memory adds up fast — 10M * float4x3 = 457MB". "Nanite has no special solution for merging — Merged unique proxies must replace instances at extreme distances — Key improvement is pushing that distance far away". "Linear scaling in instances can be ok, at least within the limit of the scale of the level you typically want loaded around you". — [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)
- [RD] Imposters as designed in 2021: "12 x 12 view directions in atlas"; "12 x 12 pixels per direction"; "8:8 Depth, TriangleID"; "40.5KB per mesh always resident"; "Ray march to adjust parallax between directions"; "Drawn directly from instance culling pass"; "Would like to replace with something better". — [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)
- [RD] Imposters since: "Made imposter building optional with the console variable 'r.Nanite.Builder.Imposters'. This setting is turned off by default." (5.1). `r.Nanite.ImposterMaxPixels` = 5 — "The maximum size of imposters measured in pixels." `r.Nanite.Streaming.Imposters` = 1 — "Load imposters used for faster rendering of distant objects. - [RD] In shadow maps small instances are dropped by footprint: `r.Shadow.Virtual.CoarsePagePixelThresholdDynamicNanite` = 4 — "If a dynamic Nanite instance has a smaller estimated pixel footprint than this value, it should not be drawn into a coarse page." — [CVars](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-console-variables-reference)
- [RD] Instance culling over time: 5.3 "Implemented instance culling hierarchy ... disabled by default (r.SceneCulling), and may be considered Beta"; 5.6 "hierarchical instance culling to work on chunks of 64 instances"; 5.9 two culling classes and adaptive fan-out ("Near the top of the hierarchy, where only a few nodes are left to test, it assigns more GPU threads to each node so that one dispatch can descend several levels at once"; "the gains grow with scene size"). — [RN 5.3](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5.3-release-notes?application_version=5.3); [RN 5.6](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-6-release-notes?application_version=5.6); [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details); [RN 5.9 Preview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-9-release-notes)
- [RD] A stress case from 2022, the Disney Moana island in UE5: "146M unique triangles, 27M instances, Resulting in 164B instanced triangles"; "Over 40fps at 2742 x 1262 on am RTX 3080. If we added hierarchical instance culling I think we could hit 60." "All the leaves are geometry, no masked cards here." - [RD] Far distance by merging: world-partition HLOD layers are of type Instancing ("ideal for imposter meshes, such as trees and foliage"), Merged Mesh, or Simplified Mesh; a merged mesh has the option "Generate Nanite Enabled Mesh". - [RD] Streaming: "Data is streamed in on demand so that only visible detail needs to reside in memory"; "we recommend solid state drives". `r.Nanite.Streaming.StreamingPoolSize` = 512 — "Size of streaming pool in MB. Does not include memory used for root pages." `r.Nanite.Streaming.NumInitialRootPages` = 2048; `MaxPageInstallsPerFrame` = 128. Since 5.5: "The Nanite streamer now adjusts the global quality target dynamically when the streaming pool is being overcommitted. This makes it converge to more uniform quality across the screen". — [Main](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine); [CVars](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-console-variables-reference); [RN 5.5](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-5-release-notes)
- [RD] Pages: "we want to use fixed sized pages"; "The first page, the root page, is always resident and contains as much of the top of" the hierarchy as fits; "Always having the root page means we always have something to render"; "~1KB average slack per page! (~1% slack for 128KB page)". — [Deck 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)
- [RD] Per-pass buffers: `r.Nanite.MaxCandidateClusters` = 16,777,216, `r.Nanite.MaxVisibleClusters` = 4,194,304, `r.Nanite.MaxNodes` = 2,097,152; "The memory cost of a candidate cluster is currently 12 bytes and a visible cluster is 16 bytes." — [CVars](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-console-variables-reference); [Technical](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)
- [RD] Assemblies as the answer for sub-instances: see section 4 ("each visible instance requiring at least one root cluster be drawn").

### Inferences
- Epic never made the hierarchy itself go below one cluster per instance. Each later step moves the problem to a different structure: a hierarchy over instances (cull groups, not members), merged proxies (replace groups), assemblies (make the members part of one hierarchy), voxels (make the last clusters cheap and correct for aggregates).
- The imposter path, the only "below one cluster" representation of 2021, was made optional and off by default one release after launch; Epic's text since treats it as worthwhile only without HLOD. Nothing "truly seamless" replaced it for rigid instances.
- Arithmetic: 16,777,216 candidate clusters × 12 bytes ≈ 201 MB and 4,194,304 visible clusters × 16 bytes ≈ 67 MB of intermediate buffers at the default sizes, plus a 512 MB streaming pool. These are desktop and console budgets.

### Gaps
- Whether imposters are built by default in 5.8/5.9 was not re-confirmed beyond the 5.1 note and the console-variable page.
- No documented rule for a Nanite instance smaller than one pixel in the main view: nothing read says it is culled, and the 2021 talk gives the reason it is not culled by default.
- Memory per instance in today's GPU scene was not found.

## 8. The technical blog on bringing Nanite to Fortnite Battle Royale Chapter 4: what went wrong with trees, what was done, the numbers

### Takeaway
Not readable directly in this session (the host demands a JavaScript check), so everything here is from search digests. They agree on the core: trees took the most development time; converted trees lost their canopies with distance until the option documented as Preserve Area was added; opaque geometry with more triangles beat masked cards; the cost of vertex-shader wind on trees of 300 to 500 thousand vertices forced a distance cut-off and a baked wind texture.

### Cited Findings
- [SE] Author Graham Wihlidal; Chapter 4 moved Fortnite to Unreal Engine 5.1 (December 2022). Trees were the area that took the most prototyping and development time; the team wanted dense forests with predictable performance. — search digests of [Epic tech blog, Bringing Nanite to Fortnite Battle Royale in Chapter 4](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/bringing-nanite-to-fortnite-battle-royale-in-chapter-4)
- [SE] Early tests used masked materials and cards; the team found that avoiding masked materials and using higher triangle counts with opaque materials was usually faster for Fortnite's content, "especially for trees and grass". — [same blog](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/bringing-nanite-to-fortnite-battle-royale-in-chapter-4)
- [SE] After conversion "the trees lost their canopies in the distance because of the simplification process, with leaves thinning out or going bare"; the fix is an option to enable only on foliage that shows the problem. — [same blog](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/bringing-nanite-to-fortnite-battle-royale-in-chapter-4)
- [RD] The matching release note: "A new **Preserve Area** option for Nanite static meshes prevents thinning out of geometry at far distances when enabled for foliage." - [SE] Other content: Nanite was used for all building meshes; a non-Nanite landscape was a major shadow cost, so Epic built the experimental Nanite landscape of 5.1, which "converts the landscape height field into a Nanite mesh at build time"; the shadow post says the most important first step to good shadow performance "is to make everything Nanite". — search digests of the [Nanite](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/bringing-nanite-to-fortnite-battle-royale-in-chapter-4) and [Virtual Shadow Maps](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/virtual-shadow-maps-in-fortnite-battle-royale-chapter-4) posts

### Inferences

### Inferences
- The search tool's digests of Epic's documentation were reliable on wording but unreliable on absence and on version detail; the two unconfirmed items in this round (voxel fixes in 5.8, section 3) are of that kind.
- **What the direct reading adds for a system that must be generic and of higher quality than hand-made levels:** (1) the most mature system of its kind is generic only in the sense of "no level authored by hand"; it still asks a person to classify each mesh (foliage or not, deformed or not) and each material (masked, animated, displaced), and it runs each class through different code; (2) its quality promise rests on a one-pixel error estimate that its author calls a heuristic with no theoretical foundation, plus a temporal filter; (3) the representation that makes aggregates correct at a distance is chosen by comparing errors between two candidate representations of the same cluster, which is the one fully automatic, per-part rule in the whole design and the part most worth copying; (4) that representation is still experimental, unmeasured and shaded incompletely after three releases, on hardware far above a phone.

- The Fortnite and Witcher news material on `www.unrealengine.com` remains unverified at the level of wording.
