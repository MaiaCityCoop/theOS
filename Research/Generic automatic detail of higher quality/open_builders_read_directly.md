## 1. `clusterlod.h` on current master: configuration, the exact loop, what it locks, what it does when a group will not simplify, how error is computed, the hierarchy, and what its comments say about quality

### Takeaway
`clusterlod.h` is one loop that is told nothing about the kind of mesh: cluster, group, lock the vertices shared between groups, simplify each group to half with attribute-aware collapses in permissive mode, fall back to the sloppy simplifier when the target is missed, declare the group terminal when less than 15% was removed, re-cluster. A group's error is the largest quadric error (positions plus area-clamped attributes, absolute units) of this step or of any child, kept monotonic by a max, and nothing in it measures lost coverage: component pruning is switched off, there is no voxel fallback, and the one foliage aid (border dilation) is opt-in and the header says it "should not be enabled everywhere".

- [read] `while (clusters.size() > 1)`: (a) `partition`: one group if there are at most `partition_size` clusters, else `meshopt_partitionClusters` on position-remapped indices, with positions when `partition_spatial`; (b) `lockBoundary`; (c) for each group: concatenate its clusters, `target = floor(triangles * simplify_ratio)`, take the group bounds as the merged sphere of its clusters' bounds, simplify; (d) if `simplified.size() > merged.size() * simplify_threshold`, set `bounds.error = FLT_MAX`, output the group with its unsimplified clusters and `continue` ("simplification is stuck; abandon the merge"); (e) otherwise set the group error, output the group, optionally dilate, split the simplified triangles into new clusters whose `refined` is this group and whose bounds are the group-merged bounds; (f) swap, `depth++`. After the loop a single remaining cluster is output as a terminal group with error `FLT_MAX` — [clusterlod.h L799-L869](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L799-L869)
- [read] `meshopt_simplifyWithUpdate` is not called: every level indexes the original vertices (dilation is the one thing that moves positions) — [clusterlod.h L458-L525](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L458-L525)

Border dilation (`dilateBorders`), the opt-in foliage aid
- [read] It measures the area of "border triangles" (a triangle with at least one edge that is unlocked and open) before and after the group's simplification; it does nothing if the area did not shrink or shrank to less than a quarter ("limit expansion to cases of moderate area shrinkage; it may be unsafe to dilate otherwise"); otherwise it moves every unlocked border vertex outward, in the plane of its border triangles, by `(old_area - new_area) / perimeter`, with a mitre limited by a floor of 0.15 on the squared averaged normal, and grows the group's bounding radius to contain the moved vertices. The group error is not changed — [clusterlod.h L560-L688](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L560-L688)
- [read] The commit message gives the reason: "at some point the only edge collapses left are ones that would remove individual leaves/grass blades ... this results in coordinated omission: across the entire mesh, most blades/leaves are similarly sized; at some point during simplification, many of them will be removed at once, and this will happen at a similar error. The loss of just one leaf is not noticeable, but because the error is similar, many clusters will flip to a coarser LOD at once, causing visible pop - and, eventually, complete erosion." "This is intended to be enabled *just* for foliage. However, to mitigate the possible artifacts if enabled broadly, we compute area just for triangles adjacent to border edges". "Since this dilation happens after every simplfication step ... the dilation itself is gradual as it follows simplification progress." — [commit 7d8eca5](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/commit/7d8eca58)

How the error is computed
- [read] Units: absolute (object space), because of `meshopt_SimplifyErrorAbsolute` ("changes the error metric from relative to absolute both for the input error limit as well as for the resulting error"); `meshopt_SimplifySparse` alone would make it "relative to subset extents" — [README L655-L664](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/README.md?plain=1#L655-L664), [meshoptimizer.h L468-L485](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/src/meshoptimizer.h#L468-L485)
- [read] What the simplifier returns: the square root of the largest collapse error it performed (`result_error = result_error < c.error ? c.error : result_error`, then `sqrtf(result_error) * error_scale`). The README calls it "an approximate measure of the deviation from the original mesh" and says that with attributes "both the target error and the resulting error combine positional error and attribute error" — [simplifier.cpp L1643](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/src/simplifier.cpp#L1643), [L2620-L2622](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/src/simplifier.cpp#L2620-L2622), [README L563](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/README.md?plain=1#L563), [README L601](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/README.md?plain=1#L601)
- [read] Position part: plane quadrics weighted by `sqrtf(area)` ("so that the error is scaled linearly; this tends to improve silhouettes"), normalised by the accumulated weight. Attribute part: "(eval(pos) - attr)^2" with each attribute extrapolated linearly over the triangle and "weighted with the square of edge length (= area)", so that "a change in weighted attribute of 1 along distance d is approximately equivalent to a change in position of d"; with the clamp, "the error [is] limited to the cumulative weight (= area), which makes perceptual error map to position error more closely" — [simplifier.cpp L751-L776](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/src/simplifier.cpp#L751-L776), [L809-L822](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/src/simplifier.cpp#L809-L822), [L847-L864](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/src/simplifier.cpp#L847-L864)
- [read] Weights: "For normalized attributes like normals and vertex colors, a weight around 1.0 is usually appropriate; internally, a change of `1/weight` in attribute value over a distance `d` is approximately equivalent to a change of `d` in position." UVs: "optional, as simplification generally preserves texture quality reasonably well by default; if included, a weight of around 10-100 is usually appropriate depending on the UV density", or "the reciprocal average density of UVs, ... `1/sqrt(average UV area)`" — [README L597-L599](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/README.md?plain=1#L597-L599)
- [read] How previous levels are merged: the group starts from the largest error among its clusters (`boundsMerge`: "merged bounds error must be conservative wrt cluster errors"), then `bounds.error = max(bounds.error * simplify_error_merge_previous, error) + error * simplify_error_merge_additive` ("enforce error monotonicity (with an optional hierarchical factor to separate transitions more)"). With the defaults (1.0, 0) this is a plain max, not a sum — [clusterlod.h L244-L254](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L244-L254), [L835-L836](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L835-L836)
- [read] The author's reason for the max, in the commit that introduced it: "Accumulating errors via += tends to overestimate the actual deformation from successive simplification. Using max() is often a much more reasonable estimate; use max() with a configurable factor to provide a little bit of forced separation between LODs." The README's general advice for LOD chains built from the previous level is the opposite wording: "resulting error values from previous levels should be accumulated for LOD selection" — [commit 236470d](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/commit/236470df), [README L674](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/README.md?plain=1#L674)
- [read] Bounds are kept monotonic too: "it is incorrect to use the precise bounds of the merged or simplified mesh, because this may violate monotonicity"; new clusters inherit the group-merged sphere, and a cluster's own `bounds` "should only be used for culling, as bounds.error is not monotonic across DAG" — [clusterlod.h L822-L824](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L822-L824), [L849-L851](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L849-L851), [L123-L124](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L123-L124)
- [read] Projection to the screen, from the header: `bounds.error / max(distance(bounds.center, camera_position) - bounds.radius, camera_znear) * (camera_proj * 0.5f)`, a value in 0..1 to "multiply by screen height to get pixels"; "for simplicity, we ignore perspective distortion and use rotationally invariant projection size estimation". The demo's threshold is `2e-3f`, commented "2 pixels at 1080p" — [clusterlod.h L104-L116](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L104-L116), [nanite.cpp L138](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/nanite.cpp#L138)
- [read] What a removed loose piece costs in the core simplifier when `meshopt_SimplifyPrune` is on (it is not, in `clusterlod.h`): each connected component's "error" is its squared radius about its mean; components whose radius is under the current collapse error are deleted, and a final pass prunes "more aggressively towards error_limit" while raising `result_error` to the radius of the largest component removed — [simplifier.cpp L1955-L2002](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/src/simplifier.cpp#L1955-L2002), [L2538-L2568](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/src/simplifier.cpp#L2538-L2568)

The hierarchy for run-time selection (`clodBuildHierarchy`, since v1.2)
- [read] "the result is a forest with one tree per DAG level": "nodes 0..level_count-1 are the roots of per-level trees, each covering groups with clodGroup::depth == level", and "remaining nodes are the tree nodes; each internal node has up to node_width children, and each leaf node refers to a single group". Leaves carry the group's `simplified` bounds; each row is ordered with `meshopt_spatialClusterPoints` on the centres and merged `node_width` at a time; a node's error is the largest of its children ("reflecting worst case error for all groups in the subtree") — [clusterlod.h L182-L191](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L182-L191), [L886-L936](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L886-L936)
- [read] Traversal rule: "traverse the tree into nodes while bounds is over error threshold; for leaves, group clusters should be rendered if cluster.refined is -1 *or* clodGroup::simplified for groups[cluster.refined].simplified is at or under error threshold". The demo builds it with node width 8 and compares a cut found through the tree with a brute-force cut — [clusterlod.h L150-L163](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L150-L163), [nanite.cpp L41-L68](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/nanite.cpp#L41-L68), [L234-L246](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/nanite.cpp#L234-L246)

### Inferences
- The error does **not** bound the distance to the full-detail surface. Each step's value is a quadric measure against the group's input (the previous level), and the default merge is a max across levels, which the author himself calls an "estimate". It is conservative only in the sense that matters for a valid cut: it never decreases toward the root.
- The error does **not** see loss of coverage. A leaf that collapses to nothing costs about its own size as a collapse error, the same for every leaf, which is exactly the "coordinated omission" the commit describes. Dilation repairs the area but adds nothing to the error, and it is off by default.
- For a builder that must take any mesh untold, the defaults already cover hard-surface and flat-shaded input (permissive mode on, sloppy as last resort, stuck test as the end). What they do not cover without a per-asset decision is: which seams to protect (`attribute_protect_mask`), the attribute weights, whether to dilate (foliage only), whether to preserve folds (thin two-sided sheets), and whether to regularise (deforming meshes). Those five are the places where "kind of asset" still enters.
- A flat-shaded building with unshared corners gives permissive mode nothing to protect unless the caller marks creases itself: with normals as weighted attributes the only thing holding a hard edge is the attribute error, clamped to the triangle's area. Whether that keeps a tent's ridge sharp at one pixel is not stated anywhere and would have to be measured.
- Ropes and other thin parts have no dedicated path. The only mentions are the "stopgap for thin and long triangles like wires" in the optional edge-length cap, fold preservation for two-sided sheets, and the remesher's warning that wires "may disappear".
- Correction to the earlier round's notes: the 1.3 JavaScript simplifier does expose `PreserveFolds`, `ErrorClamped` and `remesh`; what is still missing in JavaScript for a port of this loop is `meshopt_partitionClusters` (and `clodBuild` itself).

### Gaps
- No statement anywhere in the header or README of what screen error the defaults were tuned for, other than the demo's "2 pixels at 1080p".
- Nothing measured here: how often the default loop ends in stuck groups on faceted, card-based or rope-like meshes is not documented beyond the one Zorah figure in section 2.
- The release notes of v1.0 to v1.3 could only be read through a summarising fetch; where they matter the commit messages and the README were used instead.

## 2. The author's two long posts (30 Sep 2025 and 30 Sep 2026), the v1.0 page and the commit messages: every statement about quality, foliage and aggregates, attribute error, flat-shaded meshes, thresholds in pixels and throughput

### Takeaway
Kapoulkine states the quality contract in one sentence (a coarser cluster replaces a finer one "only if the resulting visual error is under 1 pixel (and the resulting switch is hidden by TAA or other temporal filters)") and then names where it breaks: attribute error that is far too high unless clamped to triangle area, meshes with many seams that stall, and above all foliage, where sub-pixel logic removes every leaf at once; his own fix for foliage is a per-mesh opt-in that NVIDIA's sample switches on by material (two-sided), and he says triangles are the wrong representation for it. He does not claim transitions are invisible, and closes by noting "geometry popping in cutscenes now and again" in recent games.

- [read] Why it is fundamental: "the goal of Nanite is to replace clusters with simplified versions such that the error introduced by replacing individual triangles with their coarser versions is under a pixel. ## 3. three-virtual-geometry 0.1.3: its whole guide and its build and run-time source. How it chooses between simplifying triangles, thinning and widening loose pieces, and voxel hulls; how it blends levels above one pixel; how it keeps an honest error when parts vanish; every limit and quality problem it names

### Takeaway
The library makes all three choices by one rule: every candidate (collapsed triangles, pieces thinned in pairs and widened, a voxel hull of the full-detail mesh) gets an error in object-space length, and the candidate with the smaller error wins; lost silhouette enters as a floor (how far the bounding box retreated), lost pieces as how far coverage moved, a hull as two voxel cells. Above 1.25 px of effective error it blends neighbouring levels per pixel with a noise pattern (both levels drawn, with a discard), which reads as a stipple unless temporal anti-aliasing averages it. It is five weeks old, has had no commit since 10 October 2026, no issues and no pull requests, is measured only in Chrome on one M4 Pro, and its own jungle demo sets the foliage options by hand (pruning on, hulls off, a 3 px cap, draw distances), which is the plainest evidence in these sources that "one automatic pipeline for any mesh" is not yet reached.

- [read] How the bands are formed: "With a blend band [t, t * blend], every pixel instead shows the cut for its own threshold inside the band, picked by a fixed per-pixel value n in [0, 1): s(n) = t * blend^n. Budgets and the two controllers
- [read] Capacity controller, on the GPU, per pool and per cut: "Draw buffers have fixed sizes. An overflow would drop meshlets in a different order every frame (holes). ### Inferences
- The automatic choice is real but narrower than the guide's "foliage switches automatically" suggests. It rests on three numeric tests (stalled above 1.25 times the target; thinned error below the hull's; hull error below the triangles'), all in the same unit, with no asset-kind input. That part is fully generic. What is not generic is everything the demo sets by hand for textured cards: pruning, hulls off, draw distance, a 3 px cap.
- Derived: a hull's error cannot be smaller than two cells, which is 1/32 of the object's longest side at the default 64 cells and 1/64 at 128. At a one-pixel threshold a hull can therefore only be drawn once the whole object is about 32 px (or 64 px) across, and at 3 px about 96 px (or 192 px). Hulls are a far-distance stand-in, which matches the jungle demo building its palms with a 128 grid and running at 3 px.
- Derived from the hull's construction (3×3×3 blur, iso 0.5): a sheet that fills a single layer of cells has 9 of 27 neighbours filled (0.33) and a strand that fills a single line of cells 3 of 27 (0.11), both under 0.5, so they leave no surface; a sheet lying obliquely in the grid can fill two layers in places and partly survive. Thin cloth, single cards and ropes that the closing step does not fuse into something at least two cells thick can therefore drop out of the hull, and because the extent floor is not applied to hull candidates, the hull's error would not show it. Not run here; stated as a risk to test, not as an observed defect.
- Because the group simplification uses the library's border flag, every true open edge inside a group (the rim of a card, the hem of a tent) is frozen at full detail until the tail, where "Borders can be unlocked". This is the problem the Bevy author describes for the same flag (section 5), and it is the reason separate cards "stall" and need the piece path at all.
- A flat-shaded mesh imported with its own normals has every corner as a separate vertex; the group simplification is the non-permissive `simplify`, which the meshoptimizer README says "may not be able to reduce the triangle count at all" on faceted meshes. Reading the code, such groups would stay roots (the piece path needs at least two separate pieces), the level loop would end early (nothing produced, or under 3% removed), and the tail would fall to the sloppy simplifier or to a smooth hull with gradient normals. So faceted buildings would likely get either full detail or a blob, with little in between. Not run here.
- The error is a sum up the chain (child plus step), where `clusterlod.h` uses a max. Summing is the more cautious of the two (later levels switch farther away), at the price of more triangles.
- On a 2× screen the default "1 px" is one CSS pixel, that is two device pixels of error. A requirement stated at "the screen's own resolution" needs `errorInCssPixels = false` or a threshold of 0.5, which the source comment prices at "~4x the triangles".
- Corrections to the earlier round's notes, which had read only the type files: the blend opens at 1.25 px and is full at 2.5 px (both numbers are right, as the two ends of one ramp); the build does not run in a worker; group simplification is positions-only with locked borders; the sloppy simplifier is used only in the tail.

### Gaps
- Not run: no build time, no picture and no frame time of my own; every performance figure is the author's, on one machine, in Chrome.
- No Safari, iPhone or iPad result exists anywhere I could reach; the published guide site and the three.js forum did not resolve in this session, so a report posted only there would have been missed.

## 4. NVIDIA's `nv_cluster_lod_builder` README and `vk_lod_clusters`: what they add about correctness (monotonic error, bounds) and about streaming

### Takeaway
The archived `nv_cluster_lod_builder` README is the plainest statement of why a cut is valid: whatever is compared with the threshold "must never decrease with each level of decimation", which needs bounding spheres that also bound the geometry a group was made from, and one error and one sphere per group so "the entire group [changes] LOD at the same time". The live sample `vk_lod_clusters` builds with a lightly changed `clusterlod.h`, needs per-mesh overrides because "foliage wants different weights than a solid surface", admits its vegetation fades out, and streams by group with the coarsest group of every mesh always in memory.

### Inferences
- The correctness rule has two halves that are easy to get half right: errors that never decrease toward the root, and spheres that contain the spheres of what they replace. `clusterlod.h`, three-virtual-geometry and Bevy (since 0.15) all do both; any new error term (a coverage floor, a hull bound) has to keep both, which is why three-virtual-geometry floors a hull's error at its child's.
- NVIDIA's team, with the library's author helping, still ships a per-mesh override file and a material-based proxy for foliage. That is evidence from the most complete open implementation that a single setting does not yet serve solid surfaces and foliage alike.
- The weights matter by more than an order of magnitude between scenes (UV weight 0.5 by default, 16 for textured Zorah). A generic builder needs a rule for the UV weight; the meshoptimizer README offers one ("the reciprocal average density of UVs").
- Derived: 7.9 million triangles in about 10 s on a few threads is of the same order as the per-core rate derived in section 2.

### Gaps
- The sample's shaders were not read; its run-time selection is described here from its documents.
- No statement in either repository about visible transitions at one pixel, with or without temporal filtering, other than the vegetation issue.

## 5. The quality lessons recorded by the author of Bevy's meshlet renderer (posts for 0.14, 0.15, 0.16 and later; current code)

### Takeaway
JMS55's record is a list of what goes wrong in a first implementation and what fixed it: an error that was a sum and a sphere placed arbitrarily (changed to a max, and to spheres enclosing the children's, the latter copied from Kapoulkine's demo), hierarchies that stopped at many roots because the whole mesh border was locked (fixed by locking only vertices shared between groups, and groups of 8), normals that visibly changed between cuts (fixed by giving normals to the simplifier), tiny stuck clusters (reduced by clustering triangles to share fewer vertices), and the rule that vertex quantisation must use one grid for the whole mesh or cracks appear. Left open by his own account: UV error, arbitrary weights, stuck triangles on complex meshes, and any perceptual error at all. By his own posts he has not worked on it since 0.16; alpha-masked materials are still unsupported.

## 6. Across all of them: is there any single measure of error that covers both "the surface moved" and "something disappeared"? What does each system use?

### Takeaway
No source has a principled single measure. Three partial ones exist, all ending as a length so that they can be compared with the same pixel threshold: meshoptimizer's pruning takes the radius of a removed piece as its error (whole meshes only, so not inside a cluster hierarchy); Unreal's clamp, as Kapoulkine reports it, caps a triangle's attribute error at its area in the quadric's squared units (about the triangle's own size once the root is taken), on the argument that nothing can look worse than removing the triangle; and three-virtual-geometry takes the larger of the collapse error, the retreat of the bounding box, the distance coverage moved when two pieces were merged, or two voxel cells for a hull. Everyone else uses the collapse error alone and names foliage or lost coverage as the open problem.

### Inferences
- The common unit is the useful finding: each partial measure turns "something disappeared" into a length (the radius of what vanished, the distance its coverage moved, the retreat of the outline, a voxel's size). A generic builder can therefore keep one scalar per group and one threshold, provided each kind of loss is given its own floor and the largest wins.
- Each floor has a blind side. A box retreat sees only loss at the group's outer box, not thinning inside it. A pair distance sees only pieces that were paired. A removed-component radius needs the whole mesh. A hull's two cells bound how far the hull is from the surface, not how much of the surface the hull dropped. None measures coverage (the fraction of pixels still covered), which is the quantity both Kapoulkine and NVIDIA name as lost.
- None of the sources measures the transition itself (an image difference between two neighbouring cuts at the switching distance). Every claim of invisibility rests on the one-pixel argument plus, in Kapoulkine's wording, temporal filtering; three-virtual-geometry is the only one that adds a mechanism (the blend) for when the argument does not hold.
- The automatic decisions that exist are all of one form: build two or three candidates, give each an error in the same unit, keep the smallest. That form needs no knowledge of the asset's kind. The decisions that are still made by hand (dilate or not, prune or not, protect which seams, hulls for textured cards or not) are exactly those where the error does not capture what is lost: area, texture content, and shading across a crease.

### Gaps
- Unreal's own documentation and source were not read in this task; what is said about Nanite here is Kapoulkine's report of it.
- No source gives a coverage-based or image-based error for clusters; whether one exists elsewhere (in papers on aggregate detail or in other engines) was outside this reading.
- I found no report by anyone of three-virtual-geometry in Safari or on an iPhone, and none of `clusterlod.h` built to WebAssembly.
