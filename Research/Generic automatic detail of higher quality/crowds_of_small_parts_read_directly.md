## 1. Cook, Halstead, Planck, Ryu 2007 ("Stochastic Simplification of Aggregate Detail") in full: order, kept fraction, area scaling, contrast, transition, results, limits; "Stochastic Pruning"; the hair work

### Takeaway
The paper itself could not be opened, so its own formulae remain recalled; but four later papers and three production sources that were read directly restate and use its rule the same way: keep a random subset in a fixed order, multiply each survivor's area by one over the kept fraction (a card's sides by 1/√λ, a strand's width by 1/λ), and pull colours towards the mean by √λ. The same sources report where it fails: it assumes parts are alike and evenly spread, it changes the look of what is left if the survivor keeps a single part's shading, and it loses badly against an optimised stand-in at very high reduction (14.5 dB against 23.9 dB on Disney's Moana foliage at 90 % removal).

**Search extracts about the paper, the memo and the patent**
- [search extract] The paper names four parts: pruning order, area preservation, contrast preservation and smooth animation ("pruned elements fade out rather than popping off"). "Some objects are constructed in such a way that the pruning order can be determined procedurally, but the authors found it more general and useful to determine the pruning order stochastically"; the order should not correlate with position or other attributes, so that nearby elements are not pruned together, which "spreads out the visual effects of pruning during animation and allows more aggressive pruning". — [Pixar project page (via search)](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/StochasticSimplification)
- [search extract] The amount of simplification "can depend on a number of factors, including screen size, motion blur, and depth of field". — [ACM DL abstract (via search)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/1276377.1276476)
- [search extract] The related patent says the ordering of elements "is not correlated with position, normal, color, or the like" and lists "random sampling" and "sampling with Poisson-disk constraints" as ways to build the pruning order. — [US 7,973,789, "Dynamic model generation methods and apparatus" (via search)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7973789)
- [search extract] The predecessor memo (Cook and Halstead, "Stochastic Pruning", Pixar Technical Memo 06-05): "some elements are pruned (i.e., eliminated), and the remaining elements are altered to preserve the statistical properties of the scene." — [Pixar, Stochastic Pruning (via search)](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/StochasticPruning/index.html)
- [search extract] One search summary describes the paper's notation as a "level-of-detail fraction λ" of elements to keep and a "rendering priority order"; this may be the patent's wording rather than the paper's. Hasselgren et al. also write λ. The earlier round's notes write u. Treat the symbol as unverified. — [ACM DL PDF (via search)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/1276377.1276476)

**Recalled, not re-read (verify against the paper before quoting)**
- [recalled] *Order.* Each element gets a fixed place in a random order made once; with n elements and kept fraction u the first u·n are drawn. Stratifying the order (over position, and over any property that matters) keeps every prefix evenly spread. — [Pixar, paper PDF (not reachable)](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/StochasticSimplification/paper.pdf)
- [recalled] *Kept fraction against distance.* With z the distance scaled so that pruning starts at z = 1 and h the distance at which half the elements are gone: u = z^(−log_h 2). h = 2 gives u = 1/z; h = √2 gives u = 1/z². Motion blur and depth of field allow more pruning. — [Pixar, paper PDF (not reachable)](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/StochasticSimplification/paper.pdf)
- [recalled] *Area.* Total area n·a is held constant: each survivor's area × 1/u; a leaf's two sides × 1/√u each; a hair's or blade's width × 1/u (length unchanged). No statement is recalled about the point an element is scaled about. — [Pixar, paper PDF (not reachable)](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/StochasticSimplification/paper.pdf)
- [recalled] *Contrast.* A pixel is roughly the mean of the k elements seen through it, so its variance is the element variance over k. Fewer elements per pixel means more variance: the pruned object looks grainier and more contrasty. Correction: c′ = c̄ + α (c − c̄) with α² = k_pruned / k_unpruned, where k is capped by the number of elements that can be seen through one pixel given occlusion, so no correction is needed while u·k still exceeds the cap. — [Pixar, paper PDF (not reachable)](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/StochasticSimplification/paper.pdf)
- [recalled] *Smooth transition.* Elements near the cut are not removed at once; over a band of the order they shrink to nothing, and the scale of the rest is normalised so total area stays exact. — [Pixar, paper PDF (not reachable)](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/StochasticSimplification/paper.pdf)

**The same rule in real-time code and shipped games (all read directly)**
- [read directly: MachineGames, "Strand-based hair and fur rendering in Indiana Jones and the Great Circle", SIGGRAPH Advances 2025, PDF p. 15–19] "Randomized strand order, discard tail of the strand data to do LODs"; "Compensate strand number reduction by increasing thickness". Notes: "We don't use separate LOD models, instead we use the common practice of randomizing strands order. Then we can take only a section of strands buffer, scale thickness of each strand accordingly and hair shape will still be the same." Per frame the strand count is set so that at most 2000 segments fall on a 64×64-pixel tile of the model's screen area, and at most 2 million segments in total. Limits per asset: 65k strands (PC high, Xbox Series X, PS5), 32k (PC low, Series S, Steam Deck). "Small hair models are not reduced because they don't contribute much to rendering time and reduction is more visible for them." Shipped at 60 Hz. — [Advances 2025 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Strand%20Hair%20in%20IJGC%20-%20Final%20Slides%20(Post-Conference).pdf)
- [read directly: Lipp, Jarabo, Wimmer, Bode 2026 (HPG 2026), p. 7] Strands kept per bundle: N_LOD = clamp(⌈L·(N + δ)⌉, 1, N), "where N is the full strand count per bundle, δ ∼ U[0, 1) is a per-bundle random offset that smooths LOD transitions", and L = clamp(‖AABBmax − AABBmin‖ / R_y · λ, 0, 1) is the bundle's screen-space box size over the vertical resolution times a sensitivity λ. Control points per strand fall with √L (p. 8). Far view on an RTX 5080 at 1080p: strands 127k → 21k, frame time 4.5 ms → 0.4 ms (p. 12). — [arXiv 2607.04230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04230)
- [read directly: source code of the sample for "Real-Time GPU Tree Generation" (Kuth et al., HPG 2025), file `procedural-tree-generation/LeafDensity.h`, commit adf96a5 of 21 June 2025] Leaf density falls as `clamp(densityFactor * pow(2, -distanceToCamera / densityHalfDistance), 0.0001, 1)` with `densityHalfDistance = 60`; kept leaves are enlarged by `clamp(1.f / sqrt(childDensity), 1.f, 5.f)`; children are handled in groups of 4 or 16 whose last member gets a scale between 0 and 1 so that it shrinks out rather than vanishing; comment "always keep at least one child". The scale multiplies the leaf's own model-space position (`modelSpacePosition *= leafRecord.scale * lobeScale` in `Leaves.h`), so a leaf grows about its own origin, and the leaf's pattern coordinate is taken before the scale is applied, so the pattern grows with the leaf. — [github.com/Bloodwyn/gptree](https://github.com/Bloodwyn/gptree)
- [read directly: Epic, "Working with Nanite-Enabled Content", Unreal Engine 5.9 documentation] "When Nanite simplifies the geometry in the distance by reducing the number of triangles, it eventually needs to start removing some of these disjoint elements completely. Without Nanite having more information the result will look thinned out because there was major surface area loss. Preserve Area will redistribute that lost area to the remaining triangles by dilating out the open boundary edges. Dilation for symmetric shapes like leaves has the same effect as scaling them up. In non-symmetric cases like ribbons, for example, blades of grass, it has the effect of thickening them." — [Epic documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)
- [read directly: Arseny Kapoulkine, "Billions of triangles redux", 30 September 2026] On Nanite's Preserve Area: after simplifying each cluster group "they compute the total surface area before and after simplification, and restore the area by taking the boundary edges of the resulting geometry, and expanding the vertex positions outwards, dilating the edges. This retains coverage by redistributing surface area to surviving triangles; the result is that each successive layer of the cluster DAG gets fewer, bigger, leaves." — [zeux.io](https://zeux.io/2026/09/30/billions-of-triangles-redux/)

**Results and failure cases reported by others (read directly)**
- [read directly: Hasselgren et al. 2021, Fig. 20, p. 17] Same foliage, 3000 instances, path traced: "Stochastic simplification: 510M tris, PSNR: 14.49 dB"; "Our: 20M tris, PSNR: 23.89 dB"; "Reference: 5.1B tris". Single plant: 170k triangles (stochastic), 6.5k (theirs), 1.7M (reference). Their stand-in is one textured quad per leaf group with shape, material and transparency optimised against images (p. 8). — [arXiv 2104.03989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03989)
- [read directly: Huang et al. 2024, p. 1] Fewer, thicker strands "leads to an excessively bold and dry hair appearance compared to the realistic brightness and hue, as the thick hair meant to represent a cluster of hair strands should have distinct optical properties from a single hair strand." — [arXiv 2405.10565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10565)
- [read directly: MachineGames 2025, PDF p. 16 and p. 65–66] With half the strands "the visual results are close, but lower quality version is darker. This is related to how we do self-shadowing". Baked visibility computed with all strands leaves "ghosts" that still occlude after reduction; "we have as many LODs as we have strands. That is why doing classic fixed-step LODs might be a better option", though "in practice the artifacts weren't too visible". — [Advances 2025 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Strand%20Hair%20in%20IJGC%20-%20Final%20Slides%20(Post-Conference).pdf)
- [read directly: Lipp et al. 2026, p. 8–9] Removing strands makes shadow and deep-opacity maps computed from the full set wrong ("unnatural darkening and visual popping"). Their fix follows Beer–Lambert: with a fraction β = N_remaining / N_total left, visibility exp(−σβd) equals that of a shorter path, so strands are shifted in depth by Δ = −log(β) for the comparison. — [arXiv 2607.04230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04230)
- [read directly: zeux.io, 30 September 2026] Of the area-restoring variant in cluster hierarchies: "this method doesn't have a way to control for where exactly the coverage is lost and regained; the shape of trees far away may deform a bit, as some branches lose more leaves than others"; and "for card-based trees expanding the card boundary edges may result in odd looking branches from far away if they were baked into the texture." — [zeux.io](https://zeux.io/2026/09/30/billions-of-triangles-redux/)

### Inferences
- Hasselgren's √λ is the uncapped case of the recalled contrast rule: if the number of elements per pixel falls in proportion to λ, pixel variance rises by 1/λ and scaling deviations by √λ restores it. Where occlusion already hides most elements, the true factor is nearer 1; applying √λ everywhere over-flattens dense crowns.
- Three working systems choose the kept fraction differently: linear in size on screen (Lipp: u ∝ 1/z), exponential in distance (the tree sample: u = 2^(−d/60)), a fixed density of primitives per screen area (MachineGames: u ∝ 1/z²). Only the last one holds the number of survivors per pixel constant; it is also the only one whose error can be stated in pixels (question 3).
- The tree sample's clamp of the enlargement at 5 means area is no longer paid back below a density of 1/25: beyond that distance its crowns thin. A clamp on size is needed (question 2), but the surplus should then be turned into opacity, not dropped.
- A linear shrink band of width w (as a fraction of n) ending at the cut makes the summed relative area u − w/2 if the band scales area, or u − 2w/3 if it scales side length; the exact compensation for the survivors is therefore 1/(u − w/2) or 1/(u − 2w/3), not 1/u. (Researcher's arithmetic.)
- Elements that are not small or not alike: the sources read give two rules. Do not thin what is individually visible (MachineGames exempts small hair pieces because loss shows more there); do not treat unlike parts as one population (Bako's criticism). In practice: one order per kind, stratified by size and colour, and large or unique parts left to ordinary surface simplification.

### Gaps
- Not verified against the paper: the formula for the kept fraction, the cap inside the contrast factor, the exact transition band and its normalisation, the paper's own result tables, what it says about the point of scaling, and its own list of limitations. The symbol for the kept fraction (u or λ) is unverified.
- "Stochastic Pruning" and the Pixar hair memo could not be opened; nothing beyond the search extracts above was obtained.

## 2. Is there a published rule for the point about which a survivor is enlarged so the outline of the whole does not grow, or for keeping leaf size (texture scale) constant while a card grows?

### Takeaway
No such rule was found in anything read. What the literature offers instead is consistent: do not let one survivor swell in place; replace a *group* by a stand-in that sits at the group's centre with the group's spread, bound it by the group's own extent, and express any surplus of area as partial opacity. And the published way to keep leaves at their true size is not to stretch a card but to re-project the group's real leaves onto the stand-in.

### Cited Findings
- [read directly: Epic, Unreal Engine 5.9 documentation] The only statement found on how the area is given back: boundary edges are dilated; "for symmetric shapes like leaves" this "has the same effect as scaling them up", for ribbons it thickens them. Epic recommends it "for all foliage meshes, but not for meshes that are not intended to be used as scene foliage". — [Epic documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)
- [read directly: zeux.io, 30 September 2026, with footnote 10] "redistributing area to surviving boundary is inherently dangerous: it may lead to severe outward expansion in unexpected cases"; example: on a trunk that lost area, the dilation moved the open boundary where a branch leaves a hole, "resulting in visible and unnecessary geometric extrusion". — [zeux.io](https://zeux.io/2026/09/30/billions-of-triangles-redux/)
- [read directly: meshoptimizer 1.3, `demo/clusterlod.h`, function `dilateBorders`, commit of 14 October 2026] The open-source form of the rule: measure the area of border triangles before and after simplifying a group; do nothing if area did not shrink or shrank below a quarter ("limit expansion to cases of moderate area shrinkage; it may be unsafe to dilate otherwise"); otherwise move open-border vertices outwards in the triangle's plane by distance = (old area − new area) / open perimeter, and enlarge the group's bounding sphere to fit. The option's comment: "dilate open borders for each cluster to try to mitigate area loss; most useful for foliage, should not be enabled everywhere". — [github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer)
- [read directly: Hasselgren et al. 2021, p. 18] The restated Cook rule scales "each leaf uniformly"; no pivot is named. — [arXiv 2104.03989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03989)
- [read directly: gptree sample, `Leaves.h`, `LeafDensity.h`] Leaves are scaled in their own model space and the enlargement is clamped to 5. — [github.com/Bloodwyn/gptree](https://github.com/Bloodwyn/gptree)
- [search extract] SpeedTree's modeller: "Grow scale" enlarges the leaf meshes kept "to fill in the empty space left by those that are removed"; "Jumble" randomises which are kept; the stated aim is to "help maintain the same leaf cloud volume"; normals, texture coordinates and ambient occlusion are kept between levels. Nothing on a pivot or on texture scale. — [SpeedTree modeller documentation (via search)](https://docs8.speedtree.com/modeler/doku.php?id=lod)
- [search extract] Neubert, Pirk, Deussen, Dachsbacher 2011 use "model-specific geometry reduction functions and optimized scaling functions", judged by precision and recall in image space (which pixels are filled, colours ignored). The body of the paper could not be read. — [Konstanz repository (via search)](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/bitstream/123456789/38257/1/Neubert_0-398388.pdf)
- [read directly: Kerbl et al. 2024 (Hierarchical 3D Gaussians), p. 4–5] The stand-in for a group is not an enlarged member. Its mean and covariance are the weighted mean and covariance of the children (the choice that minimises the Kullback–Leibler divergence to the children's mixture); weights are each child's contribution to the image, w′ᵢ = oᵢ √|Σ′ᵢ| (opacity times projected extent, in practice the ellipsoid's surface Sᵢ), chosen so that the parent's contribution to the image equals the sum of its children's (C_p = C₁ + C₂); colour is merged with the same weights; and the parent's opacity is Σ w′ᵢ / S_p, which "can now be larger than 1: instead of opacity, we thus call this value falloff ... resulting α values are clamped to 1". — [arXiv 2406.12080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12080)
- [read directly: Zhou et al. 2024, p. 9–10, Fig. 6 and Eq. 24] A box-shaped stand-in "results in a bloated silhouette"; fitting "a bounding ellipsoid for the geometry in each voxel" and using "the intersection of the voxel and the ellipsoid" gives "a tighter silhouette". The stand-in's coverage is defined as |A|_ω / |B|_ω: projected area of the surfaces inside over projected area of the stand-in itself. — [arXiv 2409.03761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03761)
- [read directly: van Antwerpen et al. (NVIDIA), "Real-time Path Tracing of Massive Dynamic Foliage", HPG 2026 slides, p. 9–13] Their far foliage: chunk a twig's geometry with an oriented bounding-volume hierarchy, "recursively split until node size drops below target error threshold", "replace leaf by 3 perpendicular impostors", "project leaf geometry onto OMMs" (opacity micro-maps). "Projecting geometry on 3 planes increases total area: increases screen-space coverage, increases internal occlusion. Renormalize projected area through partial opacity ... Bake stochastic opacity by dithering OMMs." The stand-ins carry the material index, a shading normal and texture coordinates of the source geometry. — [HPG 2026 slides](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/2026/_assets/slides/Dietger_slides.pdf)
- [read directly: same slides, p. 20] For the twig meshes themselves: "Unconstrained edge contraction causes visual thinning"; they "estimate local feature size as minimum contraction error causing local degenerate geometry" and "filter contractions with errors approaching local feature size", which "prevents visual thinning" and "limits simplification potential". — [HPG 2026 slides](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/2026/_assets/slides/Dietger_slides.pdf)
- [read directly: Karis, "The Journey to Nanite", HPG 2022, p. 96–97 and p. 105] Why size must not be clamped from below: with points, "Thin features become as thick as 1 pixel no matter how far away they are. So, thin wires appear to get thicker the further away they get. This is the same problem voxels have btw and it looks bad." "Points could have a coverage associated with this mip level"; without interpolation that fails "unless it is done stochastically". For voxels: "Voxels would have to express some sort of coverage information to handle this." — [HPG 2022 slides](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf)
- [read directly: Hasselgren et al. 2021, p. 8] The alternative to scaling: "instead of stochastically pruning the procedural scene graph, we replace the complex leaf geometries with textured quads" and "jointly optimize material parameters, shape, and transparency based on visual loss of rendered images"; 0.8 % and 0.4 % of the reference triangles on two Moana assets. — [arXiv 2104.03989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03989)
- [read directly: zeux.io, 30 September 2026] The only remark found on textures under enlargement is a warning: growing a card that has branches painted into it "may result in odd looking branches from far away". — [zeux.io](https://zeux.io/2026/09/30/billions-of-triangles-redux/)

### Inferences
- **Why enlarging about the tip held the outline at 102 % and then let it go to 122 %** (the project's own oak, figures from the assignment). A card whose tip lies on a hull of local radius R and which grows sideways to half-width w puts its corners at √(R² + w²) from the centre: the outline grows by about ½ (w/R)². That is 2 % at w ≈ 0.2 R and 22 % at w ≈ 0.66 R. Scaling about a card's own centre instead adds half the growth in size straight outwards. (Researcher's geometry; a locally spherical hull is assumed.)
- **Measured in pixels the swelling is bounded by the size of the survivor.** If no survivor is wider than τ pixels, growth about the outermost point is at most τ² / (8 R_px) pixels, and growth about the centre at most τ/2 pixels, where R_px is the crown's radius on screen. With τ = 2 both are under a pixel for any crown more than a few pixels across. Swelling of 109 to 134 % therefore means cards were enlarged while still many pixels wide, which is thinning done earlier than the picture allows, for the sake of cost.
- **Three published devices stop that earlier thinning from showing**, and they can be combined: (a) place and shape the stand-in from the first and second moments of the group it replaces (Kerbl), so the union keeps the crown's centre and spread; (b) cut the stand-in off at the bound of what it replaces (Zhou's ellipsoid intersected with the cell): in a shader this is one test of a fragment against the crown's or the branch cluster's ellipsoid, after which nothing can stand outside the near model's outline; (c) when the stand-in then has more area than the parts it stands for, lower its opacity to area of parts over area of stand-in (Zhou's coverage, Kerbl's falloff, NVIDIA's dithered opacity) instead of shrinking it.
- **Leaf size.** A survivor that stands for 1/u sprigs should show 1/u sprigs' worth of leaves at their true size, not one sprig magnified. The published methods that achieve this re-project the real group onto the stand-in (NVIDIA's micro-maps, Hasselgren's optimised quads, billboard clouds before them). For cards cut from one painted sprig the equivalent without baking is to keep texture coordinates in world units, so the card is a larger window onto a repeating field of sprigs with the same mean opacity, and to let the mip chain (with coverage preserved) do the filtering. No source read prescribes this; it follows from the coverage and contrast rules.

### Gaps
- Whether Cook et al. say anything about the point of scaling is unknown (paper not read).
- Neubert et al.'s optimised scaling functions, the nearest thing to a published answer on outline growth, could not be read.
- No source was found that measures outline growth against the kind of pivot.

## 3. What quantity should be the "error" of a thinned crowd so that one threshold in pixels serves surfaces and crowds alike? What do Epic's voxels use?

### Takeaway
Every system read measures a stand-in by the size on screen of the region it summarises: the deviation of a simplified patch, the box of a merged group, the width of a voxel or of a thick hair, the spacing of the primitives that remain. For a thinned crowd that region is the spacing of the survivors, which with area held constant equals the size of a survivor; one threshold in pixels then serves both. Epic's documents say only that voxels are "near pixel-sized" and that the builder switches to voxels where their simplification error is the lower one; the formula of that error is not public in anything that could be read.

### Cited Findings

**Surfaces**
- [read directly: Karis, Stubbe, Wihlidal, "Nanite: A Deep Dive", SIGGRAPH Advances 2021, p. 63–67] "Error calculated by simplifier projected to screen", "corrected for distance and angle distortion at worst-case point in sphere bounds"; "Draw a cluster when: parent error is too high && our error is small enough"; "Force error to be monotonic: parent view error >= child view error"; and on transitions: "If < 1 pixel error, they are imperceptibly different. Temporal AA sees any difference as aliasing", geomorphing or cross-fading "would be expensive at render time or require significant additional data or both". — [Advances 2021 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)
- [read directly: same deck, p. 58–59] Scale independence: "A cluster that covers N pixels should simplify and calculate its error the same as any other that covers N pixels"; position and attribute error are mixed with weights for which "there is no theoretical foundation"; "edge length limits" are part of the metric. — [Advances 2021 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)
- [read directly: Cignoni et al. 2005 (Batched Multi-Triangulation), p. 6–7] They use "the average edge length of the triangle of the patch" as the error because it "is monotonic along the levels of the DAG", and report rendering "with a pixel precision, computed as the average length of the triangles projected onto the screen". — [Batched Multi Triangulation](https://vcg.isti.cnr.it/Publications/2005/CGGMPS05/BatchedMT_Vis05.pdf)
- [read directly: meshoptimizer 1.3, `demo/clusterlod.h`] Projected error = `bounds.error / max(distance(bounds.center, camera_position) - bounds.radius, camera_znear) * (camera_proj * 0.5f)`, times screen height for pixels; an option to "limit error by edge length, aiming to remove subpixel triangles even if the attribute error is high"; errors are forced monotonic by `max(previous_error * merge_previous, current_error) + current_error * merge_additive`. — [github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer)

**Crowds, voxels, splats, strands**
- [read directly: Epic, "Nanite Foliage", Unreal Engine 5.9 documentation] "Nanite Voxels are near pixel-sized aggregate voxels"; "These voxels are imperceptible to the eye because of their size on screen"; the builder "voxelizes them into clusters of, at most, 128 4x4x4 voxel bricks. The simplification error of the voxel representation is the deciding factor for if the simplify makes the switch to voxels from triangles — it will switch to voxels if doing so would result in lower error than using triangles." At run time "voxel clusters are binned separately from triangle clusters ... sorted into depth buckets, and rasterized from front to back". The page is marked "Experimental". — [Epic documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [search extract, taken over from the earlier round and not re-checked] Karis: "LOD is chosen such that voxels are <= 1 pixel wide". — [mirror of briankaris.bsky.social](https://www.bushdrum.com/bsky/briankaris.bsky.social?tab=media)
- [read directly: Karis, HPG 2022, p. 117] By keeping the source triangles for the near view "there shouldn't be a point where voxels get big and clearly look like cubes". — [HPG 2022 slides](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf)
- [read directly: NVIDIA, HPG 2026 slides, p. 14 and p. 23] Level selection uses "precomputed LOD simplification errors" against a "screen space target error threshold". The errors that are constrained in screen space are listed by source: rigid animation, the quadric error of edge contraction, bone-animation fitting, and for the leaf stand-ins "OMM replacement OBB and micro-triangle extent", that is, the size of the box that was replaced and the size of one texel of the opacity map. "Simplification errors + screen-space error threshold → LOD transition distance." — [HPG 2026 slides](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/2026/_assets/slides/Dietger_slides.pdf)
- [read directly: Kerbl et al. 2024, p. 5, 7, 10] "Granularity" of a node is the projected size on screen of the bounding box over the leaves it contains (its largest side); the cut takes nodes "whose projected bounding box is smaller than a given target granularity τ on screen, e.g., 1 pixel"; in practice τ = 3, 6 and 15 pixels are evaluated, 3 pixels being "the minimum extent of a primitive" after the low-pass filter. — [arXiv 2406.12080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12080)
- [read directly: Yang et al. 2025 (Virtualized 3D Gaussians), p. 5] The footprint F of a cluster group is the pixel area of its bounding sphere; a cluster is drawn when F_c ≤ τ < F_p (its own group passes, its parent's fails), the tolerance being set by the user at run time. — [arXiv 2505.06523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06523)
- [read directly: Zhou et al. 2024, Fig. 1, p. 1; Bako et al. 2022, p. 7] Each instance uses the level "where the projected voxel size matches the pixel footprint (rounded to the nearest power of two)"; the level is chosen "so that the relative size of a voxel does not exceed the bandlimiting frequency of the filtering kernel". — [arXiv 2409.03761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03761); [arXiv 2211.05932](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05932)
- [read directly: Huang et al. 2024, p. 8 and p. 11] "the hair LoD level is determined based on its screen space width"; the criteria assume "that the hair width is around 2 pixels on the screen". — [arXiv 2405.10565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10565)
- [read directly: MachineGames 2025, PDF p. 17] A density of primitives per screen area: at most 2000 segments per 64×64 tile. — [Advances 2025 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Strand%20Hair%20in%20IJGC%20-%20Final%20Slides%20(Post-Conference).pdf)
- [read directly: Tencent, "Seamless Rendering on Mobile", SIGGRAPH Advances 2024, slide 12] On phones: "We use LOD error similar to UE5's Nanite for cluster LOD culling, but we design a distance curve for LodFactor selection". — [Advances 2024 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Cao-NanoMesh/AdavanceRealtimeRendering_NanoMesh0810.pdf)

**Why the surface error fails on crowds**
- [read directly: zeux.io, 30 September 2026] "at a distance where each leaf's triangle is under a pixel in size, every leaf should, logically speaking, be replaced by its simplified copy - which, in this case, is 'no leaf'. Unfortunately, this also happens across the entire mesh, in an act of coordinated omission: every cluster looks approximately the same as any other cluster, has approximately the same error, and thus is quickly replaced with the version of the cluster where most leaves are removed." — [zeux.io](https://zeux.io/2026/09/30/billions-of-triangles-redux/)
- [read directly: Epic, Unreal Engine 5.9 documentation] Aggregate geometry "from a distance appears more like a partially opaque cloud than a solid surface", so "Nanite is more likely to determine it cannot reduce aggregate geometry nearly as aggressively". — [Epic documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)
- [read directly: meshoptimizer 1.3 README] `meshopt_SimplifyPrune` "allows the simplifier to remove isolated components"; the cut-off is a component radius. The new voxel remesher merges "features closer than a voxel", closes "gaps smaller than a voxel", and "features that are thin in two dimensions, such as wires or strands, may disappear". — [github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer)

### Inferences
- **One definition covers both cases.** The error of a stand-in is the diameter, in world units, of the largest region inside which the stand-in no longer says where things are. For a simplified surface patch that is its deviation from the surface (and at most its edge length). For a stand-in that replaces a group of parts it is the diameter of the group. Projected to the screen with the same sphere-based formula, both are compared with the same threshold.
- **For a prefix of a shuffled list the groups are implicit.** At kept fraction u each survivor stands for 1/u parts; the region it speaks for has the diameter of the spacing between survivors, d(u) = d₀ / √u in projection. With area conserved and cover near one, the survivor's own size equals that spacing. So "size of the stand-in on screen", "spacing of survivors on screen" and "diameter of the merged group on screen" are the same number up to a constant, and holding it at τ pixels gives u = min(1, (z₀/z)²), z₀ being the distance at which an untouched part is τ pixels.
- **A stratified order is what makes that number small.** With a plain random order some survivors speak for much larger regions than d(u): those are the clumps and holes. Stratification is the crowd's counterpart of an error-driven simplifier.
- **Where the threshold bites at the screen's own resolution** (researcher's arithmetic, assuming a vertical field of view of 60° at 2160 lines: one pixel is 0.535 mrad): a 10 cm part is one pixel wide at 187 m, a 30 cm card two pixels at 281 m. With τ of one or two pixels thinning starts late; systems that want it earlier use 3 to 15 pixels (Kerbl) and must then hide the change in time, by interpolation rather than by the threshold (questions 6 and 7).
- MachineGames' bound is 2000 / 4096 ≈ 0.49 segments per pixel of the model's screen area: a density threshold of the same family, expressed per area.

### Gaps
- The formula for the error of a voxel cluster in Nanite, and how opacity and the spread of normals are computed per voxel, are not in the documentation; the two Unreal Fest Stockholm 2025 talks ("Large Scale Animated Foliage in The Witcher 4" and "The Future of Nanite Foliage") exist only as video and could not be read.
- No source gives a perceptual measure for crowds (how many pixels of survivor size are invisible in motion); the values in use (1, 2, 3 to 15 pixels; 0.49 segments per pixel) are engineering choices.

## 4. Prefiltering: coverage, visibility-weighted colour, the distribution of normals turned into roughness, self-shadowing. What LEAN, LEADR, SGGX and Loubet and Neyret prescribe, and what a real-time engine keeps

### Takeaway
The complete answer in the literature is a per-cell far-field scattering function plus coverage plus visibility between cells; the most recent paper stores, per cell, total surface area, moments of roughness, direction-dependent moments of the material, a distribution of normals, a tight bounding shape and compressed visibility. Real-time engines keep a small part of it: exact or dithered coverage, material fetched from the source, one normal with a scalar spread picked at random per pixel, a statistical correction for shadows, and a temporal filter to average the randomness away.

### Cited Findings

**The theory, as stated in papers that were read**
- [read directly: Karis et al., Advances 2021, p. 61] "Visibility prefiltering means expressing partial coverage"; "Triangle mesh stops making sense"; "Best solution is a hybrid: superpixel features as mesh, subpixel features expressed volumetrically" (citing "Hybrid mesh-volume LoDs for all-scale pre-filtering of complex 3D assets"); "How to render partial coverage volumetric data? Stochastic? Ray march? Point scatter? OIT?" Notes: "Subpixel partial coverage and prefiltering is especially important for aggregate geometry like leaves and grass. Solving that case is still an open question." — [Advances 2021 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)
- [read directly: Zhou et al. 2024, p. 4] The far-field assumption: when a scene is far enough "that the sensor can no longer distinguish the internal spatial structure, we may drop the positional dependency"; what remains is a function of incoming and outgoing direction only, the aggregated scattering function of the cell. — [arXiv 2409.03761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03761)
- [read directly: Zhou et al. 2024, p. 12] Stored per non-empty cell: "(1) The total surface area |A| within the voxel (for normalization). (2) The first two moments of roughness α. (3) Directional moments of material parameters ... (4) The surface normal distribution ... represented by one or a mixture of SGGX distributions ... (5) The ellipsoid of the truncated ellipsoid primitive as an affine transform. (6) Wavelet basis coefficients for the aggregated visibility". — [arXiv 2409.03761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03761)
- [read directly: Zhou et al. 2024, p. 2, 3, 10] Correlation: "We lose track of how the geometry is distributed locally within a volume when it is abstracted away", and of "long-range visibility"; classic volume transport "assumes independently distributed scatterers"; "when spatial correlation exists, transmittance is no longer exponential and cannot be modeled only by extinction coefficients". Their answer: a tight shape per cell for local structure and recorded visibility for long-range structure, after which cells are summed in any order (p. 12). — [arXiv 2409.03761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03761)
- [read directly: Zhou et al. 2024, p. 21, Fig. 19] Their stated limit: one cell cannot represent the dependence between what is visible and what colour it has ("cannot handle correlation between visibility and material in a single voxel"); it lessens as cells get smaller. — [arXiv 2409.03761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03761)
- [read directly: Bako et al. 2022, abstract (p. 1), p. 2 and p. 8] Per cell and scale: "an appearance phase function and directional coverage mask" and an albedo, compressed by a network; no original geometry at render time. Limits of earlier models: specular and glinty effects, complex layered materials, and "local occlusions are also difficult to fully capture". — [arXiv 2211.05932](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05932)

**Normals into roughness: LEAN, LEADR, SGGX**
- [read directly: Hasselgren et al. 2021, p. 2] "Linear texture prefiltering methods are incorrect when applied to spatially varying BRDF ... and surface normal maps. Common approaches instead filter the normal distribution function"; the filtered distribution is stored as Gaussians (Toksvig), "moment statistics [Olano and Baker 2010]" (LEAN) or spherical harmonics; "further work has considered, e.g., displacement mapping, and masking and shadowing effects [Dupuy et al. 2013]" (LEADR). — [arXiv 2104.03989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03989)
- [read directly: Zhou et al. 2024, p. 2] Normal-map filtering "converts the normal directions inside a footprint to a normal distribution function (NDF) to preserve highlights when viewed from afar"; displacement-map filtering "incorporates the microscale geometric details ... inside a footprint into a shading model [Dupuy et al. 2013]". — [arXiv 2409.03761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03761)
- [recalled] LEAN (Olano and Baker 2010) stores the first and second moments of the surface slope, five numbers per texel; because moments average linearly, ordinary mip-mapping filters them; the covariance (second moment minus the square of the first) is added to the base roughness, giving a wider and possibly anisotropic highlight instead of sparkle. LEADR (Dupuy et al. 2013) does the same for displaced geometry with masking and shadowing, so displaced detail can hand over to shading as it shrinks under a pixel. — [LEAN Mapping (not reachable)](https://www.csee.umbc.edu/~olano/papers/lean/)
- [read directly: Mitsuba 3 source, `include/mitsuba/render/microflake.h`, implementing Heitz et al. 2015] SGGX is six numbers, the entries of a symmetric positive-definite 3×3 matrix S. Projected area of the flakes seen from direction ω: σ(ω) = √(ωᵀ S ω). Distribution of normals: D(ω_m) = 1 / (π √|S| (ω_mᵀ S⁻¹ ω_m)²) (the code evaluates the same expression through the adjugate of S). Visible normals can be sampled in closed form. — [github.com/mitsuba-renderer/mitsuba3](https://github.com/mitsuba-renderer/mitsuba3)
- [read directly: Zhou et al. 2024, p. 3] SGGX gives "efficient microflake phase functions that support linear interpolation and closed-form importance sampling". — [arXiv 2409.03761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03761)
- [read directly: Fabre et al. 2026, p. 8] One SGGX takes 6 bytes in the compact form. But averaging eight cells into one distribution level after level "produces a loss of accuracy, specially with highly anisotropic distributions. This inaccuracy is increased with each interpolation, producing very isotropic results, that will yield rougher scattering at the rendering stage"; they keep a small clustered set of distributions per cell instead. — [arXiv 2604.13191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13191)

**Hybrid mesh and volume: Loubet and Neyret 2017**
- [read directly: Zhou et al. 2024, p. 3 and p. 14] The method "performs a binary classification on the input scene to divide it into a mesh part and a volume part at each scale"; in comparison it "tends to produce bloated, over transparent results, which is especially noticeable at coarser LoD resolution. This could be due to both misclassification (too much volume) and the neglect of correlation", and its mesh simplification can change curvature and lose highlights. — [arXiv 2409.03761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03761)
- [read directly: Bako et al. 2022, p. 2 and p. 4] It "uses geometric analysis to label regions of a scene as either belonging to macrogeometry or an aggregate microgeometry"; "the heuristics used to decide on a label can misclassify regions". A later model by the same authors adds self-shadowing inside a cell for down-sampling. — [arXiv 2211.05932](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05932)
- [search extract] From the abstract: macroscopic surfaces stay meshes; sub-resolution detail becomes a heterogeneous microflake volume through a voxelisation "designed to keep local occlusion intact". — [Wiley, CGF 36(2) (via search)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/cgf.13138)

**What real-time engines keep (all read directly)**
- [Epic, "Nanite Foliage", UE 5.9 documentation] "Many surfaces with different normals can be contained inside the region covered by a single voxel. So instead of storing a single normal per voxel, a distribution of normals is stored instead. This is similar in concept to how material roughness controls the distribution of microfacets"; "When writing to the GBuffer, a normal is chosen stochastically per pixel from this distribution. In a future release the distribution will be accounted for directly in the shading like roughness is. This will reduce noise and was already in use in the Witcher 4 demo"; the statistics are "stored in the vertex color alpha channel". — [Epic documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)
- [NVIDIA, HPG 2026 slides, p. 11–13, 16, 21–22] Coverage: partial opacity baked as a dither. Shading: material index, compacted shading normal and quantised texture coordinates per micro-triangle. Normals: "Plane projections distort the perceived normal distribution: amplifies grazing normals, suppresses face-on normals ... Apply bias: pull shading normals slightly toward incoming ray. Interpolation weights fitted to random triangle soup." Transitions: "Screen space silhouette error bounded by construction. Shading error not bounded. Shading only matches in aggregate. ... Rely on temporal denoiser". Motion: "Foliage LODs don't need to match specific poses. Foliage LODs do need to match motion distribution"; they "equate variance of first-order displacement approximation". — [HPG 2026 slides](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/2026/_assets/slides/Dietger_slides.pdf)
- [MachineGames 2025, PDF p. 82] Coverage per pixel is kept exactly as a total; only the three front fragments are shaded; C_front = 1 − (1 − C₁)(1 − C₂)(1 − C₃), the missing coverage takes a weighted mean of the three with weights wᵢ = Cᵢ · 0.25^(3−i), favouring the back ones. — [Advances 2025 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Strand%20Hair%20in%20IJGC%20-%20Final%20Slides%20(Post-Conference).pdf)
- [Lipp et al. 2026, p. 5 and p. 8–9] Strands are drawn one pixel wide ("We experimented with rendering thick strands; however, we found that this provided no visual benefit in the far field case"), opacity is rebuilt by a filter, and shadows are corrected by the depth shift −log(β). — [arXiv 2607.04230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04230)
- [Huang et al. 2024, p. 1, 9, 10] A thick strand standing for a cluster gets its own scattering model (single and multiple scattering inside the cluster, elliptical cross-section); reported 2× faster at middle distance and up to 13× far on an RTX 3090, extra memory about 78 % of the model, 6 to 12 minutes of set-up per groom. — [arXiv 2405.10565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10565)

### Inferences
- **The list an engineer can apply to any kind of part.** For each stand-in, from the parts it replaces: (0) projected area per direction, which becomes coverage: area of parts over area of stand-in, capped at one; (1) centre, weighted by each part's contribution to the image; (2) spread about the centre, which bounds the stand-in; (3) mean colour with the same weights, and its variance if contrast is to be kept; (4) mean normal and the spread of normals, the spread acting as roughness; (5) how much the parts shadow each other and their neighbours; (6) the variance of their motion. Items 0 to 4 are sums over parts and can be computed when the parts are generated; they average linearly, which is why one rule can run across all scales.
- **A scalar spread is the practical minimum for normals.** Epic stores one statistic per voxel; SGGX's six numbers add direction-dependent area, which matters for flat cards seen edge-on. Computing the matrix directly from all leaves of a group avoids the drift towards isotropy that Fabre reports for repeated averaging.
- **Independence is the hidden assumption.** Area-preserving thinning keeps optical depth and so mean opacity only if parts are placed independently. Crowns are clumped along branches; a stratified order thins every clump in proportion and so keeps the clumping, which is the cheap way to respect the correlation the offline papers store explicitly.
- **Shadows must see the same thinned crowd with the same compensation**, or be corrected statistically (Lipp). MachineGames' darker half-density hair is what happens otherwise.
- **Randomness needs a filter.** Epic's per-pixel random normal, NVIDIA's dithered opacity and the stochastic splat renderers all lean on temporal accumulation or a denoiser. An engine that draws at the screen's own resolution without temporal anti-aliasing has only multisample coverage to average with, so it should prefer the analytic forms (roughness from the spread; alpha to coverage) over random picks.

### Gaps
- LEAN, LEADR, the SGGX paper and Loubet and Neyret's paper were not opened; their content here is second-hand or recalled, except the SGGX formulae, which were read in code.
- No source read measures which of the preserved quantities matters most to a viewer for foliage.
- Coverage-preserving mip maps for alpha-tested textures are standard practice [recalled], but no reachable source was read for them in this round.

## 5. arXiv, 2022 to 2026: every method found for level of detail of aggregate geometry, vegetation, hair or other non-surface content (voxel, volume, point, splat, neural stand-ins): method, claimed quality, cost, real time or not, hardware

### Takeaway
arXiv holds almost nothing on level of detail for game vegetation: queries for foliage, vegetation, grass, "aggregate detail", "stochastic pruning", "virtualized geometry" and "geomorph" in computer science since 2022 return no graphics paper on the subject. ### Inferences
- **Ranking by picture quality, from what was read.** Highest, offline only: per-cell aggregated appearance with cells matched to the pixel (Zhou; Bako). ## 6. Batched Multi-Triangulation (Cignoni et al. 2005): the conditions that make any cut free of cracks, its requirements on error and bounds, whether it discusses geomorphing. Hoppe's progressive meshes: how a geomorph is built and what it guarantees

### Takeaway
A cut is free of cracks if and only if it is closed under dependency (every piece whose region a chosen piece overwrote is also chosen) and every replacement piece has exactly the border of what it replaced; the error and the bounding spheres must grow monotonically towards the root so that a threshold yields such a closed cut. The paper does not use geomorphs; like its relatives it relies on errors of about a pixel. Hoppe's geomorph draws the finer mesh with its vertices moved to their ancestors' positions and blends linearly: it is exactly the coarse mesh at one end and exactly the fine mesh at the other, and no face appears or vanishes abruptly because new faces grow from zero area.

### Cited Findings

**Batched Multi-Triangulation (read directly; page = PDF page of the 8-page paper)**
- [p. 2] The framework is stated for any description: "With the general term description we denote all the primitives that can be used to describe this domain, e.g. triangulated surfaces, point sets, parametric surface etc." Replacing a part f of a description by g, where both describe the same part of the domain, is "pasting"; g is a fragment and f its floor. A triangulation is conforming "if for any pair of triangles in T their intersection is either empty or it is coincident with a vertex or an edge of both triangles". — [Batched Multi Triangulation](https://vcg.isti.cnr.it/Publications/2005/CGGMPS05/BatchedMT_Vis05.pdf)
- [p. 3] The condition on a selection: "a fragment gᵢ depends on the fragments intersecting its floor"; "if we take a subsequence ... such that for each fragment ... all the fragments on which it depends are also in the subsequence (transitive closure of dependency), then S′ is also a sequence of valid pasting". Dependencies form a directed acyclic graph; a closed subset "is conveniently encoded with a cut", and the union of the patches on the arcs crossing the cut "corresponds to the result of pasting all the fragments". Refining an arc forces, recursively, every node in the backward star of the new node into the cut; coarsening "is legal only if all the arcs in the forward star of the tail node are also in the cut". — [Batched Multi Triangulation](https://vcg.isti.cnr.it/Publications/2005/CGGMPS05/BatchedMT_Vis05.pdf)
- [p. 3] The condition on the pieces: "In the case of meshes the pasting operation implies that the old region f and the new g must have the same boundary, so in the simplification or refinement algorithm the boundary ... must be preserved." — [Batched Multi Triangulation](https://vcg.isti.cnr.it/Publications/2005/CGGMPS05/BatchedMT_Vis05.pdf)
- [p. 3] Three conditions for a graph that adapts well: "1) the length of a path connecting the root to any leaf is logarithmic in the number of nodes", "2) the diameter of a fragment decreases geometrically with the distance of the corresponding node from the root", "3) a fragment should not share boundaries with all the fragments intersecting its floor" (otherwise "there will be no simplification along this border"). — [Batched Multi Triangulation](https://vcg.isti.cnr.it/Publications/2005/CGGMPS05/BatchedMT_Vis05.pdf)
- [p. 4] How the construction meets them: a sequence of coarser and coarser partitions; "when simplifying from step i to step i + 1, first we assemble the patches of Ψ*ᵢ to build patches of Ψᵢ₊₁, then we simplify them without touching the borders of Ψᵢ₊₁ patches and finally we split the result of simplification according to the Ψ*ᵢ₊₁ partition"; "patch borders with the same color always match". — [Batched Multi Triangulation](https://vcg.isti.cnr.it/Publications/2005/CGGMPS05/BatchedMT_Vis05.pdf)
- [p. 6] Error and bounds: the screen-space error comes from "an object-space view-independent error measure and the bounding sphere of each patch"; "The relation between the error of the arcs of the graph is preserved by imposing that each bounding sphere encloses the bounding sphere of all the arcs in the subgraph." Error based on Hausdorff distance "often needs to be corrected in order to respect the partial order among the node of the DAG"; they use average edge length, which is monotonic. The selection is interruptible and "is guaranteed that the representation is conformal even if not to the required accuracy". — [Batched Multi Triangulation](https://vcg.isti.cnr.it/Publications/2005/CGGMPS05/BatchedMT_Vis05.pdf)
- [p. 2, 7] Geomorphing is not part of the method. It is mentioned once, as other authors' "techniques to alleviate popping effects due to small triangle counts". Results: about 4 million triangles per frame at 35 frames a second with pixel precision on a GeForce 6800 GT; "the number of triangles is halved at each iteration", and the structure is about 40 % larger on disk than the input. — [Batched Multi Triangulation](https://vcg.isti.cnr.it/Publications/2005/CGGMPS05/BatchedMT_Vis05.pdf)

**Its relatives on the same site (read directly)**
- [Adaptive TetraPuzzles, Cignoni et al. 2004, p. 4–5, 7] "The extraction of a consistent mesh is ensured by constructing the saturated object space errors/spheres so that they are equal for all the tetrahedra in a diamond and monotonically decreasing when descending the hierarchy. Since the projection to screen space is also monotonic, the diamond composition property ensures that we always extract a conforming volumetric mesh". The scale that turns quadric error into world units is set as "the smallest value ... leading to no image difference in a fixed number of random views, when setting the screen space tolerance below 1 pixel". On popping: "it is possible to use very small pixel thresholds, virtually eliminating popping artifacts, without the need to resort to costly geomorphing features". — [Adaptive TetraPuzzles](https://vcg.isti.cnr.it/Publications/2004/CGGMPS04/vbdam_sig04.pdf)
- [Ponchio and Dellepiane 2016 (the Nexus format for the web), p. 3–5] Earlier patch methods either disabled simplification of borders or relied "on global, spatial GPU geomorphing" to make borders agree; the Pisa methods "guarantee coherence between different resolutions while at the same time ensuring that no boundary persists for more than one level. While not progressive in a strict sense, given current rendering speed, the density of triangles on screen is so high that popping effects are not noticeable." For point clouds the same structure is used with "a point filtering approach, where half of the points are removed at each level". — [Multiresolution and fast decompression for optimal web-based rendering](https://vcg.isti.cnr.it/Publications/2016/PD16/FastDec_Ponchio.pdf)
- [Karis et al., Advances 2021, p. 63–67] Nanite states the same two requirements (same border for interchangeable groups; monotonic error and bounds so that "parent error is too high && our error is small enough" picks one cut) and likewise uses no geomorph. — [Advances 2021 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)

**Hoppe (read directly)**
- [Progressive Meshes, SIGGRAPH 1996, PDF p. 3, §3.2] Between consecutive meshes: the geomorph has the connectivity of the finer mesh Mⁱ⁺¹ and positions v_j(α) = α v_jⁱ⁺¹ + (1 − α) v_sⁱ for the two vertices of the split and unchanged otherwise, so that "M^G(0) looks like Mⁱ and M^G(1) looks like Mⁱ⁺¹ — in fact M^G(1) = Mⁱ⁺¹", "without any visible 'snapping'". Between any coarser M^c and finer M^f: each fine vertex has "a unique ancestor vertex" in the coarse mesh and v_j(α) = α v_j^f + (1 − α) v^c_{A(j)}. "the faces of M^c are a proper subset of the faces of M^f, and ... those faces of M^f missing from M^c are invisible in M^G(0) because they have been collapsed to degenerate (zero area) triangles." (Formula checked against a rendering of the page.) — [Progressive Meshes](https://hhoppe.com/pm.pdf)
- [same page] Attributes: "Discrete attributes by their nature cannot be smoothly interpolated. Fortunately, these discrete attributes are associated with faces of the mesh, and the 'geometric' geomorphs described above smoothly introduce faces." Scalar attributes are interpolated like positions; "normals are best interpolated over the unit sphere"; a corner with no counterpart in the coarse mesh keeps its value. — [Progressive Meshes](https://hhoppe.com/pm.pdf)
- [View-Dependent Refinement of Progressive Meshes, SIGGRAPH 1997, PDF p. 6] Between two selectively refined meshes: find a mesh M^G "whose active vertex front is everywhere lower than or equal to that of" both; its faces are "a superset of both"; each of its vertices has a unique ancestor in each; blend v_j(α) = (1 − α) v_{G→A(j)} + α v_{G→B(j)}. "More research is needed to determine the feasibility and usefulness of generating geomorphs at runtime." — [View-dependent refinement](https://hhoppe.com/vdrpm.pdf)
- [Smooth View-Dependent Level-of-Detail Control, IEEE Visualization 1998, PDF p. 4–5] The run-time form. Refinement: "The mesh connectivity is still modified immediately, but we initially assign the new vertex v_u the same geometry as its sibling v_t, and only gradually modify the geometry of v_u to its refined state over the next gtime frames" (set to one second). A change is morphed "if and only if the region of the affected surface is visible", because an invisible region "may have unbounded screen-space error". Refinements "can be composed arbitrarily, even with overlapping lifetimes". Coarsening is harder: "the geometry interpolation must take place first, and only then can the mesh connectivity be coarsened", so it is allowed "one layer at a time" at half the duration. The error test looks ahead to where the viewer will be when the morph ends. Result: "Although the model may at times have a projected geometric error of a few pixels, results indicate that geomorphs make this error nearly imperceptible", "effective enough that we can increase the pixel error tolerance to improve frame rate (up to 72 frames/sec) with few noticeable artifacts". Memory 88n + 112m + 52g bytes, g morphing vertices being a fraction of the m active ones. — [Smooth view-dependent LOD](https://hhoppe.com/svdlod.pdf)
- [Efficient Implementation of Progressive Meshes, 1998, PDF p. 6] A geomorph "is essentially a copy of the mesh M^f, but whose attributes at vertices and wedges interpolate between their values in M^f and those of their vertex and wedge ancestors in M^c"; building one costs "approximately twice as much time as simple iteration". — [Efficient implementation of progressive meshes](https://hhoppe.com/efficientpm.pdf)

### Inferences
- **What a geomorph guarantees and what it does not.** Guaranteed: continuity in time of every vertex and attribute; identity with the coarse mesh at one end and the fine mesh at the other; no face appears at more than zero area. Not guaranteed: any bound on the error of the blend in between (it is a straight-line blend, not a simplification), and nothing about silhouettes. Hoppe's own evidence is that with a geomorph an error of a few pixels passes unnoticed, where without one the threshold must be about a pixel.
- **For crowds the border condition is empty and the dependency condition is trivial.** Separate parts share no borders, so there is nothing to crack. A prefix of one ordered list is a chain of dependencies: every prefix is a valid cut. What remains of the theory is the monotonic error and the nested bounds, needed as soon as groups of parts are arranged in a hierarchy (for culling, streaming or varying density inside one object).
- **The crowd's geomorph is the shrink at the cut.** A part that leaves shrinks to zero area at a point, as a collapsed face does; a part that stands in for a group starts at the group's parameters, as Hoppe's new vertex starts at its sibling's position and as Kerbl's children start at the parent's. This is the one construction that Cook's "smooth animation", Hoppe's geomorph and the splat hierarchy have in common.
- **Two ways to make a change of detail invisible, and they trade against each other**: keep the error under about a pixel and switch at once (Batched Multi-Triangulation, TetraPuzzles, Nexus, Nanite with temporal anti-aliasing), or allow a few pixels and morph (Hoppe 1998, Kerbl 2024). Without temporal anti-aliasing the first needs a threshold truly under a pixel; the second is the cheaper route to "nothing visible".

### Gaps
- Far Voxels (Gobbetti and Marton 2005) is not on vcg.isti.cnr.it and was not read; statements about it in the earlier round stay recalled.
- No source read combines cluster hierarchies with run-time geomorphs on current GPUs.

## 7. From all of it: the practical rules that make a far crowd look like the near crowd made small, with nothing swelling, shrinking or changing style

### Takeaway
One rule covers every kind of part: arrange the parts of an asset in a fixed, stratified order; draw a prefix whose length keeps the on-screen spacing of survivors at a threshold in pixels; let each survivor stand for the group it replaces by matching that group's area, centre, spread, mean colour and spread of normals, never reaching beyond the group's own bound and turning any surplus of area into opacity; and move every part in or out continuously. The sources read support each clause separately; no single paper states them together, and no paper covers the choice of pivot or the scale of the texture.

### Cited Findings (each rule with the source read that backs it)
1. **One fixed order, a prefix, at least one part always kept.** "the common practice of randomizing strands order" and taking "only a section of strands buffer" (MachineGames, PDF p. 15); "always keep at least one child" (tree sample); a system that "ensures at least one cluster group remains selected to prevent object disappearance" (Yang et al., p. 9). — [Advances 2025 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Strand%20Hair%20in%20IJGC%20-%20Final%20Slides%20(Post-Conference).pdf); [gptree](https://github.com/Bloodwyn/gptree); [arXiv 2505.06523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06523)
2. **Pay the area back exactly.** Sides × √(1/λ) for a leaf (Hasselgren, p. 18); thickness for a strand (MachineGames; Lipp, p. 3); scaling for symmetric shapes and thickening for ribbons (Epic). — [arXiv 2104.03989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03989); [Epic documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)
3. **Keep the stand-in inside what it replaces and express surplus area as opacity.** Tight shape against a "bloated silhouette" and coverage = area inside over area of stand-in (Zhou, p. 9–10); opacity as summed contribution over the parent's extent (Kerbl, p. 4); "renormalize projected area through partial opacity" (NVIDIA, p. 11); a size that never drops below a pixel "looks bad" without coverage (Karis 2022, p. 96). — [arXiv 2409.03761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03761); [arXiv 2406.12080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12080); [HPG 2026 slides](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/2026/_assets/slides/Dietger_slides.pdf); [HPG 2022 slides](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf)
4. **Place and shape the stand-in from the group's weighted mean and covariance.** (Kerbl, p. 4.) — [arXiv 2406.12080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12080)
5. **Pull colours towards the mean as parts are shed**, by √λ in the simple case. (Hasselgren, p. 18.) — [arXiv 2104.03989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03989)
6. **Carry the spread of normals, not one normal.** (Epic, "a distribution of normals is stored"; Zhou, p. 12.) — [Epic documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage); [arXiv 2409.03761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03761)
7. **Give the survivor the shading of a group, not of a part.** Otherwise the look turns "bold and dry" (Huang, p. 1) or darker through self-shadowing (MachineGames, PDF p. 16). — [arXiv 2405.10565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10565); [Advances 2025 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Strand%20Hair%20in%20IJGC%20-%20Final%20Slides%20(Post-Conference).pdf)
8. **Thin the shadow casters the same way or correct statistically**, depth shift −log(β). (Lipp, p. 8–9.) — [arXiv 2607.04230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04230)
9. **Measure every stand-in by the size on screen of what it summarises, against one threshold.** (Kerbl, p. 5; NVIDIA, p. 23; Epic, "near pixel-sized"; Karis 2021, p. 59.) — [arXiv 2406.12080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12080); [HPG 2026 slides](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/2026/_assets/slides/Dietger_slides.pdf); [Advances 2021 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)
10. **Change continuously, and not everywhere at once.** New pieces start from their parent's state (Hoppe 1998, p. 4; Kerbl, p. 5); a random offset per group "smooths LOD transitions" (Lipp, p. 7); the failure to avoid is "coordinated omission", every cluster switching together (zeux.io). — [Smooth view-dependent LOD](https://hhoppe.com/svdlod.pdf); [arXiv 2607.04230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04230); [zeux.io](https://zeux.io/2026/09/30/billions-of-triangles-redux/)
11. **Match the distribution of motion, not poses.** (NVIDIA, p. 6.) — [HPG 2026 slides](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/2026/_assets/slides/Dietger_slides.pdf)
12. **Do not thin what is individually visible; do not mix unlike parts in one population.** (MachineGames, PDF p. 15; Bako, p. 3.) — [Advances 2025 slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Strand%20Hair%20in%20IJGC%20-%20Final%20Slides%20(Post-Conference).pdf); [arXiv 2211.05932](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05932)
13. **Do not hand crowds of cards to a surface simplifier.** Its natural last step is "no leaf" (zeux.io); its pruning option removes isolated components (meshoptimizer README); its area-restoring option is "not rigorous unless you know your geometry is triangle-based foliage" and distorts cards with painted branches (zeux.io). — [zeux.io](https://zeux.io/2026/09/30/billions-of-triangles-redux/); [github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer)
14. **Check with numbers.** Perceptual difference between the two sides of a transition (FLIP: Yang et al., p. 6; NVIDIA, p. 22), similarity of consecutive frames (Huang, p. 9), and filled-pixel precision and recall (Neubert et al., search extract). — [arXiv 2505.06523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06523); [arXiv 2405.10565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10565); [Konstanz repository (via search)](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/bitstream/123456789/38257/1/Neubert_0-398388.pdf)

### Inferences
- **What is generic across kinds, and what differs.** Generic: order, prefix, exact repayment of projected area, moment-matched placement, bound, opacity for surplus, pulled colours, spread of normals, shadow consistency, continuous change, one threshold. Specific to a kind: only which dimensions of a part are enlarged. A strand grows in width; a card in both sides; a small solid object in all three, by 1/√u, since it is projected area that is repaid. One statement covers them: enlarge a part's projected area by 1/u along the dimensions in which it is small on screen.
- **Why today's hand-made levels pump, in the terms of these sources.** Growth of crowns to 109 to 134 % is survivors enlarged in place while still many pixels wide, with no bound (rule 3) and no moment-matched placement (rule 4). The fall to 53 to 57 % is area no longer repaid: an enlargement clamp, as in the tree sample, or opacity lost in the mip chain. The change of style is one sprig magnified where a group of sprigs at true size should be shown (question 2).
- **The representation of highest quality that the platform can carry.** The sources read give no evidence that voxels or splats as a far level can be drawn at the screen's own resolution at 60 frames a second in Safari on Apple devices; they give evidence that depth-tested alpha-cut or opaque triangles can. The highest quality within reach is therefore the near primitive itself, carried to the far distance by the rules above, ending in a single element with coverage below one. Its quality is bounded by how well rules 3, 4, 6 and 7 are met, which is where the measured methods (Hasselgren's 23.9 dB against 14.5 dB) part from plain thinning.
- **What changed against the earlier round's notes.** Confirmed by direct reading: Nanite's built-in impostors ("12 x 12 view directions", "12 x 12 pixels per direction", "8:8 Depth, TriangleID", "40.5KB per mesh always resident", and "switching to it can sometimes be noticed as a pop"; Karis 2021, p. 97); Karis's reasons against voxels for surfaces (2021, p. 6–8); the Preserve Area wording; the figures for assemblies, now also on the English page. New: Epic's page states that a normal is "chosen stochastically per pixel" today and that the statistics live in the vertex colour's alpha; the feature is still experimental in the 5.9 documentation; the tree sample of the HPG 2025 paper was read (density 2^(−d/60), enlargement 1/√density clamped to 5); the foliage section of zeux.io and the dilation code were read; NVIDIA's HPG 2026 method was read; MachineGames documents prefix thinning with thickness compensation in a shipped 60 Hz game. Still unverified: every formula of Cook et al.; SpeedTree's documentation; Karis's "voxels <= 1 pixel".

### Gaps
- No source read states rules 3 and 4 for alpha-cut cards generated by code; the combination proposed here (bound test per fragment, coverage from area ratio, texture coordinates in world units) is the researcher's and is untested.
- No measurement was found of how small the threshold must be without temporal anti-aliasing for a prefix change to be invisible in motion.
- The papers that would settle the open points were not reachable: Cook et al. 2007 and its memo (graphics.pixar.com), Neubert et al. 2011 (kops.uni-konstanz.de), Loubet and Neyret 2017 (HAL), the SGGX and LEADR papers, and the Unreal Fest talks on Nanite Foliage.
