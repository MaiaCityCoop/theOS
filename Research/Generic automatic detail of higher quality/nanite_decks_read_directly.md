## 1. Build: how the cluster DAG is made

### Takeaway
The Deck states the build as a loop: cut the triangles into clusters of 128; then, while more than one cluster is left, group clusters, merge each group's triangles into one list, simplify that list to 50 % with only the group's outer boundary locked, and split it into clusters of 128 again. Groups (8 to 32 clusters each) are chosen by graph partitioning with METIS so that as few edges as possible are locked; because groups are chosen afresh at every level, a boundary locked at one level lies inside a group at the next, and that regrouping is what makes the structure a DAG. Every cluster of a group stores the same "unioned" error value and bounding sphere, and the build enlarges a parent's stored error and bounds until they are at least as big as its children's. The Deck gives no formula for the union, no build time, and no rule for a group that cannot be halved.

### Cited Findings
**The loop**
- `RD` *slide*: "Cluster original triangles / While NumClusters > 1 / **Group** clusters to clean their shared boundary / **Merge** triangles from group into shared list / **Simplify** to 50% the # of triangles / **Split** simplified triangle list into clusters (128 tris)". *Notes*: "In our case a cluster is 128 triangles"; "Simplify to half the number of triangles"; "Repeat this process until there is only 1 cluster left at the root." — [Deck p.45](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=45)
- `RD` *slide* labels of the worked picture: "Pick group of N clusters", "Merge and Simplify", "Boundary unchanged", "Split into N/2 clusters", with the DAG drawn underneath. The picture groups 4 clusters of 4 triangles and ends with 2 clusters of 4 triangles. *Notes*: "Note that the boundary is locked so we are not causing cracks with any neighboring clusters in the original mesh."; "In the DAG this corresponds to splitting the parent into two, while all children are still connected to both parents."; "We put them back in the pool to choose from for grouping and the process continues until everything has been reduced to a single cluster at the root of the DAG." — [Deck p.46](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=46)
- `RD` *notes*: the split step "is actually the same thing as the initial clustering process / Take a list of triangles and build clusters of 128". — [Deck p.52](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=52)

**Triangles and vertices per cluster**
- `RD` *slide*: "NumTris per cluster <= max — Fully fill waves in rasterizer"; "NumVerts per cluster — Prim shader limit". *Notes*: "The number of triangles per cluster needs to be as close to but no greater than 128 to work with the rasterizer"; "The number of vertices can't be above a limit to work with primitive shaders". — [Deck p.50](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=50)
- `RD` *slide*: "128 triangle clusters => threadgroup size 128"; "If more than 128 verts loop (max 2)". *Notes*: "fetch and transform another to support up to 256 per cluster"; "a max of 128 triangles per cluster". — [Deck p.85](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=85)
- `RD` *slide*: the 64-bit visibility word is 30 bits "Depth", 27 bits "Visible cluster index", 7 bits "Triangle index" (7 bits = 128 triangles). — [Deck p.84](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=84)

**How the first (leaf) clusters are formed**
- `RD` *slide*: four things to optimise: "Cluster bounds extent — Culling efficiency"; "NumTris per cluster <= max"; "NumVerts per cluster"; "Number of cluster boundary edges — Boundaries are locked so limits simplification". *Notes*: "Unfortunately this is a multi-dimensional optimization problem." — [Deck p.50](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=50)
- `RD` *slide*: "Pick two and hope the rest works out: Number of cluster boundary edges / NumTris per cluster <=max"; "Exactly the same problem as cluster grouping — Graph is the dual of the mesh"; "Requires strict upper bound on partition size — Graph partitioning algorithms don't guarantee this — Managed to coerce it with small slack and fallbacks". *Notes*: "I've managed to coerce it though with a small amount of slack and fallbacks when it very rarely fails". *Bonus notes*: "Better results should be possible with custom graph partitioning code which takes bounds into account for cost metric." — [Deck p.51](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=51)

**How clusters are grouped, and what is minimised**
- `RD` *slide*: "**Group** those with the most shared boundary edges — Less boundary edges == less locked edges"; "This problem is called graph partitioning"; "Partition a graph optimizing for minimum edge cut cost: Graph nodes = clusters / Graph edges = connect clusters with directly connected triangles / Graph edge weights = number of shared triangle edges / Additional graph edges for to spatially close clusters — Adds spatial info for island cases"; "Minimum graph edge cut == least locked edges"; "Use METIS library to solve". — [Deck p.48](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=48)
- `RD` *notes*: "The less locked edges the better because locked edges restrict the simplifier from reducing triangles."; "We add additional graph edges for spatially close but not connected clusters to make sure there aren't islands in the graph that would get grouped arbitrarily."; "The minimum edge cut of this graph corresponds to the least number of locked edges across the mesh for this level which is basically the ideal thing to optimize for."; "We use the popular METIS library." — [Deck p.48–49](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=48)
- `RD` Clusters per group. *Slide*: "Clusters are small (~2KB) / Groups can be large (8-32 clusters)". *Notes*: "They range from 8 to 32 clusters and each cluster is up to about 2KB." This is said on a streaming slide, not on the build slides. — [Deck p.125](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=125)
- `RD` *notes*, why clusters are grouped and not triangles: "Prior work groups the triangles themselves which results in variable number of triangles per group / But we want groups of multiples of 128 triangles such that they can be divided into clusters of exactly 128. / Grouping clusters instead of triangles allows this." — [Deck p.44](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=44)

**What is locked, and why**
- `RD` *slide*: "If each cluster decides LOD independent from neighbors, cracks! / Naive solution: Lock shared boundary edges during simplification / Independent clusters will always match at boundaries". — [Deck p.31](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=31)
- `RD` *slide*: "Locked boundaries: Collects dense cruft — Especially between deep subtrees". *Notes*: "the same boundaries persist over many levels and dense triangle cruft collects there."; "With a balanced tree you can draw such a line down the middle from LOD0 all the way to the root. There will be so much cruft on the boundary between those subtrees that you won't even be able to half the triangles per level and it completely breaks down." — [Deck p.32](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=32)
- `RD` *slide*: "Can detect these cases during build / Group clusters: Force them to make the same LOD decision / Now free to unlock shared edges and collapse them". *Notes*: "If they always make the same LOD decision then they can't mismatch. Cracks come from differences in level between neighboring clusters so no difference in level means no possibility for cracks. We are now free to unlock any shared edges between the clusters and collapse them during simplification just like they were interior edges. Now obviously we can't group all clusters for a level or we get back to good old discrete LODs. So we need to group clusters only where needed." — [Deck p.33](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=33)
- `RD` *notes*: "The key idea is to alternate group boundaries from level to level by grouping different clusters." ([Deck p.35](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=35)); "What is a boundary in one level becomes the interior in the next level" ([Deck p.37](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=37)); "This means locked edges on the boundaries don't stay locked over many levels because they aren't the same boundaries. Thus they can't collect dense cruft. Locked one level, unlocked the next." ([Deck p.39](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=39))

**Why it is a DAG**
- `RD` *slide*: "**Merge** and **split** makes this a DAG instead of a tree". *Notes*: "This is a good thing in that you can't draw a line from LOD0 all the way to the root without crossing an edge. Meaning there can't be locked edges that stay locked and collect cruft." — [Deck p.47](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=47)
- `RD` *notes*: "Grouping and hierarchy building is a unified operation expressed as a graph that we can divide using graph partitioning. Nodes that are dependent on one another are no different than siblings which means the structure is really a DAG. The number of triangles being worked with is always in multiples of some fixed granularity meaning we can exactly fill our 128 triangle clusters. We do not form groups with individual triangles. The merge and split formulation in symmetrical and understands clearly that no correspondence or constraints in terms of triangle to cluster assignment needs to be made between parents and children. All siblings are connected to all their parents." — [Deck p.54](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=54)

**Which error and which bound are stored, and how they are made monotonic**
- `RD` *slide*: the simplifier "Returns **estimate of error** introduced — Later projected on screen to number of pixels error — The **hardest part**!" — [Deck p.55](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=55)
- `RD` *slide*: "Cluster error from simplification is object space scalar". — [Deck p.68](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=68)
- `RD` *notes*: "If they use the same data to decide they will decide the same thing. Same input, same output / So all clusters in the group store the same unioned error value and sphere bounds used to project that error to the screen." — [Deck p.63](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=63)
- `RD` *slide*: "Force error to be **monotonic**: Parent view error >= child view error / Careful implementation to make sure runtime correction is also monotonic". *Notes*: "we need to make the error calculation for parents to always be at least as large as their children. This is forced during the offline DAG building by modifying the parent's stored error and bounds used for projecting it. Both need to be as big as their children." — [Deck p.66](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=66)
- `RD` *slide*: the two stored quantities are named "ParentError" and "ClusterError"; the culling hierarchy stores per node the "Max of children's ParentError". *Notes*: "As with any BVH, the parents conservatively bound their children which in this case also includes ParentError. Because parents are shared in a group, the leaves of the tree are group-sized lists of clusters." — [Deck p.70](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=70)

- `RD` Materials stay per triangle inside a cluster. *Notes*: "we could have forced clusters to only have a single material assignment and basically treated them like separate meshes. - `RD` *slide*: "Import and build time matters too — All of the Nanite builder code is highly optimized too". No time is given. — [Deck p.57](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=57)

### Inferences
- **The 50 % target is met on average on Epic's own content** (my arithmetic on the Deck's figures): 882 M stored triangles from 433 M input triangles is a factor of 2.04, and a series that halves at every level sums to 2 (1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + …). — figures from [Deck p.144](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=144)
- **Group arithmetic** (mine): a group of 8 to 32 clusters is 1,024 to 4,096 triangles going into the simplifier and 512 to 2,048 coming out, that is 4 to 16 new clusters.
- **Every reason the Deck gives for the number 128 belongs to the renderer, none to level of detail**: a full compute thread group ("wave"), the primitive-shader vertex limit, and the 7-bit triangle index in the 64-bit visibility word. A renderer that draws clusters with the ordinary rasteriser is not bound to 128 by anything in the Deck's level-of-detail argument. — from [Deck p.50](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=50), [Deck p.84](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=84), [Deck p.85](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=85)
- **"Unioned"** most plainly reads as: one value per group that covers all members (largest error, a sphere around all of them). The Deck says the parent's stored error and bounds are raised to be "as big as their children" but does not say whether the new simplification error is combined with the children's by taking the larger or by adding. — from [Deck p.63](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=63), [Deck p.66](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=66)
- **The grouping graph is built from shared triangle edges, with extra edges only for nearness.** For a mesh made of many unconnected pieces there are no shared edges to cut and nothing to lock, and the simplifier can only shrink or remove whole pieces. The Deck does not spell this out, but it is consistent with its own statement that such geometry is where the method does badly (section 5). — from [Deck p.48](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=48)

### Gaps
- **No build time anywhere in either deck**, per mesh or per triangle. The only statements are that "Import and build time matters too" and that the builder is "highly optimized" ([Deck p.57](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=57)).
- **Not in either deck:** the formula for the unioned error (larger-of or sum); whether the sphere bounds a cluster or its whole group; the names of the stored fields beyond "ParentError" and "ClusterError"; the error given to clusters that have no parent; what the builder does when a group cannot be simplified to 50 %; the amount of "slack" in the leaf partition; whether METIS is used by recursive bisection; any welding or clean-up of the input mesh before clustering.
- `RECALLED` (Unreal Engine source, licence-gated, not opened): the stored parent error is the larger of the children's errors and the group's new simplification error; clusters without a parent get a very large parent error so that the root passes the test. Medium confidence; not in the decks.

## 2. Selection: the rule, the projection, the threshold, and how the cut is found on the GPU

### Takeaway
The rule is on two slides, once in words and once as a formula: draw a cluster when "Parent error is too high && Our error is small enough", written out as "ParentError > threshold && ClusterError <= threshold". The error is the simplifier's object-space scalar, projected to the screen at the worst-case point inside the stored bounding sphere and corrected for distance and "angle distortion"; the aim is less than one pixel of error (two pixels by default for shadow maps). The test needs no neighbours because all clusters of a group hold the same numbers ("Same input => same output") and because the error is forced to be monotonic, which makes the cut unique. On the GPU the cut is found by walking a tree with eight children per node, keyed on ParentError, with "persistent threads" in a single compute dispatch that also does occlusion culling and emits the streaming requests.

### Cited Findings
**The rule and its wording**
- `RD` *slide* "LOD run-time": "Find cut of the tree for desired LOD / View dependent based on perceptual difference". *Notes*: "At run time we find a cut of the tree that matches the desired LOD. That means different parts of the same mesh can be at different levels of detail based on what's needed. This is done in a view dependent way based on the screen space projected error of the cluster. A parent will draw instead of its children if we determine you couldn't tell the difference from this point of view." — [Deck p.29](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=29)
- `RD` *slide* "LOD selection in parallel": "What defines the cut? — Difference between parent and child / Draw a cluster when: Parent error is too high && / Our error is small enough / Can be evaluated in parallel!". *Notes*: "The cut happens at the point where a parent's error is too high, but it's child's error is small enough to be valid to draw. Parent says no but the child says yes. This is entirely local, does not depend on the entire path to this node, and thus can be evaluated in parallel." — [Deck p.65](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=65)
- `RD` *slide* "Hierarchical culling", the same rule as a formula: "Render: `ParentError > threshold && ClusterError <= threshold`" and "Cull: `ParentError <= threshold || ClusterError > threshold`", with the caption "Parent is already precise enough. No need to check child" under the first half of the cull condition. — [Deck p.70](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=70)
- `RD` *notes*: "But all that gives us is whether this cluster has small enough error that we could draw it. We don't want to draw all low error clusters. Many of them represent the same area but at different detail." *Slide*: "Don't want to traverse the DAG at run-time". — [Deck p.64](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=64)

**Projection of the error to pixels**
- `RD` *slide* "LOD selection": "Two submeshes with same boundary, but different LOD / Choose between them based on screen-space error: Error calculated by simplifier projected to screen / Corrected for distance and angle distortion at worst-case point in sphere bounds / All clusters in group must make same LOD decision: How? Communicate? No! / Same input => same output". *Notes*: "Error calculated by the simplifier is projected to the screen accounting for distance and projection angle distortion. This is calculated at the point that maximizes the projected error within a bounding sphere around the cluster." — [Deck p.63](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=63)
- `RD` *notes*: "for each iteration of the build operations we produced two sets of clusters with a different number of triangles, but with the same boundary. So they are interchangeable in the original mesh, without causing any cracks. This is the essence of the level-of-detail system." — [Deck p.63](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=63)
- `RD` *slide* "Surface angle based LOD" (this page has no speaker notes): "Cluster error from simplification is object space scalar: Unknown direction / Position error could be directional / Attribute error mixed in makes this hard"; "Projection to screen does not account for surface angle: Similar to if mipmaps only were purely a function of distance / Common for tessellation factor calculation as well"; "Means glancing angle surfaces over tessellate"; "Solving requires anisotropic LOD: Not possible through cluster selection / Cluster selection must be isotropic like mip selection"; "Glancing angle costs happen with other schemes too: Overdraw in point based / Surface skimming in SDFs and SVOs". — [Deck p.68](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=68)

**The threshold aimed at**
- `RD` *slide*: "If < 1 pixel error, they are **imperceptibly** different". *Notes*: "if we only draw clusters that are less than 1 pixel of error". — [Deck p.67](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=67)
- `RD` *notes*: "Remember we want zero perceptual loss of detail. How small do the triangles need to be such that the error is less than a pixel big and is effectively lossless?" — [Deck p.79](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=79)
- `RD` Shadows. *Slide*: "LOD matching < 1 pixel of error". *Notes*: "Just like in the primary view, Nanite picks the LOD matching 1 pixel error. In the case of shadows this means the pixels of the mip level it is rasterizing to." *Bonus notes*: "We actually apply a Nanite LOD bias for shadow rendering to account for the multiplier of shadow rays over primary. Defaults to 2 pixels of error. [...] this reduction of detail is barely noticeable and worth the savings." — [Deck p.120](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=120)
- `RD-docs` The shipped engine names the setting as an edge length, not an error: "the smallest desired Nanite triangle edge (as configured by r.Nanite.MaxPixelsPerEdge)". The pages fetched do not state its default. — [Epic docs: Working with Nanite-Enabled Content](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)

**Why it is parallel and has one answer**
- `RD` *slide*: "Only if there is **one unique cut** / Force error to be **monotonic**: Parent view error >= child view error / Careful implementation to make sure runtime correction is also monotonic". *Notes*: "There will be a unique cut if the selection function for all paths from the root to the leaves switches from no to yes once and never switches back. Our selection function is a thresholding of the view dependent error function. Guaranteeing a single transition along the path is the same as forcing the error function of every path to be monotonic." — [Deck p.66](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=66)
- `RD` The HPG deck credits both tricks to terrain work (BDAM, Cignoni et al. 2003, its reference [49]). *Slide*: "Dependent nodes must make same LOD decision: How? Communicate? No! / Same input => same output". *Notes*: "So far we've seen dependent nodes need communication such that they act in unison. That's unnecessary. So long as they use the same data to decide they will decide the same thing." ([HPG p.54](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=54)) *Notes*: "BDAM uses another neat trick to avoid communication. [...] We can just tell this branch of the tree that there is higher error at a deeper level during the build." ([HPG p.55](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=55)) *Notes*: "What this is doing is enforcing that the error function is monotonic. These two things together mean that no communication is required between nodes. That makes it easy to evaluate in parallel so should be easy to port to GPU." ([HPG p.56](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=56))

**Why it is crack-free**
- `RD` Within a mesh: group members decide together ([Deck p.33](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=33), quoted in section 1), and a group and its parents have "the same boundary" so "they are interchangeable in the original mesh, without causing any cracks" ([Deck p.63](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=63)).
- `RD` Under streaming. *Slide*: "Cut DAG at runtime to only loaded geometry: Needs to always be a valid cut of full DAG / Similar to LOD cutting. No cracks!" ([Deck p.122](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=122)). *Slide*: "A cluster can overlap any parent cluster / Rendering cluster => None of the parents should render / Parent not rendered => All siblings (or their descendants) need to render to fill the hole / Full group needed before a cluster in the group can be rendered / We should stream at group granularity" ([Deck p.123](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=123)).
- `RD` Between the two rasterisers. *Notes*: "We were initially nervous about cracks between SW and HW clusters but thankfully DirectX has a very strict spec for rasterization rules. By following that we can exactly match HW meaning no pixel cracks." — [Deck p.87](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=87)
- `RD` **Not** between separate objects. *Slide*: "Only leaf level is perfectly aligned: Simplifier decisions are not consistent across objects / Runtime LOD decisions are not synchronized". *Notes*: "At higher LODs the simplifer could have made different decision along the shared boundary and at runtime LOD decisions will not be perfectly synchronized. This still works fairly well in practice as only the leaf data can be observed up close and the higher LOD levels already target at most pixel error." — [Deck p.132–133](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=132)

**How the cut is found on the GPU**
- `RD` *slide*: "Remember: LOD decision entirely local — We can use any data structure we want to accelerate this!". *Notes*: "we can make cluster selection perfectly parallel, but it turns out to be extremely wasteful when scaled up. For large scenes the vast majority of clusters are too detailed to be selected. We shouldn't even be considering most of them. For quick rejection, we need a hierarchy. [...] But traversing a DAG is more complicated than we would like, especially if it has to happen in parallel. [...] The LOD decision can be evaluated locally using just the cluster error and the parent error, so we don't need to use the DAG at all." — [Deck p.69](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=69)
- `RD` *slide*: "ParentError <= threshold — Tree based on ParentError, not ClusterError! / BVH8: Max of children's ParentError / Internal node: 8 children nodes / Leaf node: List of clusters in group". *Bonus notes*: "Typically, for a given view, there are orders of magnitude more clusters that are too detailed than there are clusters that are not detailed enough. We should focus on accelerating the culling of too detailed clusters." — [Deck p.70](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=70)
- `RD` The naive traversal is one dependent compute dispatch per tree level ("Global synchronization — Wait for idle between every level", "Empty dispatches at the end"). — [Deck p.71](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=71)
- `RD` *slide* "Persistent threads": "Can't spawn new threads. Reuse them instead! / Manage our own job queue / Single dispatch with enough worker threads to fill GPU / Use simple multi-producer multi-consumer (MPMC) job-queue to communicate between threads". — [Deck p.72](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=72)
- `RD` *slide*: "While work queue isn't empty: Dequeue a node / Test / Enqueue any passing children"; "No recursion-depth or fanout limits / No need to repeatedly drain the GPU / 10-60% saving (typically ~25%), depending on scene complexity"; "Relies on scheduling behavior: Requires that once a group has started executing, it will not be starved indefinitely / Scheduling behavior not defined by D3D or HLSL / Works on consoles and all relevant GPUs we have tested / An optimization. Not a requirement for Nanite". *Notes*: "The persistent threads approach is 25% faster on average than the naive one." — [Deck p.73](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=73)
- `RD` Cluster tests run in the same shader. *Slide*: "Leaves are clusters with shared parents / Cluster culling in same persistent shader / Two queues: Process from cluster queue while waiting for nodes to appear in node queue / Coalesced into batches of 64". — [Deck p.74](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=74)
- `RD` Occlusion culling is folded into the same traversal, in two passes. *Slide*: "Explicitly tracking previously visible state gets complicated: LOD selection likely different / Visible clusters from previous frame might not even be in memory anymore! / Test if currently selected clusters would have been visible last frame: Test previous HZB using previous transforms". *Notes*: "we end up running almost the entire Nanite pipeline twice, where the second pass is essentially just cleaning up the disoccluded regions. [...] the second pass is usually a tiny fraction of the main pass. Note that any culling not related to occlusion only needs to evaluate once, So frustum and LOD culling is only done in the first pass". — [Deck p.75–76](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=75)
- `RD` The whole flow: instance culling per instance from the GPU scene, then "Persistent Hierarchy/Cluster Culling" ("This does both LOD and visibility and outputs visible clusters"), then the software and hardware rasterisers, then a build of the depth pyramid, then the same stages again for what was found occluded, then the material passes. — [Deck p.77](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=77)
- `RD` The occlusion test itself. *Slide*: "Calculate screen rect from bounds / Test against lowest mip where screen rect <= 4x4 pixels". *Notes*: "cone based backface culling is not typically worthwhile as almost all backfacing clusters are occlusion culled anyways." — [Deck p.17](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=17)
- `RD` The same traversal produces the streaming requests. *Slide*: "Full culling hierarchy always resident: It is metadata about cluster groups (tiny) / Traversal can go several levels beyond what is loaded / Immediately request all levels needed for target quality" ([Deck p.126](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=126)); "Persistent shader outputs page requests during culling traversals: Requests page ranges with priority based on LOD Error" ([Deck p.127](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=127)).

### Inferences
- **The two inequalities settle a disagreement between write-ups**: the parent must be strictly above the threshold and the cluster at or below it ([Deck p.70](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=70)). When a projected error lands exactly on the threshold, exactly one of parent and child passes.
- **The projection is conservative by construction**: it is evaluated at the point of the sphere where the projected error is largest ([Deck p.63](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=63)), not at the sphere's centre as some reimplementations do. Together with a parent sphere "as big as" the children's ([Deck p.66](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=66)), this is what keeps the projected error monotonic for every camera position; the slide's "Careful implementation to make sure runtime correction is also monotonic" points at exactly this.
- **Nothing in the selection needs the pixel-scale renderer.** The Deck calls the persistent-thread traversal "An optimization. Not a requirement" ([Deck p.73](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=73)) and says "We can use any data structure we want to accelerate this" ([Deck p.69](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=69)); the first prototype had "the cluster LOD selection, culling, and triangle rasterization working end to end" ([HPG p.133](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=133)), and the closed-doors demo a few months later ran on a PS4 Pro with the software rasteriser only ([HPG p.134](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=134)). The selection can therefore be carried to another renderer unchanged; what cannot be carried without the software rasteriser is the one-pixel threshold (section 3).
- **Glancing surfaces cost triangles, not quality**: because the error is a scalar without direction, surfaces seen edge-on are drawn finer than needed ([Deck p.68](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=68)).

### Gaps
- **Not in either deck:** the projection formula; the name or default of the threshold setting; how instance scale enters; the error assigned to root clusters; any statement about selection without a hierarchy for small scenes.
- `RECALLED` (high): `r.Nanite.MaxPixelsPerEdge` defaults to 1. `RECALLED` (medium): shipped engine source uses four children per hierarchy node, not the eight on the 2021 slide. Neither was checked.

## 3. Invisibility: sub-pixel error, temporal anti-aliasing, popping, and why nothing morphs

### Takeaway
One slide, "Seamless LOD", carries the whole argument: the choice between parent and child is binary; geomorphing and cross-fading are named and declined because they "would be expensive at render time or require significant additional data or both"; the switch is held to be invisible because clusters with less than one pixel of error are "imperceptibly different" and "temporal antialiasing smoothes out any change", which "does our work for us so long as the error is subpixel". There is no morph and no blend anywhere in either deck. In the HPG talk "Popping? TAA enough to hide?" is listed among the "scary unknowns" that "luckily worked out". The decks admit visible changes in three places: the imposters for tiny instances, streaming that arrives late, and wherever the error estimate, which Karis calls a heuristic that "can fail", is wrong.

### Cited Findings
**The argument itself**
- `RD` *slide* "Seamless LOD": "Binary choice either parent or child / Won't this visibly pop? / Need smooth transitions? Geomorphing / Cross fading / If < 1 pixel error, they are **imperceptibly** different / Temporal AA sees any difference as aliasing". — [Deck p.67](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=67)
- `RD` *notes*, complete: "So in each frame we are selecting either the parent or the child. Won't this visibly pop when it switches? Do we need to smooth the transitions with geomorphing or cross fading? That would be expensive at render time or require significant additional data or both. But if we only draw clusters that are less than 1 pixel of error they are imperceptibly different and temporal antialiasing smoothes out any change. TAA is built to blend subpixel differences over time. It does our work for us so long as the error is subpixel. This is the reason why getting an accurate error estimate is so important." — [Deck p.67](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=67)
- `RD` *slide* "Many unknowns still": "Popping? — TAA enough to hide?", next to "How about big triangles?", "Memory to hit 1 pixel? — Estimates swung from 512MB to 2GB depending on how I calculated it" and "Is visbuffer only efficient for large triangles?". *Notes*: "Sometimes they are scary unknowns that could hugely impact the overall viability but there is no way to know without a working implementation. Here's a few I remember being concerned about for Nanite that luckily worked out." — [HPG p.132](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=132)
- `RD` The demo the numbers come from used temporal upsampling. *Slide*: "Average ~2496x1404 upsampled to 4k: TAAU at the time / Would use TSR now". — [Deck p.110](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=110)
- `RD` *slide* "Hindsight" (decisions made "based on faulty conclusions, or with a lack of understanding of workflow, or with blindspots in future needs", per the notes): "Significant temporal upsampling would be prevalent: Nanite scales with **output** resolution pixels / Tracing scales with **render** resolution pixels". — [HPG p.140](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=140)

**Sub-pixel error needs pixel-sized triangles**
- `RD` *slide* "Pixel scale detail": "Can we hit pixel scale detail with triangles > 1 pixel? Depends how smooth / In general no / We need to draw pixel sized triangles". *Notes*: "Can we do that with triangles that are larger than pixels? In a lot of cases yes. Triangles are adaptive and can go where they're needed. But in general no. Pixel sized features need pixel sized triangles to represent them without visible error." — [Deck p.79](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=79)
- `RD` *notes*: "most of the existing research tested with laser scanned sculptures. These meshes are fairly smooth. They can hit 1 pixel error with average triangles that are considerably larger than a pixel. I feared that is not the case here. These surfaces are not smooth or continuous. I have no reason to believe I'm going to be able to rasterize pixel sized triangles. There is a major performance cliff with hardware rasterization at that scale. I do not want to invest heavily in tech that can only make lumpy rocks." — [HPG p.91](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=91)
- `RD` *notes*: "there are only so many pixels on screen. Why should we draw more triangles than pixels?" — [Deck p.27](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=27)
- `RD` Why the ordinary rasteriser is the obstacle. *Slide*: "Tiny triangles: Terrible for typical rasterizer / Typical rasterizer: Macro tile binning / Micro tile 4x4 / Output 2x2 pixel quads / Highly parallel in pixels not triangles / Modern GPUs setup 4 tris/clock max — Outputting SV_PrimitiveID makes it even worse". *Notes*: "Primitive shaders or mesh shaders can be faster but are still bottlenecked and not designed for this." — [Deck p.80](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=80)
- `RD` The answer was a software rasteriser in compute, which rests on 64-bit atomic writes. *Slide*: "Use 64 bit atomics! InterlockedMax". *Notes*: "The payload needs to be small enough to pack in 34 bits or less. Without that we wouldn't be able to do fast software rasterization." — [Deck p.84](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=84)

**Where the decks admit a visible change**
- `RD` The error estimate. *Notes*: "What we have definitely isn't perfect and can fail although it's rare to be noticable." — [Deck p.56](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=56)
- `RD` Imposters for tiny instances. *Notes*: "There is definitely a quality loss that can be noticed in some cases meaning switching to it can sometimes be noticed as a pop. Usually that only happens when many of the same mesh are next to one another like repeating wall sections and the switch is noticeable because there is a bulk change and a directly neighboring thing to compare it to. [...] I would love to replace this with something that is just as fast but consumes less memory and is truly seamless." — [Deck p.97](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=97)
- `RD` Streaming. *Notes*: had the hierarchy been streamed too, "IO latency would be multiplied by the number of levels we are off, which would translate to more visible pop in" ([Deck p.126](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=126)); a smaller memory format means "less cache misses which means less IO and chance for pop in" ([Deck p.129](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=129)).
- `RD` Shadows are drawn at their own level of detail. *Notes*: "That does not mean the triangles drawn to the shadow map are exactly the same as those drawn in the primary view. That mismatch can cause incorrect self shadowing. We address that discrepancy with a short screen space trace to span the zone where they could differ." — [Deck p.120](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=120)
- `RD` Seams between separate objects above the finest level are not prevented, only kept small ("the higher LOD levels already target at most pixel error"). — [Deck p.133](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=133)

**What is kept exactly, and what is given up**
- `RD` The stated goal. *Slide* "The Dream": "Directly use film quality source art — No manual optimization required / No loss in quality". *Notes*: "And of course, all that with no loss in quality! What's the point of running fast if it no longer looks like what the artist originally created?" — [Deck p.2](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=2), [Deck p.3](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=3)
- `RD` The finest level is the source mesh, not a resampling of it. On voxels: "even with all that the finest resolution stored still won't be what was authored / No matter what we are resampling. / Do we have to store the original mesh too and draw that if you get close enough?" ([Deck p.7](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=7)); "So my conclusion is the mip0 data, so to speak, is best to retain in its original format." ([HPG p.104](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=104)); "The source triangles handle magnification." ([HPG p.117](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=117)).
- `RD` Resampling is what the method refuses, because of what it does to authored detail: "Uniform resampling means loss" ([Deck p.6](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=6)); artists "need to be able to make them where they are important and understand clearly the exact effect of their changes." ([HPG p.40](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=40)).
- `RD` Given up at build time: exact positions and attributes ("first the positions and the various attributes are first quantized globally based on a combination of explicit artist control and heuristics", [Deck p.130](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=130); the position grid is "user-selectable", [Deck p.132](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=132)); stored tangents ([Deck p.134](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=134)); and alignment between neighbouring objects above the finest level ([Deck p.133](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=133)).
- `RD` Given up in principle, by the Deck's own account: the effect on shading of detail that falls below a pixel, and partial coverage ("Prefiltering (future work)", [Deck p.60](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=60), [Deck p.61](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=61); quoted in sections 4 and 5).

**Why geomorphing was dropped: Progressive Buffers**
- `RD` What the scheme is. *Notes*: "This is the first example of what I'll refer to as hierarchical level of detail or HLOD. [...] HLOD is a hierarchy of triangle mesh chunks. Each level of the hierarchy is a simplified version of the last using typical mesh simplification algorithms. A view dependent cut of this hierarchy is rendered each frame." — [HPG p.28](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=28)
- `RD` *slide*: "Progressive Buffers: Solves cracks through geomorphing — Everything morph band must be resident / Node must be smaller than band / What happens when required data not resident?". *Notes*: "To make sure level transitions don't cause cracks progressive buffers geomorph based on distance. If every vertex knows where it will move to in the next simplified level it can make sure it does so before the next level is chosen. That way there won't be cracks. Because the geomophing is based purely on distance there is an assumption that everything in a LOD band is loaded. If a node at the desired level isn't present there are problems. Progressive buffers are more like clipmaps than sparse virtual textures. I want a sparse solution that adapts to visibility and where there's detail." *Bonus notes*: "A node can only geomorph to the next lower level. It thus can't span 2 transition zones at once. That means all nodes must be spatially smaller than a LOD band." — [HPG p.29](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=29)
- `RD` *notes* (continued): "This is guaranteed because the hierarchy is a sparse octree. A node's dimensions are dictated by its level. Either that means the number of triangles per node has to be highly variable or it will draw and store more triangles than are necessary for flat regions." — [HPG p.30](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=30)
- `RD` *notes*: "I like the idea of HLOD but I don't like that crack solution." — [HPG p.31](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=31)
- `RD` The same objection in 2021, with cost added. *Notes*: "In the case of progressive buffers a single node can match with both one level and another at the same time because geomorphing is calculated per vertex. Ignoring the fact that geomorphing is both data and computationally costly, this means a node (cluster) can only span 1 LOD without cracking. To guarantee this, LOD always is applied in strict distance bands and nodes must be smaller than the width of those bands such that a node can't fall in more than two bands at once. This means LOD can't be based on visual error of that node would introduce, meaning it can't be adaptive. It also means that if a LOD isn't resident for a certain portion of the surface it may be unable to obey the band requirement and thus will crack." — [Deck p.42](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=42)
- `RD` Karis later softens one of his own objections. *Notes*: "I said progressive buffers were like a mesh version of clipmaps. They don't need to be. They can be made sparse by adding a clamp to the geomorph to be no more detailed than their neighbor." The price, on the slide: "Boundary vertex data: Lower detail position / Neighbor node we border / Node data: List of neighbors / Restricted tree". — [HPG p.47](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=47), [HPG p.48](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=48)
- `RD` He never built it. *Notes*: "the majority of what I'm telling you about only existed in my head" ([HPG p.21](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=21)); "But I ultimately didn't have a chance to pursue these ideas. The project never happened and I joined Epic!" ([HPG p.33](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=33)).

**The other rejected ways of joining levels, each with the reason given**
- `RD` *Per-vertex view-dependent progressive meshes.* *Slide*: "Need to be able to index boundary verts in any state / Impossible to have cracks due to precision / Complex and expensive computationally and in terms of memory — Granularity of triangles, too fine". *Notes*: "While fine granularity means the number of triangles to hit an error threshold is optimal that isn't the ultimate goal. We can rasterize triangles fairly fast so spending too much time or data to find the perfect view dependent mesh to render is counterproductive." ([Deck p.40–41](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=40)) HPG *notes*: "At no point can there be cracks. They operate at a very fine granularity though, making decisions per vertex is excessive. Comparing to VT this would be like streaming texture data at the granularity of texels." ([HPG p.49](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=49)); they "require a persistent version of the mesh stored at the last seen detail level. [...] That kills instancing and is simply too much memory." ([HPG p.50](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=50))
- `RD` *Skirts.* *Notes*: "First, we have no guarantee that our meshes are manifold and watertight such that they can be operating on volumetrically. Second, even if they were, T-junctions without cracks only helps if different triangulations end at a plane. [...] This is all complex, requires lots of clipping and generating lots of extra hidden geometry which makes it less efficient than locking boundaries." ([Deck p.41](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=41)) HPG *notes*: "We have no guarantee that our meshes are manifold and can be operated on volumetrically. In fact it is super common for game meshes to be open. Also two sided." ([HPG p.32](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=32)); skirts cannot follow "boundary edges that zigzag" ([HPG p.42](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=42)).
- `RD` *Stitching at run time.* *Notes*: "we need to do really heavy work at run-time to stitch boundaries which don't match". — [HPG p.44](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=44)
- `RD` *Boundaries locked for ever.* *Notes*: "It is garbage though and fails at the heavily reduced levels once you run out of non-boundary edges to collapse." — [HPG p.46](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=46)
- `RD` *Regular spatial partitions (octree, right-triangle hierarchies, TetraPuzzles).* *Slide*: "Fixed spatial partitioning results in: Surfaces slightly stick into partition — Uneven NumTris per cluster / Surfaces wave in and out of partition — Non-contiguous triangles in clusters — Excessive boundary edges". *Notes*: "Is it entirely possible that only a single triangle falls in a tetrahedron. That cluster would not be efficient to render." ([HPG p.58](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=58)); the same in 2021: "we have no control over number of triangles per cluster [...] The amount of wasted lanes in the rasterizer due to this lack of control makes it very inefficient to render." ([Deck p.42](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=42)).

**The other rejected representations, each with the reason given**
- `RD` *Voxels and signed distance fields.* "This is a 2M poly bust / Resampled to 13M narrow band SDF voxels [...] with 6 times the amount of data / It looks blobby / The reason for this is voxelization is a form of uniform resampling and Uniform resampling means loss / This is the 3D equivalent of converting vector graphics into pixel graphics. For organic uniformly sampled meshes like the sculpted skin in this example, resampling likely won't be very destructive. For hard surface modelling on the other hand it can be extremely so" ([Deck p.6](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=6)); "Voxel colors are not an acceptable replacement nor is triplanar projection"; "Voxels with UVs? Maybe but what to do about UV seams?"; "Features which vanish as signed distance fields when they are thinner than a voxel / Or leak attributes from one side to the other when they are less than a few voxels. / Or how the hell to animate them"; "it would need many years of compounding research and industry experience to be able to replace explicit surfaces completely. We aren't there yet and even if we were it's unclear if it would be better." ([Deck p.8–9](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=8)) HPG adds the arithmetic and a prototype: "Surface data scales quadratically with resolution. Volume data scales cubically." ([HPG p.102](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=102)); "voxels must be half the size of the shortest triangle edge or less to not lose detail. That would be an insane amount of data! [...] even with perfect adaptive resolution the Nyquist rate means 4x the voxels as vertices." ([HPG p.103](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=103)); "surfaces need to be at least 1 voxel thick to exist and 2 voxels thick to not leak attributes" ([HPG p.106](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=106)); "UVs have seams and filtering across a seam is invalid" ([HPG p.107](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=107)); "Morph targets only really make sense with explicit surfaces." ([HPG p.108](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=108)); "Voxels have too many hard problems remaining. My velocity in solving them is far too low." ([HPG p.125](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=125)); "The cubic scaling of volume data is too expensive. Boundary representations are required even with voxels, just to manage the data size." ([HPG p.126](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=126)).
- `RD` *Points.* "Points are super fast to blast to the screen. But unless you accept massive amounts of overdraw, points require hole filling / How do we know the difference between a small gap that should be there and a hole that should be filled? It's impossible to know for certain without extra connectivity data. Also known as the index buffer in a triangle mesh." ([Deck p.13](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=13)) HPG: "Thin features become as thick as 1 pixel no matter how far away they are. So, thin wires appear to get thicker the further away they get. This is the same problem voxels have btw and it looks bad." ([HPG p.96](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=96)); "If all gaps of a certain size are filled things are going to look bloby, like there's surface tension." and the "dart board" case where the nearer point hides the information needed to fill behind it ([HPG p.99](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=99)); "There are no bounds on how much animation can stretch a surface [...] point clouds are not a robust data structure to express surfaces by themselves. [...] Basically just reinvented triangle meshes and index buffers." ([HPG p.100](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=100)).
- `RD` *Subdivision surfaces.* "Subdivision by definition is amplification only / Great for up close but / Doesn't get simpler than base cage / I spoke with our artists and / The cage is very often higher than typical game low polys / In film it's an order of magnitude worse. / We need artist authoring choices disconnected from rendering cost." ([Deck p.10](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=10)) HPG: "Subd is amplification. A simplification solution would be needed in addition. Two different, very complex solutions, one for up, the other for down. [...] Funny enough simplification all by itself is elegant and unified! A static mesh can be subdivided offline and one method can handle both up and down." ([HPG p.70](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=70)).
- `RD` *Displacement maps, geometry images, MAPS.* "You can't really because displacement can't increase the genus of a surface. [...] projecting to normal or displacement maps is a form of uniform resampling. Works really well for organic surfaces that already are uniformly sampled / But can be really destructive to hard surface features if not very carefully controlled by the artist. So great for up close and amplification but / Not good enough for general purpose simplification." *Bonus notes*: "Geometry images are essentially like vector displacement maps relative to the origin instead of another surface. All the above issues are the same." ([Deck p.11–12](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=11)) HPG: Karis's first idea was "virtual geometry images. Virtual texture tiles are replaced by instanced triangle grids", but "the base cages aren't that low. I'd like to reduce anything down really far and not have to rely on the artist modelling things in a particular way." ([HPG p.22](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=22)); "Both of these approaches choke if the surface isn't connected. [...] We cannot assume topology is simple, connected, and low genus. Geometric detail can't be represented only as displacement in all situations, at all scales." ([HPG p.39](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=39)); artists "really didn't like the fact that their art was being resampled and the details they fussed over were being lost. No matter how close they got it never looked exactly like what they modelled." ([HPG p.40](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=40)); "That was a big reason why I steered away from geometry images and displacement towards irregular meshes. I want to solve hard surface and mechanical as well." ([HPG p.91](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=91)).
- `RD` *Ray tracing instead of rasterising.* "Ray tracing is LogN which is nice but not enough. We couldn't fit all the data of these scenes in memory even if we could render it fast enough." *Bonus notes*: "Once the triangles become subpixel the rays effectively become incoherent, every additional level a cache miss." ([Deck p.25](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=25)) HPG: "the idea of picking out 1 triangle for this pixel, then skipping 1000s before finding the next triangle is cache miss city. Clearly inferior from the performance point of view to LOD. Besides LOD is needed for streaming anyways." ([HPG p.94](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=94)).
- `RD` *His conclusion.* "I spent a very long time exploring other options and for our requirements have found no higher quality or faster solution than triangles. There are good uses for these other representations but triangles are the core of Nanite. If you can build an art style around the aesthetics of one of these other representations and embrace their pros and cons they could be incredibly valuable but Unreal can't impose an art style." — [Deck p.14](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=14)

### Inferences
- **The invisibility claim has exactly two legs and no third.** (a) The level drawn differs from the next finer one by less than a pixel of *estimated* error; (b) a temporal filter averages what is left. Remove either leg and the Deck offers nothing in its place: it does not say what happens at a threshold above one pixel, or without temporal anti-aliasing. A renderer that must draw larger triangles, or that renders at the screen's own resolution without temporal accumulation, is outside what the Deck claims.
- **Leg (a) is a statement about an estimate, not about a bound.** The error is a hand-tuned mix of position and attribute error that the author calls a "Complete heuristic hack" (section 4). The Deck never claims a guaranteed maximum deviation; Hausdorff distance appears only as a question under "Can do better?".
- **Leg (a) is the reason the software rasteriser exists.** By Karis's own account smooth surfaces reach one pixel of error with triangles "considerably larger than a pixel", but hard-surface, thin and non-continuous geometry needs pixel-sized triangles ([Deck p.79](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=79), [HPG p.91](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=91)). Which of the two an asset is decides how much a renderer without pixel-sized triangles loses.
- **Geomorphing was rejected on four stated grounds, all of them costs or constraints, none of them picture quality**: it ties level to distance bands so it "can't be adaptive"; it needs the whole band resident; it forces nodes smaller than a band; and it is "both data and computationally costly". Karis himself notes later that the residency objection can be lifted with a clamp. Nothing in either deck says a morph would look worse; the 2021 slide's position is that below a pixel it is unnecessary.
- **"Nanite scales with output resolution pixels" is listed under hindsight.** My reading: with temporal upsampling the picture is rendered at fewer pixels than are shown, a tracer's cost follows the rendered count, but Nanite's triangle density has to follow the shown count. The slide has no notes explaining it, so this reading is mine.

### Gaps
- **Not in either deck:** any test or measurement of how visible a switch is; any statement about switching without temporal anti-aliasing; any statement about thresholds above one pixel other than the two-pixel default for shadows; any morph or blend between cluster levels.
- The recorded talks were not watched; only the published slide files with their notes were read.

## 4. The error metric used by the simplifier

### Takeaway
The simplifier is ordinary edge-collapse decimation driven by a quadric error metric that folds vertex attributes (the Deck names normals, UVs and vertex colours) into the position error with weights; it places each new vertex and its attributes where the error is least and returns an estimate of the error introduced, which is the number later projected to pixels. Karis calls that estimate "the hardest part", says about a man-year went into it, and describes the mixing of position and attribute error as a "Complete heuristic hack" with "no theoretical foundation". Its stated weaknesses: the material is unknown at build time, so the effect of a normal or UV error cannot be known; it "can fail"; it has no direction, so glancing surfaces are over-refined; nothing filters lost detail into shading; and, in hindsight, noisy vertex attributes "weigh heavily" on it.

### Cited Findings
**What the simplifier is**
- `RD` *slide* "Mesh simplify": "Edge collapsing / Picks smallest error edge first / Error calculated using Quadric Error Metric (QEM) / Optimizes position of new vertex for minimal error / Highly refined / Returns **estimate of error** introduced — Later projected on screen to number of pixels error — The **hardest part**!". — [Deck p.55](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=55)
- `RD` *notes*: "For this we use typical edge collapsing decimation. Error is calculated using the Quadric Error Metric / We optimize for minimal error in new positions and attributes."; "It returns an estimate of the error introduced by simplifying and this has been the most challenging aspect to get right. This estimated error is later projected on screen to a number of pixels of error to determine which LOD to select so is foundational to both quality and efficiency. Perceptual heuristic for pixel error in a highly efficient to evaluate form and one that can be directly optimized for has consumed an enormous amount of time. If I were to guess, probably a man year worth of effort has gone into this problem in one form or another." — [Deck p.55](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=55)
- `RD` The Deck's references for the metric are its [62] to [65]: Garland and Heckbert 1997 and 1998, Hoppe 1999 ("New quadric metric for simplifying meshes with appearance attributes"), Hoppe and Marschner 2000. — [Deck p.153](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=153)

**Which attributes it weighs, and what it cannot know**
- `RD` *notes*: "In some ways it is an impossible task because at this stage we don't know what material will be applied to this mesh. For example we don't know how shiny it is to know the exact impact of normal error / Or how much UV error is apparent based on the texture / Or if there are vertex colors, what they are even used for. What we have definitely isn't perfect and can fail although it's rare to be noticable. It certainly could be more aggressive by factoring in more aspects about human perception." — [Deck p.55–56](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=55)
- `RD` *slide* "Error metric" (this page has no speaker notes): "Basic quadrics are integral of distance^2 error over area / Quadrics with attributes mix all errors in one with weights — Complete heuristic hack / Can do better? Hausdorff mesh distance? / Render results and use image based perceptual? / No concept of rate distortion optimization / Import and build time matters too / Want collapses to optimize for same metric as returned pixel error". — [Deck p.57](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=57)
- `RD` *bonus notes*: "What I want is to know how far does the camera needs to be such that the error generated by simplification is imperceptible. The math to project world space size to size in pixels is easy. That means I need to know in world space how much error was generated such that I can transform it into number of pixels. Perception is so much more complicated than this but this is the framework I'm working under. Position deviation error is exactly in the right units and framework to do this with. Attribute error such as difference in normals is not. I've looked at countless papers for what to do about that and I've found no solutions beyond what I have been doing, which is to mix position and attribute errors together as if they were the same units but with weights for how important each is relatively. There is no theoretical foundation for this. The only defense for it is experimentally. It's a dumb heuristic to mix them as if they are the same thing. They aren't but I still don't have anything better." — [Deck p.58](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=58)
- `RD-docs` In the shipped engine UV error can be switched out of the selection: "If disabled, error from UVs is no longer accounted for when Nanite selects the LOD to render because error due to arbitrary vertex attributes that aren't interpolatable can't be generally reasoned about." — [Epic docs: Nanite Technical Details](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)

**How scale is handled (the "small guy on the shoulder")**
- `RD` *slide*: "Scale independence / Need to know size on screen to know weights — Chicken and egg problem / Assume most clusters draw at constant screen size / Surface area normalized / Edge length limits / Lots of tuning / Great care with floating point precision — Many places in quadrics with inherent catastrophic cancellation". — [Deck p.58](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=58)
- `RD` *bonus notes*: "What's even worse is the balance dictated by the weights isn't scale invariant. Attribute deviation, particularly normals, is scale independent. How perceptible is a 1 degree change in normal? How about if it was a mile away? Doesn't matter. All that matters is how many pixels that error covers. Position deviation on the other hand is scale dependent." — [Deck p.58–59](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=58)
- `RD` *bonus notes*: "Per group of clusters I pass to the simplifier is small enough that the same sort of behavior roughly happens in both the big and small copy case. Something that Nanite's structure enables but doesn't exist in the general simplifier. [...] From a LODing perspective those most often will project on screen to the same average triangle size. So I calculate the average surface area per triangle and rescale the mesh such that that is roughly a constant I chose as a good mix of position to attribute balance. With that it is now scale invariant even down to the subpart."; "A cluster that covers N pixels should simplify and calculate its error the same as any other that covers N pixels."; "Making this fix changed the number of triangles drawn in our demos from being somewhat variable to basically constant. For example the room of statues used to be 2-3x as expensive as the cave scene. After this change both scenes rasterized the same number of triangles." — [Deck p.59](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=59)

**How the error of a group is estimated and stored**
- `RD` The simplifier runs once per group ("Per group of clusters I pass to the simplifier", [Deck p.59](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=59); "Merge triangles from group into shared list / Simplify to 50% the # of triangles", [Deck p.45](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=45)) and returns one estimate ([Deck p.55](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=55)); "all clusters in the group store the same unioned error value and sphere bounds used to project that error to the screen" ([Deck p.63](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=63)); the build then makes each parent's stored error and bounds "as big as their children" ([Deck p.66](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=66)).

**Weaknesses the author states**
- `RD` It has no direction. *Slide*: "Cluster error from simplification is object space scalar: Unknown direction / Position error could be directional / Attribute error mixed in makes this hard"; "Means glancing angle surfaces over tessellate". — [Deck p.68](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=68)
- `RD` Nothing turns lost detail into shading. *Slide* "Prefiltering (future work)" (no speaker notes): "Can't prefilter BxDF — Material assignment is unknown / Normal distribution could be filtered: Similar to normal map prefiltering to roughness / SGGX (volumetric) / Needs to apply to diffuse as well / Fed to BxDF / Non-uniform triangle sizes cause issues: Vertex footprint isn't symmetrical — Lerps across all adjacent triangles / Filter kernel won't match pixels / Minification and magnification simultaneously / Unsolved". — [Deck p.60](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=60)
- `RD` Hindsight. *Slide*: "Noisy vertex attributes weigh heavily on perceptual error — Coupling the frequency of positions and attributes bloats cost". — [HPG p.140](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=140)
- `RD` Hindsight on what artists actually deliver. *Slide*: "Artist highpolys for baking normal maps weren't UVed: Artists textured lowpoly / UV unwrap tools don't scale / DCCs can't handle multi-million poly meshes". — [HPG p.140](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=140)

**Shading and normals around the metric**
- `RD` Normals are stored per vertex in "Octahedral coordinates"; tangents are not stored: "Implicit. 0 bits! / Derived from UV gradients of the triangle". *Notes*: "There are potentially some continuity problems with this approach compared to explicit tangent space. But for high-poly models it is generally much less of a problem. We will also support explicit tangents in the future." — [Deck p.134](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=134), [Deck p.135](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=135)
- `RD` Materials are evaluated once per pixel after visibility is known, from attributes interpolated across the visible triangle ("Derive barycentric coordinates for pixel / Load and lerp attributes"). — [Deck p.23](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=23), [Deck p.99](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=99)
- `RD` Texture filtering with pixel-sized triangles. *Slide*: pixel quads "Also span: Depth discontinuities / UV seams / Different objects — Not good!". *Notes*: "Finite differences of discontinuities are nonsense and often huge which results in high mip levels being used like the artifacts in this image." The fix: "Compute analytic derivatives: Attribute gradient across triangle / Propagate through material node graph using chain rule"; "<2% overhead for material pass". — [Deck p.106](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=106), [Deck p.107](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=107)
- `RD` Why real geometry was wanted instead of normal maps. *Notes*: "the largest visual difference between real geometry and normal maps most often comes from detailed self shadowing." — [Deck p.112](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=112)
- `RD-docs` Later engine versions added the stored tangents the Deck promised: "Explicit Tangents: When true, the original model tangents are stored and used per asset. [...] Enabling the setting increases storage by roughly 10% but can be preferable when implicit tangents are not precise enough." — [Epic docs: Nanite Technical Details](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)

### Inferences
- **One pixel of error is a perceptual score, not a distance.** The value returned mixes position and attribute error by weights after the group has been rescaled to a fixed average triangle area. It is calibrated by experiment ("The only defense for it is experimentally"), so the claim that less than one pixel of error is imperceptible is as good as that tuning, on the content it was tuned on.
- **Attributes drive the triangle count as much as shape does.** Because normal error is weighted into the same number and "is scale independent", a surface with noisy normals or noisy vertex data cannot be simplified far without the estimate rising; the HPG hindsight slide says exactly this cost was underestimated.
- **The "impossible task" is a consequence of Unreal's separation of mesh and material, not of the method.** Karis's complaint is that the builder does not know the material. A pipeline that generates mesh and material together knows at build time how shiny a surface is and what its vertex colours mean, and could weight the error accordingly. This is my reasoning; the decks only state the constraint.
- **The loss the Deck itself files as unsolved is shading, not shape**: when relief smaller than a pixel is simplified away, nothing widens the highlight or darkens the diffuse to stand for it ([Deck p.60](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=60)).

### Gaps
- **Not in either deck:** the weights themselves; which attributes get which weight; what the "Edge length limits" are; the constant chosen for the average triangle area; any comparison of the estimate with a measured image difference; any failure example.

## 5. Kinds of geometry: what is handled, what is not and why, and what the authors proposed

### Takeaway
The requirement Karis set himself was an arbitrary imported triangle mesh with no assumption about topology (open, two-sided, non-manifold, disconnected, non-uniform, hard-surface), with UVs and instancing. The 2021 version covered rigid, opaque geometry, by his count ">90% of the geometry". It did not support translucent or masked materials or any non-rigid deformation, and it was "not great" with aggregates (grass, leaves, hair), for which "the property of scaling cost with screen resolution doesn't hold". The stated reasons are two: a triangle mesh cannot express partial coverage, and occlusion culling works on clusters, not pixels, so geometry "riddled with holes" is overdrawn. Whole instances smaller than a cluster's worth of pixels fall outside the hierarchy and were covered by small baked imposters that can pop. The proposals were a hybrid of mesh and volume for sub-pixel detail, hierarchical instancing, and a roadmap; Epic's later engine added masked materials, limited vertex offsets, skinned meshes, tessellation and a voxel-based foliage path.

### Inferences
- **The 2021 method is general over the topology of a surface and not general over what a thing is.** It asks nothing of a mesh (closed or open, connected or not, smooth or mechanical), which is the sense in which Karis calls simplification "elegant and unified". But it assumes that the thing drawn is an opaque surface that stays put relative to itself. The three exclusions on the 2021 "Future" slide (translucent and masked, deforming, aggregate) are exactly the three ways of not being that.
- **Epic's answer to aggregates, five years on, is the hybrid the 2021 slide sketched**: triangles while a feature is larger than a pixel, a volume (voxel bricks with a distribution of normals) below that, with the simplifier's error deciding the switch. The 2021 questions "Stochastic? Ray march? Point scatter? OIT?" were answered with rasterised voxel bricks and a normal chosen at random per pixel. It is still marked experimental in the documentation served today.
- **A second, separate limit is the instance, not the triangle.** The hierarchy ends at one cluster of 128 triangles per instance, each instance costs a transform (48 bytes by the Deck's arithmetic) and a culling test, and in 2021 and 2022 there was no hierarchy above instances. Karis's hindsight list says merging was "needed closer than expected". A system meant to be generic needs an answer above the single mesh that the decks do not contain.
- **Karis argues against seeking one mechanism for everything as a design method, while defending simplification as the widest single mechanism he found.** Both are in the HPG deck ([HPG p.69](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=69), [HPG p.70](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=70), [HPG p.84](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=84)). A reader looking for support for a fully generic system with nothing written per kind of asset finds in these decks a method that is generic for rigid opaque surfaces, and an author who shipped that first and extended it by separate mechanisms afterwards.

### Gaps
- **Not in either deck:** any statement on thin triangle geometry (wires, blades, fins) in Nanite itself; any treatment of two-sided rendering of open surfaces in the level-of-detail build; how an instance smaller than a pixel is treated (the Deck states the dilemma and the imposter, not a rule); the screen size at which the imposter takes over; any measurement of foliage cost.
- **Not checked in this session:** whether the foliage, tessellation and skinned-mesh features have left experimental status in a released engine; the documentation pages read are labelled 5.9 and still mark foliage and tessellation as experimental.
- `RECALLED` (not checked; engine versions and dates are from memory): which release brought each later change. Masked and two-sided materials and vertex offsets with 5.1 (November 2022); stored tangents with 5.3; compute-based material shading, experimental tessellation and a hierarchy over instances with 5.4 (2024); skinned meshes, experimental, with 5.5; Nanite Foliage, experimental, with 5.7 (November 2025). High confidence for 5.1, 5.5 and 5.7, medium for the rest. The documentation read above confirms that these features exist today, not when they arrived.

## 6. Numbers stated in the decks

### Takeaway
The decks give sizes, counts and timings for one demo frame and one data set, and constants of the format; they give no build time at all. The frame: 25,041,711 triangles in 199,420 clusters, from 896,322 instances, at an average of about 2496×1404 upsampled to 4K, in about 2.5 ms for culling and rasterisation plus about 2 ms for materials. The data set: 433 M input triangles stored as 882 M triangles in 4.61 GB on disk (5.6 bytes per stored triangle) and 7.67 GB in memory. The software rasteriser is "3x faster" than the fastest hardware path Epic built and is used for clusters whose edges are under 32 pixels.

### Inferences
- **"108ms" on the performance slide is a misprint for 108 µs** (my reading): every other row is in microseconds, and the eight rows then add up to 2,237 µs, in line with the "~2.5ms" headline; 108 ms alone would be forty times the total. The two material rows add up to 2,301 µs against the "~2ms" headline.
- **Triangles per pixel** (my arithmetic, assuming the frame of the numbers slide ran at the average resolution of the performance slide): 25,041,711 triangles over 2496 × 1404 = 3,504,384 rendered pixels is about 7.1 rasterised triangles per rendered pixel, or about 3.0 per pixel of the 4K output. The count includes both occlusion passes and everything the rasteriser was handed; the Deck says there is "No per triangle culling", so back-facing and hidden triangles inside visible clusters are in it.
- **Clusters are nearly full**: 25,041,711 / 199,420 = 125.6 triangles and 99.5 vertices per rasterised cluster. Of 1,995,599 candidate clusters in both passes, 199,420 were drawn, one in ten.
- **Bytes per triangle, derived** (the Deck's "GB" are binary: 4.61 × 2^30 / 882 M = 5.61, matching its "5.6 bytes"): memory format 7.67 GB / 882 M = 9.3 bytes per stored triangle, or 19.0 bytes per input triangle; raw 31.5 bytes per stored triangle.
- **The hardware is not named on the two measurement pages.** The picture and the wording ("this demo", "the console path works for the two demos we've shown", [Deck p.104](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=104)) point to the console demos, and the earlier round's recollection said PlayStation 5, but pages 109 and 110 do not say so; the only page that names PS5 is the transcoding slide ([Deck p.138](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=138)).
- **The 11.4 and 14.4 bytes per input triangle are two different content sets**, not a contradiction: the Deck's figure is the first demo, the documentation's is the Valley of the Ancients sample.

### Gaps
- **Build time: no figure in either deck.**
- **No raster time at any resolution other than the one demo average, and nothing at native 4K.** No figure for any GPU other than the unnamed demo hardware, the PS4 Pro prototype and one RTX 3080 fly-through.
- **No in-memory size of the root page** ("First page contains top level(s) of DAG / Always resident", [Deck p.124](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=124)); no figure for the size of the culling hierarchy beyond "tiny".

## 7. What the decks name as future work or as unsolved

### Takeaway
The 2021 Deck is explicit about what it leaves open: prefiltering of shading and of coverage ("Unsolved", "still an open question"), a better error metric, a replacement for imposters, hierarchical instancing and merging, occlusion culling that does not lean on the previous frame, stored tangents, ray tracing, and the three unsupported classes of content. The HPG deck adds a hindsight list of misjudgements and ends with "this problem isn't solved yet".

### Inferences
- **Sorted by what they threaten**, the open items fall into two groups. Picture quality: the heuristic error, the missing prefiltering of shading, the imposter pop, the shadow mismatch, seams between objects. Reach: aggregates, translucency, deformation, instance counts. The Deck treats the first group as refinements and the second as "big chunks of the support matrix we do not cover yet".
- **Several of the reach items have since been addressed by adding mechanisms next to the cluster hierarchy, not by extending it** (section 5): voxels for aggregates, assemblies for instancing inside a mesh, skinning with its own culling class, a tessellator for displacement.

### Gaps
- Neither deck ranks these items or says which were being worked on, beyond "heavily reworked right now" for materials and "Increased focus" for compression.

## 8. Verdict on each statement of the earlier round

### Takeaway
Read against the decks, the earlier round's account of the build, the selection rule and the rejected alternatives holds, and most of what it could only recall is now confirmed with a page number: the group size of 8 to 32, the eight-way hierarchy, the role of temporal anti-aliasing, the whole imposter slide and the whole results table. Four things need correcting: the recalled per-frame counts were wrong (the real table is in section 6), the drawn-triangle figure is 25 million and not about 20 million, the measurement pages do not name PlayStation 5, and Karis did not come to clusters after voxels but the other way round. ### Inferences
- **The earlier round's reconstruction of the algorithm can be used as it stands**, with three amendments: the group size is the Deck's own figure; the projection is taken at the worst point of the sphere, not its centre; and the stored error is a "unioned" value whose exact formula the Deck does not give.
- **The earlier round's central inference about invisibility is now a quotation.** It reasoned that the switch is abrupt and relies on sub-pixel error; the Deck says so and names temporal anti-aliasing as the second condition. Its further inference, that the property is conditional on the threshold, is supported by the Deck's own statement that pixel-sized features need pixel-sized triangles, but the Deck does not discuss larger thresholds.
- **Items that stay unconfirmed are all implementation details of Unreal's source** (leaf cluster size range, root parent error, projection formula, default of the threshold variable, the non-mesh-shader hardware path). None of them is in the published talks.
