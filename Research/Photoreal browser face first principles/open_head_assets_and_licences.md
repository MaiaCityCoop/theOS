# Open and commercially licensable human head assets for a publicly hosted commercial browser game, and what each licence permits (state of 18 November 2026)

How each claim is known is marked on every line:

- **[read]** the licence text or file was read at the source in this session. Only github.com and raw.githubusercontent.com could be opened, so "the source" is the licence file, README or data file in the asset's own GitHub repository (or, for Creative Commons, the legal code in Creative Commons' own repository).
- **[measured]** counted or measured by me on the file downloaded from the source repository (image sizes from file headers, vertex and triangle counts from the glTF or OBJ data).
- **[extract]** taken from a web-search extract; the page itself could not be opened from this environment. Wording must be re-checked at the link before it is relied on or quoted in public.
- **[recalled]** from memory, not verified in this session. Kept out of "Cited Findings" and listed only under Gaps or Inferences.

Verdict words used for "usable in a publicly hosted commercial web game?": **yes**, **yes with attribution** (or "yes with notice" where a licence text must be shipped), **unclear**, **no**. These report what the texts say; they are not legal advice.

Access limits of this session (they shape every Gaps list below): every vendor and institute host tried was blocked by the network policy (unrealengine.com, metahuman.com, polyhaven.com, 3dscanstore.com, texturing.xyz, vgl.ict.usc.edu, flame.is.tue.mpg.de, casual-effects.com, creativecommons.org, studio.blender.org, huggingface.co, sketchfab.com, ambientcg.com, readyplayer.me, triplegangers.com, ten24.info, renderpeople.com, faces.dmi.unibas.ch, web.archive.org). Web search worked for twelve queries and then the shared search budget of the whole run was used up, so the vendor licences (TexturingXYZ, 3D Scan Store paid terms, Triplegangers, Ten24, RenderPeople), Sketchfab, the Smithsonian, Blender Studio characters, the Basel model licence and the Unreal Engine EULA text could not be searched further. Those items are reported as "not openable" rather than guessed.

Earlier notes in `research_notes/Photoreal human body architecture/body_geometry_and_shape_models.md` already cover the body-model licences (SMPL family, Anny, MakeHuman, MPFB2, MB-Lab, MHR, SOMA-X, Rocketbox, Blender Human Base Meshes). They are not repeated here; this file extends them for heads, faces, skin maps, expressions, hair and lighting.

## Overall: which head assets can a publicly hosted commercial web game legally ship, and what would each give a project with its own procedural head mesh?

### Takeaway
Shippable with certainty from the texts read: the Lee Perry-Smith head scan (CC BY 3.0, one man, colour, specular and normal maps at 1024 and a displacement map at 4096 in the three.js copy), ICT-FaceKit (MIT, geometry and 53 ARKit-style expression shapes, no textures), Google's GNM Head (Apache-2.0, statistical head with eyes, teeth and tongue, no skin textures), Meta's MHR (Apache-2.0, 72 FACS shapes), the MakeHuman and MPFB assets including the 52 ARKit-named "Face Units" targets (CC0), Microsoft Rocketbox (MIT, 19 adult women and 21 adult men, with 2048 head colour, normal, wrinkle-normal and specular maps as measured on one of them), and Poly Haven HDRIs (CC0). Every high-quality scan of a woman found (Digital Emily 2, Eisko Louise, the 3D Scan Store free female head, The Heretic's assets) is licensed for non-commercial or evaluation use only, and MetaHuman's fit for a web app that hands files to browsers is unclear from the extracts obtained.

### Inferences
- For a project that keeps its own procedural head with its own topology, nothing above can be dropped in as texture maps: every map is tied to its asset's UV layout. The practical uses are (a) a reference shape to fit or compare against (LPS, GNM, ICT, MHR, MakeHuman), (b) a source of maps to re-project onto the project's own UVs after fitting one surface to the other (LPS maps, Rocketbox maps, MakeHuman skins), and (c) a source of expression deltas to transfer by deformation transfer or wrapping (ICT-FaceKit, Face Units, MHR, GNM).
- Two tiers of provenance matter for a project with a "no scans" value (as recorded in the earlier body notes): artist-made and permissive (MakeHuman, MPFB2, Face Units, the MPFB example avatar), versus scan-derived and permissive (LPS, ICT-FaceKit, GNM Head, MHR). Rocketbox is permissive but its provenance was not read.
- The only pore-level skin detail found under a licence that clearly allows shipping is the Lee Perry-Smith displacement map (one man, 4096). No equivalent for a woman was verified.
- "Redistribution" splits cleanly: public licences (CC0, CC BY, MIT, Apache-2.0) allow the files themselves to sit in a public repository and be served to browsers; end-user licence agreements (Epic, Unity, scan vendors) typically allow only embedding in a product, which a web app that serves retrievable files may not satisfy. The TalkingHead README states the practical concern in plain words (quoted in the legal section).

### Gaps
- The twelve searches that ran did not reach TexturingXYZ, Triplegangers, RenderPeople, Sketchfab, the Smithsonian, Blender Studio characters or the Basel licence. Those rows are "unclear", not "no".
- No asset was found (or could be verified) that combines a real woman's scan, skin maps and a licence allowing commercial redistribution.

## Lee Perry-Smith / Infinite-Realities head scan as shipped in three.js

### Takeaway
The head is licensed CC BY 3.0 Unported: commercial use, redistribution and modification are allowed, provided the author, title, source and licence are credited and changes are labelled. The three.js copy holds a 17 684-triangle mesh, 1024-pixel colour, specular and normal maps and a 4096-pixel 8-bit displacement map of one real man.

### Inferences
- Verdict: **yes with attribution**. Commercial use, shipping the files to browsers, keeping them in a public repository and modifying them are all inside the Section 3 grant.
- A credit that carries every element the licence names would read along these lines: "Infinite, 3D Head Scan" by Lee Perry-Smith (www.triplegangers.com, www.ir-ltd.net), licensed under CC BY 3.0 Unported, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/, with a note of what was changed (for example "re-projected to this project's UV layout"). three.js itself shows only the name and a link; that is three.js's practice, not a statement of what the licence requires.
- Converting the maps to KTX2 or re-baking them to other UVs is covered by "modifications as are technically necessary to exercise the rights in other media and formats" or is an Adaptation that must be labelled as changed; encrypting them so that recipients cannot reuse them would touch the "effective technological measures" clause.
- The scan depicts an identifiable real man. The licence text is silent on publicity and personality rights, so the licence grants copyright permission only. If the subject is Lee Perry-Smith himself, the licensor and the depicted person are the same, which lowers but does not remove that question (the phrase "Do what you want with the files" is informal, and Section 4(c) and the no-endorsement clause still apply).
- For a project with its own head mesh: a reference surface to fit against and a source of real pore-level relief. The maps follow the scan's UVs, so use on another topology needs a fitted correspondence and a re-bake. One identity, neutral expression, no eyes, teeth, hair or blendshapes.

### Gaps
- casual-effects.com (Morgan McGuire's Computer Graphics Archive) and ir-ltd.net could not be opened, so the archive's own description, the original map resolutions and formats (the extract implies a 16-bit displacement original) and the archive's suggested credit line were not read.
- Who the scanned person is was not confirmed. [recalled] The scan is commonly described as Lee Perry-Smith's own head.
- Whether higher-resolution colour maps than 1024 were ever released was not checked.

## Is there a female counterpart to the Lee Perry-Smith head? Head scans of women and other sexes, ages and skin tones with skin maps and a licence that allows a publicly hosted commercial web game

### Takeaway
No verified one. Every scan of a real woman with skin maps that was found is restricted to non-commercial or evaluation use (Digital Emily 2, Eisko Louise, the 3D Scan Store / Ten24 free female head; Unity's Heretic assets, a man, likewise), and no CC0 or CC BY female head scan could be verified in this session (Sketchfab, the likeliest place, could not be opened). What is shippable for women is not a scan: Microsoft Rocketbox (MIT, 19 adult women; 2048 head colour, normal, wrinkle and specular maps measured on one) and the MakeHuman / MPFB skins and meshes (CC0, painted colour maps), plus texture-free statistical heads that can produce female shapes (GNM Head, ICT-FaceKit, MHR).

## USC ICT assets: ICT-FaceKit, Digital Emily 2 / Wikihuman, other Vision and Graphics Lab releases

### Takeaway
ICT-FaceKit's "ICT Face Model Light" is MIT-licensed and contains geometry only: a 26 719-vertex head topology with eyes, teeth and lashes, 100 identity PCA modes and ARKit-named expression shapes, but no albedo, specular or displacement data. The richer "Full ICT Face Model" is announced under "a different USC specific license" and is not in the repository; Digital Emily 2 is non-commercial.

- **[read, in full]** LICENSE: "MIT License / Copyright (c) 2020 USC Institute for Creative Technologies / Permission is hereby granted, free of charge, to any person obtaining a copy of this software and associated documentation files (the "Software"), to deal in the Software without restriction, including without limitation the rights to use, copy, modify, merge, publish, distribute, sublicense, and/or sell copies of the Software ... subject to the following conditions: The above copyright notice and this permission notice shall be included in all copies or substantial portions of the Software." — [ICT-FaceKit LICENSE](https://github.com/USC-ICT/ICT-FaceKit/blob/master/LICENSE)

- **[read]** Topology table: all geometry 26 719 vertices and 26 384 faces; face 9 409 vertices; head and neck 1 839; mouth socket 2 046; eye sockets 384 each; gums and tongue 2 977; teeth 4 412 (32 individually listed teeth); eyeballs 1 570 each (sclera 770, iris 800); lacrimal fluid, eye blend and eye occlusion meshes; eyelashes 684 vertices per eye as "Eyelash poly strips". - **[read]** Expressions: "Current expression shapes adopt the naming convention of the Apple ARKit, but with "Left" and "Right" specified with "_L" and "_R". - **[extract]** Digital Emily 2: non-commercial (see the previous section) — [CG Channel, 3 June 2015](https://www.cgchannel.com/2015/06/download-digital-human-labs-digital-emily-2-model/)

Anyone wishing to use the model or code for commercial purposes should contact William Smith" — [AlbedoMM README](https://github.com/waps101/AlbedoMM)

### Inferences
- ICT-FaceKit verdict: **yes with notice** (ship the MIT copyright and permission notice with the data or anything substantially derived from it). Provenance: statistical model learned from light-stage scans, on an artist-designed production topology.
- For a project with its own head: the most complete permissively licensed facial rig reference found. Its expression shapes are full-head OBJ files on a fixed topology, so they can be turned into per-vertex deltas and transferred onto another topology by fitting the project's neutral head to ICT's neutral (or the reverse) and applying deformation transfer; the README's FACS table and the ARKit names give a ready control vocabulary. The eye assembly (sclera, iris, lacrimal fluid, occlusion and blend meshes, lash strips) is a concrete, licensed pattern for the layered eye geometry that the earlier notes describe.
- The identity PCA modes describe scan statistics, not individuals; no likeness question arises from the neutral or from mode combinations.
- The MIT licence covers "software and associated documentation files"; USC applied it to the whole repository including the mesh data ("ICT-FaceKit is released under the MIT license"), which is the common reading, but the licence text itself was written for code.

### Gaps
- vgl.ict.usc.edu could not be opened. The licences and contents of other Vision and Graphics Lab releases (Light Stage Data Gallery, ICT-3DRFE, the skin micro-geometry data of Graham 2013 and Nagano 2015) were not read. [recalled] These are distributed for research use.
- Whether the "Full ICT Face Model" was ever released, and under what terms, was not found.

## Statistical face models: which are research-only and which allow commercial use

### Takeaway
Three statistical head models are usable commercially by their own texts: Google's GNM Head (Apache-2.0, July 2026), ICT-FaceKit (MIT) and, per the extract of its licence page, FLAME 2023 Open (CC-BY-4.0, with extra terms that need reading). FaceScape, Microsoft's Face Synthetics and SynthMoCap data, Meta's Multiface and Goliath, AlbedoMM and all earlier FLAME versions are restricted to non-commercial research; the Basel, FaceWarehouse and LYHM licence pages could not be opened.

### Cited Findings

#### Usable
- **[read]** GNM: "The ecosystem is released under the Apache 2.0 permissive license suitable for both non-commercial and commercial applications." "we are beginning our open-source release with **GNM Head**, our high-fidelity statistical 3D model of the human head." The LICENSE file is the Apache License 2.0 text. Technical report dated 28 July 2026 (arXiv 2607.23687); repository head commit 16 November 2026 — [google/gnm README](https://github.com/google/gnm); [LICENSE](https://github.com/google/gnm/blob/main/LICENSE)
- **[read]** GNM Head contents: "a state-of-the-art parametric 3D statistical model of the human head, learned from a large dataset of 3D scans"; "a dense 3D face mesh comprised of the skin, the eyes, the teeth, and the tongue"; identity parameters `[batch_size, 253]` (170 head, 3 eyeball, 80 teeth components); expression parameters `[batch_size, 383]` (100 left eye, 100 right eye, 150 lower face, 32 tongue, 1 iris); joints for "the rotation of the neck and eyeballs"; the model file "contains the template shape, identity basis, expression basis, skinning weights, and UV layout" — [GNM shape README](https://github.com/google/gnm/blob/main/gnm/shape/README.md)

### Inferences
- GNM Head verdict: **yes with notice** (Apache-2.0: ship the licence and state changes). It is the most capable permissive head model found: identity, a large expression basis, eyeballs, teeth and tongue, UVs and a sampler that produces female and male identities across four demographic groups. It ships no skin maps, so it helps with shape, expression and UV conventions, not with appearance. Its weights are hosted on Hugging Face and Kaggle, whose model pages (and any separate model card terms) could not be opened; the repository's statement covers "the ecosystem".
- FLAME 2023 Open verdict: **yes with attribution**, conditional on reading the licence page. - For a project with its own head, a statistical model's value is (a) a plausibility prior and a set of shapes to test the procedural generator against, (b) an expression basis to transfer, and (c) a standard topology and UV layout that tools and trackers understand (ARKit names for ICT, FLAME for most research trackers, GNM for Google's perception stack).
- Microsoft's Face Synthetics and SynthMoCap cannot be used even to "improve any product", by their licence wording; only the DAViD SynthHuman images are permissively licensed.

## MetaHuman: the licence since Unreal Engine 5.6, other engines, and shipping assets in a publicly downloadable web application

### Takeaway
Since Unreal Engine 5.6 (June 2025) MetaHumans fall under the Unreal Engine EULA and Epic says they "can be used with any engine or creative software", free below 1 million US dollars of revenue. ## CC0 and permissive bodies and heads: which include face topology, UVs, expression shapes or skin textures

### Takeaway
The MakeHuman family (MakeHuman, MPFB2, Anny) is the only artist-made, CC0 source that covers a whole head: face topology with UVs, eyes, teeth, tongue, eyebrow, eyelash and hair meshes, 34 expression units per ethnic group, the 52 ARKit-named "Face Units" targets, and painted skins in separately downloaded packs. MHR and GNM (Apache-2.0) add scan-derived shape and richer expression bases without textures; MB-Lab's AGPL makes it unsuitable; the CC0 limits are that only bundled assets are covered and that the skins are not in the code repositories.

### Takeaway
Permissive hair is thin: ICT-FaceKit ships eyelash strips (MIT), and the MakeHuman ecosystem supplies mesh eyebrows, eyelashes and hairstyles (CC0 or CC BY per asset) that were measured inside one CC0 example avatar. The research hair models and generators that were checked are non-commercial (CT2Hair, DiffLocks) or have unclear data terms (Perm); no CC0 strand-groom library was verified.

### Cited Findings
- **[read]** ICT-FaceKit includes "Eyelashes left/right", 684 vertices and 540 faces each, as "Eyelash poly strips"; MIT — [ICT-FaceKit README](https://github.com/USC-ICT/ICT-FaceKit)
- **[measured]** MakeHuman-ecosystem facial hair as shipped in TalkingHead's CC0 `mpfb.glb`: eyebrows `mind_eyebrows_02` 12 243 vertices and 17 622 triangles (flat black material, 23 morph targets so that they follow brow and lid shapes); eyelashes `mind_eyelashes_02` 16 992 vertices and 25 488 triangles (33 morph targets); hair `ponytail01` 3 902 vertices and 5 352 triangles with a 2048 x 2048 RGBA texture and alpha masking — [TalkingHead avatars/mpfb.glb](https://github.com/met4citizen/TalkingHead/blob/main/avatars/mpfb.glb)
- **[read]** That avatar "is licensed under CC0"; the ecosystem's assets are "licensed CC0/CC-BY" — [TalkingHead README](https://github.com/met4citizen/TalkingHead)
- **[read]** MPFB2 lists asset kinds "Skins, Ink layers, Eyes, Eyebrows, Eyelashes, Hair, Teeth, Tongue, Clothes, and Poses", and has an experimental hair and fur editor whose assets are `hair.blend` and `fur.blend` files in a `haireditor` directory, with services for baking hair to cards (`haircardservice.py`, `hairbakeservice.py`) — [MPFB2 asset service doc](https://github.com/makehumancommunity/mpfb2/blob/master/docs/services/assetservice.md); [MPFB2 hair editor doc](https://github.com/makehumancommunity/mpfb2/blob/master/docs/services/haireditorservice.md)
- **[read]** MakeHuman's code repository itself holds no hair, eyebrow or eyelash assets (`data/hair` and `data/eyebrows` contain only a placeholder thumbnail); they come with the separately downloaded assets — [makehuman data](https://github.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman/tree/master/makehuman/data)
- **[read]** CT2Hair (Meta, strand hair from CT scans): "Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial (CC BY-NC) 4.0" — [CT2Hair README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/CT2Hair)
- **[read]** DiffLocks (Meshcapade and Max Planck, strand hair from one image): "DiffLocks License (Non-Commercial Scientific Research Use Only)"; "Any other use, in particular any use for commercial, pornographic, military, or surveillance, purposes is prohibited." — [DiffLocks LICENSE](https://github.com/Meshcapade/difflocks/blob/main/LICENSE)
- **[read]** Perm (parametric hair model): the code is under the MIT licence ("Copyright (c) 2024 Chengan He"); it was trained on and distributes fits to "all 343 USC-HairSalon hairstyles", whose own terms were not read — [Perm LICENSE](https://github.com/c-he/perm/blob/main/LICENSE); [Perm README](https://github.com/c-he/perm)
- **[read]** Unity's hair, eyebrow, eyelash and stubble attachment system is in the Digital Human package under the Unity Companion License (not usable outside Unity) — [com.unity.demoteam.digital-human](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/com.unity.demoteam.digital-human)

### Inferences
- Verdicts: ICT lash strips **yes with notice**; MakeHuman-ecosystem brows, lashes and hair **yes** where the individual asset is CC0, **yes with attribution** where it is CC BY (check each asset's licence field); CT2Hair and DiffLocks **no**; Perm **unclear** for its trained weights and data.
- For eyebrows and eyelashes the project can generate its own strands or cards procedurally on its own head (follicle positions and directions are simple to author), which avoids both the licence and the topology-fit question; the measured MakeHuman assets show the budget others spend (about 17 000 to 25 000 triangles per pair for mesh lashes and brows, which is high for a browser and suggests cards or shader strands instead).
- A scalp hairstyle is the larger gap: no permissive strand groom was verified, and mesh-card styles from MakeHuman are game-quality.

### Gaps
- Blender's bundled hair assets and Blender Studio's character grooms could not be opened. [recalled] Blender ships a CC0 "hair curves" asset bundle of procedural node groups, and Blender Studio characters include grooms under CC BY.
- Cem Yuksel's hair model files, USC-HairSalon and Hair20K licences were not read. [recalled] They are offered for research use with citation.
- AMD TressFX sample assets and their licence were not found at the paths tried.
- Epic's MetaHuman grooms fall under the MetaHuman section's conclusion.

## HDRI environments for portrait lighting

### Takeaway
Poly Haven's HDRIs are CC0 with redistribution explicitly allowed, including inside a product that is sold, and are offered at "16k+" resolution, unclipped; they can be shipped in a web game without attribution. A browser would ship a small prefiltered derivative rather than the 16K source.

### Cited Findings
- **[read]** Licence statements as quoted in the skin-texture section: all assets CC0; "You can use our assets for any purpose, including commercial work"; "You do not need to give credit"; "You can redistribute them ... or even in a product you sell." — [Poly Haven site source, license.json](https://github.com/Poly-Haven/polyhaven.com/blob/master/public/locales/en/license.json)
- **[read]** Poly Haven explains the choice: more restrictive licences would block uses by "data scientists, software developers, automotive engineers and AI researchers"; it asks those who benefit financially to consider supporting it on Patreon (a request, not a condition) — [Poly Haven site source, license.json](https://github.com/Poly-Haven/polyhaven.com/blob/master/public/locales/en/license.json)
- **[extract]** The home page describes the HDRIs as "16k+ resolution, and always unclipped for one-click realistic lighting"; asset pages list EXR downloads up to 16K and 24K (example: the "Workshop" page) — [polyhaven.com](https://polyhaven.com); [Poly Haven "workshop"](https://polyhaven.com/a/workshop)
- **[extract]** The licence page notes that Poly Haven "maintains a standard Terms of Service agreement to prevent any misuse of this website", separate from the CC0 terms on the assets — [polyhaven.com/license](https://www.polyhaven.com/license)

### Inferences
- Verdict **yes**. CC0 needs no credit; recording the asset name and download date in the repository is still useful for provenance.
- Studio and indoor HDRIs photograph real places and may show equipment or, rarely, people or signage; CC0 covers Poly Haven's copyright, not third-party marks in the image (CC0 Section 4, quoted in the legal section).
- For portrait lighting a web build needs only a low-resolution, prefiltered environment (the earlier browser notes cover PMREM and KTX2); the 16K source matters for sharp reflections in the eyes, where a higher-resolution crop of the key-light region is worth keeping.

### Gaps
- polyhaven.com and its API were blocked, so the list of studio and portrait HDRIs, their individual maximum resolutions and dynamic range were not read.
- A separate project titled "Open HDRI: 25 Free 29K HDRIs" appeared in a search result (Digital Production, 29 October 2025); its licence was not read — [Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2025/10/29/open-hdri-25-free-29k-hdris-more-to-come/)
- ambientCG's HDRIs (also CC0 by reputation) were not checked.

## Legal context: likeness and personality rights when shipping a scan of a real person, and what "CC BY" obliges in a web app

### Takeaway
Copyright licences and likeness rights are separate: CC BY 3.0 says nothing about publicity or privacy, CC BY 4.0 expressly does not license them, and CC0 waives only the affirmer's own rights and disclaims clearing anyone else's, so shipping a recognisable scanned face rests on the scanned person's consent, which none of the open licences documents. For attribution in a web app, the CC texts ask for author, title, source link, licence link and a note of changes, placed in a way "reasonable to the medium", which CC BY 4.0 says may be a link to a page that holds the information.

### Cited Findings
- **[read]** CC BY 4.0, Section 2(b)(1): "Moral rights, such as the right of integrity, are not licensed under this Public License, nor are publicity, privacy, and/or other similar personality rights; however, to the extent possible, the Licensor waives and/or agrees not to assert any such rights held by the Licensor to the limited extent necessary to allow You to exercise the Licensed Rights, but not otherwise." — [CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://github.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/blob/main/docs/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en.html)
- **[measured]** CC BY 3.0 Unported (the licence of the Lee Perry-Smith scan) contains none of the words "publicity", "privacy", "personality" or "likeness" — [CC BY 3.0 legal code](https://github.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/blob/main/docs/licenses/by/3.0/legalcode.en.html)
- **[read]** CC0 lists among the rights it waives "publicity and privacy rights pertaining to a person's image or likeness depicted in a Work", but the waiver is of "all of Affirmer's Copyright and Related Rights", and Section 4(c) says: "Affirmer disclaims responsibility for clearing rights of other persons that may apply to the Work or any use thereof, including without limitation any person's Copyright and Related Rights in the Work. Further, Affirmer disclaims responsibility for obtaining any necessary consents, permissions or other rights required for any use of the Work." — [CC0 1.0 legal code](https://github.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/blob/main/docs/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode.en.html)
- **[read]** No CC licence warrants that the licensor had the rights: CC BY 3.0 Section 5 offers the work "AS-IS" without "WARRANTIES OF TITLE ... NONINFRINGEMENT"; CC0 Section 4(b) makes "no representations or warranties of any kind concerning the Work" — [CC BY 3.0 legal code](https://github.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/blob/main/docs/licenses/by/3.0/legalcode.en.html); [CC0 1.0 legal code](https://github.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/blob/main/docs/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode.en.html)
- **[read]** CC BY 3.0 also protects the author's reputation and bars implied endorsement: "You must not distort, mutilate, modify or take other derogatory action in relation to the Work which would be prejudicial to the Original Author's honor or reputation"; "You may not implicitly or explicitly assert or imply any connection with, sponsorship or endorsement by the Original Author" — [CC BY 3.0 legal code](https://github.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/blob/main/docs/licenses/by/3.0/legalcode.en.html)
- **[read]** How dataset owners and vendors treat faces: FaceScape restricts its data because "facial data involves the privacy of participants"; Unity's Heretic licence makes the user responsible for not infringing "rights of publicity or privacy" and bars "pornographic" use; Microsoft notes of its synthetic faces that "Some of our rendered faces may be close in appearance to the faces of real people. Any such similarity is naturally unintentional"; the Max Planck family of licences (read for SKEL in the earlier body notes) and DiffLocks prohibit "pornographic" use — [FaceScape README](https://github.com/zhuhao-nju/facescape); [Heretic sample LICENSE.md](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/com.unity.demoteam.digital-human.sample/blob/master/LICENSE.md); [FaceSynthetics README](https://github.com/microsoft/FaceSynthetics); [DiffLocks LICENSE](https://github.com/Meshcapade/difflocks/blob/main/LICENSE); [SKEL LICENSE.txt](https://github.com/MarilynKeller/SKEL/blob/master/LICENSE.txt)
- **[extract]** Wikimedia Commons attaches a personality-rights warning to freely licensed images of identifiable people: such rights can restrict some uses regardless of copyright (from the earlier realism notes) — [mirror of a Commons file page](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Anterior_view_of_human_female_and_male,_with_labels_2.png)
- **[read]** What CC BY 3.0 requires when the work is distributed (as a web app does when it sends the files to a browser): the licence text or its URI "with every copy of the Work You Distribute or Publicly Perform"; the author's name, the title, the source URI and, for an adaptation, "a credit identifying the use of the Work in the Adaptation", "reasonable to the medium or means You are utilizing"; the credit "may be implemented in any reasonable manner", but in an adaptation or collection it must appear at least "as part of these credits and in a manner at least as prominent as the credits for the other contributing authors" where such credits exist — [CC BY 3.0 legal code](https://github.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/blob/main/docs/licenses/by/3.0/legalcode.en.html)
- **[read]** What CC BY 4.0 requires (Section 3(a)): retain "identification of the creator(s)", "a copyright notice", "a notice that refers to this Public License", "a notice that refers to the disclaimer of warranties", "a URI or hyperlink to the Licensed Material to the extent reasonably practicable"; "indicate if You modified the Licensed Material"; and "include the text of, or the URI or hyperlink to, this Public License". "You may satisfy the conditions in Section 3(a)(1) in any reasonable manner based on the medium, means, and context in which You Share the Licensed Material. For example, it may be reasonable to satisfy the conditions by providing a URI or hyperlink to a resource that includes the required information." — [CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://github.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/blob/main/docs/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en.html)
- **[read]** Neither CC BY version lets the distributor lock the material: CC BY 3.0 forbids "effective technological measures" that restrict recipients' rights; CC BY 4.0 Section 2(a)(5) ("No downstream restrictions") forbids applying "any Effective Technological Measures to, the Licensed Material if doing so restricts exercise of the Licensed Rights by any recipient" — [CC BY 3.0 legal code](https://github.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/blob/main/docs/licenses/by/3.0/legalcode.en.html); [CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://github.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/blob/main/docs/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en.html)
- **[read]** MIT and Apache-2.0 assets carry a notice duty instead of attribution to a person: MIT, "The above copyright notice and this permission notice shall be included in all copies or substantial portions of the Software." — [ICT-FaceKit LICENSE](https://github.com/USC-ICT/ICT-FaceKit/blob/master/LICENSE)

### Inferences
- A web game "distributes" each asset to every visitor, so attribution has to be reachable by the visitor, not only present in the repository. The texts support a three-place pattern: a credits or licences page reachable from the game (author, title, source link, licence link, statement of changes, and for MIT and Apache assets the notice texts); a licence or notice file next to the asset in the public repository; and, where the format allows, the same line in the file's metadata (glTF `asset.copyright` or `extras`). CC BY 4.0 says outright that a link to a page with the information can be reasonable; CC BY 3.0 says "reasonable to the medium" without the example. This is a reading of the texts, not advice.
- Compressing or converting a CC BY asset (KTX2, meshopt) is a permitted technical modification; re-projecting maps to other UVs, cropping tiles or blending them into a procedural skin makes an adaptation that must say it was changed. Obfuscating or encrypting the files so that they cannot be reused would conflict with the technological-measures clauses.
- Likeness: the open licences settle copyright only. For the Lee Perry-Smith head the depicted man appears to be connected to the licensor, and the scan has been redistributed since 2010 in three.js, in Unity's render-pipeline tests and in McGuire's archive; I found no report of an objection, which suggests tolerance but is not a release. For any scan of a third person (a Sketchfab upload, a vendor scan) the missing document is the model release: what the person agreed to, in particular commercial use, use in a game, alteration of the face, and nudity. A game that puts a recognisable real face on a nude body is the use most likely to exceed a generic release and to meet the "derogatory", "pornographic" or "honor or reputation" wording that several licences contain.
- Using a scan only for what is not recognisable (pore relief, fine wrinkle statistics, specular micro-detail), on a head whose shape and features are the project's own, removes the likeness question while keeping the copyright duty of attribution.
- Statistical models (GNM, ICT-FaceKit, MHR, FLAME Open) do not depict a person; a generated face can still resemble someone by chance, which is the point Microsoft's note makes.

### Gaps
- No statute, case law or regulator guidance on the right of publicity, the right to one's own image, or biometric-data rules for 3D face scans was retrieved; the search budget ran out before that part could be researched. The section above reports only what the licence texts and dataset owners say.
- creativecommons.org was blocked; the CC legal codes were read from Creative Commons' own `cc-legal-tools-data` repository, which is the source of the published pages. Creative Commons' FAQ and its best-practice page for attribution (title, author, source, licence) were not read.
- The Apache License 2.0 notice and "state changes" clauses were not re-read in this session; the repositories' LICENSE files were confirmed to be the Apache 2.0 text by their headers only.
- Whether Infinite-Realities or Lee Perry-Smith has published a statement about uses of the head scan beyond the licence file was not found.
