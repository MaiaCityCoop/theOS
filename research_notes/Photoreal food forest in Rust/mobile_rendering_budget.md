## 1. Hardware envelope: GPU capability, sustained performance, console comparison, memory ceilings

### Takeaway
An upper-mid phone of late 2026 (A17 Pro, A18, Snapdragon 8 Gen 3) is at best a PS4 / Steam Deck / Switch 2-handheld class GPU when cool (about 1,400–1,600 points in 3DMark Steel Nomad Light, which is 10–12 fps at 1440p in that test) and it keeps only about 60–75% of that after a 20-minute load; 2026 flagships (A20 Pro, Dimensity 9600 Pro) are about 2.6–2.8 times faster at peak. Memory is the firmer wall on 8 GB phones: Android 17 hard-kills a visible app at 5,120 MiB and Google Play penalises games above 3.5 GB, iOS shows about 6 GiB in developer measurements but Apple documents nothing, so a 3–3.5 GB total process budget is the safe design point.

**Per-app memory ceilings**

- [D] Android 17 (API 37) enforces per-app cgroup limits by RAM tier. For the 8 GB tier (6,800–9,216 MiB total) the hard limit is 5,120 MiB while the app is visible and 3,072 MiB when not visible, with swap caps of 3,072 MiB; the soft limit that triggers reclaim and throttling is 90% of that (4,608 MiB visible). 12 GB tier: 8,192 / 4,096 MiB. 6 GB tier: 4,096 / 2,048 MiB. Vendors may not ship allowlists or overrides — [AOSP Memory Limiter](https://source.android.com/docs/core/perf/memory-limiter)
- [D] Google Play "bad behaviour" thresholds for games (anonymous RSS plus swap, 90th percentile): foreground 3.50 GB on 8 GB devices, 2.75 GB on 6 GB, 4.00 GB on 12 GB, 5.00 GB on 16 GB; background 2.75 GB on 8 GB. Store visibility impact starts February 2027 — [Android vitals](https://developer.android.com/google/play/vitals)
- [D] The Android 17 metric is anonymous RSS plus uncompressed swap and excludes file-backed and GPU-private memory; exceeding the limit kills the process without a stack trace — [Android games memory overview](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/memory-overview)
- [S] Google's older rule of thumb: games typically use between a quarter and a half of physical RAM — [Android games memory allocation guide](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/memory-allocation)
- [R] iPhone 15 Pro Max (8 GB): footprint plus the value of the available-memory call summed to exactly 6 GiB (6,442,450,944 bytes) in a developer's samples, no entitlement mentioned; the app then kept allocating 735 MB past that point without being killed. Apple's engineer replied that the two numbers are not meant to be correlated and that limits are deliberately undocumented and change regularly (thread from about October–November 2026) — [Apple Developer Forums 854762](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/854762)
- [R] iPhone 16 Pro Max (8 GB) with the increased-memory-limit entitlement: about 6,144 MB; iPhone 17 Pro Max (12 GB): also about 6,144 MB, 6,656 MB with the debugging-limit entitlement added (June 2026, no Apple reply) — [Apple Developer Forums 834805](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/834805)
- [D] Apple's entitlement documentation gives no number and says the higher limit exists only on some device models — [Apple entitlement doc](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.kernel.increased-memory-limit)
- [R] An apparent halving of the per-app limit on 8 GB iPads in iPadOS 26.4 (6 GB to 3 GB) was diagnosed by Apple engineers as virtual address space exhaustion caused by JavaScriptCore reserving too much address space, fixed in a 26.6 beta; the workaround discussed is the extended-virtual-addressing entitlement. Relevant because a WebView shell loads JavaScriptCore — [Apple Developer Forums 826325](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/826325)
- [S] A WebKit bug thread reports Safari's limit on iPhone 15 Pro at about 3 GB and a soft limit of about 1.5 GB for a web content process — [WebKit Bugzilla 277848](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=277848)
- [M] A shipped reference: Where Winds Meet at maximum settings used about 2.15 GB on an iPhone 17 Pro Max with over 4 GB still available, and about 2.25 GB on an M5 iPad Pro (Dame Tech, December 2025) — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Where-Winds-Meet-Mobile-performance-compared-on-Apple-iPhone-17-Pro-Max-and-M5-iPad-Pro-and-Nubia-RedMagic-11-Pro-This-is-the-best-mobile-device-to-play-on.1184902.0.html)

### Inferences
- Three practical phone tiers for a 2026–2027 release, by Steel Nomad Light peak: (a) 1,000–1,700: Tensor G4, A17 Pro, A18, A18 Pro, Snapdragon 8 Gen 3; (b) 2,100–3,300: A19, A19 Pro, Snapdragon 8 Elite and Elite Gen 5, Dimensity 9400/9500; (c) 3,700–4,000: A20 Pro, Dimensity 9600 Pro. "8 GB of RAM" does not define a GPU tier: the 8 GB iPhones span 1,398 to 2,130 and an 8 GB Pixel 9a sits at 1,060.
- Console mapping, ordinal only because the benchmarks differ: tier (a) at cool peak is in the PS4 / Steam Deck / Switch 2 handheld band (about half of a Radeon 780M handheld); after throttling it is below a PS4. Tier (b) at peak matches a 25–30 W handheld PC; tier (c) approaches Xbox Series S in burst throughput but not in sustained power.
- The planning number for a 60 fps game is the throttled one. Design the GPU frame to about 11 ms on a cool device so that a 30–35% loss still fits 16.6 ms; on Android flagships with 56–61% stability this margin is not generous.
- 8 GB M-series iPads and Macs are faster than tier (a) (M1 about 1.35 times an A17 Pro, M2 about 1.6, M3 about 1.9 by Steel Nomad Light) but drive 2.5–4 times more display pixels, so they need the same roughly 1 megapixel internal resolution with a larger upscale factor, or 30–50% more internal pixels at most.
- Memory budget for 8 GB phones: at most 3.0–3.5 GB total process footprint. Reasons: Play's 3.5 GB foreground threshold, Android's 4,608 MiB soft limit, iOS behaviour that is undocumented and has regressed between OS releases, and the shipped example at about 2.2 GB. On iOS all GPU memory is unified and counts; on Android the documented metric excludes GPU-private memory but the cgroup accounting of driver allocations is not stated, so do not rely on it.
- Realistic GPU-resource share inside that: roughly 1.0–1.5 GB. Arithmetic sketch (my own, not sourced): render targets at about 0.9 megapixel internal plus 3 megapixel output history and swapchain 150–200 MB; three 2048² shadow maps 25–50 MB; a 600–800 MB texture pool; 200–300 MB of geometry; under 50 MB of probes and lookup tables. With ASTC 6×6 (128 bits per 36 texels = 3.56 bits per pixel, plus a third for mips) a 2048² texture is about 2.5 MB and a 1024² about 0.6 MB, so 700 MB holds about 280 2K textures or 1,100 1K textures. Thousands of plant species times growth stages cannot be resident; they need shared material atlases, procedural parameters instead of unique textures, and streaming.
- A WebView shell (Tauri uses the system WebView) adds separate processes with their own limits on iOS and loads JavaScriptCore; the 2026 address-space regression shows the shell can affect the native renderer's memory headroom.

### Gaps
- No Steel Nomad Light result for Steam Deck, PS4, Xbox Series S or Switch 2 exists (SteamOS and consoles are not supported platforms), so the phone-to-console mapping is ordinal. The Geekerwan chart does not name its benchmark.
- UL's device pages do not define "stability"; from background knowledge it is the ratio of the lowest to the best loop over a 20-loop stress run, unverified here.
- Apple publishes no per-device memory limit. The 6 GiB figures are two developers' observations; a baseline for an 8 GB iPhone without any entitlement on a current iOS, and any figure for iPhone 16 or 17 (non-Pro, 8 GB), were not found.
- RAM sizes of the iPad Air and MacBook Air models are not on the cited pages. Background knowledge (unverified here): iPad Air M1–M3 and base MacBook Air M1–M3 ship with 8 GB; macOS has no per-app footprint cap and swaps instead. No GPU score for an 8 GB MacBook Air was retrieved.
- Measured scores for Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 and for mid-range 8 GB Android chips (Snapdragon 7 series, Dimensity 8000 series, Exynos) were not retrieved; only Tensor G4 stands in for that class.
- Whether Android's cgroup limit counts GPU driver memory is not stated in the AOSP document.
- No sourced figure for the sustained thermal power a passively cooled phone can dissipate; only the 5.4–7.2 W in-game measurements above.

## 2. What the best-looking shipped mobile open-world and nature-heavy games do, with measured fps and resolution; Crimson Desert on handhelds

### Takeaway
The best shipped mobile open worlds hold about 60 fps on 2024–2025 flagships at roughly 786p to native resolution, with stylised or semi-realistic art and at 5–7 W; realistic console ports on iPhone run at 20–30 fps from internal resolutions as low as 680×300. Crimson Desert has no phone, tablet or Steam Deck version, its own floor is an Xbox Series S at 720p and 40 fps with ray tracing off, and its Mac version requires 16 GB.

## 3. Tile-based deferred GPU constraints; best practice for dense vegetation, GPU-driven and virtual geometry, far field, terrain

### Takeaway
Alpha-tested foliage is the worst case because discard defeats or weakens the hidden-surface removal that mobile GPUs rely on and masked-out pixels cost about as much as shaded ones: Arm's own forest demo ran at 15.75 fps on a 2024 flagship until masked materials and wind offsets were removed. GPU-driven cluster culling is cheap on 2022-and-later flagships (well under 1 ms in Tencent's measurements) and custom virtual-geometry pipelines work, but Unreal's Nanite is not supported by the mobile renderer and published Nanite-on-phone results are 30–45 fps for static opaque content.

### Cited Findings

**Why discard and overdraw hurt on tile-based GPUs**
- [D] Apple GPUs process all geometry of a pass per tile, discard occluded primitives and shade only visible ones, keeping colour and depth in fast on-chip tile memory; Apple notes that A11 and later improved fragment-discard performance, i.e. discard is a known cost — [Apple, TBDR article](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/tailor-your-apps-for-apple-gpus-and-tile-based-deferred-rendering)
- [D] Mali's Forward Pixel Kill stops in-flight fragment threads only when a later opaque write covers the same pixel (2013 article, concept still current) — [Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/killing-pixels---a-new-optimization-for-shading-on-arm-mali-gpus)
- [S] Arm forum guidance: fragments that use discard or alpha-to-coverage do not count as opaque occluders for Forward Pixel Kill; blending disables early depth-stencil testing and Forward Pixel Kill; micro-triangles reduce its effectiveness — [Arm forum](https://community.arm.com/support-forums/f/graphics-gaming-and-vr-forum/54421/subpasses-alpha-blending-fpk-early-zs)
- [D] Immortalis-G925, Mali-G725 and G625 (2024) add a hardware fragment pre-pass that culls opaque fragments per sample regardless of draw order, so CPU-side front-to-back sorting is no longer needed. It stops at the first incompatible draw in a tile. Draws that discard or write depth in the shader are late-Z: they must run part of the fragment shader and shade their varyings inside the pre-pass, which can increase total work. Advice: put discard and depth writes early in the shader, keep alpha-to-coverage alpha simple, keep the position shader cheap because it can run up to three times per draw, avoid fat G-buffers because they shrink the tile size, keep render-target data at 128 bits per pixel or less (2024-11-28) — [Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/immortalis-g925-the-fragment-prepass)
- [M] Measured gain of that pre-pass on shipped games: 0.6% to 6.5% fewer GPU cycles, up to 39% less fragment read bandwidth, up to 16.1% less floating-point arithmetic and 13.9% less texturing — [Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/immortalis-g925-the-fragment-prepass)
- [D] Arm's art guide (2020, possibly outdated in detail): avoid alpha test unless required because it disables GPU optimisations; overdraw can severely affect mobile performance; for foliage in motion alpha test still looks better than alpha blend because blend breaks leaf ordering; always profile both — [Arm, Real-Time 3D Art Best Practices](https://documentation-service.arm.com/static/625591e9caabfd7b3c13f176)
- [V] An Arm engineer's rule of thumb (about 2014, old): well-built apps have opaque overdraw of 20–100% of visible fragments, which the hardware can generally hide; reducing it in the application is always better — [Arm forum](https://community.arm.com/support-forums/f/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-forum/5114/how-efficient-is-fragment-discarding-for-large-amounts-of-triangle-overlap)

**Measured cost of masked foliage and wind on a flagship phone**
- [M] Arm's Mori forest demo, modified Unreal Engine 5.5.2 desktop renderer with Nanite on a Vivo X200 Pro (Immortalis-G925 MC12), June 2026: baseline about 15.75 fps; with World Position Offset (vertex wind) disabled 27.33 fps; with masked materials disabled almost 40 fps. Arm states masked-out pixels cost about as much as rendered ones and that wind offsets split meshes into more clusters and raise culling cost — [Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mori-to-nanite-billions-of-triangles-on-mobile)
- [M] Arm's fix was opaque geometry: trees rebuilt at about 8.66 million triangles each (3.75 million trunk, 4.91 million leaves) with no masks and no wind, terrain as a 4-million-polygon playable mesh plus a 400,000-polygon distant mesh sharing three 8K textures, target 30 fps. Stated limits: the scene is static, with no wind or grass animation and little material variety — [Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mori-to-nanite-billions-of-triangles-on-mobile)

**GPU-driven rendering and virtual geometry on phones**
- [M] Tencent Games' mobile cluster pipeline (SIGGRAPH 2024, Advances in Real-Time Rendering): meshes split into clusters of at most 128 triangles with a Nanite-like hierarchy; GPU instance culling against a hierarchical depth buffer, then cluster frustum, occlusion and normal-cone culling and GPU level-of-detail selection; hardware rasterisation only into a 32-bit visibility buffer (25-bit cluster, 7-bit triangle). Software rasterisation was rejected because of the extra depth pass, missing 64-bit atomics and higher bandwidth. Masked vegetation is a separate bin that tests the mask in the pixel shader. Materials are shaded per material over 64×64-pixel screen tiles because bindless is not available on mainstream mobile — [Tencent](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Cao-NanoMesh/AdavanceRealtimeRendering_NanoMesh0810.pdf)
- [M] Same talk, Immortalis-G715 device: source scene of 80 million rock triangles (40 meshes of 2 million), 4 million landscape triangles and 10,000+ grass meshes of 20 triangles; about 60 fps over 10 minutes (57.9–59.6 average, against 3.8 fps without the pipeline); added GPU time about 3 ms: instance culling 0.11, cluster culling 0.07, binning 0.2, rasterisation 0.72, material classify 0.92, material shading 0.87; about 117,000 primitives and 190 draw calls after culling; streaming adds 1.55 ms and 37 MB. On an unnamed five-year-old low-end phone the same passes cost about 20 ms. Render resolution is not stated — [Tencent](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Cao-NanoMesh/AdavanceRealtimeRendering_NanoMesh0810.pdf)
- [D] Unreal's Nanite documentation (5.9 page) lists current consoles and desktops with DirectX 12 and Shader Model 6; it supports opaque and masked materials, limits wind offsets, and does not mention mobile — [Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine)
- [V] Arm (2026-03-18): Nanite is not supported by Unreal's mobile renderer, only by the desktop renderers; running it on Android needs engine changes, a recent Mali driver, 64-bit image atomics and compute-shader derivatives; compute work disables framebuffer compression on Mali and many small dispatches are costly. Arm does not recommend virtual shadow maps on Mali G1 because clip distance lacks hardware support — [Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mali-and-unreal-engine-s-nanite-enabling-the-future-of-mobile-graphics)
- [M] Arm's Nanite measurements in the Mori test at 80% resolution scale: tree meshes of 30,000 / 76,000 / 634,000 triangles (up to 1.2 / 2.2 / 18 million rendered) cost 29.7 / 37.4 / 199.4 ms without Nanite and 23.8 / 22.8 / 26.2 ms with it (42–44 and 38 fps); at 50% resolution scale 18.0 / 17.9 / 20.5 ms (49–56 fps) — [Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mali-and-unreal-engine-s-nanite-enabling-the-future-of-mobile-graphics)
- [V] Qualcomm (January 2026) titles its post as running AAA Unreal Engine 5.4 content at 30 fps on Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5; [S] the post is described as covering Nanite, Lumen, virtual shadow maps and cloth on Snapdragon. Only the title could be read — [Qualcomm](https://www.qualcomm.com/developer/blog/2026/01/run-unreal-engine-5-content-30fps-snapdragon-mobile)
- [D] Unity 6 GPU Resident Drawer: draws through the batch renderer group with GPU instancing and its own GPU occlusion culling; needs the Forward+ path and compute-shader support and excludes OpenGL ES; no performance figures are given — [Unity](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/analysis/graphics-performance-profiling/in-urp/reduce-draw-calls-urp/reduce-rendering-work-on-cpu/gpu-resident-drawer)
- [D] Bevy 0.16 (April 2025) GPU-driven rendering by platform: Windows/Linux Vulkan gets GPU transform, multi-draw with GPU culling and bindless; macOS and iOS Metal get GPU transform but not multi-draw with GPU culling, and bindless with much lower limits; Android Vulkan is partial, with a denylist of buggy drivers that fall back to CPU-driven rendering; WebGPU gets GPU transform only. Occlusion culling is experimental — [Bevy 0.16](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-16/)
- [D] Bevy 0.20.1's meshlet (virtual geometry) plugin is documented as working only on Vulkan and Metal backends, requires 64-bit texture atomics, is incompatible with MSAA and has a much higher base overhead than the standard renderer; phones are not mentioned — [docs.rs, Bevy MeshletPlugin](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/pbr/experimental/meshlet/struct.MeshletPlugin.html); desktop cost on an RTX 4070: about 3.5–4.5 ms for 115 billion source triangles — [Bevy 0.17](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-17/)
- [D] Apple supports 64-bit atomics from the Apple9 family (A17 Pro, A18, M3, M4) and mesh shading from Apple7 — [Apple Metal Feature Set Tables](https://developer.apple.com/metal/Metal-Feature-Set-Tables.pdf)
- [D] wgpu 30 feature notes: multi-draw indirect count is DX12 and Vulkan only; mesh shaders and ray queries are experimental and native-only; 64-bit atomics, texture atomics, binding arrays and subgroups are native-only; timestamp queries inside passes are generally unavailable on tile-based GPUs — [docs.rs, wgpu Features](https://docs.rs/wgpu/latest/wgpu/struct.Features.html)

**Terrain and virtual texturing**
- [M] Tencent's lightmap atlas memory on mobile (RGBA8 with mips): 512² over 1 MB, 1024² over 4 MB, 2048² over 16 MB; 100 instances cost 100–1,600 MB, which is why they dropped lightmaps — [Tencent](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Cao-NanoMesh/AdavanceRealtimeRendering_NanoMesh0810.pdf)
- [D] Unreal's runtime virtual texturing page gives formats (two to three block-compressed layers for colour, normal, roughness) and tuning variables but no pool sizes and no mobile statement — [Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/runtime-virtual-texturing-in-unreal-engine)

### Inferences
- Best practice for dense vegetation on phones, in order of effect: (1) use real opaque triangles for leaves, fronds and grass blades near the camera, where mobile GPUs' hidden-surface removal works fully; (2) where masks are unavoidable, cut the card geometry tight to the texture, cap layers, and render them after all opaque geometry; (3) give masked geometry a depth-only pre-pass with the cheapest possible shader and run the expensive shader once with an equal depth test, so discard never sits in the costly shader; (4) never alpha-blend foliage; (5) restrict vertex wind to a near radius because it defeats cluster culling and shadow caching; (6) keep the G-buffer or forward outputs at 128 bits per pixel or less.
- Alpha-to-coverage needs MSAA, which conflicts with visibility-buffer and meshlet pipelines (Bevy's is documented as incompatible) and with temporal upscalers; for a 60 fps phone budget a temporal upscaler's anti-aliasing plus hard alpha test, optionally stochastic at the edge, is the practical choice. This is my judgment; no mobile measurement of alpha-to-coverage cost was found.
- A hierarchical depth buffer with compute culling is affordable (Tencent: 0.38 ms for instance and cluster culling plus binning on a 2022 flagship). In a wgpu renderer the limit is the API surface, not the hardware: without multi-draw indirect count on Metal, iOS needs instancing with compute-compacted instance buffers and a bounded number of draws per mesh and level of detail.
- Virtual geometry should not be a dependency of the first prototype on phones. The two working mobile examples are a custom engine (hardware raster only, no 64-bit atomics) and a modified Unreal build at 30–45 fps with static opaque content. Bevy's implementation needs 64-bit texture atomics (A17 Pro and later on Apple, a Vulkan extension on Android) and has no published phone results. Discrete level-of-detail chains plus hierarchical levels of detail and impostors are the low-risk route, and they are easier to generate procedurally for thousands of species than a cluster hierarchy.
- Arm's opaque-leaf answer (8.66 million triangles per tree) only works with a cluster pipeline; with discrete levels of detail the same idea means a few thousand opaque leaf triangles per near tree and masked cards further out.
- Virtual-texture cache arithmetic (mine): one 4096² cache layer is 64 MB uncompressed RGBA8, about 17 MB at 8 bits per pixel, about 7.5 MB at ASTC 6×6. Real-time ASTC encoding on the device is expensive, so a runtime-composited terrain cache on phones tends to be uncompressed or lightly compressed; two layers of 4096² uncompressed already cost 128 MB.

## 4. Lighting options and their mobile cost

### Takeaway
At 60 fps on an upper-mid phone, lighting has to be cascaded shadow maps with caching plus baked or probe-based indirect light; virtual shadow maps, Lumen and hardware ray tracing are all outside that budget (Arm's 2025 flagship GPU reaches 40–46 fps with half-resolution ray-traced shadows and reflections at below 1080p, and Lumen costs about 8 ms on current consoles). The only measured fully dynamic mobile global illumination found costs under 3 ms and 30 MB on a 2022 flagship, which is too much for tier (a) but plausible for 2025–2026 flagships.

### Cited Findings
- [D] Virtual shadow maps (Unreal 5.9 page): supported on PS5, Xbox Series and PCs, need 64-bit atomics, and are described as a poor fit for platforms that do not use Nanite; non-Nanite geometry is much more expensive to render into them; skeletal animation and wind offsets invalidate cached pages every frame; for grass and some foliage Epic suggests contact shadows alone can replace high-resolution shadow maps; the directional light uses 17 clipmap levels by default — [Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/virtual-shadow-maps-in-unreal-engine)
- [M] Tencent's mobile pipeline renders shadow depth from coarser cluster levels (about 100 instead of 200 draw calls with barely visible difference) and names shadow caching as one of the most effective methods — [Tencent](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Cao-NanoMesh/AdavanceRealtimeRendering_NanoMesh0810.pdf)
- [D] Lumen (Unreal 5.9 page): built for PS5, Xbox Series, Switch 2 and high-end PCs; not supported by the mobile renderer; on Android only through the desktop renderer on Vulkan; iOS is not mentioned; about 8 ms for global illumination and reflections at 1080p internal resolution on current consoles at the Epic setting, with a High setting aimed at 60 fps; foliage enters its surface cache only if it uses Nanite — [Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine)

### Inferences
- Shadows: two or three cascades to roughly 80–150 m, far cascades cached and updated every few frames, shadow casters drawn from coarser levels of detail, alpha-tested casters only in the nearest cascade, and screen-space contact shadows for grass and small plants. Virtual shadow maps are not an option: they presuppose a Nanite-class pipeline, need 64-bit atomics, and wind invalidates their cache.
- Indirect light: bake it. For a time-of-day game that means irradiance probes or volumes stored as low-order spherical harmonics for several sun positions and blended, plus a per-vertex ambient-occlusion and sky-visibility term for every plant. Because the plants are generated procedurally, that per-vertex term can be computed when the plant is generated, which fits the content target well. A fully dynamic voxel solution at under 3 ms on a 2022 flagship does not fit a tier (a) 60 fps budget next to everything else, but is a credible option for tier (b) and (c).
- Hardware ray tracing: not at 60 fps on tier (a). The best Arm data is 40–46 fps on a 2025 flagship GPU for two half-resolution effects in small test scenes, forests are the worst case for ray tracing because every leaf card needs an alpha test during traversal and micromaps are not hardware-accelerated on current Mali, and the wgpu ray-query path is experimental. Treat it as a desktop-only tier.
- Screen-space effects: the one mobile data point is 1.0–1.7 ms for a screen-space ray-march pass at about 0.6–1.2 megapixels on a 2024–2025 Mali flagship. Ambient occlusion at half the internal resolution should fit in well under 1 ms on tier (a); it is the first optional pass to cut.
- Leaf translucency is a few instructions in the forward shader (two-sided lighting with a transmission term scaled by a thickness or subsurface colour) and is cheap relative to its visual value in backlit forest scenes. No measured cost was found.

### Gaps
- Not searched: Qualcomm and Imagination ray-tracing data, measured cost of ambient occlusion techniques on phone GPUs, cascaded-shadow costs in shipped mobile games, published mobile implementations of spherical-harmonic lightmaps, precomputed radiance transfer or time-of-day probe blending. The recommendations above for baked lighting are engineering judgment, not sourced measurements.
- Qualcomm's and Apple's statements on what ray tracing can afford at 60 fps were not found beyond Apple's four-times claim; no Imagination material was retrieved.
- The apparent conflict between wgpu's documentation (ray query on Vulkan only) and Bevy 0.20's note that Solari runs on macOS was not resolved; the wgpu page was read only up to its first 100,000 characters.
- The details of Lumen Lite (what it removes, whether it needs hardware ray tracing) are not in the available coverage; a PC Gamer test article could not be read.

## 5. Sky, atmosphere, clouds, fog and water at mobile cost

### Takeaway
A lookup-table atmosphere after Hillaire (2020) is cheap enough for phones and already exists in Bevy and in Unreal's mobile renderer, and it carries most of the hazy-vista look. Real-time volumetric clouds and froxel fog are console features (under 2 ms and about 1 ms on a PS4) that Unreal's mobile renderer does not support at all, so on an upper-mid phone they must be layered or very low-resolution approximations; no sourced mobile data on water was found.

### Cited Findings
- [D] Bevy 0.16 ships procedural atmospheric scattering based on the optimisations in Hillaire's EGSR 2020 paper, described by its authors as suitable for mobile devices and WebGPU; in 0.16 it did not yet light surfaces or environment maps — [Bevy 0.16](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-16/). Bevy 0.17 adds a faster lookup-texture mode (default) and a slower ray-marched mode — [Bevy 0.17](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-17/). Bevy 0.18 makes sunlight pick up atmospheric colour and integrates the atmosphere with volumetric fog — [Bevy 0.18](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-18/)
- [D] Unreal's sky atmosphere evaluates the sky in low-resolution lookup tables (transmittance, multiple scattering, a fast sky view, a distant sky light, and an aerial-perspective volume aligned to the camera frustum) and is supported on mobile with a sky-dome mesh; enlarging the aerial-perspective volume can raise memory use significantly — [Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/sky-atmosphere-component-in-unreal-engine)
- [D] Unreal's platform table for volumetric clouds: not supported on mobile; supported on PS4 and Xbox One with the recommendation to use shadow maps instead of secondary ray marching; clouds are traced at quarter or half resolution and reconstructed; the page's budget example allots 0.6 ms to sky and cloud inside a real-time sky capture that is time-sliced over 9 frames — [Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/volumetric-cloud-component-in-unreal-engine)
- [V] Guerrilla's 2015 volumetric cloud prototype for Horizon rendered in under 2 ms on a PS4 — [Guerrilla Games](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-authoring-real-time-volumetric-cloudscapes-with-the-decima-engine)
- [D] Unreal's volumetric fog uses a low-resolution frustum-aligned voxel grid with heavy temporal reprojection; it costs 1 ms on a PS4 at High and 3 ms on a GTX 970 at Epic (eight times more voxels), and is switched off at the Low and Medium shadow quality levels; mobile is not mentioned — [Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/volumetric-fog-in-unreal-engine)
- [M] Assassin's Creed Mirage on iPhone 15 Pro removes volumetric lighting and reduces water detail and screen-space reflections at its lower settings — [Shazoo, summarising Digital Foundry](https://shazoo.ru/2024/07/11/158528/sliskom-ambiciozno-analiz-assassins-creed-mirage-na-ios-ot-digital-foundry)
- [M] Where Winds Meet shows visibly better water reflections and foliage on an M5 iPad Pro than on phones at maximum settings, i.e. water quality is tiered by device even in a 2025 flagship mobile title — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Where-Winds-Meet-Mobile-performance-compared-on-Apple-iPhone-17-Pro-Max-and-M5-iPad-Pro-and-Nubia-RedMagic-11-Pro-This-is-the-best-mobile-device-to-play-on.1184902.0.html)
- [M] For scale: a screen-space ray-march pass at 0.6–1.2 megapixels costs 1.0–1.7 ms on recent Mali flagships — [Arm, Vulkanised 2026](https://vulkan.org/user/pages/09.events/vulkanised-2026/Mobile-Ray-Tracing-Demystified-Iago-CalvoLista-Arm.pptx.pdf)

### Inferences
- Atmosphere: keep the full lookup-table model on every tier and amortise table updates over several frames; apply aerial perspective either from the frustum volume or analytically in the forward shader. This is the highest value-per-millisecond feature for vast hazy views.
- Clouds: a PS4 needed up to 2 ms for a quarter-resolution, temporally reprojected ray march. A tier (a) phone has less sustained throughput than a PS4, so a 1–2 ms cloud pass means tracing at about one sixteenth of the internal resolution with 8–16 frames of reprojection, a short march and no secondary shadow march, or falling back to two or three scrolling 2D layers lit from a lookup table. Recommend the layered version for tier (a) and the low-resolution ray march for tier (b) and (c). Cloud shadows on terrain can be a scrolling 2D mask.
- Fog: analytic height fog plus the aerial-perspective table costs almost nothing; a froxel grid cost a PS4 1 ms, so on tier (a) it only fits as a very small grid (for example 80×45×32) and should be optional. Light shafts through trees are the visible loss.
- Water (no sources, engineering judgment only): a sum of a few Gerstner waves in the vertex shader with scrolling detail normals, sky and probe reflection, depth-based colour and foam is the tier (a) option; an FFT ocean and screen-space or planar reflections belong to tier (b), (c) and desktop. Streams in forest close-ups are mostly a material and flow-map problem, not a simulation problem.

### Gaps
- The Hillaire paper itself could not be fetched (file over the size limit, the alternative host refused). Its lookup-table sizes and per-step timings on a desktop GPU and on an iPhone 6s are therefore not cited; from memory the paper reports a total of roughly 1 ms on that phone, unverified.
- No published mobile implementation of volumetric clouds with timings was found, and none could be searched for; the "1–2 ms" target in the brief is not confirmed by any mobile source.
- No sourced data on froxel fog on phones, and none on water (FFT against Gerstner, reflection methods, costs) on phones.
- Guerrilla's page gives only the 2 ms figure; resolution and reprojection details (from memory: quarter resolution, updated over 16 frames) are unverified.

## 6. Upscaling and frame generation on mobile, and what a wgpu/WGSL renderer can use

### Takeaway
The cheapest proven temporal upscaler is Qualcomm's GSR 2 in its two-pass fragment form: about 0.9–1.1 ms on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 for a 1.5–2 times upscale to 2800×1260, open source (BSD 3-clause) in plain GLSL, so it can be ported to WGSL. Arm ASR is open (MIT) but costs 2.5–6.5 ms on 2022–2023 flagships, MetalFX is closed and Metal-only with no published costs, and frame interpolation is not a way to reach 60 fps on a phone (a developer measured 11–18 ms per interpolated frame on an A19).

### Cited Findings
- [D] MetalFX availability by GPU family: spatial upscaling from Apple3, temporal upscaling from Apple7 (A14, M1 and later), frame interpolation from Apple5, denoised upscaling from Apple9 (A17 Pro, A18, M3 and later) — [Apple Metal Feature Set Tables](https://developer.apple.com/metal/Metal-Feature-Set-Tables.pdf)
- [D] Apple, WWDC 2025: frame interpolation comes to all Apple platforms and can be chained with the temporal upscaler; the input should already run at 30 fps or more; the temporal upscaler now accepts dynamically sized inputs and an optional reactive mask; do not set the maximum scale above 2× unless needed; the session gives no costs and no device list — [Apple WWDC25 session 211](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/211/)
- [R] Measured MetalFX frame interpolation on an iPhone 17 (A19), iOS 26.6: about 18 ms per interpolated frame in a game and about 11 ms in a bare triangle demo at 2622×1206, rising towards 30 ms once the GPU drops to a lower performance state; Apple's engineer could point to no published timings (about October–November 2026) — [Apple Developer Forums 853871](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/853871)
- [D] Snapdragon Game Super Resolution: BSD 3-clause; GSR 1 is a single-pass spatial upscaler and sharpener needing one input texture (2023); GSR 2 is a temporal upscaler (2.0.0 released 2024-10-21) — [Qualcomm GitHub](https://github.com/SnapdragonGameStudios/snapdragon-gsr)
- [M] GSR 2 cost on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 at maximum GPU frequency, output 1260×2800: two-pass fragment shader 0.905 ms from 630×1400 (2.0×), 1.024 ms from 740×1648 (1.7×), 1.107 ms from 840×1866 (1.5×); two-pass compute 1.80–2.00 ms; three-pass compute 2.02–2.40 ms. Inputs are colour, depth and motion vectors at render resolution; shaders are GLSL — [Qualcomm GitHub, GSR 2 README](https://github.com/SnapdragonGameStudios/snapdragon-gsr/blob/main/sgsr/v2/README.md)
- [V][S] Qualcomm's Adreno Neural Fusion SDK (September 2026) is a separate, AI-based super-resolution and frame-generation product supported from Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 — [Qualcomm](https://www.qualcomm.com/developer/blog/2026/09/introducing-adreno-neural-fusion-sdk-for-snapdragon-mobile-platforms)
- [D] Arm Accuracy Super Resolution: MIT licence, derived from AMD FSR 2 v2.2.2, with an Unreal plug-in and a generic library for custom engines — [Arm GitHub](https://github.com/arm/accuracy-super-resolution)
- [D][M] Arm ASR generic library: only a Vulkan back end is built in, OpenGL ES 3.2 is possible through the integrator's own back end; shaders are HLSL and GLSL with 16-bit float types; most work was moved to fragment shaders. Render times at a 2800×1260 target: 6.5 ms on Immortalis-G715 and 4.1 ms on G720 at 1.5× (Quality), 4.6 ms and 2.5 ms at 2× (Performance); at 2400×1080: 5.3 / 2.9 ms and 3.6 / 2.0 ms — [Arm GitHub, generic library](https://github.com/arm/accuracy-super-resolution-generic-library)
- [V] Arm ASR in an Unreal 5.4 demo on Immortalis-G720 at 2800×1260 (native 19 fps): frame-rate gain of 17% at 1.5× (840p internal), 33% at 1.7× (741p) and 87% at 2× (630p), 2025-03-18 — [Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/join-the-upscaling-revolution-with-arm-asr); the first announcement (2024-07-10) tested 540p to 1080p — [Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/introducing-arm-accuracy-super-resolution)
- [D] A community FSR 2 binding for wgpu exists, but it links AMD's native library, documents only a Windows build and targets Vulkan — [GitHub, fsr2_wgpu](https://github.com/JMS55/fsr2-wgpu)
- [D] Bevy: DLSS since 0.17, only on Nvidia RTX through the Vulkan back end on Windows and Linux, explicitly with no macOS, web or mobile support; no FSR, XeSS or MetalFX integration is mentioned in the 0.17–0.20 notes; the built-in option is temporal anti-aliasing — [Bevy 0.17](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-17/), [Bevy 0.20](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-20/)
- [M] Internal resolutions seen in shipped or published mobile work: Wuthering Waves 1705×786 on a 2868×1320 iPhone (59% per axis) — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Apple-iPhone-16-Pro-Max-outshines-Galaxy-S25-Ultra-in-gaming-efficiency-and-thermals-with-Wuthering-Waves-and-Genshin-Impact.957565.0.html); Assassin's Creed Mirage 680×300 to 720p — [Shazoo](https://shazoo.ru/2024/07/11/158528/sliskom-ambiciozno-analiz-assassins-creed-mirage-na-ios-ot-digital-foundry); Arm's Nanite demo at 50–80% resolution scale — [Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mali-and-unreal-engine-s-nanite-enabling-the-future-of-mobile-graphics); Zelda on Switch 2 handheld 648p–1080p dynamic with FSR 1 — [Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/zelda-breath-of-the-wild-and-tears-of-the-kingdom-nintendo-switch-2-frame-rate-resolution/)
- [V] Crimson Desert leans on frame generation at the low end: FSR 3 frame generation for the ROG Xbox Ally targets and MetalFX frame interpolation for every 60 fps Mac tier — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2026/03/10/crimson-desert-specs-for-pc-console-and-other-platforms-revealed)

### Inferences
- Usable from a wgpu/WGSL renderer on all five platforms: GSR 1 (one pass, one texture) and GSR 2's two-pass fragment variant. Both are short GLSL shaders with a permissive licence and no API-specific features, so a WGSL port is a contained task. This is the recommended default upscaler; FSR 1 is an equivalent spatial fallback.
- Arm ASR is portable in principle (MIT, GLSL and HLSL sources) but is a much larger FSR 2-derived pipeline, depends on 16-bit float shader types, and its measured cost (4.6–6.5 ms on an Immortalis-G715) takes a third of a 60 fps frame on tier (a). Consider it only for tier (b) and (c) or for 30 fps modes.
- MetalFX and DLSS are closed libraries that need native textures. They can be reached from wgpu only by dropping to the underlying Metal or Vulkan objects, as Bevy does for DLSS on desktop; nothing ready-made exists for MetalFX in the Rust stack as of the Bevy 0.20 notes. MetalFX temporal upscaling is worth a later Apple-only path for quality, but its cost on phones is unpublished, so it must be measured before being budgeted.
- Frame interpolation is out for this target. Apple requires a 30 fps base, the only measurement found is 11–18 ms per generated frame on a 2025 phone, and it adds latency. Design for a real 60 fps.
- Typical internal resolution is 50–67% of native per axis (a 1.5–2× upscale), which on today's phone panels means about 590p–790p internal height. For tier (a) plan on roughly 0.75–0.95 megapixels internal.

### Gaps
- No published cost of the MetalFX spatial or temporal upscaler on any iPhone was found; the frame-interpolation figure is one developer's report that includes synchronisation overhead.
- GSR 2's quality against FSR 2, ASR or MetalFX on foliage (ghosting and shimmer on thin alpha-tested leaves) was not found; Qualcomm compares only against a simple temporal upscale.
- Whether WGSL in current wgpu supports the 16-bit float types ASR's shaders use on phone drivers was not checked.
- Arm's neural super sampling and Qualcomm's neural SDK give no millisecond costs and are limited to 2026 hardware.
- FSR 3 on mobile and any existing WGSL ports of FSR 1, FSR 2 or GSR could not be searched for.

## 7. Proposed per-pass frame budget for 16.6 ms on an upper-mid phone, and distance from the Crimson Desert look

### Takeaway
A 60 fps nature renderer fits an A17 Pro or Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 only at about 0.75–0.95 megapixels internal resolution with roughly 40% of the per-pixel cost of 3DMark Steel Nomad Light, about 13.5 ms of GPU work in the throttled state and no ray tracing, virtual geometry, volumetric clouds or dynamic global illumination. The result is expected to look like a clean PS4-era open world at 60 fps with convincing haze and distant forest, clearly short of Crimson Desert in near-field density, lighting richness, shadow range and water.

### Cited Findings (calibration anchors for the budget)
- [M] An A17 Pro runs Steel Nomad Light (2560×1440) at 10 fps and a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 at 11 fps at peak, with 71% stability — [UL iPhone 15 Pro](https://benchmarks.ul.com/hardware/phone/Apple+iPhone+15+Pro+review), [UL Galaxy S24 Ultra](https://benchmarks.ul.com/hardware/phone/Samsung+Galaxy+S24+Ultra+review)
- [M] An A18 Pro phone holds 59 fps in Wuthering Waves at 1705×786 internal and 5.4 W — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Apple-iPhone-16-Pro-Max-outshines-Galaxy-S25-Ultra-in-gaming-efficiency-and-thermals-with-Wuthering-Waves-and-Genshin-Impact.957565.0.html)
- [M] GPU culling, binning, visibility-buffer raster and material shading for an 80-million-triangle source scene cost about 3 ms in total on an Immortalis-G715 (culling and binning 0.38 ms of that), and about 20 ms on a five-year-old low-end phone — [Tencent](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Cao-NanoMesh/AdavanceRealtimeRendering_NanoMesh0810.pdf)
- [M] A 2× temporal upscale to 2800×1260 costs 0.9 ms on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 with GSR 2's fragment variant, against 4.6 ms with Arm ASR on an Immortalis-G715 — [Qualcomm GitHub](https://github.com/SnapdragonGameStudios/snapdragon-gsr/blob/main/sgsr/v2/README.md), [Arm GitHub](https://github.com/arm/accuracy-super-resolution-generic-library)
- [M] Dynamic voxel global illumination costs under 3 ms on an Immortalis-G715; baked lightmaps under 1 ms — [Tencent](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Cao-NanoMesh/AdavanceRealtimeRendering_NanoMesh0810.pdf)
- [D] On a PS4, froxel fog costs 1 ms at High — [Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/volumetric-fog-in-unreal-engine); a volumetric cloud prototype under 2 ms — [Guerrilla Games](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-authoring-real-time-volumetric-cloudscapes-with-the-decima-engine)
- [M] Masked materials and vertex wind cut a Nanite forest from about 40 fps to 15.75 fps on an Immortalis-G925 phone — [Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mori-to-nanite-billions-of-triangles-on-mobile)
- [M] Half-resolution ray-traced shadows and reflections reach 40–46 fps at below 1080p on Arm's 2025 flagship GPU — [Arm, Vulkanised 2026](https://vulkan.org/user/pages/09.events/vulkanised-2026/Mobile-Ray-Tracing-Demystified-Iago-CalvoLista-Arm.pptx.pdf)
- [V] Crimson Desert's floor: Xbox Series S at 720p and 40 fps without ray tracing, ROG Xbox Ally at 720p and 40 fps with frame generation, Mac from M2 Pro with 16 GB; no phone version — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2026/03/10/crimson-desert-specs-for-pc-console-and-other-platforms-revealed), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimson_Desert)
- [D] Per-pass GPU timing through wgpu timestamp queries inside passes is generally unavailable on tile-based GPUs — [docs.rs, wgpu Features](https://docs.rs/wgpu/latest/wgpu/struct.Features.html)

### Inferences

**Sizing rule.** Steel Nomad Light at 3.7 megapixels costs about 100 ms per frame on an A17 Pro at peak (10 fps). Scaled linearly to 0.85 megapixels that is about 23 ms, so a renderer with roughly 40% of that benchmark's per-pixel cost lands near 9.5 ms on a cool device and near 13.5 ms after a 30% thermal loss. Linear scaling flatters the result because geometry, shadow and culling costs do not shrink with resolution, which is why the geometry passes below get the largest share.

**Proposed budget, upper-mid phone (A17 Pro or Snapdragon 8 Gen 3), 60 fps, throttled state.** Internal resolution about 1280×590 to 1408×650, temporal upscale of 1.8–2.0× per axis to the native panel. All values are GPU milliseconds in the sustained state; on a cool device each should measure about 30% lower.

| Pass | Budget (ms) | Basis and conditions |
|---|---|---|
| GPU culling, level-of-detail selection, hierarchical depth build (compute) | 0.5 | Tencent measured 0.38 ms on a 2022 flagship |
| Shadow cascades: two near cascades per frame, far cascade cached, coarse levels of detail, masked casters only in cascade 0 | 1.8 | Tencent's coarse-level and cache findings; no direct phone measurement |
| Depth pre-pass: all opaque, then masked geometry with a discard-only shader | 2.2 | Arm pre-pass and masked-cost findings |
| Main shading, depth-equal: terrain, trunks, rocks, leaves, grass; sun, baked or probe indirect light, per-vertex occlusion, leaf transmission | 3.6 | Tencent raster plus material passes were 2.5 ms; realistic materials cost more |
| Far field: impostor and hierarchical canopy layers | 0.6 | No measurement; mostly overdraw of large quads |
| Sky, aerial perspective and analytic height fog from lookup tables | 0.4 | Lookup-table model; table updates amortised |
| Clouds: 2D layers, or a 1/16-resolution ray march reprojected over 8–16 frames | 0.6 | PS4 prototype under 2 ms at higher quality |
| Water, when on screen: Gerstner waves, probe and sky reflection, depth fade | 0.6 | No measurement |
| Contact shadows and ambient occlusion at half internal resolution | 0.6 | A screen-space march costs 1.0–1.7 ms at 0.6–1.2 megapixels on newer Mali flagships |
| Particles and other blended effects | 0.4 | Keep coverage small |
| Temporal upscale (GSR 2 two-pass fragment class) | 1.2 | 0.9–1.1 ms measured on Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 |
| Bloom, tone mapping, grading at output resolution | 0.6 | No measurement |
| User interface and composition | 0.4 | No measurement |
| **Total** | **13.5** | Leaves about 3 ms (19%) for variance, driver and compositor overhead |

- CPU side: the same 16.6 ms applies. Keep draws in the low hundreds (Tencent's culled scene used 190) because the Metal path in today's Rust stack has no multi-draw with GPU culling.
- What 2025–2026 flagships (1.7–2.6 times the GPU) should buy, in order: internal resolution up to about 67–75% of native, a third shadow cascade every frame and longer shadow range, denser near-field vegetation, low-resolution volumetric clouds, a small froxel fog grid, then a dynamic voxel or probe global-illumination pass of about 2–3 ms.
- 8 GB M-series iPads and Macs: the same budget with 1.4–2× the GPU but far more display pixels; keep about 1–1.3 megapixels internal and accept a softer image, or use the Apple-only MetalFX path once its cost has been measured.
- Verification caveat for headless, agent-driven development: per-pass budgets cannot be checked from wgpu's in-pass timestamp queries on tile-based phone GPUs. Per-pass numbers need vendor profilers on real devices (Arm's Streamline is the tool Arm names), and thermal behaviour needs 20-minute runs on real phones. Simulator or desktop measurements do not transfer.

**AAA features to drop on phones at 60 fps**
- Hardware ray-traced global illumination, reflections and shadows.
- Virtual shadow maps and long-range dynamic shadows from all vegetation.
- Nanite-class virtual geometry as a requirement (keep it an optional flagship or desktop path).
- Lumen-class dynamic global illumination and screen-space global illumination on the upper-mid tier.
- Real-time volumetric clouds with self-shadowing, high-resolution froxel volumetrics and light shafts.
- Full-resolution screen-space reflections and high-quality ambient occlusion.
- Wind animation on every plant at every distance.
- Alpha-blended foliage, MSAA with alpha-to-coverage, heavy layered mist particles.
- Native-resolution rendering, 120 fps modes, and frame generation as a route to 60 fps.
- Unique high-resolution textures per species; tessellated or displaced terrain.

**Features to bake**
- Indirect light: probe or volume irradiance for several times of day, blended at runtime.
- Per-plant ambient occlusion and sky visibility, computed per vertex when the plant is generated.
- Terrain-scale occlusion and distant terrain shadowing (horizon data parameterised by sun angle).
- Far-field forest: impostor atlases with albedo, normal and depth so they can be relit, generated per species and growth stage on demand and cached, never all resident.
- Level-of-detail chains for every generated plant.
- Atmosphere lookup tables, cloud lighting, wind fields, water caustic patterns.

**Features to approximate**
- Leaf translucency: two-sided lighting with a transmission term.
- Shadows: two or three cascades with caching, contact shadows for grass, ambient occlusion alone beyond the last cascade.
- Clouds: lit 2D layers or a very low-resolution ray march; cloud shadows as a scrolling mask.
- Haze and fog: analytic height fog plus aerial perspective from the atmosphere tables.
- Water: Gerstner waves, detail normals, probe reflections, depth-based colour and foam.
- Dense forest to the horizon: three-dimensional plants to about 40 m, simplified meshes and cards to about 200 m, impostors to about 1 km, a baked canopy layer on the terrain beyond.
- Meadows: opaque blade geometry to about 30–50 m with density falloff, terrain colour matched to the grass beyond.
- Anti-aliasing and resolution: a temporal upscaler from 50–60% scale.
- Reflections: sky and reflection probes only.
- Surface detail on bark, rock and soil: normal maps instead of triangles.

**Distance from the Crimson Desert look, stated plainly**
- Crimson Desert's feature set cannot be run on a phone today. Its own minimum is a console with two to three times the peak GPU of an upper-mid phone, at 720p and 40 fps, with ray tracing off, and its Mac version needs 16 GB. Asking for 60 fps on a throttled phone adds roughly another factor of three to four. The gap is about one console generation plus the frame-rate and thermal handicaps.
- Vast hazy vistas: close. Atmosphere tables, aerial perspective and good far-field baking deliver most of this, and a phone screen hides the missing detail.
- Dense forest to the horizon: recognisably simplified. Distant canopy reads correctly; the mid-field (40–300 m) shows flatter trees, less parallax and no self-shadowing detail.
- Meadows of grass and flowers: good within a short radius, visibly thinning beyond 30–50 m.
- Red-rock desert: the easiest biome; mostly opaque geometry and texture work, limited by memory, not GPU.
- Coasts and rivers: clearly below. No ray-traced or screen-space reflections, simple waves, baked caustics.
- Close-up forest floor with ferns and streams: clearly below, and the hardest case. It combines layered masked geometry, wind, fine shadows and indirect light, which are exactly the items the phone budget removes. Expect two or three layers of ferns instead of many, baked instead of dynamic indirect light, and contact shadows instead of true fine shadows.
- Not possible on a phone today in any form: sustaining peak performance for a long session (expect a 25–40% loss, more on some Android flagships), keeping thousands of plant species resident in memory, and holding 60 fps with any ray-traced lighting on 2023–2024 chips.

### Gaps
- The budget is an engineering estimate. Apart from culling, the visibility and material passes, the upscaler and the screen-space march, no pass has a direct measurement on the target phones; shadow, water, cloud and far-field numbers are untested allocations.
- Tencent's measurements come from a Mali Immortalis-G715 at an unstated resolution; how an A17 Pro or Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 compares pass by pass is not sourced. No per-pass breakdown of any shipped mobile open-world game was found.
- The linear pixel-scaling argument from Steel Nomad Light is a rough calibration; the benchmark's own pass structure is not published on the cited page.
- The qualitative comparison with Crimson Desert is a judgment from its published platform targets, not from a technical breakdown of its renderer, which was not retrieved.
- No evidence was found for how procedural generation of thousands of species interacts with shader and pipeline counts on phones (pipeline compilation hitches, bindless limits on Metal); this needs its own investigation.
