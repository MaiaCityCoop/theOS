# Procedural generation of thousands of real plant species as 3D models through all growth stages, including harvestable fruit

Research date: 2026-11-19. Web research only.

**Legend used in every finding:** **[M]** = measured or documented fact (number, date, licence text, API count). **[V]** = vendor or author claim, not independently measured. **[R]** = reported by trade press or a search-result summary, primary page not opened by me. Everything in "Inferences" is my own reasoning. "Unverified recollection" = from my training knowledge, not re-checked in this session.

**Method limit (read this first):** the shared web-search budget ran out after roughly 45 searches. All later verification was done by opening known primary pages directly (project pages, repositories, dataset home pages, GBIF API). Several pages refused automated fetches (Epic documentation, unrealengine.com, CG Channel, SpeedTree store, POWO, Flora of North America, eFloras, USDA PLANTS, ACM DL, PMC). What could not be verified is listed under "Gaps" in each section, never in "Cited Findings".

**Bottom line in one paragraph (inference, supported below):** The credible route is a self-built, deterministic, developmental generator in Rust: one typed generator per growth form (about a dozen), driven by a compact per-species definition of a few KB whose numbers come from open trait data, floras read by a language model, and photo-derived leaf, bark, flower and fruit descriptors. Plants are generated on the device or a server per biome palette and cached by content hash; nothing like 3,000 x 6 x 4 baked assets can be shipped (about 110 GB by my arithmetic). Growth over time is a solved research problem for single plants (L-systems since 1988/1993, self-organising trees 2009, functional-structural models), but nobody has published or shipped a library of thousands of botanically distinct procedural species, and nobody has shown procedural plants without scanned parts and human tuning matching Megascans or SpeedTree hero assets at close range. Both of those are unproven and should be treated as the project's main research risks.

## 1. Algorithm families: which fit continuous growth and species breadth?

### Takeaway
Only developmental models (parametric and differential L-systems, the bud-automaton models of the AMAP lineage, self-organising tree models, functional-structural plant models) give the same individual plant at every age; shape-description generators and almost all learned generators give one finished tree. No method, classical or learned, has been demonstrated across thousands of species, so breadth must come from a typed parameter space (growth form, architectural model, organ generators), not from picking a different algorithm.

### Cited Findings

#### L-systems and the architectural-model taxonomy
- [M] "The Algorithmic Beauty of Plants" (Prusinkiewicz and Lindenmayer, 1990) is free as a full PDF and per chapter; chapter 3 is "Developmental models of herbaceous plants". Old, but still the reference for the formalism — [algorithmicbotany.org papers index](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/)
- [M] A parametric L-system with three productions and six constants (two contraction ratios, two branching angles, divergence angle 137.5 degrees, width decrease 0.707) reproduces four visibly different monopodial tree forms (ABOP figure 2.6). This is the smallest published example of "species = a handful of numbers plus fixed rules" — [ABOP chapter 2](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/abop/abop-ch2.pdf)
- [M] ABOP describes de Reffye's approach (the origin of the CIRAD AMAP models) as a bud automaton: at each clock signal a bud can do nothing, become a flower, become an internode with new apices, or die, "according to stochastic laws characteristic for each variety and each species", and states: "The basic types of developmental rules incorporated into this method correspond to the 23 different types of tree architectures identified by Hallé, Oldeman and Tomlinson" — [ABOP chapter 2, p. 55](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/abop/abop-ch2.pdf)
- [M] Barthélémy and Caraglio (Annals of Botany 99(3), 2007) describe architectural analysis as "a detailed, multilevel, comprehensive and dynamic approach to plant development"; its keywords include physiological age, phase change, annual shoot, meristem and branching (full text was paywalled for my fetch) — [doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcl260](https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcl260)
- [M] "Developmental Models of Herbaceous Plants for Computer Imagery Purposes" (Prusinkiewicz et al., SIGGRAPH 1988) uses L-systems for herbaceous plants, is "suitable for generating realistic plant images and animating developmental processes", and extends to leaves and flowers — [paper page](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/developmental.sig1988.html)
- [M] "Animation of Plant Development" (Prusinkiewicz, Hammel, Mjolsness, SIGGRAPH 1993) introduces a combined discrete/continuous model that merges L-system productions with differential equations, so development is animated like time-lapse photography; shown on three flowering herbs (Campanula rapunculoides, Lychnis coronaria, Hieracium umbellatum) — [paper page](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/animdev.sig93.html)
- [M] "Visual Models of Plants Interacting with Their Environment" (Mech and Prusinkiewicz, SIGGRAPH 1996) extends L-systems with two-way exchange between plant and environment: competition for light within and between trees, clonal plants colonising space, roots competing for water, branches limited by collisions — [paper page](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/enviro.sig96.html)
- [M] "The Use of Positional Information in the Modeling of Plants" (SIGGRAPH 2001) expresses local attributes of plant architecture as functions of position along the stems, which gives posture and gradual variation of features with few controls — [paper page](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/positional.sig2001.html)

#### Space colonisation and self-organising trees
- [M] "Modeling Trees with a Space Colonization Algorithm" (Runions, Lane, Prusinkiewicz, 2007) extends the open leaf-venation model to 3D; authors say it "generates surprisingly realistic tree structures" and that its parameters relate to tree characteristics recognised in landscaping. The abstract says nothing about growth over time — [paper page](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/colonization.egwnp2007.html)
- [M] "Self-organizing Tree Models for Image Synthesis" (Pałubicki et al., SIGGRAPH 2009) generates temperate-climate trees and shrubs on the hypothesis that form emerges from buds and branches competing for light or space, regulated by internal signalling; listed applications include "animations of tree development" and interactive pruning and bending — [paper page](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/selforg.sig2009.html)
- [M] "Synthetic Silviculture" (Makowski, Hädrich, Scheffczyk, Michels, Pirk, Pałubicki, ACM TOG 38(4), 2019): every plant is a 3D surface mesh; geometry is modelled efficiently by exploiting self-similarity within and between plants; captures growth, plant interactions, tropisms and competition for resources; parameter space covers nine distinct plant ecologies. Scale is stated as "up to 500K plants" on the project page — [project page](https://storage.googleapis.com/pirk.io/projects/synthetic_silviculture/index.html); the abstract on Google's publication page says "up to 90K plants" — [Google Research page](https://research.google/pubs/synthetic-silviculture-multi-scale-modeling-of-plant-ecosystems/). The two sources conflict; I could not open the PDF to settle it.
- [M] "Capturing and Animating the Morphogenesis of Polygonal Tree Models" (Pirk et al., SIGGRAPH Asia 2012) takes a static polygonal tree, builds a skeleton graph, and computes developmental stages that "approximate the tree's natural growth", guided by botanical growth models and allometric rules so the animation converges to the given model; all intermediate stages can be explored — [project page](https://storage.googleapis.com/pirk.io/projects/tree_growth/index.html)
- [M] "Interactive Modeling and Authoring of Climbing Plants" (Hädrich et al., Eurographics 2017) represents a plant as connected anisotropic particles with light sensitivity, wind and obstacle response; "runs in real-time and supports up to 20 plant instances with 25k branches in parallel" — [project page](https://storage.googleapis.com/pirk.io/projects/climbing_plants/index.html)
- [M] "Rhizomorph: The Coordinated Function of Shoots and Roots" (Li, Klein, Michels, Benes, Pirk, Pałubicki, ACM TOG 42(4), SIGGRAPH 2023) grows trees together with root systems, with a soil model of water and nutrient gradients and long-distance signalling between shoots and roots; taproot, heart and lateral root systems emerge from the rules; code is public — [Purdue page](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/publications/Li23ToG), [phys.org summary](https://phys.org/news/2023-07-root-problem-tree-digital-twin.html)
- [M] The published Rhizomorph growth code ships species growth parameters as "a 70-dimensional vector per species" (as described by a 2025 paper that used it for training data) — [arXiv 2502.04762 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04762v1)
- [M] Later work in the same line: Ecoclimates (2022), Procedural Urban Forestry (2022), plant wilting simulation (SIGGRAPH Asia 2023), Scintilla wildfire vegetation (2024), and "Interactive Invigoration: Volumetric Modeling of Trees with Strands" (SIGGRAPH 2024), which replaces cylinders by fixed-size volumetric strands to capture lateral development — [publication list](https://storage.googleapis.com/pirk.io/index.html), [strands page](https://storage.googleapis.com/pirk.io/projects/invigoration/index.html)

#### Functional-structural plant models (all simulate growth over time)
- [M] Helios Plant Architecture plug-in (UC Davis; C++ with Python API "PyHelios"; LGPL-2.1): library of crops (bean, maize, rice, soybean, tomato, wheat, cucumber), trees (almond, apple, walnut, pistachio, olive), grapevines on two trellis types, and weeds (bindweed, cheeseweed, puncturevine). A plant is built "with a given age"; `advanceTime()` grows it by days; phenology moves through dormancy break, flower initiation, flower opening, fruit set, fruit maturity and senescence by per-species day thresholds; perennials cycle through winter dormancy. Each geometry update is costly, so the documentation recommends large time steps. No dependencies for the plug-in itself; CPU parallelism with OpenMP — [Helios plant architecture documentation](https://plantsimulationlab.github.io/Helios/_plant_architecture_doc.html), [Helios repository](https://github.com/PlantSimulationLab/Helios)
- [M] L-Py (OpenAlea) mixes L-systems with Python, is released under CeCILL (GPL-compatible; repository badge says GPL-3.0), and "can be integrated as a simple growth simulation module into more complex computational pipelines" — [L-Py repository](https://github.com/openalea/lpy)
- [M] GroIMP is licensed GPLv3 — [GroIMP repository](https://gitlab.com/grogra/groimp)
- [M] AMAPstudio is "a software suite for plants architecture modelling"; "Some parts are distributed with a free license"; its start page was last modified 2021-12-20; AmapSim produced the maritime pine stands shown at ages 7, 19 and 60 — [AMAPstudio](https://amapstudio.cirad.fr/)
- [M] CPlantBox describes plant geometry by organs (roots, stems, leaves) that "evolve over time"; C++ core with Python wheels; GPL-3.0; Linux and macOS only — [CPlantBox repository](https://github.com/Plant-Root-Soil-Interactions-Modelling/CPlantBox)
- [M] Fruit-crop FSPMs exist for peach (L-PEACH, 2005), apple (MAppleT, 2008) and kiwifruit vine (2011) — [algorithmicbotany.org papers index](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/)

#### Learned and inverse methods, 2022 to 2026
- [M] DeepTree (Zhou, Li, Benes, Fei, Pirk; IEEE TVCG 2023): a network predicts local branch growth from a single node and is queried repeatedly from the root outward; "situated latents" also encode responses such as growing around obstacles; evaluated against procedurally generated trees — [project page](https://storage.googleapis.com/pirk.io/projects/deep_tree/index.html), [arXiv 2305.05153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05153v1)
- [M] Latent L-systems (Lee, Li, Benes; ACM TOG 43(1), article 7): a transformer trained on a large set of 3D tree models encoded as L-strings generates new L-strings that are converted to trees — [Purdue page](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/publications/Lee_2023_ToG)
- [M] Tree-D Fusion (ECCV 2024): 600,000 "simulation-ready" tree models, each tied to a real street tree in Google's Auto Arborist imagery (23 North American cities, over 300 genus categories); two diffusion models reconstruct an envelope, then a space colonisation algorithm fills it with branches and leaves — [arXiv 2407.10330](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.10330v1)
- [M] TreeStructor (IEEE TGRS vol. 63, published 7 April 2025) reconstructs forests from point clouds by retrieving best-fitting meshes from a set of predefined branch parts; about 6% better than the prior state of the art; cannot detect dead trees, bushes or understory debris — [Purdue page](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/publications/Zhou25TRGS), [Purdue news, Jan 2026](https://ag.purdue.edu/news/2026/01/ai-helps-find-trees-in-a-forest-researchers-achieve-3d-forest-reconstruction-from-remote-sensing-data.html)
- [M] "Autoregressive Generation of Static and Growing Trees" (Wang, Zhang, Klein, Michels, Yan, Wonka; arXiv, 7 Feb 2025) is the one learned method I found that generates growth: ten growth stages are tokenised and concatenated into one sequence "from its initial sprout to full maturity". Training data came from the Rhizomorph simulator; six species (elm, Vitellaria, hickory, shadbush, spruce, tulip tree); trees capped at 200 to 1,000 branches; growth results are shown only qualitatively; the model outputs the skeleton and "leaves are added procedurally" — [arXiv 2502.04762](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04762v1)
- [M] TreesFormer (under review, 2026) predicts parametric L-system grammars from one orthophoto plus a surface model, trained only on synthetic procedurally generated trees. Its benchmark table gives F1 scores of 0.81 (TreesFormer), 0.76 (TreeON), 0.58 (SVDTree), 0.57 (Latent L-Systems) and 0.01 (Tree-D Fusion) — [project page](https://angelikigram.github.io/treesformer/)
- [M] CropCraft (arXiv Nov 2024, revised Jan 2026, 3DV 2026 oral) optimises a parametric plant-morphology model against depth from a neural radiance field (inverse procedural modelling) for crops in field images — [arXiv 2411.09693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09693)
- [M] FloraForge (arXiv, 11 Dec 2025) uses a language model to co-design Python generators for parametric plant geometry (hierarchical B-spline surfaces) with a domain expert refining through natural language; shown on maize, soybean and mung bean, fitted to point clouds — [arXiv 2512.11925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11925)
- [M] PlantDreamer (arXiv May 2025, ICCV 2025 workshop) is text-to-3D with Gaussian splats, a depth ControlNet and a fine-tuned LoRA, initialised from L-system meshes; authors report higher fidelity than general text-to-3D models — [arXiv 2505.15528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15528)
- [M] GrowSplat (arXiv May 2025, IEEE CASE 2025) builds temporal "digital twins" by registering a sequence of Gaussian-splat reconstructions of a real plant captured over time (sequoia and quinoa sequences); it reconstructs, it does not generate — [arXiv 2505.10923](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.10923v2)
- [M] "Real-Time GPU Tree Generation" (Kuth et al., High-Performance Graphics 2025) is a procedural model built to be evaluated on the GPU every frame, with seasonal details such as blossoms, fruits and snow (details in section 6) — [publication record](https://forschung.hs-coburg.de/en/publikationen/5388-real-time-gpu-tree-generation)

### Inferences
- Growth-over-time support by family (my classification from the sources above):

| Family | Same individual at every age? | Evidence of species breadth | Use for this project |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parametric / differential L-systems (1988 to 2001) | Yes, by construction | Dozens of hand-written models; herbs, flowers, trees | Core formalism for herbs, inflorescences, annuals |
| Bud automaton with architectural models (de Reffye / AMAP lineage) | Yes | "23 architectures" taxonomy; per-species stochastic laws | Best taxonomy for typing woody species |
| Self-organising trees (2009), Synthetic Silviculture (2019), Rhizomorph (2023) | Yes; light and space competition | Tens of tuned species; 70 parameters per species in Rhizomorph | Core for trees and shrubs in a forest (shade response matters in a food forest) |
| Functional-structural models (Helios, GroIMP, L-Py, CPlantBox) | Yes, with phenology and fruit | Roughly 20 crops in Helios; one species per research paper elsewhere | Reference designs for phenology, flower-to-fruit and organ prototypes |
| Shape generators (space colonisation as used in tools, Weber–Penn style) | No; a finished crown is described | Presets only | Fast static trees; growth has to be faked |
| Learned generators (DeepTree, Latent L-systems, Tree-D Fusion, TreesFormer) | No, except one 10-stage sequence model with qualitative results | Trees only; trained on procedural output | Not a basis; possibly useful later for fitting parameters to photos |
| Gaussian-splat or diffusion plants | No structure, no growth, no harvest semantics | Single plants | Not usable for a growing, harvestable plant |

- Learned tree generators are trained on the output of procedural simulators, so they cannot exceed the botanical variety of those simulators. They add speed and image conditioning, not new species knowledge.
- A species definition can be very small. Published examples: six constants (ABOP figure 2.6), 70 numbers (Rhizomorph). A definition of a few hundred numbers plus curves is consistent with the literature.
- For a food forest, competition for light matters to gameplay (canopy, understory, shade tolerance). That argues for the self-organising family for woody plants, because shade response is built in instead of bolted on.
- Unverified recollection: Weber and Penn (1995) define a tree by roughly 50 geometric parameters per species with no time axis; the Hallé–Oldeman models are named after botanists (for example Corner for unbranched palms, Tomlinson for basally suckering plants such as banana, McClure for bamboo, Rauh, Troll, Massart, Leeuwenberg) and are defined by growth rhythm, branching pattern, axis orientation and position of flowering; later work added reiteration and physiological age. I could not open a primary source for these details in this session.

## 2. Tools as benchmarks: growth and season features, headless use, licences

### Takeaway
No existing tool can be embedded in a shipped Rust game as an on-device generator, and none is documented to produce continuous growth for games. The commercial tools are benchmarks and, at most, build-time helpers; the open-source pieces that run headless (Helios, Blender as a module, Houdini Engine, EZ-Tree and its Rust port, the MIT-licensed MTree C++ core) are either scientific, static, or toy-level.

- [M] Other Rust crates found are L-system string rewriters without a mesher: `rusty-systems` 5.0.0 (MIT, context-free and stochastic L-systems) — [rusty-systems](https://lib.rs/crates/rusty-systems); a Bevy L-system demo exists as `pi-game/sequoia` — [sequoia](https://github.com/pi-game/sequoia)

#### Infinigen (Princeton)
- [M] BSD-3-Clause since 26 June 2023 (earlier GPL); about 7.5k stars; built on Blender — [Infinigen repository](https://github.com/princeton-vl/infinigen), [arXiv 2306.09310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09310)
- [M] Plant coverage in the paper: one tree system (random walks plus space colonisation) for trees, bushes and some cacti with 26 interpretable parameters across three generators; 30 small-plant generators with 258 parameters. Named generators with parameter counts: flower 8, flower stem 15, maple leaf 7, pine 4, broadleaf 14, ginkgo 11, pinecone 13, fern 15, grass tuft 10, mushroom 17, seaweed 7, kelp 14, cactus spikes 6, globular cactus 5, columnar cactus 9, prickly pear 5, plus six coral generators. Seasons exist only as a leaf-colour distribution ("more yellow and red colors for autumn"); no growth over time is described — [Infinigen paper, HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.09310)
- [M] Cost: about 3.5 hours wall time for a pair of 1080p images of one scene (about 1 hour GPU), about 24 GB memory, average scene 16 million polygons, measured on two Xeon Silver 4114 CPUs and one NVIDIA GPU — [Infinigen paper, HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.09310)

## 3. Growth forms beyond trees; published models for flowers, leaves and fruit

### Takeaway
Published procedural models exist for nearly every growth form a food forest needs, but they are scattered across separate papers and tools, each covering one form; none of the game or film tools covers them all with growth. Leaves (shape, venation) and flowers (inflorescence structure, pigment patterns) are well modelled; fruit growth is modelled physiologically, but I found no general published model of fruit shape and ripening colour.

### Cited Findings
- [M] Herbaceous perennials and annual herbs: L-system developmental models with flowering (1988) and continuous-time animation of three herb species (1993) — [1988 paper](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/developmental.sig1988.html), [1993 paper](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/animdev.sig93.html)
- [M] Annual vegetables, cereals and legumes with growth: Helios library includes bean, maize, rice, soybean, tomato, wheat and cucumber, plus weeds — [Helios documentation](https://plantsimulationlab.github.io/Helios/_plant_architecture_doc.html); FloraForge shows maize, soybean and mung bean as parametric B-spline plants — [arXiv 2512.11925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11925)
- [M] Grasses: Infinigen has a grass-tuft generator (10 parameters) and scatters dense layers of small assets for grassland and forest floor — [Infinigen paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.09310)
- [M] Vines and climbers: real-time particle-based climbing plants (2017) — [project page](https://storage.googleapis.com/pirk.io/projects/climbing_plants/index.html); a vine generator in SpeedTree 10 — [SpeedTree "What's new"](https://docs.unity3d.com/speedtree-modeler/manual/whats-new.html); grapevines on two trellis systems in Helios — [Helios documentation](https://plantsimulationlab.github.io/Helios/_plant_architecture_doc.html); a functional-structural kiwifruit vine model (2011) — [papers index](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/)
- [M] Shrubs and berries: the self-organising model covers "trees and shrubs" — [paper page](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/selforg.sig2009.html); a 2023 paper models the diversity of woodland strawberry inflorescences — [papers index](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/)
- [M] Ground covers and clonal plants: a 3D architectural model of white clover (2000) — [papers index](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/); clonal plants colonising space in the 1996 environment-interaction framework — [paper page](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/enviro.sig96.html)
- [M] Roots and root crops: Rhizomorph grows shoots and roots together with emergent taproot, heart and lateral systems — [Purdue page](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/publications/Li23ToG); CPlantBox models roots, stems and leaves over time — [CPlantBox](https://github.com/Plant-Root-Soil-Interactions-Modelling/CPlantBox)
- [M] Palms and bamboo: present as presets in Sapling Tree Gen 1.0.0 (static) — [Blender Extensions](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/sapling-tree-gen/); oil palm was modelled by CIRAD's stochastic bud automaton by 1990 — [ABOP chapter 2](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/abop/abop-ch2.pdf); SpeedTree has frond generators with seasonal drop, curl and fold — [SpeedTree "What's new"](https://docs.unity3d.com/speedtree-modeler/manual/whats-new.html)
- [M] Cacti, ferns, fungi, aquatic plants: Infinigen generators for globular, columnar and prickly-pear cacti, fern, mushroom, seaweed and kelp; lichen and moss exist as materials — [Infinigen paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.09310)
- [M] Leaf shape: one developmental program generates simple, lobed and compound leaves and margin serrations, generalised from three reference species (Runions, Tsiantis, Prusinkiewicz, 2017) — [paper page](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/leaves2017.html); earlier models for lobed leaves (2003) and compound leaves (1992) — [papers index](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/)
- [M] Leaf venation: veins grow toward auxin sources while the blade grows; open (tree-like) and closed (looped) patterns; "can simulate many types of venation patterns" (2005) — [paper page](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/venation.sig2005.html); a 2026 paper shows reticulate venation in Pilea is a Voronoi diagram — [papers index](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/)
- [M] Flowers: "Modeling Dense Inflorescences" (SIGGRAPH 2016) places florets first and grows the branching structure to them, for corymbs and umbels, and animates their development — [paper page](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/inflorescence.sig16.html); "Modeling Flower Pigmentation Patterns" (SIGGRAPH Asia 2021) combines vascular patterning, positional information, reaction-diffusion and random patterns on flower geometry — [paper page](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/pigmentation_patterns.sigasia2021.html); phyllotaxis models for flower heads (1992, 2006, 2021, 2022) — [papers index](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/)
- [M] Fruit: a 3D volumetric fruit model with embedded vasculature and water and dry-matter transport for tomato and nectarine, limited to the cell-expansion phase (2016) — [paper page](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/mcieslak.fps2016.html); in Helios, flowers and fruit are prototype functions or OBJ models attached to the inflorescence of each phytomer, and flowers turn into fruit through fruit-set and maturity thresholds — [Helios documentation](https://plantsimulationlab.github.io/Helios/_plant_architecture_doc.html); blossoms and fruits as seasonal detail in real-time GPU tree generation (2025) — [publication record](https://forschung.hs-coburg.de/en/publikationen/5388-real-time-gpu-tree-generation)
- [M] Senescence-related: a physically inspired simulation of plant wilting (SIGGRAPH Asia 2023) — [publication list](https://storage.googleapis.com/pirk.io/index.html); leaf textures in Helios can change as leaves age — [Helios documentation](https://plantsimulationlab.github.io/Helios/_plant_architecture_doc.html)

### Inferences
- Coverage needs about a dozen typed generators, each with its own growth logic, sharing organ sub-generators (leaf, flower, fruit, bark): (1) broadleaf trees and shrubs, (2) conifers, (3) palms, cycads and tree ferns (crown of fronds on an unbranched stem), (4) grasses, cereals and bamboo (tillering culms), (5) rosette herbs and leafy vegetables, (6) erect herbs with inflorescences, (7) vines and climbers (support seeking), (8) creeping and stoloniferous ground covers, (9) giant herbs such as banana and ginger, (10) cacti and succulents, (11) ferns, (12) root and tuber crops (above-ground part plus a harvest organ revealed on digging), (13) fungal fruiting bodies.
- The architectural-model taxonomy is the natural "type" field of a woody species definition; for herbs the inflorescence type and leaf arrangement play the same role.
- Fruit ripening colour and fruit shape have to be designed here: a profile-curve or superellipsoid shape family, a growth curve, and a colour ramp (unripe, ripe, overripe) taken from photographs per species. This is my proposal, not a published model.
- Banana, bamboo and palm growth (as opposed to static presets) are the forms with the least published procedural work that I could verify; expect to design these from botany texts.

### Gaps
- No published procedural growth model verified for banana, bamboo clumps, or mushrooms over time.
- No general model of fruit ripening colour found (search budget ran out before this could be searched).
- Xfrog's component-based method (Lintermann and Deussen), relevant for flowers and herbs, could not be opened.

## 4. Parameter sources for thousands of species

### Takeaway
Open data can fill size, growth form, phenology months, climate tolerance and edibility for thousands of species, but not branching architecture, and coverage is sparse: a 2020s merge of four big trait databases was 1.5% complete. The best edible-plant databases are non-commercial or share-alike, so a commercial game needs either permission or to rebuild those facts from openly licensed sources and floras with a language model, a route one 2025 study measured at an F1 score above 75%.

### Inferences
- What each kind of source can drive in a generator: Tallo and BAAD give height-to-crown and height-to-stem curves (trees only); TRY, BIEN, GIFT and AusTraits give maximum height, growth form, woodiness, leaf size, seed mass; PFAF-type databases give habit, height and width, deciduousness and the monthly calendar of leaf, flower and seed; Ecocrop gives climate tolerance and crop cycle length for biome assignment; the taxonomy backbones give the keys that join everything and allow fallback to genus or family values.
- Nothing in these databases gives branching angles, phyllotaxis, architectural model, leaf outline, inflorescence type or fruit shape as numbers. Those must come from flora descriptions read by a language model, from photographs, or from family-level defaults. This is the largest data gap in the whole plan.
- Licence split for a commercial game: usable with attribution (Tallo, BAAD, TRY public data, AusTraits, WCVP, World Flora Online, GBIF); share-alike that may attach to a derived species database (Permapeople); non-commercial and therefore unusable without a licence (PFAF, Useful Temperate and Tropical Plants); all rights reserved (Agroforestree). Whether extracted facts are free of database rights is a legal question I cannot settle; it needs counsel.
- A measured F1 of about 75% for automatic trait extraction means roughly a quarter of extracted values are wrong or missing. For a game this is tolerable only with range checks against genus and family priors and a visual review step, even if that review is done by agents.
- Expect a three-level fallback in the species definition: measured value for the species, otherwise genus median, otherwise growth-form default, with the provenance recorded per parameter.

### Gaps
- USDA PLANTS (server timeout), Flora of North America and eFloras (403) and POWO (403): licences, structure and bulk access not verified. Unverified recollection: USDA PLANTS publishes a "characteristics" table (mature height, growth rate, bloom period, fruit period, flower and fruit colour) for a few thousand species and US federal data are generally public domain; Flora of North America runs on a semantic wiki with parsed characters.
- BIEN and GIFT data licences: not found on the pages opened.
- Number of species in Ecocrop and the status of FAO's own site: not verified (the FAO page renders client-side).
- Phenology networks (for example national phenology networks) and fruit or flower colour datasets were not researched; search budget exhausted.
- How many of the roughly 8,000 edible species have height, growth form and phenology in openly licensed sources: nobody appears to have measured this; it should be the first data task of the prototype.

## 5. Appearance sources without artists

### Takeaway
There is plenty of legally usable imagery (about 54 million plant herbarium records with images under CC0 or CC BY, and about 12 million iNaturalist plant observations with images under CC0 or CC BY), and open single-image material estimators exist, but none of them outputs leaf translucency, their best versions carry research-only or use-restricted licences, and nobody has published a pipeline that turns such images into game-ready plant materials without human review.

### Cited Findings
- [M] GBIF, queried 2026-11-19: 64,045,420 preserved plant specimen records have a still image; by record licence CC BY 4.0 32,819,579 (51.2%), CC0 21,599,239 (33.7%), CC BY-NC 4.0 9,626,602 (15.0%) — [GBIF API query](https://api.gbif.org/v1/occurrence/search?kingdomKey=6&basisOfRecord=PRESERVED_SPECIMEN&mediaType=StillImage&limit=0&facet=license)

- [M] Procedural plant shaders exist in open code: Infinigen's bark materials simulate "bark expansion and fracturing with Voronoi and Perlin noise displacements" (9 parameters; birch 5), and its leafy materials set translucency and roughness "to simulate light passing through waxy leaves" — [Infinigen paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.09310)
- [M] Translucency in a shipped game: Crysis approximated leaf subsurface scattering with an artist-made subsurface texture that "can be relatively low-res (for example, 128 x 128)", multiplied into a view- and light-dependent back-lighting term (2007, old but still the common pattern) — [GPU Gems 3, chapter 16](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-16-vegetation-procedural-animation-and-shading-crysis)
- [M] The PROSPECT leaf model (R package version 2.0.1; variants PROSPECT-D and PROSPECT-PRO) simulates leaf directional-hemispherical reflectance and transmittance from biochemical and biophysical leaf traits — [prospect package](https://jbferet.gitlab.io/prospect/)
- [R] The full Megascans library is free again on Fab (November 2026) under the Fab Standard License, which allows commercial projects and forbids standalone redistribution — [Digital Production, 13 Nov 2026](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/11/13/quixel-megascans-free-again)

### Inferences
- A workable artist-free leaf pipeline, as a proposal: take leaf outline and size from herbarium sheets (they include rulers; outlines are reliable, colours are not, because specimens are dried and faded), take colour from living-plant photos with CC0 or CC BY licences, generate venation and the normal and height maps procedurally (2005 venation model), and derive translucency from thickness heuristics or a physical leaf model such as PROSPECT instead of from the photo.
- None of the single-image estimators listed outputs transmission. Leaf translucency, the single most visible cue in back-lit foliage, therefore has no off-the-shelf estimator. It must be modelled.
- Storing only derived descriptors (outline coefficients, colour palettes, bark class and statistics) rather than the images avoids redistributing photos, and keeps a species definition small. Whether descriptors derived from CC BY images still require attribution is a legal question; keeping an attribution ledger per species is cheap insurance.
- Bark is easier than leaves: classify each species into one of perhaps 10 to 20 bark archetypes (smooth, furrowed, plated, peeling and so on) from photos and tune a procedural bark shader by colour and scale statistics. Bark datasets are small (23 species in BarkNet), so classification has to lean on iNaturalist photos tagged by species.
- The non-commercial share of iNaturalist (81% of plant observations) is the main reason the usable photo pool is about 12 million rather than 64 million observations.

### Gaps
- iDigBio licences and counts, Bark-101 (certificate error), and Pl@ntNet-300K's licence mix were not verified.
- Licences of RGB↔X weights and CHORD weights were not opened; the Marigold RAIL++-M and Open RAIL-M terms need reading before commercial use.
- No measurement found of how well any 2024 to 2026 material estimator works on leaves or bark photographs taken outdoors.
- No per-species count of usable leaf scans or organ-tagged photos for the edible species list; this has to be measured.

## 6. Runtime representation, harvestable fruit, wind, LODs and impostors, and the storage arithmetic

### Takeaway
Shipping tools and libraries represent age as separate assets (seedling, sapling, mature) with shader-driven seasons; continuous growth exists only in simulators that rebuild geometry and in one 2025 research system that regenerates trees on a desktop GPU every frame. Baking 3,000 species x 6 stages x 4 seasons comes to roughly 110 GB by my arithmetic, so the only shippable design is a compact species definition (a few KB each, about 10 MB for 3,000) with generation per biome palette and a content-addressed cache; the time this takes on a phone is unmeasured.

### Cited Findings

#### How age and season are stored today
- [M] Quixel's Megascans tree pack for European beech has 17 assets split by age class: 8 forest trees, 1 field tree, 4 saplings, 4 seedlings; a dynamic shader gives "procedural controls over seasons, health, wind"; unique LODs per tree; "Simple and Pivot Painter wind implementations" — [Fab listing](https://fab.com/s/5db64af5b801)
- [M] The Grove stores growth as one object per simulated year with shape-key blending inside each year; topology changes between years — [Animate growth and wind](https://www.thegrove3d.com/learn/animate-growth-and-wind/)
- [M] Helios regenerates scene geometry on each `advanceTime()` call "at substantial cost" — [Helios documentation](https://plantsimulationlab.github.io/Helios/_plant_architecture_doc.html)
- [M] A static mature mesh can be given a growth history after the fact: Pirk et al. (2012) derive all intermediate developmental stages from a polygonal tree — [project page](https://storage.googleapis.com/pirk.io/projects/tree_growth/index.html)

#### Generating on the GPU instead of storing
- [M] "Real-Time GPU Tree Generation" (Kuth, Oberberger, Faber, Seyedmasih, Baumeister, Meyer, Pfeifer; High-Performance Graphics, 20 June 2025): using GPU work graphs with mesh nodes, the system renders "gigabytes of tree geometry" each frame "from kilobytes of generation code", with continuous frame-specific LOD and tessellation, detailed animation and seasonal details such as blossoms, fruits and snow; generating and rendering the unique trees of the test scene to the G-buffer took 3.13 ms on an AMD Radeon RX 7900 XTX — [publication record](https://forschung.hs-coburg.de/en/publikationen/5388-real-time-gpu-tree-generation)
- [R] The same paper is summarised as limiting generation to 10 ms per frame and reaching 12 to 50 frames per second for 500,000 trees; the 2024 predecessor added 79,710 instances in 3.74 ms on the same GPU — [GPUOpen PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/Real-Time_GPU_Tree_Generation.pdf), [2024 paper page](https://coburggraphicslab.github.io/publication/Kuth24RPG.html)

#### Instanced parts, fruit and flowers
- [R] Unreal 5.7 Nanite Foliage uses Nanite Assemblies (instanced parts) "to cut storage and rendering costs" and Nanite Skinning for wind; the vegetation editor outputs "Nanite skeletal assemblies" — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/unreal-engine-5-7-is-now-availabe)
- [M] Infinigen builds foliage by instancing: leaves are placed on the nodes of a twig skeleton, and each tree reuses five twig templates through instancing — [Infinigen paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.09310)
- [M] Helios attaches flowers and fruit as prototype objects to each phytomer's inflorescence and switches flower to fruit by phenology thresholds — [Helios documentation](https://plantsimulationlab.github.io/Helios/_plant_architecture_doc.html)

#### Wind data
- [M] Crysis (2007): one painted RGB colour per vertex plus alpha: red is stiffness of leaf edges, green is per-leaf phase variation, blue is overall leaf stiffness, alpha is precomputed ambient occlusion; main bending scales with normalised height and is length-corrected; detail bending sums four smoothed triangle waves; wind is computed per instance on the CPU — [GPU Gems 3, chapter 16](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-16-vegetation-procedural-animation-and-shading-crysis)
- [M] Tree It writes wind data as vertex colours into its FBX export — [Tree It](http://www.evolved-software.com/treeit/treeit); Megaplants use "Dynamic Wind, which uses skeletal meshes" — [Fab listing](https://www.fab.com/listings/f32d5cdc-de95-4ec1-a0b1-fc501ea5e0c5); Sapling Tree Gen offers wind with or without bones — [Blender Extensions](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/sapling-tree-gen/); PlantFactory and Megascans trees export Pivot Painter wind data — [Bentley page](https://www.bentley.com/software/e-on-software-free-downloads/), [Fab listing](https://fab.com/s/5db64af5b801)

#### LOD chains and impostors
- [M] EZ-Tree's default LOD chain cuts triangles to about 40% and 20% — [EZ-Tree repository](https://github.com/dgreenheck/ez-tree); meshoptimizer (MIT) provides topology-preserving, attribute-aware and "sloppy" simplifiers for automatic LODs — [meshoptimizer repository](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer)
- [M] Octahedral impostors blend the nearest three captured views and so avoid popping; hemi-octahedral impostors double precision but are wrong from below; a typical bake uses 16 frames per axis, 256 views; baked maps are albedo plus alpha, specular plus smoothness, normals plus depth, emission plus occlusion; impostors are "not a substitute for 'real meshes'" and should extend the last LOD; skinned meshes are not supported — [Amplify Impostors manual](https://wiki.amplify.pt/index.php?title=Unity_Products:Amplify_Impostors/Manual)

#### Base rates for the arithmetic
- [M] meshoptimizer: index compression typically 1 to 1.2 bytes per triangle; vertex compression typically 2 to 4 times on already quantised data; decoding 3 to 6 GB/s on desktop CPUs; its example quantised layout (half-float position, 10-10-10 normal, half-float UV) adds up to 14 bytes per vertex — [meshoptimizer repository](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer)
- [M] ASTC texture compression spans 0.89 to 8 bits per pixel; 6x6 blocks are 3.56 bits per pixel; the reference encoder is Apache-2.0 — [astc-encoder repository](https://github.com/ARM-software/astc-encoder)
- [M] Published sizes of species parameter sets: 70 numbers (Rhizomorph) — [arXiv 2502.04762](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04762v1); 28 top-level parameters (EZ-Tree) — [EZ-Tree repository](https://github.com/dgreenheck/ez-tree); 26 for Infinigen's tree system — [Infinigen paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.09310); The Grove's presets are plain-text JSON files — [technical overview](https://www.thegrove3d.com/learn/technical-overview/)

### Inferences

**Storage arithmetic for baking everything (assumptions stated; all numbers are mine).**
- States: 3,000 species x 6 stages x 4 seasons = 72,000 plant states, one individual each, no variation.
- Mesh per state, phone-class average across growth forms: 15,000 triangles and 10,000 vertices at LOD0, 20 bytes per vertex (14 as in the meshoptimizer example plus 6 for wind and growth attributes), 16-bit indices. LOD0 raw = 200,000 + 90,000 = 290,000 bytes. A chain at 100%, 40%, 20% and 10% is 1.7 times that: 0.49 MB raw. Compressed (vertices divided by 3, indices 1.1 bytes per triangle): about 0.14 MB. Times 72,000 = about 10 GB on disk, about 35 GB decoded.
- Octahedral impostor per state: two 1024 x 1024 textures (colour plus alpha, normal plus depth) at 3.56 bits per pixel with mip chain = 2 x 0.62 MB = 1.24 MB. Times 72,000 = about 90 GB. At 512 x 512 it is about 22 GB; at 2048 x 2048 about 358 GB. Note that a 1024 atlas with 16 x 16 views gives only 64 x 64 pixels per view.
- Organ and bark textures per species if seasons are done in the shader: six 1024 x 1024 maps = 3.7 MB; times 3,000 = about 11 GB. With a separate leaf atlas per season it is about 28 GB.
- Total: about 110 GB for one individual per state with 1024 impostors. Tree-heavy content at higher polygon counts or several individuals per state multiplies this. This cannot ship on a phone and is impractical on desktop.

**What avoids shipping it.**
- Species definition: about 300 scalars, 20 short curves, a monthly phenology calendar and colour palettes fit in roughly 3 KB binary; 3,000 species is about 10 MB. This matches the scale of published parameter sets (28 to 70 numbers for trees alone).
- Seasons should not multiply geometry: for most plants the woody skeleton is the same in all four seasons and only the instance set (leaves, flowers, fruit) and shader parameters change. Stages should not multiply geometry either if growth is a continuous parameter (below). That reduces 72,000 baked states to one skeleton per individual plus instance lists.
- Per-biome palette of 50 to 150 species, generated on first use (on device or on a server) and cached under a hash of generator version, species definition, age bucket, season, seed and LOD. Estimated resident GPU memory for 100 species with three age buckets each: about 0.9 GB with 1024 textures and impostors, about 0.34 GB with 512 textures and impostors (1.5 MB meshes + 0.9 MB textures + 0.9 MB impostors per species). The second figure is plausible on an 8 GB phone; the first is not comfortable.
- A server-built pack for one biome of 100 species with all seasons at 512 resolution is about 0.5 GB by the same arithmetic, so "generate on a server, download per biome" is a realistic fallback if phones generate too slowly.

**Continuous growth without regenerating every frame (design proposal, unproven in any shipped game I could find).**
- Generate the plant's full skeleton up to its maximum age once, with a birth time and growth duration stored per branch and per organ slot. Mesh it with per-vertex attributes: branch birth time, normalised position along the branch, and a pivot or parent reference. The vertex shader scales length and radius by a growth curve of (plant age minus birth time); branches not yet born collapse to zero and are culled. Leaves, flowers and fruit are instances with their own birth and death times.
- Re-mesh only when the plant crosses an age bucket (for example six buckets), with a dithered cross-fade, to restore sensible triangle density. This is the "discrete stages with cross-fade" approach as a safety net under the continuous one.
- The same per-branch hierarchy data serves wind (the Crysis three-channel scheme, or pivot-based hierarchical bending). A generator knows the hierarchy, so it can emit wind attributes at no extra authoring cost. Bone-based wind as in Unreal's Dynamic Wind is heavier and impostors cannot be skinned, so vertex-shader wind is the fit for phones.
- Harvestable fruit: the generator emits attachment slots (branch, position, orientation, set date, ripening duration). Fruit and flowers render as instanced meshes per species with per-instance scale and a ripeness colour ramp. Harvest state is a bit set per plant; picking removes the instance and starts a regrowth timer. Helios (flower-to-fruit thresholds on phytomer slots) and Unreal's assemblies (instanced parts) are the closest published precedents; neither is a game harvest system.
- Impostors per species, stage and season have to be baked at runtime or on a server from the generated mesh, because they cannot be shipped (90 GB). Baking 256 small views per state is a few hundred draw calls; the cost on a phone GPU is unmeasured.

**Limits of the GPU-generation precedent.** The 2025 work-graph system shows that per-frame generation from kilobytes of code is possible on a high-end desktop GPU. It depends on GPU work graphs with mesh nodes, a recent desktop Direct3D feature; I know of no equivalent on Metal, mobile Vulkan or wgpu (unverified recollection). Treat it as proof of the principle, not as a portable technique.

### Gaps
- No measured generation time for a growth-capable tree generator on a phone-class CPU or GPU. This is the number the prototype must produce first.
- No shipped game found that documents continuous vertex-shader growth, or per-species runtime generation at this scale; I could not search for game post-mortems after the search budget ran out.
- Epic's Pivot Painter and Nanite Foliage documentation could not be opened; details of what data they store are from secondary sources only.
- App-store size limits and typical phone GPU memory budgets were not looked up; the "0.34 GB is plausible" judgement is mine.

## 7. Evidence on quality and on library scale

### Takeaway
I found no evidence that procedural plants made without scanned parts and without human tuning reach the close-up realism of Megascans or SpeedTree hero assets, and no evidence that anyone has built a procedural library of thousands of botanically distinct species. The realistic expectation is plants that are distinct and believable at middle and far distance, with close-up quality depending on how good the photo-derived leaf, bark and fruit materials turn out; both scale and close-up quality are unproven.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Tree realism is rarely measured: the authors of ICTree (ACM TOG, SIGGRAPH Asia 2021) built a perceptual realism predictor from 4,000 participants comparing over one million image pairs, with one model working on geometry features and one on images; code and data are public but "should not be used for commercial purposes without our explicit permission" — [ICTree project page](https://cphoto.fit.vut.cz/ictree/)
- [V] A SIGGRAPH 2016 course by leading plant-modelling researchers states that viewers easily notice imperfections in plants and that full-detail plant models exceed practical limits of storage, transmission, modelling and rendering — [course page](https://storage.googleapis.com/pirk.io/projects/plant_life/index.html)
- [M] The current high-end approach combines scans with procedure rather than replacing scans: Megascans tree packs are tagged photogrammetry and ship as fixed assets per age class (17 for European beech) — [Fab listing](https://fab.com/s/5db64af5b801); Megaplants are procedural recipes built from branch-foliage meshes, textures and material instances — [Fab listing](https://www.fab.com/listings/f32d5cdc-de95-4ec1-a0b1-fc501ea5e0c5)
- [M] Scale of the newest flagship library: Megaplants launched with five species in November 2025; a year later it is still labelled Experimental with the publisher advising against shipping — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/create-lush-nanite-foliage-ready-forests-with-this-free-quixel-asset-pack), [Fab seller page](https://www.fab.com/sellers/Quixel%20Megaplants/about)
- [V] SpeedTree's method is "a blend of procedural modeling and manual editing", and its library assets carry "a full set of 8k resolution PBR textures" — [modeling approach](https://docs.unity3d.com/speedtree-modeler/manual/modeling-approach.html), [Unity SpeedTree page](https://unity.com/products/speedtree)
- [V] Laubwerk (bought by Maxon, January 2025) is described as having "hundreds of detailed and authentic 3D plant assets" — [Maxon announcement](https://www.maxon.net/article/maxon-welcomes-laubwerk-to-their-expanding-family-of-innovative-tools)
- [M] Fully procedural libraries are generic, not species-level: Infinigen has three tree generators and 30 small-plant generators; its authors say their trees use real geometry "much denser" than Sapling or SpeedTree, at about 3.5 hours per rendered scene — [Infinigen paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.09310). Sapling Tree Gen has 29 presets — [Blender Extensions](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/sapling-tree-gen/). Helios names about 20 species — [Helios documentation](https://plantsimulationlab.github.io/Helios/_plant_architecture_doc.html)
- [M] The largest "tree per real individual" dataset, Tree-D Fusion with 600,000 models over 300+ genera, fills a diffusion-estimated crown envelope using space colonisation; on a 2026 benchmark by other authors it scored F1 0.01 for structural reconstruction — [arXiv 2407.10330](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.10330v1), [TreesFormer page](https://angelikigram.github.io/treesformer/)
- [M] The only learned growth model found covers six species, with qualitative results only — [arXiv 2502.04762](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04762v1)
- [V] The Grove's developer says its realism depends on detailed twig models that are not usable in game engines — [The Grove FAQ](https://www.thegrove3d.com/learn/faq/)
- [M] For comparison, the edible-plant databases list 8,000 to 13,000 species (PFAF over 8,000; Useful Temperate Plants 9,186; Useful Tropical Plants 13,362) — [PFAF](https://pfaf.org/user/Default.aspx), [temperate.theferns.info](https://temperate.theferns.info/), [tropical.theferns.info](https://tropical.theferns.info/)

### Inferences
- Where procedural plants look real today, the leaf, twig and bark detail comes from scans or photographs and the species recipe was tuned by a person. A pipeline with neither is outside published experience. Expect the first results to look generic up close.
- Where they fall short, in order of visibility: leaf material (translucency, specular, colour variation), twig-level structure (real twigs carry buds, scars, leaf clustering that generic generators lack), bark at eye level, and uniformity (every individual looking like a seeded copy). Silhouette and branching at distance are the easy part.
- "Thousands of botanically distinct species" will in practice mean distinct by organs and proportions: leaf outline and arrangement, flower, fruit, bark class and colour, height and crown ratios, phenology dates. Distinct branching architecture per species is not supported by data for most species and will be shared within families or growth forms. That is still far beyond any existing library in species count, and it is the honest claim to make.
- The content target (3,000 species) is one to two orders of magnitude beyond what I could verify for any existing 3D plant library (5 species for Megaplants at launch, "hundreds" for Laubwerk, 29 presets or about 20 named species for open generators). Scale therefore has to come from automation, and automation quality (about 75% F1 for trait extraction) will be the limit, not rendering.
- A quality gate is needed that agents can run: render each generated species at fixed ages and seasons and compare against reference photographs and against benchmark assets (Megascans or Megaplants renders, SpeedTree, The Grove) with an image-based realism score. ICTree shows such a predictor can be trained, but its code is non-commercial and covers trees only. No ready gate exists for herbs, vines or fruit.
- For a small set of hero food plants that players see at arm's length (for example the 30 to 50 most common fruit trees and vegetables), plan on higher effort: more photo-derived data per species, or licensed scanned parts. The Megascans library being free under the Fab Standard License makes it a candidate source for leaf and bark material, subject to reading the licence for use in a non-Unreal engine.

### Gaps
- Species or model counts for SpeedTree's library, PlantCatalog, Megascans plants and Xfrog could not be retrieved (403 or empty pages). Whether any of them exceeds a few hundred species is unverified.
- No head-to-head perceptual study of procedural versus scanned plants was found.
- No evidence either way on whether CIRAD's AMAP library or any commercial vendor has reached a thousand parameterised species; this was not answerable from the pages reachable.
- How the visual target game builds its vegetation was not researched (outside this brief).
