## 1. What Pearl Abyss has published or shown about the BlackSpace Engine, and how Crimson Desert ships and performs

### Takeaway

Crimson Desert (released 2026-03-19 on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Windows and macOS; Switch 2 planned for the first half of 2027) is a hybrid renderer, not a path tracer: ray-traced diffuse GI through surfel radiance caches at 1/16 resolution and ray-traced reflections at 1/4 resolution over a BVH that reaches only 100 to 150 m and leaves out grass and fine vegetation, with classic cascaded shadow maps, screen-space shadows and SSAO doing the rest, and ray tracing switched off entirely on the low PC presets, on Xbox Series S and on the weakest supported Macs. Its floor is far above the project's target hardware: the official minimum is a GTX 1060 with 16 GB RAM for 30 fps, Series S gets 720p at 40 fps without ray tracing, the Steam Deck cannot hold 30 fps at the lowest settings, and an 8 GB A18 Pro (phone-class chip) only reaches about 50 fps with frame generation at 180p internal resolution.

#### Global illumination, reflections, shadows (the best technical source is PC Games Hardware's analysis of the launch build)

- [A] Ray tracing is active on PC down to the Medium preset and is switched off automatically at Low and Minimum. It covers indirect light, indirect shadowing and reflections. — [PC Games Hardware, page 3](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/3/)
- [A] The ray-tracing acceleration structure covers "roughly 100 to 150 meters" around the camera and holds the world, large objects, buildings and characters; "small details such as fine vegetation, grass, foliage and decorative objects are excluded from the BVH and thus from ray tracing". — [PC Games Hardware, page 3](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/3/)
- [A] GI uses surfel-based radiance caches filled at runtime, at drastically reduced resolution: down to one sixteenth of output resolution for GI and one quarter for reflections, depending on quality level; only some pixels fire rays and the rest read the nearest cache. — [PC Games Hardware, page 3](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/3/)
- [V via A] "Ray-traced shadows were too expensive according to the developers"; sun shadows are classic shadow-map cascades (distance, vegetation, close detail) plus many screen-space shadows, and screen-space reflections and ambient occlusion stay in use even with ray tracing on. The developers call their light sampling "Many Light Sampling". — [PC Games Hardware, page 3](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/3/)
- [A] Reflection fallback chain: ray tracing near the camera, signed-distance-field reflections at medium distance, screen-space reflections beyond. — [PC Games Hardware, page 3](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/3/)
- [A] Enabling DLSS Ray Reconstruction or FSR Ray Regeneration fires extra per-pixel rays into the radiance caches (an approximated extra bounce), forces lighting to maximum and costs a lot of frame rate on both vendors. — [PC Games Hardware, page 3](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/3/)
- [A] Digital Foundry judged the ray-traced GI "surprisingly natural and competitive with Unreal Engine 5's Lumen" at lower cost, with ray-traced reflections on water and glossy floors combined with screen-space methods. — [Multiplayer.it summarizing Digital Foundry, 2026-02-28](https://multiplayer.it/notizie/crimson-desert-su-pc-impressiona-digital-foundry-grazie-al-blackspace-engine.html)
- [S] A report on Digital Foundry's coverage says DF confirmed with Pearl Abyss that the diffuse GI is ray traced and "appears to be evaluated per pixel, not just through big, low-res probes". This is compatible with the surfel-cache description above only if "per pixel" refers to the final gather. The sentence comes from a search-result summary; I did not open the page. — [finalboss.io](https://finalboss.io/crimson-desert-on-ps5-pro-keeps-its-wild)
- [S] A fan wiki claims the PC version is "built around full path tracing" with Neural Radiance Caching; this contradicts PCGH's analysis and Digital Foundry's hybrid description and has no primary source. — [thegameswiki](https://thegameswiki.com/crimson-desert/wiki/blackspace-engine); contradicted by [PC Games Hardware, page 3](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/3/)

- [A] The launch build shows volumetric clouds and smoke with shadowing. SDR output looks strongly oversaturated; HDR looks more natural. — [PC Games Hardware, page 2](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/2/); [page 4](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/4/)

#### Distant rendering and level of detail

- [V] The continent uses "Distance Rendering": the nearby environment is prioritized to optimize draw distance, combined with seamless loading; fast skydiving descents were shown. — [Pearl Abyss press release via Zaikei](https://zaikei.co.jp/releases/2860515/)

- [A] Vegetation is shadowed into the distance; the Cinematic preset mainly buys draw distance and fine detail. — [PC Games Hardware, page 2](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/2/)

#### Vegetation, terrain, water

- [V] A "shape-change" feature lets varied trees be placed quickly instead of repeating one tree; trees and grass sway in the wind. — [Pearl Abyss press release via Zaikei](https://zaikei.co.jp/releases/2860515/)
- [A] In the demo each leaf and branch responded to the player when climbing or taking cover behind a tree. — [8bitdigi](https://8bitdigi.com/gdc-2025-an-inside-look-into-the-blackspace-engine-with-crimson-desert/)
- [A] Heavy dithering is used for vegetation, shadows, screen-space reflections, ambient occlusion and contact shadows, so native 4K still looks grainy; screen-space shadows and AO drop out when occluded, so foliage under a forest canopy can flash brightly; tessellated surfaces and parallax maps show flicker and stepping at mid range. — [PC Games Hardware, page 4](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/4/)
- [A] Other observed features: tessellated surfaces, rain puddles, tracks left in snow and sand, many particles. — [PC Games Hardware, page 2](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/2/)
- [V] Water uses FFT ocean simulation for waves and tides and a shallow-water flow simulation for currents; GPU simulation for hair, manes and cloth. — [Pearl Abyss press release via Zaikei](https://zaikei.co.jp/releases/2860515/)
- [A] The fluid system has collision, flow direction and wave physics; stream flow changes when the character enters. — [PC Games Hardware, page 2](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/2/); [8bitdigi](https://8bitdigi.com/gdc-2025-an-inside-look-into-the-blackspace-engine-with-crimson-desert/)

- [V] At gamescom 2026, lead engineer Jin-hwan Kim and graphics lead Jin-ho Yoon (interview by MeinMMO) acknowledged performance "deficiencies" with many characters, effects, lights and shadows on screen, said ray-traced lighting on lower-spec hardware posed "major difficulties", and said the Switch 2 port needs heavy optimization including keeping memory use under control. — [Otakukart](https://otakukart.com/crimson-desert-lead-engineer-admits-the-blackspace-engine-has-deficiencies-in-complex-scenes-but-pearl-abyss-is-working-to-address-them/)

- [S] An unverifiable comment from an alleged former engine-team member claims the engine was overhauled every year for performance and that a "small team" produced the content. — [Wccftech, 2026-03-12](https://wccftech.com/crimson-desert-different-level-unreal-engine-5/)

### Inferences

- The fallback when ray tracing is off (Low/Minimum PC presets, Series S, M2 Pro and M2 Max Macs) is not documented anywhere I could read. Since the engine keeps SSAO, screen-space shadows, SSR and SDF reflections even with ray tracing on, the non-RT path is most likely those plus some cheap ambient term. That is a guess.
- Pearl Abyss's renderer is built so that the expensive, physically based parts stay close to the camera (BVH radius 100 to 150 m, no fine vegetation in it) while the far view relies on impostors, shadow cascades and atmosphere. The famous vistas are therefore not ray traced in any meaningful sense; they are a level-of-detail and atmosphere achievement.
- The hardware floor is the key fact for the project: Pearl Abyss, with a seven-year engine effort, could not get below 720p at 40 fps without ray tracing on Xbox Series S (the weakest current home console), needs frame generation on a ROG Ally, and fails on a Steam Deck. An 8 GB phone is well below all of these. A 60 fps phone version of this exact look does not exist and Pearl Abyss has not announced one.
- The Mac chart shows ray tracing "off" on M2 Pro and M2 Max and "on" for M3 and later, which matches Apple adding hardware ray tracing with the M3 generation; the game treats hardware ray tracing as a requirement for its GI rather than offering a software path on Mac.

## 2. Which elements dominate the look in wide vistas versus close-ups

### Takeaway

The published evidence says the vista look is carried by cheap, mostly screen-resolution-independent systems (sky and aerial perspective, haze volume, low-resolution time-sliced clouds and their shadows, local tone mapping, and far-field proxies for trees), while the close-up forest look is carried by expensive ones (dense alpha-tested or micro-polygon vegetation, shadows and contact occlusion on that vegetation, and indirect light). The forest and river-valley close and mid range is where every tested game is slowest.

### Cited Findings

#### Wide vistas

- [D] Ghost of Tsushima's whole atmosphere stack is tiny on a base PS4: haze froxel volume (128 x 64 x 64, 10 cm to 100 km, exponential depth) about 0.5 ms as async compute; haze filtering at 1080p 0.36 to 0.67 ms; clouds rendered into a 768 x 768 paraboloid texture time-sliced over 60 frames; local tone mapping (bilateral grid 64 x 32 x 64) about 0.25 ms at 1080p; converting sky luminance to SH about 0.06 ms and updating the sky-lit probe grid for 73 tiles about 0.12 ms. — [Patry, "Real-Time Samurai Cinema", SIGGRAPH 2021, HTML slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/jpatry_advances2021/index.html)
- [D] The sky and aerial perspective model used in Unreal costs 0.31 ms in total at 720p on a GeForce GTX 1080 and 1.03 ms of LUT generation on an iPhone 6s, and is decoupled from screen resolution (verified in slide text). — [Hillaire, SIGGRAPH 2020 course slides, page 22](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2020-shading-course/hillaire/s2020_pbs_hillaire_slides.pdf)
- [D] Horizon Zero Dawn's volumetric cloudscape rendered in about 2 ms on PS4 with 20 MB of RAM, only because of a quarter-resolution buffer updating 1 of every 16 pixels per frame; the unoptimized version cost about 20 ms (verified in slide text). — [Schneider, SIGGRAPH 2015](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/The%20Real-time%20Volumetric%20Cloudscapes%20of%20Horizon%20-%20Zero%20Dawn%20-%20ARTR.pdf)
- [D] Ghost of Yotei bakes distant mountains (models plus terrain materials) into textures; distant mountains hold over one million trees, rocks and bushes that GPU culling cuts to about sixty thousand items for G-buffer rendering; a light-space cloud shadow map gives god rays in fog far from the camera. — [Sucker Punch, PlayStation Blog, 2025-10-23](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201)
- [S] In Ghost of Tsushima, grass and foliage are removed at long range and replaced by an artist-authored texture drawn in place of the terrain material. — [Thread summarizing the GDC talk](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1688939411575119872)
- [A] Reviewers singled out Crimson Desert's far view: 3D vegetation at long range and volumetric clouds (Digital Foundry), vegetation shadowed into the distance (PCGH), and the whole world visible from high points (Game Informer). — [Multiplayer.it on DF](https://multiplayer.it/notizie/crimson-desert-su-pc-impressiona-digital-foundry-grazie-al-blackspace-engine.html); [PCGH page 2](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/2/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimson_Desert)
- [A] In Avatar: Frontiers of Pandora the far view has no 2D billboard trees and vegetation is cleanly shadowed in the distance. — [PC Games Hardware, 2023-12-07](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Avatar-Frontiers-of-Pandora-Spiel-73347/Specials/Avatar-Frontiers-of-Pandora-Snowdrop-Tech-Benchmarks-Test-1435474/2/)
- [V] Unreal's Nanite voxels are "near pixel-sized" and are meant to keep geometry detail, animation and material properties of foliage at distance, replacing older tricks that added surface area back to surviving triangles. — [Epic, Nanite Foliage documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage?lang=en-US)

#### Close-ups and mid range

- [M] The most demanding area PCGH found in Crimson Desert is a valley with a river and forest. — [PC Games Hardware, page 2](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/2/)
- [M] In Kingdom Come: Deliverance 2 a dense forest is the worst case: about 50 fps in the forest test scene corresponds to about 60 fps elsewhere; an RX 7600 gets 38.3 fps average at 1440p Ultra with FSR Quality, an RX 7800 XT 60.1 fps, an RTX 4090 145.2 fps (Ryzen 7 9800X3D, February 2025). The Experimental preset, which mainly adds shadows and detail at medium and long range, costs 29% on an RTX 5090 at 4K and 42% on an RX 7900 XTX at 3440 x 1440. — [ComputerBase](https://www.computerbase.de/artikel/gaming/kingdom-come-deliverance-2-benchmark-test.91321/seite-2)
- [A] Crimson Desert excludes grass, fine vegetation and foliage from ray tracing, so close-up plant lighting depends on shadow cascades, screen-space shadows and SSAO; these fail visibly under a canopy (bright flashes when occluded) and vegetation is rendered with heavy dithering. — [PCGH page 3](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/3/); [PCGH page 4](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/4/)
- [A] Avatar uses ray tracing for sun shadows, contact shadows and AO, uses reduced meshes for fine vegetation in ray-traced reflections, and applies tessellated displacement generously to ground and plants. — [PC Games Hardware](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Avatar-Frontiers-of-Pandora-Spiel-73347/Specials/Avatar-Frontiers-of-Pandora-Snowdrop-Tech-Benchmarks-Test-1435474/2/)
- [D] "Large quantities of dense vegetation", translucent geometry and thin walls are named as the main difficulties of ray-traced GI in Assassin's Creed Shadows. — [SIGGRAPH 2025 Advances course page](https://www.realtimerendering.com/advances/s2025/index.html)
- [V] Unreal's own guidance treats foliage as a special case for lighting: foliage shading models get their own reflection roughness cutoff, and the ray-tracing scene should stay under 100,000 instances after culling on current consoles. — [Epic, Lumen Performance Guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-performance-guide-for-unreal-engine)
- [S] Ghost of Tsushima grass blades are generated on the GPU from a curve with 15 vertices at high detail and 7 at low detail, blended by moving vertices; blades are widened as they thin out with distance. — [Thread summarizing the GDC talk](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1688939411575119872)
- [V] Unreal's Nanite Foliage demo trees have 1 to 10 million polygons each, with about 500,000 instances in a scene. — [Epic, Nanite Foliage documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage?lang=en-US)

### Inferences

- Ranking for vistas, by visual weight per millisecond (my judgement from the costs above): 1) aerial perspective and haze, 2) sun and sky lighting with a good tone curve and exposure, 3) a continuous far-field tree and terrain representation with correct colour and shading, 4) clouds and cloud shadows, 5) terrain material blending at distance (which can be a baked texture). Every one of these has a published implementation under about 2 ms on a 2013 console, and three of them are close to free.
- Ranking for close-ups, by cost: 1) vegetation geometry density and the overdraw of alpha-tested or dithered leaves, 2) shadows on vegetation (cascades redraw all of it), 3) contact shadows and ambient occlusion in the understory, 4) indirect light under a canopy, 5) leaf translucency (cheap in the shader, but only convincing with good shadows), 6) water. Items 1 to 4 are where Crimson Desert, Avatar, Kingdom Come 2 and Assassin's Creed Shadows all spend their budget and all show artifacts.
- For a phone target this split is good news and bad news. A hazy vista "to the horizon" is achievable with known cheap techniques. A dense fern-and-stream forest floor with correct contact shadows and bounce light at 60 fps is the part that has no proven cheap equivalent, and it is also the part a growing game looks at most.
- No source measures leaf translucency, fern density or stream rendering separately; the close-up ranking is inference from where the benchmarks and artifacts cluster.

### Gaps

- No published per-pass frame breakdown exists for a Crimson Desert vista or forest scene.
- Red Dead Redemption 2's atmospherics talk (the canonical vista reference) has no timings I could retrieve; see section 3.
- No source quantifies how much of a vista's cost is far-field vegetation versus atmosphere in any of these games.

## 3. Best public technique reference and reported cost, per element

### Takeaway

Solid published costs exist for atmosphere (0.3 to 1 ms), haze volumes (about 0.5 to 1.2 ms on PS4), volumetric clouds (2 ms on PS4; 0.2 to 5 ms for voxel clouds), local tone mapping (0.25 ms), procedural placement (about 0.25 ms GPU), ray-traced GI on consoles (1.7 to 4 ms, or Lumen's 4 ms and 8 ms budgets) and Nanite foliage data sizes; published costs are missing or were unreachable for Red Dead Redemption 2's atmosphere, Ghost of Tsushima's grass, Far Cry 5's terrain, Assassin's Creed Shadows' ray tracing and Avatar's ray tracing. Almost every number is from console-class hardware; only the sky model and a few mobile talks give phone numbers.

### Cited Findings

#### Sky, atmosphere and aerial perspective

- [D] Hillaire 2020 (Epic), verified in slide text. PC, GeForce GTX 1080: transmittance LUT 256 x 64 0.01 ms, sky-view LUT 200 x 100 0.05 ms, aerial perspective volume 32^3 0.04 ms, multi-scattering LUT 32^2 0.07 ms, plus 0.14 ms to render sky and atmosphere at 720p, total 0.31 ms. iPhone 6s: transmittance 256 x 64 0.53 ms, sky-view 96 x 50 0.27 ms, aerial perspective 32 x 32 x 16 0.11 ms, multi-scattering 32^2 0.12 ms, total LUT cost 1.03 ms. Supports ground-to-space views and dynamic time of day. The scalability slide marks atmosphere as working on mobile and clouds as questionable: "Only cloud rendering is not reasonably achievable on mobile. For now." Source is from 2020; the phone is far older than the project's targets. — [Hillaire, SIGGRAPH 2020 slides](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2020-shading-course/hillaire/s2020_pbs_hillaire_slides.pdf); paper: [Eurographics Digital Library](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14050)
- [D] Ghost of Tsushima (2021 talk, base PS4): Rayleigh and Mie scattering precomputed into 3D LUTs with multiple scattering, resampled to 2D each frame; haze froxel grid 128 x 64 x 64 at about 0.5 ms; a custom colour space to improve Rayleigh scattering. — [Patry, SIGGRAPH 2021, HTML slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/jpatry_advances2021/index.html)
- [D] Red Dead Redemption 2 (Bauer, Rockstar, SIGGRAPH 2019): the talk covers sky, cloud and fog rendering, volumetric effects and the ambient lighting model for sky light, using voxelization and raymarching for scattering and transmittance, with optimizations for the consoles of the time. Slides are a 232 MB PowerPoint file only. — [SIGGRAPH 2019 Advances course page](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2019/index.htm)
- [D] Hillaire's summary of Bauer's method: a unified model rendering nearby volumetrics, fog and clouds together, traced at quarter resolution with temporal upsampling ("instant result but looks blurrier"), versus Schneider's method of updating 1 pixel per 4 x 4 tile (16-frame latency but looks full resolution). — [Hillaire, SIGGRAPH 2020 slides](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2020-shading-course/hillaire/s2020_pbs_hillaire_slides.pdf)
- [A] A frame analysis of Red Dead Redemption 2 on PC (2020): sky and clouds are mostly GPU compute; sun shadows are 4 cascades of 1024 x 1024 at medium settings; a baked top-down world lightmap supplies large-scale lighting; a 2048 x 2048 mud/track map drives terrain parallax; three separate depth downsamples feed SSAO, SSR and volumetric fog; about 25 fps at medium settings on a GTX 1050 Ti laptop. — [imgeself graphics study](https://imgeself.github.io/posts/2020-06-19-graphics-study-rdr2)

#### Volumetric clouds

- [D] Nubis in Horizon Zero Dawn (2015, PS4): about 2 ms, 20 MB of RAM (two 3D noise textures of 128^3 and 32^3 plus one 2D texture), 64 to 128 march samples with 6 light samples per step, quarter-resolution buffer updating 1 of 16 pixels per frame with reprojection; without that trick about 20 ms (verified in slide text). — [Schneider, SIGGRAPH 2015](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/The%20Real-time%20Volumetric%20Cloudscapes%20of%20Horizon%20-%20Zero%20Dawn%20-%20ARTR.pdf)
- [D] Nubis Evolved in Horizon Forbidden West (2022, PS5): flyable cloud environments and storm effects, detailed at 1080p without temporal upscaling, building on the system that rendered in under two milliseconds on PS4. — [Guerrilla, Nubis Evolved](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-evolved); [SIGGRAPH 2022 course page](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/index.html)
- [D] Nubis Cubed (2023): voxel-based clouds with compressed signed distance fields, built in under six months; the slides state rendering takes "between 0.2 and 5 milliseconds" depending on whether clouds are seen from afar or flown through (sentence quoted from the slide text by the search tool; the PDF is too large for me to open, and the platform, presumably PS5, is not named in the quote). — [Guerrilla, Nubis Cubed](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-cubed); [slides PDF](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2023/Nubis%20Cubed%20%28Advances%202023%29.pdf)
- [D] Ghost of Tsushima's cheaper alternative on PS4: clouds drawn into a 768 x 768 paraboloid sky texture, time-sliced over 60 frames. Ghost of Yotei (PS5) adds parallax using stored average depth, blends three cloud frames and lets clouds draw in front of mountains. — [Patry, SIGGRAPH 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/jpatry_advances2021/index.html); [PlayStation Blog, 2025-10-23](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201)

#### Tone mapping and exposure

- [D] Ghost of Tsushima: illuminance-based exposure, local tone mapping through a bilateral grid at about 250 microseconds on base PS4 at 1080p. — [Patry, SIGGRAPH 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/jpatry_advances2021/index.html)

#### Global illumination

- [D] Ghost of Tsushima (PS4, no ray tracing): a regular grid of SH probes storing sky visibility, 16 x 16 x 3 probes per 200 m tile, relit at runtime from the current sky (sky-to-SH about 0.06 ms, grid update for 73 tiles about 0.12 ms); 235 reflection probes with up to 128 relit at a time for about 0.7 ms. — [Patry, SIGGRAPH 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/jpatry_advances2021/index.html)
- [D] idTech 8 / DOOM: The Dark Ages (SIGGRAPH 2025), ray-traced GI through cascaded irradiance volumes (16 x 16 x 16 x 6 cascades, 12^3 on lower specs) plus a radiance cache and final gather, shipped values in a large vista hotspot with vegetation: Xbox Series S at 900p about 2.11 ms (1.71 ms with async compute), Series X at 1440p about 1.9 ms (1.4 ms), PS5 at 1440p about 2.05 ms (1.55 ms), PS5 Pro at 1800p about 1.92 ms, PC with RTX 4080 at 4K about 1.7 ms. Needs hardware ray tracing; "low spec" is defined as Series S (verified in slide text). — [Sousa, SIGGRAPH 2025](https://www.realtimerendering.com/advances/s2025/content/SOUSA_SIGGRAPH_2025_Final.pdf)
- [D] Frostbite surfel GI (GIBS, SIGGRAPH 2024): on Xbox Series X at native 1440p in Skate, about 3.2 ms average before and 2.5 ms after optimization, never above 3 ms, with up to 200,000 rays per frame, about 100,000 live surfels and 25,000 probes; the 2021 version cost 6 to 7 ms; the whole system fits in under 500 MB on Series S; a 60 fps sports title had only a 2 ms budget and froze updates during play (verified in slide text). — [Apers, SIGGRAPH 2024](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/EA-GIBS2/Apers_Advances-s2024_Shipping-Dynamic-GI.pdf)
- [V] Unreal Lumen: default budgets for GI plus reflections are 8 ms at 30 fps (Epic quality) and 4 ms at 60 fps (High quality) at a 1080p internal resolution on current consoles, upsampled to 4K by TSR; each lower quality level costs roughly half the one above; a Medium level and an "Irradiance Field Gather" mode target 60 fps on Switch 2 including handheld; Low disables Lumen. — [Epic, Lumen Performance Guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-performance-guide-for-unreal-engine)
- [V] Lumen platform limits: not supported on PS4/Xbox One; software ray tracing needs at least a GTX 1070, hardware ray tracing an RTX 2000 or RX 6000 series; supported on Android Vulkan only with the desktop renderer, not with the mobile renderer; software tracing covers 200 m from the camera (up to 800 m), far-field traces extend to 10 km and need HLOD; software mode does not support world position offset (so no vertex-animated foliage in the lighting scene). — [Epic, Lumen Technical Details](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine)
- [V] Assassin's Creed Shadows (Ubisoft): first in the series with ray-traced GI, fully dynamic for diffuse and specular; the baked alternative would have needed about 2 TB of data and two years of bake time (Assassin's Creed Origins: about 450 GB and three months if fully baked, cut to 9 GB with a coarser method). On consoles the 60 fps performance mode uses baked GI because "reaching 60 FPS ... while using RTGI would mean sacrificing quite a lot"; ray tracing there is limited to the player's hideout; pre-RTX PC GPUs (minimum GTX 1070) get a software ray-traced GI path only in the hideout. Not Steam Deck compatible at launch. — [Ubisoft tech Q&A, 2025-02-12](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/game/assassins-creed/news/4XbPPtFyQEtIMWrA9xVDmZ); [bake figures from the GDC 2025 talk as reported](https://consolepcgaming.com/assassins-creed-shadows-lighting-would-take-2-years-and-2tb-of-data-to-bake); [SIGGRAPH 2025 course page](https://www.realtimerendering.com/advances/s2025/index.html)
- [V] Avatar: Frontiers of Pandora (Snowdrop): per-pixel ray-traced lighting and reflections replace the earlier probe-based GI; rays are traced from surfaces in random directions with limited bounces; indirect light also feeds fog. — [Massive Entertainment](https://massive.se/article/snowdrops-ray-tracing-shines-a-light-on-pandora). [A] Ray tracing is mandatory with a software fallback for GPUs without ray-tracing hardware; minimum GPU RX 5700 / GTX 1070 (8 GB), recommended RX 6700 XT / RTX 3060 Ti; 16 GB VRAM advised for full detail. — [PC Games Hardware, 2023-12-07](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Avatar-Frontiers-of-Pandora-Spiel-73347/Specials/Avatar-Frontiers-of-Pandora-Snowdrop-Tech-Benchmarks-Test-1435474/2/). The GDC 2024 talk is "Ray Tracing in Snowdrop: Scene Representation and Custom BVH" by Oleksandr Koshlo (members-only). — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1034404/Ray-Tracing-in-Snowdrop-Scene)
- [A] Kingdom Come: Deliverance 2 (CryEngine branch at roughly the 5.11 level, DirectX 12 only): no hardware ray tracing; indirect light from sparse voxel octree cone tracing (SVOTI) whose voxels get coarser with distance, screen-space directional occlusion, screen-space reflections with cube-map fallback, volumetric fog shadows. — [PC Games Hardware, 2025-02-03](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Kingdom-Come-Deliverance-2-Spiel-74644/Tests/Tech-Test-Review-Benchmarks-Cryengine-1464706/3/). [M] 8 GB VRAM is enough for Ultra; 1080p at 60 fps in the forest needs at least an RTX 4060 or RX 6800 / 7700 XT. — [ComputerBase](https://www.computerbase.de/artikel/gaming/kingdom-come-deliverance-2-benchmark-test.91321/seite-2)

#### Vegetation placement

- [D] Horizon Zero Dawn (van Muijden, GDC 2017, PS4): the world is generated around the player at runtime on the GPU from painted 2D maps; slide text gives about 250 microseconds average GPU load while the player moves and about 4 MB per km² of map data (32 bits per m²). Slides exceeded the fetch limit; the figures come from a search extract of the slide text and should be rechecked. 2017 source. — [Guerrilla talk page](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/gpu-based-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn); [slides PDF](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf)
- [D] Ghost of Tsushima (Pohlmann, GDC 2021): "millions of object instances", placement and texturing rules written by artists in a small functional language that runs as bytecode on the GPU. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027352/Samurai-Landscapes-Building-and-Rendering)
- [D] Far Cry 5 (Carrier, 2018): about 100 km² filled by a Houdini pipeline that generates biomes from "recipes" reacting to terrain, freshwater networks, cliffs, terrain texturing, and grass that follows terrain humidity and a wind vector map, regenerated as the terrain changed daily. No runtime costs given. — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2018/03/22/the-procedural-world-generation-of-far-cry-5/); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/houdini-procedural-world-generation-of-far-cry-5/)

#### Vegetation rendering, grass and wind

- [D] Ghost of Tsushima grass (Wohllaib, GDC 2021): individual blades generated and animated on the GPU with memory and performance limits for "acres" of grass. — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027214/Advanced-Graphics-Summit-Procedural-Grass). [S] Blade details from a secondary summary: compute-shader generation per terrain tile from height, material and grass-type textures; 15-vertex and 7-vertex blades; clumping by procedural noise; short blades folded to look twice as dense; hand-built flowers and pampas grass distributed the same way. — [Thread summary](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1688939411575119872)
- [D] Ghost of Tsushima wind: one authored wind vector whose strength varies over the map with scrolling Perlin noise, no fluid simulation, plus invisible "vorticle" particles for local swirls; uses "only a small fraction of the PS4's computational capacity". — [Game Developer, 2021-07-23](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/using-vorticles-to-simulate-wind-in-i-ghost-of-tsushima-i-)
- [D] Ghost of Yotei doubled the grass and item budget of its GPU compute renderer on PS5 and added a "cut buffer" for cuttable grass and flowers. — [PlayStation Blog, 2025-10-23](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201)
- [V] Unreal Nanite Foliage (Experimental in UE 5.7; Epic advises "caution when shipping with it"): assemblies of up to 65,000 part instances cut the largest demo tree from about 3.5 GB to about 29 MB on disk and from about 36 MB to about 2.7 MB of streaming memory per view; this allows about 500,000 instances of dozens of tree variants with 1 to 10 million polygons each; distant clusters switch to voxel bricks (4 x 4 x 4 voxels, up to 128 bricks per cluster); wind is skeletal skinning rather than vertex offset, and 100,000 updating bones cost about 0.1 ms on the GPU; animation stops below 0.1 of screen size; the wind plugin has no near-field simulation or player collision. — [Epic, Nanite Foliage documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage?lang=en-US)
- [V] Nanite itself needs DirectX 12 with Shader Model 6 class hardware ("current console and desktop platforms"). — [Epic, Nanite documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine)
- [V] The Witcher 4 Unreal Engine 5 tech demo (State of Unreal, 2025-06-03) ran live on a standard PS5 at 60 fps and showed Nanite Foliage, fast geometry streaming and Mass crowds; CD Projekt and Epic say it used hardware ray-traced Lumen and that Epic considers software Lumen "a thing of the past"; CD Projekt says 60 fps on Series S will be "extremely challenging" and that the demo is "not The Witcher 4 itself". About 100 Epic staff were reported assigned to CD Projekt's needs. — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=405351); [Wccftech on the DF interview, 2025-06-15](https://wccftech.com/the-witcher-4-is-made-with-console-first-approach-60-fps-on-xbox-series-s-will-be-extremely-challenging-to-achieve-says-cdpr/); [PC Games Hardware](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/The-Witcher-4-Spiel-73629/Specials/Tech-Demo-Hands-on-Impressions-Unreal-Engine-5-1474063/); [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/witcher-4-tech-demo-shows-the-game-running-at-60-fps-with-ray-tracing-on-base-ps5/)
- [D] God of War (2019 talk): 3D wind simulation, boneless tree and leaf sway, ground plants reacting to characters, and card clusters as LODs and shadow proxies. — [SIGGRAPH 2019 course page](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2019/index.htm)

#### Geometry level of detail and far field

- [V] Assassin's Creed Shadows uses "micropolygon" assets that adjust detail by distance and visibility so artists no longer author separate LOD models, on a GPU-driven pipeline. — [Ubisoft, 2024-09-12](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/game/assassins-creed/news/2NvGq2f2HRuu11c03KJ0DG/assassins-creed-shadows-building-the-next-generation-of-assassins-creed); [80.lv on the GDC 2025 talk](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-2025-talk-rendering-assassin-s-creed-shadows)
- [D] Ghost of Yotei: compute-driven occlusion culling takes over a million distant items down to about 60,000 drawn; distant mountains are baked to textures. — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=411201)

#### Terrain

- [D] Far Cry 5 (Moore, GDC 2018): a GPU compute pipeline does LOD selection, culling, stitching and rendering of the height-field terrain and supports procedural cliffs and displacement. No timings on the public page. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025480/Terrain-Rendering-in-Far-Cry)
- [D] Call of Duty (Etienne, SIGGRAPH 2023): adaptive virtual texturing covers up to 16.8 x 16.8 km at 25 pixels per inch; a 240 x 136 feedback buffer drives it; the height map is virtualized from 32 MB down to about 1.2 MB (144 pages of 64 x 64); decompressing a 68 x 68 height page takes about 1 ms on PS4; per frame it composites at most 4 virtual-texture pages on low-end mobile and up to 20 on high-end PC; ASTC on mobile, BC elsewhere (verified in slide text). — [Etienne, SIGGRAPH 2023](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2023/Etienne%28ATVI%29-Large%20Scale%20Terrain%20Rendering%20with%20notes%20%28Advances%202023%29.pdf)
- [D] Concurrent binary tree terrain tessellation (Intel, 2024): planetary-scale adaptive triangulation in under 0.2 ms on PS5-level hardware. — [SIGGRAPH 2024 course page](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/index.html)

#### Water

- [V] Crimson Desert: FFT ocean plus shallow-water simulation (see section 1). — [Pearl Abyss press release via Zaikei](https://zaikei.co.jp/releases/2860515/)
- [D] Horizon Forbidden West: breaking waves are offline Houdini simulations baked into reusable deformations and placed along artist-drawn wavefronts, because a real-time simulation at that quality "is not plausible"; tessellation is raised locally around waves (verified in slide text; no timings). — [Malan, SIGGRAPH 2022](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/SIGGRAPH2022-Advances-Water-Malan.pdf)
- [D] Far Cry 5's rendering talk covers its water system and a physically based time-of-day cycle. — [SIGGRAPH 2018 course page](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2018/index.htm)

### Inferences

Cost sheet assembled from the findings above (console numbers are at the stated resolution; "phone verdict" is my judgement for an 8 GB phone at 60 fps, see section 4 for the hardware basis):

| Element | Reference cost | Hardware, year | Phone verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sky and aerial perspective LUTs | 1.03 ms; 0.31 ms | iPhone 6s; GTX 1080, 2020 | Keep as published |
| Haze froxel volume | about 0.5 ms plus 0.4 to 0.7 ms apply | base PS4, 2021 | Approximate: smaller grid or analytic height fog plus the aerial-perspective LUT |
| Volumetric clouds, ray-marched | about 2 ms (20 ms without temporal trick) | PS4, 2015 | Drop ray marching per frame; use a time-sliced sky texture as Ghost of Tsushima did |
| Cloud shadows on terrain | part of the above, light-space map | PS5, 2025 | Keep as a scrolling 2D shadow texture |
| Local tone mapping | 0.25 ms | base PS4, 2021 | Keep, or use a global curve |
| Sky-visibility SH probe GI | about 0.2 ms update in total | base PS4, 2021 | Keep; best fit for an outdoor game without ray tracing |
| Ray-traced GI | 1.7 to 2.1 ms; 2.5 to 4 ms; Lumen 4 ms | Series S to PS5 Pro, 2024 to 2025 | Drop on phones (needs hardware ray tracing and console-class bandwidth); optional on desktop |
| Procedural placement on GPU | about 0.25 ms, 4 MB per km² | PS4, 2017 | Keep in principle; check compute-shader cost on tile-based GPUs |
| Grass blades from compute | not retrieved | PS4, 2021 | Approximate: far lower density, texture beyond a short radius |
| Micro-polygon trees (Nanite Foliage) | 1 to 10 M polygons per tree, voxels far | PS5, 2025, experimental | Drop on phones; classic LODs plus impostors |
| Terrain virtual texturing | 4 pages per frame on low-end mobile | mobile to PC, 2023 | Keep, proven on mobile |
| Shadows on vegetation | no public number | — | Approximate: few cascades, short range, baked or blob occlusion beyond |
| FFT ocean and shallow water | no public number | — | Approximate: small FFT or summed waves; baked flow maps for streams |

- The published numbers cluster around a simple pattern: anything stored in a small lookup table or low-resolution volume and reused across the screen (sky, haze, probes, tone mapping, time-sliced clouds) costs well under 1 ms even on 2013 hardware, while anything that scales with visible geometry or with rays (dense foliage, shadows, GI) costs milliseconds on 2020 consoles.
- Crimson Desert's own choices agree with the references: it keeps shadow maps instead of ray-traced shadows, caps its ray-traced world at 150 m, and leaves grass out. A team with far less engineering capacity should assume it must cut at least as hard.
- Costs measured on PS5 (about 10 teraflops per the idTech talk) cannot be divided down to a phone linearly: bandwidth, thermal limits and missing features matter more than arithmetic throughput (see section 4).

## 4. Cheaper variants proven on low-power hardware, and the visual loss

### Takeaway

The only parts of this look with published phone-class costs are the sky and aerial perspective (about 1 ms on a 2015 iPhone), virtual-textured terrain (scaled down to low-end mobile in Call of Duty), a Nanite-like cluster renderer from Tencent (3 ms on a high-end phone but 20 ms on a five-year-old one), and many-light shading from HypeHype; dynamic GI, ray-marched clouds and micro-polygon foliage have no proven 60 fps phone implementation. Every time the real Crimson Desert renderer has been pushed down toward handheld hardware the result has been 180p to 720p internal resolution, no ray tracing, frame generation and unstable frame rates.

### Cited Findings

#### What phone hardware is, in console terms

- [D] HypeHype (SIGGRAPH 2025): a 99-dollar phone today has about 15 GB/s of memory bandwidth and about 250 GFLOP/s, "about the same as Xbox 360 and PS3"; even high-end phones peak at around 85 GB/s; Xbox Series X and PS5 Pro have over 500 GB/s and 12 and 17 TFLOP/s. The Adreno 610 in such phones runs at 3 W; the Adreno 830 in 1000-dollar phones "matches around 10 year old consoles and desktop GPUs" at 5 W. Phones are passively cooled, so bandwidth-heavy work turns into heat and throttling; tile-based GPUs want pixel shaders rather than compute shaders, few render passes, and 16-bit float math (verified in slide text). — [Lempiäinen, SIGGRAPH 2025 notes](https://www.realtimerendering.com/advances/s2025/content/s2025_stb_lighting_v1.1_notes.pdf)
- [D] HypeHype (SIGGRAPH 2023): Xbox 360 and PS3 equal low-to-mid tier phones in GPU performance; the latest 1000-dollar phones reach Xbox One and PS4 levels on paper but run at higher native resolution and are thermally constrained, "thus in reality, we can't quite reach that generation of visual fidelity yet on mobile devices in real games"; mobile GPUs lack 64-bit atomics (used by software rasterizers of the Nanite kind), have slow gradient sampling and spotty wave intrinsics, so "traditional CPU-based rendering is still the best bet for mainstream mobile phones". Their target is a locked 60 fps on mid-tier phones without throttling at the fidelity of the best Xbox 360 and PS3 games. 10,000 unique draw calls on one thread come close to 60 fps on 99-dollar Android phones and take under 1 ms on the AMD integrated GPUs found in the Steam Deck (verified in slide text). The renderer is designed for Vulkan, Metal and WebGPU. 2023 source, possibly outdated for the newest phones. — [Aaltonen, SIGGRAPH 2023](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2023/AaltonenHypeHypeAdvances2023.pdf)
- Nintendo Switch 2: Nvidia T239, 12 GB RAM, about 1.71 TFLOPS handheld and 3.07 TFLOPS docked, memory bandwidth about 68 GB/s handheld and 102 GB/s docked, DLSS and hardware ray tracing support. — [Wikipedia: Nintendo Switch 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Switch_2)

#### Techniques with phone or handheld numbers

- [D] Sky and aerial perspective: 1.03 ms of LUT work on an iPhone 6s, with a visual match to the PC version; ray-marched clouds "not reasonably achievable on mobile" as of 2020. — [Hillaire, SIGGRAPH 2020 slides](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2020-shading-course/hillaire/s2020_pbs_hillaire_slides.pdf)
- [D] Tencent's mobile "seamless rendering" (a Nanite-style cluster and visibility-buffer pipeline, SIGGRAPH 2024): clusters of up to 128 triangles, a 32-bit visibility buffer, no mesh shaders or bindless because phones lack them; an 80-million-triangle test scene costs about 3 ms of GPU time on a high-end phone (instance culling 0.11 ms, cluster culling 0.07 ms, binning 0.2 ms, rasterization 0.72 ms, material classify 0.92 ms, material shading 0.87 ms) and about 20 ms on a five-year-old low-end phone; frame rate around 60 fps on the test device with lower power draw than classic mesh rendering; no ray tracing. A companion voxel GI ("brick GI") needs 30 MB for a 512 m area versus 160 MB of lightmaps and about 3 ms of GPU time (verified in slide text). — [Cao, SIGGRAPH 2024](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Cao-NanoMesh/AdavanceRealtimeRendering_NanoMesh0810.pdf)
- [D] Terrain: Call of Duty's virtual-textured terrain scales "from low-end mobile devices to high-end PCs" by compositing at most 4 pages per frame on low-end mobile versus 20 on PC and using ASTC. — [Etienne, SIGGRAPH 2023](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2023/Etienne%28ATVI%29-Large%20Scale%20Terrain%20Rendering%20with%20notes%20%28Advances%202023%29.pdf)
- [D] Lighting with many local lights on phones: HypeHype's stochastic tile-based lighting adds 0.66 ms on an Adreno 610 and about 0.35 ms on an Adreno 830 over sun-only lighting at one sample per pixel; with 50 lights in view about 1.5 ms on the budget phone and 0.5 ms on the high-end phone; sun shadows are evaluated per 2 x 2 pixel quad and the sun uses cascaded shadow maps. — [Lempiäinen, SIGGRAPH 2025 notes](https://www.realtimerendering.com/advances/s2025/content/s2025_stb_lighting_v1.1_notes.pdf)
- [V] Unreal: Lumen does not run in the mobile renderer; on Switch 2 Epic targets 60 fps (including handheld) only with the Medium level or the cheaper "Irradiance Field Gather" mode. — [Epic, Lumen Technical Details](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine); [Epic, Lumen Performance Guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-performance-guide-for-unreal-engine)
- [D] PS4-era fallbacks that need no ray tracing, with timings measured at 1080p on a base PS4 (a 2013 console): sky-visibility SH probe grid, 128 x 64 x 64 haze volume, time-sliced paraboloid clouds, bilateral-grid tone mapping (Ghost of Tsushima). — [Patry, SIGGRAPH 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/jpatry_advances2021/index.html)

#### What happens when the real AAA renderers are scaled down

- [V] Crimson Desert on Xbox Series S: 720p at 40 fps or 1080p at 30 fps, ray tracing off. ROG Xbox Ally: 720p, frame generation, 40 fps target. — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/crimson-desert-gets-detailed-pc-console-mac-specs/)
- [M] Crimson Desert on Steam Deck: 29.8 fps average with 1% lows of 14.9 fps at the lowest settings and FSR Ultra Performance; rated Unsupported. — [SteamDeckHQ](https://steamdeckhq.com/news/crimson-desert-steam-deck-verified-badge/)
- [M] Crimson Desert on a MacBook Neo with an A18 Pro (an Apple A-series chip with a 5-core GPU) and 8 GB of shared RAM: 180p upscaled to 540p with frame generation for about 50 fps at the Minimum preset. — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Cheap-MacBook-Neo-runs-Crimson-Desert-pulling-respectable-50-FPS-at-comically-low-resolution.1256332.0.html)
- [M] Base PS5 at "1080p 60" is described as blurry and unstable (high 30s to 60 fps). — [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2026/03/crimson-deserts-performance-mode-not-recommended-on-base-ps5-heres-25-minutes-of-direct-capture-footage)
- [V] Assassin's Creed Shadows dropped ray-traced GI for baked GI to reach 60 fps on PS5 and Series X and was declared not Steam Deck compatible at launch. — [Ubisoft tech Q&A](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/game/assassins-creed/news/4XbPPtFyQEtIMWrA9xVDmZ). A Switch 2 version shipped on 2025-12-02. — [Wikipedia: Assassin's Creed Shadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassin%27s_Creed_Shadows)
- Kingdom Come: Deliverance 2 is scheduled for Switch 2 in 2027; Crimson Desert for Switch 2 in the first half of 2027. — [Wikipedia: KCD2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Come:_Deliverance_II); [Wikipedia: Crimson Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimson_Desert)
- Genshin Impact (Unity, stylized) has run on Android and iOS since 2020-09-28 but its announced Switch version was never released, reportedly due to hardware limitations; it cost about 100 million US dollars to develop and market, with nearly 300 developers by 2021 and about 700 working on it by one later estimate. — [Wikipedia: Genshin Impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genshin_Impact)
- The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild uses a cel-shaded style with landscapes drawn from traditional painting; reviewers criticized an unstable frame rate and a "low resolution of 900p" at launch. Tears of the Kingdom reviews were split on frame rate; the Switch 2 Editions (2025-06-05) raise resolution and frame rate. — [Wikipedia: Breath of the Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Breath_of_the_Wild); [Wikipedia: Tears of the Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Tears_of_the_Kingdom)

### Inferences

- An 8 GB phone sits somewhere between "Xbox 360" and "thermally throttled PS4" in HypeHype's terms. The realistic ceiling for a locked 60 fps on such a device, by the account of a team that specializes in exactly this, is the fidelity of the best Xbox 360 / PS3 open-world games, not of a 2026 console game. A sustained 30 fps PS4-class look at reduced resolution is plausible only on current flagships.
- Translating the reference costs: the PS4-era stack that produces a convincing hazy vista (sky LUTs, small haze volume, time-sliced cloud texture, SH sky probes, tone mapping) adds up to roughly 2 to 3 ms on a base PS4 at 1080p (my sum of the published per-pass figures; that the game ran at 30 fps on PS4 is from memory, unverified). On a phone at 60 fps with a shorter frame and less bandwidth, it has to shrink again (smaller volumes, lower resolution, fewer updates per frame), but nothing in it depends on features phones lack. This is the part of the Crimson Desert look that transfers.
- What does not transfer: per-pixel ray-traced GI, ray-marched volumetric clouds every frame, micro-polygon or voxelized foliage, shadowed vegetation to the horizon, and fluid-simulated fog. The visual loss is flatter indirect light under canopies, softer or missing contact shadows in grass and ferns, visible LOD transitions on trees, card-like foliage up close, lower grass density, static-looking clouds, and a soft image from low internal resolution.
- Stylization is the proven workaround. Every open world that actually ships at stable frame rates on phone-class or Switch-class hardware (Genshin Impact, both Zelda games) chose a painterly look that hides missing GI, coarse geometry and low resolution. No photoreal open world of Crimson Desert's density holds 60 fps on such hardware in any source I found.
- Tencent's result shows a Nanite-like path is technically possible on high-end phones but not across an install base: the same scene costs 3 ms on a flagship and 20 ms (more than a whole 60 fps frame) on a five-year-old device. For one codebase across iOS, Android and desktop this argues for classic discrete LODs plus impostors as the baseline, with a cluster renderer only as a desktop-tier extra.
- HypeHype's warnings bear directly on a wgpu-class engine: prefer pixel-shader passes over compute on tile-based GPUs, keep the pass count low, design for 16-bit floats, and do not rely on mesh shaders, bindless resources or 64-bit atomics. GPU-driven techniques from the PS4 talks (compute-generated grass, GPU placement) are therefore not automatically cheap on phones even where they were cheap on consoles.

### Gaps

- Measured resolution and frame rate for Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom (Switch and Switch 2 Edition) and for Assassin's Creed Shadows on Switch 2 were not retrievable (Digital Foundry and Eurogamer blocked). From memory, unverified: Tears of the Kingdom targets 900p docked and 720p handheld at 30 fps with dynamic resolution and a spatial upscaler.
- Genshin Impact's console and mobile rendering talks (Unite 2020 console rendering talk; the GDC 2021 talk is about art direction, per [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027538/-Genshin-Impact-Crafting-an)) were not retrieved; no per-technique mobile costs for Genshin are in these notes.
- No rendering talks were retrieved for other mobile open worlds (Wuthering Waves, Infinity Nikki, Arknights: Endfield, Where Winds Meet) or for Sky: Children of the Light's mobile cloud technique.
- No measured data was found for hardware ray tracing GI on phones in a shipped open-world game.
- The Tencent and HypeHype device tables are images in the slides; exact device models and test resolutions are not in the extracted text.
- I found no published phone-class cost for grass rendering, vegetation shadows or water.

## 5. Team sizes, timelines and tooling behind these looks

### Takeaway

Every game in this comparison took five to eight years with between 160 and about 2,000 people and budgets from 60 million to over 500 million US dollars, on top of an engine that already existed; the nature look depended on dedicated procedural tooling (Houdini pipelines, GPU placement systems with artist-facing rule editors) and on long single-feature efforts such as a year and a half on wind response. A small agent-driven team can reuse the published runtime techniques, but the content, tooling and tuning effort behind the look is what the headcount paid for.

### Cited Findings

- Crimson Desert: development from early 2018 to March 2026 (about 7 years) on an engine upgraded from Black Desert Online's; the team built a custom XML scripting system for non-programmers; no headcount or budget is public. — [Wikipedia: Crimson Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimson_Desert)
- Red Dead Redemption 2: over eight years of development; all Rockstar studios merged into one team described as 1,600 people, about 2,000 in total; analyst estimates of 370 to 540 million US dollars for development and marketing. — [Wikipedia: Red Dead Redemption 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Dead_Redemption_2)
- Horizon Forbidden West: over 300 full-time staff at peak, about five years, about 212 million US dollars per Sony documents, on the existing Decima engine. — [Wikipedia: Horizon Forbidden West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon_Forbidden_West)
- Ghost of Tsushima: Sucker Punch had 160 staff; six years (2014 to 2020); reported budget 60 million US dollars; about a year and a half was spent making foliage and objects react correctly to wind, and the wind guidance feature took about a week to build and roughly a year to refine. — [Wikipedia: Ghost of Tsushima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_of_Tsushima)
- Assassin's Creed Shadows: led by Ubisoft Quebec with 15 additional studios, in development from 2020 to March 2025, expenses above 100 million euros. Baking its lighting instead of ray tracing would have taken two years and 2 TB. Its technical architect's advice is to move from "maximum quality" to "scalable solutions at every stage of the production", including "procedural generation or even AI". — [Wikipedia: Assassin's Creed Shadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassin%27s_Creed_Shadows); [bake figures](https://consolepcgaming.com/assassins-creed-shadows-lighting-would-take-2-years-and-2tb-of-data-to-bake); [GDC interview with Nicolas Lopez](https://gdconf.com/article/how-ubisoft-brought-feudal-japan-to-life-rendering-assassin-s-creed-shadows-with-nicolas-lopez/)
- Kingdom Come: Deliverance 2: 131 employees in 2019 growing to about 250 by 2024; July 2019 to February 2025 (about five and a half years) on a heavily customized CryEngine. — [Wikipedia: KCD2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Come:_Deliverance_II)
- Avatar: Frontiers of Pandora: Massive Entertainment with eleven other Ubisoft studios; announced March 2017, released December 2023; team size not stated. — [Wikipedia: Avatar: Frontiers of Pandora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatar:_Frontiers_of_Pandora)
- Genshin Impact: about three years and eight months to launch, nearly 300 developers by 2021, about 100 million US dollars. — [Wikipedia: Genshin Impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genshin_Impact)
- Breath of the Wild took five years, with Monolith Soft helping to design landscapes and topography; Tears of the Kingdom was content-complete by March 2022 and then polished for about another year. — [Wikipedia: Breath of the Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Breath_of_the_Wild); [Wikipedia: Tears of the Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Tears_of_the_Kingdom)
- The Witcher 4 demo: about 100 Epic employees were reported assigned to CD Projekt's needs, working in a loop where CD Projekt found bottleneck scenarios and Epic optimized. — [PC Games Hardware](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/The-Witcher-4-Spiel-73629/Specials/Tech-Demo-Hands-on-Impressions-Unreal-Engine-5-1474063/)
- Tooling examples: Far Cry 5's Houdini pipeline regenerated biomes, rivers, cliffs and vegetation for about 100 km² as the terrain changed daily; Horizon Zero Dawn gave artists a graph editor for placement rules evaluated on the GPU so they could "paint a tree line" or turn desert into swamp; Ghost of Tsushima gave artists a functional language compiled to GPU bytecode; Guerrilla built the Nubis Cubed voxel cloud renderer and modelling pipeline in under six months, with a co-developer credited on the original 2 ms optimization. — [PlayStation Blog on Far Cry 5](https://blog.playstation.com/2018/03/22/the-procedural-world-generation-of-far-cry-5/); [Guerrilla placement talk](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/gpu-based-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn); [GDC Vault, Samurai Landscapes](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027352/Samurai-Landscapes-Building-and-Rendering); [Guerrilla, Nubis Cubed](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-cubed); [Schneider 2015](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/The%20Real-time%20Volumetric%20Cloudscapes%20of%20Horizon%20-%20Zero%20Dawn%20-%20ARTR.pdf)
- Frostbite's surfel GI went from 6 to 7 ms in 2021 to 2.5 ms in 2024: three years of optimization by an engine team to make one feature fit 60 fps on consoles. — [Apers, SIGGRAPH 2024](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/EA-GIBS2/Apers_Advances-s2024_Shipping-Dynamic-GI.pdf)

### Inferences

- The smallest team in this set is Sucker Punch at 160 people for six years, and it shipped a deliberately stylized, no-ray-tracing renderer on one fixed console. The rest are 250 to 2,000 people. None targeted phones, and none shipped one codebase across five operating systems.
- The individual rendering features are smaller than those totals suggest: several were one or two engineers for months (the Nubis cloud system, the Tsushima atmosphere and tone-mapping work, the Horizon placement system), and they are documented well enough to re-implement. That is the realistic opening for a small team with coding agents: sky, haze, probe lighting, tone mapping, placement and terrain texturing are each bounded, well-specified projects.
- What is not bounded is tuning and content. A year and a half on wind response, three years to halve a GI cost, a year of polish after content-complete: these are iteration loops judged by human eyes on target hardware. Agents working headless cannot close that loop without an automated visual and performance gate on real phones, and no source here shows such a pipeline producing an AAA nature look.
- The project's content plan (thousands of real species generated procedurally in every growth stage) replaces the art teams that made up most of these headcounts. None of the cited games did this: Crimson Desert's "shape-change" feature and Far Cry 5's recipes vary and place authored plants; they do not generate species. That part of the plan has no precedent in this material and should be treated as research, not engineering.
- Blunt calibration: a prototype that shows a convincing hazy vista with far-field forest, a time-of-day sky and a modest near-field meadow at 60 fps on a current flagship phone is a credible small-team goal using only PS4-era published techniques. A prototype that shows Crimson Desert's close-up forest floor at 60 fps on an 8 GB phone is not supported by any evidence found; the engine's own makers have not done it.

### Gaps

- Pearl Abyss team size (game and engine), budget, and the size of its tools effort: not found.
- Engine-team sizes (as distinct from whole-studio headcounts) for Decima, Anvil, Snowdrop, RAGE and CryEngine at Warhorse: not found.
- No source documents any studio using AI coding agents to build rendering technology of this class, so there is no calibration data for agent-driven development; Ubisoft's remark about "procedural generation or even AI" is advice, not a report of results.
- Person-months per rendering feature are not published for any of these games; the "one or two engineers for months" statement in the inferences rests on talk authorship and the "under six months" Nubis Cubed remark only.
