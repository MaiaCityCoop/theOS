## 1. Perception research: which cues dominate realism and uncanny-valley judgements of faces?

### Takeaway
The lab evidence converges on two things: (a) the eyes/eye region and the skin surface are the cues people use most to decide "real vs artificial", with over-smooth skin and missing corneal reflections identified as the measurable CG tells; (b) what produces eeriness is not low realism as such but *inconsistent* realism between parts (for example photoreal skin with unreal eyes, or realistic material on an off shape). Shape dominates realism ratings when it is stylised; shading matters more than colour.

### Cited Findings
- Uncanny-valley meta-analysis (Diel, Weigelt & MacDorman, ACM Trans. Human-Robot Interaction, 2022): 468 studies screened, 72 included, 247 effect sizes; overall effect Hedges' g = 1.01 [0.80, 1.22]. By stimulus type: face distortion g = 1.46 [0.69, 2.24], distinct entities g = 1.20 [1.02, 1.38], realism render g = 0.99 [0.62, 1.36], morphing g = 0.94 [0.64, 1.24]. Not broken down by facial feature. [EXTRACT] — [Cardiff ORCA record](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/148120/); [IU ScholarWorks record](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/1adaaee1-5748-4213-a370-b9b3d33e2238)
- MacDorman, Green, Ho & Koch 2009, "Too real for comfort?" (Computers in Human Behavior 25(3), 695-710) (pre-2018), four studies: texture photorealism and polygon count increased human likeness; texture photorealism sharpened judgements of ideal facial proportions; atypical proportions were more disturbing on photorealistic faces than on other faces; "a mismatch in the size and texture of the eyes and face was especially prone to make a character eerie". [EXTRACT] — [IU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/e8fb6458-ddad-4067-b877-1207d720ba0c). The university press release adds that eyes enlarged 50 percent combined with photorealistic skin gave strong creepiness. [SECONDARY] — [IU news](https://newsinfo.iu.edu/news/email/normal/11945.html)
- Seyama & Nagayama 2007 (Presence) (pre-2018): the valley appeared only when morphed faces had abnormal features such as bizarre eyes; an almost perfectly realistic appearance is "a necessary but not a sufficient condition for the uncanny valley". [EXTRACT] — [paper PDF listing](https://web.mit.edu/zoz/Public/PRS1511.pdf)
- MacDorman & Chattopadhyay 2016 (Cognition) (pre-2018), N = 548: reducing the *consistency* of realism between features made faces eerier and colder; the manipulated feature groups were eyes/eyelashes/mouth and, separately, skin/nose/eyebrows. [EXTRACT] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26435049/). Companion study, N = 365: inconsistent realism produced eeriness mainly for human-like characters. [EXTRACT] — [Journal of Vision](https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2552688)
- Looser & Wheatley 2010, "The tipping point of animacy" (Psychological Science 21(12)) (pre-2018): on doll-to-human morph continua the point where a face is judged alive sits close to the human end, is a categorical boundary, and "the eyes are disproportionately informative"; a cropped-feature experiment found the eyes the most important single feature. [EXTRACT] — [paper PDF listing](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/celooser/files/looserwheatleypsychsci.pdf). The "about two-thirds of the way to human" figure is from the press release only. [SECONDARY] — [APS release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/what-makes-a-face-look-alive-study-says-its-in-the-eyes.html)
- Counter-evidence on eye primacy: an NDSU study reported in 2014 found that "the eyes and the skin both matter to about the same degree"; skin-tone negation effects were significant, the eye-specific effect was not. [SECONDARY] — [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/06/140610152745.htm)
- Fan et al. 2014, "Human Perception of Visual Realism for Photo and Computer-Generated Face Images" (ACM Trans. Applied Perception 11(2), art. 7; expanded from the SIGGRAPH Asia 2012 brief "Real or Fake?") (pre-2018), two experiments (original/inverted/misaligned faces; original/scrambled/face parts): both holistic and piecemeal processing contribute; shading information is more important than colour for holistic realism; inversion makes realism harder to judge for realistic images only; eyes are the face part with the strongest influence; holistic processing becomes more dominant at lower resolution. Sample sizes not retrieved. [EXTRACT] — [A*STAR repository](https://oar.a-star.edu.sg/communities-collections/articles/13118); [Lindenwood repository](https://digitalcommons.lindenwood.edu/faculty-research-papers/206)
- Vaitonytė, Blomsma, Alimardani & Louwerse 2021, "Realism of the face lies in skin and eyes" (Computers in Human Behavior Reports 3): virtual-agent faces were judged less human-like than photographs; computational image analysis showed the virtual faces "had smoother skin and a reduced number of corneal reflections"; smoothing skin and removing corneal reflections in real photographs (male and female, four races) shifted speeded real-or-not decisions; a third rating experiment on altered and unaltered virtual and human faces "confirmed the causal role of both features". Sample sizes and effect sizes not retrieved. [EXTRACT] — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/341dc5b8369044e98604654003db1446); [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349487221_Realism_of_the_face_lies_in_skin_and_eyes_Evidence_from_virtual_and_human_agents)
- Follow-up 2022 (Cognitive Research): corneal reflections and skin contrast (roughness vs smoothness) also improve memory for both human and virtual faces. [EXTRACT] — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s41235-022-00445-y)
- Zell et al. 2015, "To Stylize or not to Stylize?" (ACM TOG 34(6)) (pre-2018): "shape is the dominant factor when rating realism and expression intensity", material is the key component for appeal; realism alone is a bad predictor of appeal or eeriness; stylised materials lower the realism of realistic shapes, but realistic materials do not raise the realism of stylised shapes. [EXTRACT] — [Bielefeld record](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/record/2772349); confirmed in Zell's dissertation abstract [EXTRACT] — [Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/2632663). A third-party summary adds that the realistic-vs-toon *lighting/shading* difference was very small in that study and that blurring a realistic texture helped appeal. [SECONDARY] — [paper-summary blog](https://100-paper-fungoes.hatenadiary.com/entry/2022/04/30/194728)
- McDonnell, Breidt & Bülthoff 2012, "Render me Real?" (ACM TOG 31(4)) (pre-2018), as summarised by a 2025 paper: cartoon and the most realistic render styles were most appealing, in-between styles least; anomalies such as removing blinks and gaze or freezing half the face were unpleasant; more realistic characters make viewers more sensitive to imperfections. [SECONDARY] — [arXiv 2509.17803](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.17803); bibliographic record [Tübingen](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/handle/10900/51780?show=full)
- CG-versus-photograph detection: Holmes, Banks & Farid 2016 (ACM TAP 13(2), art. 7) (pre-2018): observers have "considerable difficulty" telling CG portraits from photographs, more than five years earlier, are biased towards answering "photographic", and a small amount of training greatly improves accuracy. [EXTRACT] — [paper PDF listing](https://farid.berkeley.edu/downloads/publications/tap16.pdf). Reported numbers: photographs identified 92 percent of the time, CG only 60 percent; after training 76 percent for CG and 85 percent for photographs. [SECONDARY] — [rendering blog](https://ufo3d.com/photorealistic-rendering/). Re-analysis (Mader, Banks & Farid 2017, Perception): average d' across six resolutions 2.25 and resolution had only a relatively small effect. [EXTRACT] — [paper PDF listing](https://farid.berkeley.edu/downloads/publications/perception17.pdf)
- Synthesised (GAN) faces: Nightingale & Farid 2022 (PNAS): N = 315, real-vs-synthetic accuracy 48.2 percent (95% CI [47.1, 49.2]); with training and feedback 59.0 percent. [EXTRACT] — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8872790). Miller et al. 2023 (Psychological Science): White AI faces were judged human more often than real faces ("AI hyperrealism"). [EXTRACT] — [Aberdeen record](https://abdn.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/ai-hyperrealism-why-ai-faces-are-perceived-as-more-real-than-huma). A later review attributes this to AI faces being more average, familiar, attractive and less memorable. [SECONDARY] — [Perception article listing](https://sage.cnpereading.com/doi/10.1177/03010066261442089)
- Eurographics 2026 short paper "Beyond FID": global image statistics (FID) miss "localized semantic artifacts", which are what drive human realism judgements of generated faces. [EXTRACT] — [Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/items/1f506762-ef14-4f66-b9eb-115502d6bbc0)
- Expression and motion: Tinwell et al. 2011 (Computers in Human Behavior 27(2), 741-749) (pre-2018): a lack of upper-face movement increased uncanniness significantly for fear, sadness, disgust and surprise, but not for anger and happiness; even fully animated characters were rated more uncanny than humans. [EXTRACT] — [Aalborg record](https://vbn.aau.dk/en/publications/facial-expression-of-emotion-and-perception-of-the-uncanny-valley/). Companion paper: exaggerated mouth articulation increased uncanniness for characters expressing happiness. [EXTRACT] — [Springer chapter](https://www.springerprofessional.de/effect-of-emotion-and-articulation-of-speech-on-the-uncanny-vall/3759532)
- Real-time digital doubles: a 2025 study building MetaHuman doubles of participants found the realistic avatars rated creepier and less attractive, with familiar faces judged more strictly. Sample size not retrieved. [EXTRACT] — [arXiv 2509.17748](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.17748)
- Hair and eyebrows: no study on hair's contribution to *realism* was found. For *recognition*, Sadr, Jarudi & Sinha 2003 (Perception) (pre-2018) found removing eyebrows from familiar faces hurt recognition significantly more than removing the eyes. [EXTRACT] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12729380/)
- Inversion reduces uncanniness ratings (2023, Frontiers in Psychology), consistent with uncanniness depending on fine configural detail. [EXTRACT] — [Frontiers PDF listing](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1222279/pdf)

### Inferences
- The described head has both of the two measured CG tells from Vaitonytė 2021 at once: smooth even skin, and (under flat ambient light) nothing distinct for the cornea to reflect.
- The consistency findings (MacDorman 2009, 2016) imply that raising one feature to photoreal while others stay stylised (drawn eyebrows, no hair, mirror symmetry) can make the face read *worse*, not better. Effort should lift the weakest feature first.
- "Beyond FID" (2026) and the feature-mismatch work point the same way: realism is gated by the worst local artefact, not by average quality.
- The eye-primacy claim is well supported but not unanimous (the NDSU result puts skin on a par), so "eyes and skin together" is the safer reading than "eyes first".

### Gaps
- Sample sizes and effect sizes for Fan 2014, Vaitonytė 2021, Zell 2015 and Looser & Wheatley 2010 could not be retrieved (full texts blocked).
- No study found that isolates subsurface scattering, specular highlights or hair as separate realism factors with effect sizes.
- No study found that ranks all these cues against each other in one design; any ranking is a synthesis across studies with different stimuli.
- Unverified recall (from training knowledge, not seen this session; verify before citing): Miller et al. 2023 is remembered as reporting that observers used cues such as facial proportionality and familiarity, and that some cues were used in the wrong direction.

## 2. What digital-human practitioners report as mattering most

### Takeaway
Practitioners do not publish numeric rankings; what they say repeatedly is that (a) the eyes "make or break" the result and need dedicated tech (refraction, occlusion at the lids, tear line, lash shadows, limbus), (b) skin realism lives in the specular micro-detail and in non-uniform colour, and (c) skin and eye shading are "futile" without the camera side: tone mapping, depth of field, grain, bloom and good anti-aliasing. All flagship faces start from scans of a real person, so natural shape, asymmetry and texture come for free.

### Cited Findings
- Activision, "Next Generation Character Rendering" (Jimenez & von der Pahlen, GDC 2013) (pre-2018): the session abstract states that efforts toward photorealistic character rendering are futile if they do not include subsurface scattering, realistic eye shading, depth of field, film grain, plausible bloom, film-like tone mapping and accurate anti-aliasing (extract shown with elisions by the search tool). [EXTRACT] — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1018270/Next-Generation-Character)
- Jimenez's earlier blog, as quoted by the press (2012) (pre-2018): efforts toward ultra-realistic skin are futile unless paired with HDR, bloom, depth of field and other supporting techniques. [SECONDARY] — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/19221731/ultra-realistic-rendering-creates-beautiful-eerie-animation)
- Jimenez at GDC 2013, quoted: "You may think that this [type of research] is not relevant to character rendering. I thought the same, but I was wrong." The extract does not show which research he meant. [SECONDARY] — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013-04-04-gdc-2013-activisions-pursuit-of-photorealism.html)
- Digital Ira (USC ICT + Activision, SIGGRAPH 2013 Real-Time Live) (pre-2018): goal was a real-time face that holds up in close-up, any lighting, any viewpoint; 30 scanned expressions, eight used; rendering used screen-space subsurface scattering, translucency, eye refraction and caustics, and a two-lobe specular model with microstructure. [EXTRACT] — [USC ICT project page](https://vgl.ict.usc.edu/Research/DigitalIra). The SIGGRAPH 2014 course "Digital Ira and Beyond" (384 pages) has dedicated sections on microgeometry capture and on rendering eyes. [EXTRACT] — [SIGGRAPH history archive](https://history.siggraph.org/learning/digital-ira-and-beyond-creating-photoreal-real-time-digital-characters-by-pahlen-jimenez-danvoye-debevec-fyffe-et-al/). The course PDF itself could not be opened (host blocked): [course PDF](https://vgl.ict.usc.edu/Publications/2014/Digital%20Ira%20and%20Beyond%20Creating%20Photoreal%20Real-Time%20Digital%20Characters.pdf)
- Digital Emily (Image Metrics + USC ICT, 2008-2010) (pre-2018): face replacement built on scanned diffuse, specular and animated displacement maps; cross-polarised capture separates the specular component, which is then used to reconstruct high-resolution geometry. [EXTRACT] — [USC ICT project page](https://vgl.ict.usc.edu/Research/DigitalEmily). [SECONDARY, transcript mirror] — [talk transcript](https://digitalcast.jp/v/14149)

[SECONDARY] — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/the-last-of-us-2-is-so-detailed-naughty-dog-had-to-overhaul-their-rendering-to-get-characters-eyes-perfect); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/so-much-work-went-into-the-last-of-us-part-iis-eyes-1845162613)
- Naughty Dog, Uncharted 4 (SIGGRAPH 2016) (pre-2018): skin and eyes used Disney diffuse, GGX specular and screen-space subsurface scattering; hair used Kajiya-Kay. [EXTRACT] — [SIGGRAPH history archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=71423)
- Call of Duty: Modern Warfare II (2022): characters are digital doubles of their actors; DI4D processed over four hours of 4D facial capture, combined with Light Stage data, also used to refine the per-actor FACS rigs. No statement ranking rendering factors was found. [SECONDARY] — [Animation Magazine](https://animationmagazine.net/2023/07/delivering-the-facial-animation-for-call-of-duty-modern-warfare-ii)
- Approximations are perceptually sufficient for subsurface scattering: Jimenez et al. 2009 (ACM TAP) (pre-2018), two-alternative forced choice at about 60 cm: "independent of distance and light position, the images rendered using the novel [screen-space] shader have as high visual realism as a previously developed physically-based shader". [EXTRACT] — [paper PDF listing](http://giga.cps.unizar.es/~diegog/ficheros/pdf_papers/TAP_Jimenez_LR.pdf)

### Inferences
- Across studios the recurring list is: eyes; skin specular micro-detail; subsurface scattering; hair of all kinds (brows, lashes, stubble, vellus); and the camera/post pipeline. Eyes are the only item a practitioner explicitly calls decisive ("makes or breaks").
- Every flagship pipeline is scan-based. None of the sources describes reaching photoreal with a procedural, symmetric head, so there is no practitioner evidence that it can be done; the features scans bring implicitly (asymmetry, age detail, colour variation) have to be added on purpose.
- The Naughty Dog lesson (eyes that only worked in some lighting, fixed by starting from photo reference under several lights) matches this project's method of gating against a photograph.

### Gaps
- No practitioner source with an explicit numeric or ordered ranking of factors was found. The Digital Ira course notes, the Unity posts and the Naughty Dog slides could not be opened to look for one.
- Nothing retrieved on a Call of Duty face *rendering* talk, or on MetaHuman/Epic documentation that ranks skin, eyes and hair.
- The exact Jimenez wording should be checked on GDC Vault and iryoku.com before quoting verbatim.

## 3. How much comes from lighting, exposure, tone mapping, grading, depth of field and lens, versus the asset?

### Takeaway
No source quantifies the split, and no controlled A/B study of image-based lighting or a filmic tone curve on the same face asset was found. What exists: one 2024 experiment showing portrait lighting alone changes eeriness and realism ratings of a photoreal virtual human; older evidence that shading carries more of realism than colour; and strong practitioner statements that skin and eye shading are "futile" without the photographic pipeline.

### Cited Findings
- Wisessing & McDonnell 2024, "Blinded by the light" (ACM Symposium on Applied Perception): photoreal virtual humans rendered under seven portrait lighting patterns (key-light angle varied; fill and rim fixed). Lighting did not raise perceived emotion intensity, but "lighting design can effect eeriness ratings", presented as new for the uncanny valley; frontal, Paramount and Rembrandt lighting all kept eeriness low. Sample size not retrieved. [EXTRACT] — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3675231.3675245). A later paper summarises it as showing that portrait lighting alone can significantly change perceived realism and believability. [SECONDARY, via search summary of the same listing] — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3675231.3675245)
- Wisessing et al. 2020, "Enlighten Me" (ACM TOG 39(3)): brighter conditions increased appeal across all experiments; dark shadows (low key-to-fill ratio) lowered appeal. Mostly stylised characters. [EXTRACT] — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3383195)
- Fan et al. 2014 (pre-2018): shading information is more important than colour for holistic realism judgements of faces. [EXTRACT] — [A*STAR repository](https://oar.a-star.edu.sg/communities-collections/articles/13118)
- Zell et al. 2015 (pre-2018): switching lighting/shading between realistic and toon made only a very small difference to ratings. [SECONDARY] — [paper-summary blog](https://100-paper-fungoes.hatenadiary.com/entry/2022/04/30/194728)
- Rademacher et al. 2001 (pre-2018): controlled experiments on five factors of "photograph or CG?" judgements: shadow softness, surface smoothness, number of light sources, number of objects, variety of shapes. Per-factor results not retrieved. [EXTRACT] — [Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/EGWR.EGWR01.235-248)
- Practitioner statements that the camera side is required: see section 2 (GDC 2013 abstract; KSL quote of Jimenez). [EXTRACT]/[SECONDARY] — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1018270/Next-Generation-Character); [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/19221731/ultra-realistic-rendering-creates-beautiful-eerie-animation)
- Lighting dependence of eyes at Naughty Dog (good only under some lighting until reworked against photo reference). [SECONDARY] — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/the-last-of-us-2-is-so-detailed-naughty-dog-had-to-overhaul-their-rendering-to-get-characters-eyes-perfect)
- Tone mapping and linear lighting: Hable's "Uncharted 2: HDR Lighting" (GDC 2010) (pre-2018) covers gamma/linear-space lighting and filmic tone mapping as a package. [EXTRACT] — [slides listing](https://www.slideshare.net/naughty_dog/lighting-shading-by-john-hable). Frictional Games on adopting it: HDR and tone mapping are "all for nothing" if calculations are not done in linear colour space. [SECONDARY] — [Frictional blog](https://frictionalgames.com/?p=1090)
- Image-based lighting as standard practice for matching CG to photographs: capture an HDR panorama where the object is to appear and light with it. [EXTRACT] — [USC ICT](https://vgl.ict.usc.edu/Research/SSLP)
- Photographic defaults as cues: observers lean towards answering "photographic" (Holmes et al. 2016). [EXTRACT] — [paper PDF listing](https://farid.berkeley.edu/downloads/publications/tap16.pdf). AI images that pass as photographs commonly show one focal subject and shallow depth of field (descriptive, not causal). [EXTRACT] — [arXiv 2409.17484](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.17484v1)
- Corneal reflections, one of the two measured realism cues, are by definition images of the light sources on the cornea. [EXTRACT] — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s41235-022-00445-y)

### Inferences
- Lighting is not independent of the asset cues: the two strongest measured cues (corneal reflections, skin roughness/contrast) only exist in the image if there are distinct, shaped light sources. Flat ambient light removes both, whatever the asset quality. This is the main argument for fixing lighting before judging any asset change.
- The evidence supports "lighting can move eeriness and realism ratings of the same asset" (Wisessing 2024) but not a percentage share. Any statement such as "half of realism is lighting" would be unsupported.
- Warm, soft, frontal-to-Rembrandt portrait lighting (the reference photograph's style) is within the patterns found to keep eeriness low.
- Zell 2015's small lighting effect does not contradict this: that comparison was realistic vs toon shading on mixed stylisation levels, not flat ambient vs portrait lighting on a realistic head.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found of image-based lighting vs simple lights, of filmic vs linear/clamped tone curves, of depth of field, or of film grain on face realism judgements. Evidence here is practitioner assertion only.
- Sample size and effect sizes for Wisessing & McDonnell 2024 not retrieved.
- Unverified recall (not seen this session): Rademacher 2001 is remembered as finding shadow softness and surface smoothness significant for perceived realism, while number of lights, number of objects and shape variety were not. Verify before citing.

## 4. Which micro-scale features matter at portrait distance versus a few metres?

### Takeaway
There is no study that maps individual micro-features to viewing distance. Indirect evidence says fine detail matters most in close-up and mostly through its effect on highlights, while at lower resolution or greater distance holistic shading takes over and resolution matters little for the real-or-CG decision.

### Cited Findings
- Skin smoothness and corneal reflections are the two features with experimental (causal) support for human-likeness. [EXTRACT] — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/341dc5b8369044e98604654003db1446)
- Pores and fine wrinkles are conveyed chiefly by specular reflection (Debevec). [SECONDARY] — [talk transcript](https://digitalcast.jp/v/14149). Measured skin microstructure at 10 micrometres improves reproduction of specular effects (pre-2018). [EXTRACT] — [Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v32i2pp335-344)
- Resolution had only a relatively small effect on telling CG portraits from photographs (Mader, Banks & Farid 2017) (pre-2018). [EXTRACT] — [paper PDF listing](https://farid.berkeley.edu/downloads/publications/perception17.pdf). Holistic processing becomes more dominant at lower resolution (Fan 2014) (pre-2018). [EXTRACT] — [A*STAR repository](https://oar.a-star.edu.sg/communities-collections/articles/13118)
- In virtual reality, beyond 3 m participants had to enlarge a virtual human's head to make out facial details. [EXTRACT] — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3571074)
- Peach fuzz: "subtle but important" for skin shading (Unity, 2022). [EXTRACT] — [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/news/introducing-enemies-the-latest-evolution-in-high-fidelity-digital-humans-from-unity)
- Eye wetness and contact: tear-line mesh with blurred normals between lid and eyeball, shader ambient occlusion at the lids (Unity, 2020). [EXTRACT] — [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/technology/making-of-the-heretic-digital-human-character-gawain). Eyelash and eyelid shadows on the eyeball and a correct limbus (Naughty Dog, 2020). [SECONDARY] — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/so-much-work-went-into-the-last-of-us-part-iis-eyes-1845162613)
- Skin colour variation: skin colour is driven mostly by melanin and haemoglobin; a measured model varies haemoglobin maps with expression at negligible rendering cost (Jimenez et al. 2010) (pre-2018). [EXTRACT] — [paper PDF listing](https://www.iryoku.com/skincolor/downloads/A-Practical-Appearance-Model-for-Dynamic-Facial-Color.pdf). Newer capture work models dynamic albedo (blood flow), dynamic specular intensity and per-frame normals (Disney Research, 2018). [EXTRACT] — [paper PDF listing](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Practical-Dynamic-Facial-Appearance-Modeling-and-Acquisition-Paper1.pdf)
- Translucency (thin regions) was part of the Digital Ira feature set (pre-2018); no perception data attached. [EXTRACT] — [USC ICT project page](https://vgl.ict.usc.edu/Research/DigitalIra)
- A screen-space subsurface approximation matched a physically based one in perceived realism "independent of distance and light position" (pre-2018). [EXTRACT] — [paper PDF listing](http://giga.cps.unizar.es/~diegog/ficheros/pdf_papers/TAP_Jimenez_LR.pdf)

### Inferences
- (Arithmetic inference with stated assumptions, not a sourced fact.) If a face about 150 mm wide spans about 800 pixels in a portrait framing, one pixel covers roughly 0.2 mm. Individual pores are near or below that, so even at portrait distance they act mainly by breaking up the highlight and roughening the shading, not as resolved dots. At a few metres (face about 100-200 pixels wide, roughly 1 mm per pixel) only their averaged effect on gloss survives.
- Likely portrait-distance set, from the practitioner and perception evidence together: corneal highlight shape, tear line and lid contact shadow, lash shadowing, highlight breakup on forehead/nose/cheeks, colour mottling (redder nose, cheeks and ears), fine wrinkles at the eye corners, vellus rim. Likely few-metre set: overall shading and shadow softness, large-scale colour zones, eye highlight presence, hair silhouette, expression shape.
- Because subsurface approximations already test as perceptually equivalent, further work on the scattering model is probably low-yield compared with surface detail and colour variation.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found on visibility thresholds for pores, fine wrinkles, peach fuzz or tear line by distance or pixel density.
- No perception study found on ear/nose back-scatter (translucency) as a realism cue.
- Fan 2014's and Mader 2017's resolution levels were not retrieved, so "lower resolution" cannot be translated into pixels across the face.

## 5. Facial asymmetry and imperfection

### Takeaway
Real faces are never symmetric: 3D surface studies put average whole-face asymmetry in healthy adults at roughly 0.6-0.8 mm, with individual landmarks differing by up to about 3-5 mm before clinicians call it abnormal. Whether perfect symmetry lowers *realism* has not been tested directly; the symmetry literature is about attractiveness and is method-dependent.

### Cited Findings
- Whole-face asymmetry in healthy adults from 3D stereophotogrammetry: mean RMS 0.63 ± 0.16 mm (cohort of 350) and 0.80 ± 0.24 mm (cohort of 100); another study reports mean 0.72 mm (SD 0.72, range 0.25-3.04 mm), higher in males (0.77 mm) than females (0.67 mm) and increasing with age. The search tool merged several papers, so the mapping of each number to a paper is uncertain. [EXTRACT, source mapping uncertain] — candidates: [PubMed 38637251 (2024)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38637251); [PMC4614853](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4614853/)
- Landmark-level tolerances: maximal normal asymmetry at the outer eye corner (exocanthion) 3.04 mm; normal range for lateral facial dimensions 4-5 mm; Farkas & Cheung as cited there: asymmetry under about 3 mm is indiscernible in a normal face, and left-right orbital difference can reach 4 mm without the face looking asymmetric (pre-2018). [EXTRACT/SECONDARY for the Farkas figure] — [Huang et al. 2013, Orthodontics & Craniofacial Research](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ocr.12010)
- Children aged 6-12 (N = 600): normal cohort average 0.96 ± 0.52 mm. [EXTRACT] — [PMC6571690](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6571690/)
- Simulated asymmetry on virtual (FaceGen) faces, 2023: degree, type and location all affect ratings; the chin was the most sensitive location; orthodontists were more critical than laypersons; the paper states that perfect facial symmetry does not exist in real people. [EXTRACT] — [BMC Oral Health](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12903-023-03167-9)
- Attractiveness, disputed: some studies found slightly asymmetric faces preferred over perfectly symmetric ones (Kowner 1996) (pre-2018). [SECONDARY] — [thesis on arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.4676). Rhodes et al. 1998 (pre-2018) argue that this came from mirror-half "chimeras", which introduce structural abnormalities; when symmetry is made by blending a face with its mirror image, perfectly symmetric versions were preferred. [EXTRACT] — [paper PDF listing](https://homepages.uc.edu/~martinj/Taste%20Food%20&%20Wine/Aesthetics_of_Food_&_Drink/Rhodes%20-%20Facial%20symmetry%20and%20the%20perception%20of%20beauty.pdf)
- Virtual characters: an EPFL study (2017) (pre-2018) suggests purely symmetric faces and expressions may contribute to an artificial look, but reports mixed results (basic emotions rated less realistic with asymmetry added; complex emotions differed). [EXTRACT, truncated] — [EPFL postprint](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/227463/files/postprint_evaluating-sensitivity-virtual-characters-facial-asymmetry.pdf). A Purdue thesis found expression asymmetry significantly affects believability, appeal and naturalness of a virtual agent (direction not shown in the extract). [EXTRACT] — [Purdue thesis](https://hammer.purdue.edu/articles/thesis/THE_EFFECT_OF_FACIAL_EXPRESSION_ASYMMETRY_ON_THE_BELIEVABILITY_APPEAL_AND_NATURALNESS_OF_VIRTUAL_AGENTS/24718734)
- Imperfection as a realism cue: AI faces rated "more real than real" are more average and more attractive than real faces, which shows that regularity by itself does not make a face read as fake. [SECONDARY] — [Perception article listing](https://sage.cnpereading.com/doi/10.1177/03010066261442089). Against that, over-smooth skin does lower human-likeness. [EXTRACT] — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/341dc5b8369044e98604654003db1446)

### Inferences
- A sensible engineering target, derived from the norms above: break mirror symmetry by about 0.5-1 mm RMS over the surface, with a few landmark offsets of 1-3 mm (eye corners, mouth corners, nostrils, brow height), and never more than about 3 mm at any landmark. This is an inference from clinical norms, not a tested realism threshold.
- The stronger case for asymmetry is in *texture and expression* rather than bone shape: mirrored skin marks, mirrored highlights and a perfectly even smile are things a viewer can spot as duplicated, whereas sub-millimetre shape asymmetry is below what clinicians call discernible.
- The evidence does not support "symmetry is a major realism killer"; it supports "over-regular surface is". Rank asymmetry as moderate, with thin evidence.

### Gaps
- No study found that compares a perfectly symmetric CG face with a naturally asymmetric version of the same face on a real-or-CG judgement.
- No practitioner statement on asymmetry was retrieved (scan-based pipelines get it implicitly).
- Number-to-paper mapping for the 0.63, 0.80 and 0.72 mm figures needs checking against the primaries; the figure 3.04 mm appears in two extracts and may be one study reported twice.

## 6. The smile: what distinguishes a genuine one, and how renderings fail at it

### Takeaway
A whole-face (Duchenne) smile is lip-corner pull (AU12, zygomaticus major) plus cheek raise (AU6, the outer ring of orbicularis oculi): the cheek mass moves up and towards the eye, the eye opening narrows from below, skin under the eye bunches and lines appear at the outer eye corner. Observers reliably rate such smiles as more genuine, although as a signal of felt emotion the marker is disputed and largely tracks smile intensity. For a render, the relevant point is perceptual: a strong mouth smile without the matching eye-region deformation reads as posed or wrong.

### Cited Findings
- Coding: Duchenne smile = AU6 + AU12; AU6 reflects orbicularis oculi, pars orbitalis; AU12 is zygomaticus major pulling the lip corner diagonally up towards the cheekbone. [EXTRACT] — [PMC9828522](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9828522); [FACS table in PMC7347182](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7347182/table/pone.0235545.t001)
- AU6 appearance: skin around temples and cheeks is drawn towards the eyes, narrowing the eye opening; cheeks lift; crow's-feet wrinkling at the outer eye corners. Electrical stimulation of the orbital part "elevated the cheek superomedially, pushing the skin towards the eye"; not all textbook AU6 changes were reproduced by stimulating that muscle alone. Lid tightening (AU7, palpebral part) is a separate action. [EXTRACT] — [Waller et al., PMC2826128](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2826128/)
- Nasolabial fold: the retrieved coding tables attribute furrow deepening to AU10 (upper lip raiser) and AU11 (nasolabial deepener, zygomaticus minor), not to AU6. [EXTRACT] — [PMC9828522](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9828522); [FACS table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7347182/table/pone.0235545.t001)
- Perception meta-analysis (Gunnery & Ruben 2016, Cognition and Emotion 30(3), 501-515) (pre-2018): Duchenne smiles and the people showing them are rated more positively (authentic, genuine, real, attractive, trustworthy); the difference is larger for video than for photographs and for elicited than posed smiles. Pooled effect size not retrieved. [EXTRACT] — [PhilPapers record](https://api.philpapers.org/rec/GUNPOD)
- Causal test (Etcoff et al. 2021, Frontiers in Psychology): photographs of the same patients smiling before and after botulinum toxin paralysed the lateral orbicularis oculi; smiles with the muscle active were rated "more felt, spontaneous, intense, and happier"; after treatment faces looked younger but not more attractive. [EXTRACT] — [UCL Discovery PDF listing](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10120320/1/Etcoff%20et%20al%282021%29.pdf)
- Dispute about meaning: Girard et al. 2019 (136 participants): controlling for smile intensity "substantially reduced" the link between Duchenne smiles and positive emotion; the Duchenne smile is "likely an artifact of smile intensity". [EXTRACT] — [PMC7193529](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7193529). Girard et al. 2021 (Affective Science 2(1), 32-47): eye constriction carries limited information about *felt* emotion but more about *perceived* positive emotion, including some unique information beyond other smile features, and depends on context. [EXTRACT] — [author page](https://affcom.ku.edu/publications/articles/girard2021a.html)
- Deliberate production: 71 percent of posed smiles met Duchenne criteria (Gunnery et al. 2013), against an older claim that only about 20 percent of people can control AU6 voluntarily (Frank & Ekman 1993); Duchenne smiles showed much higher AU12 intensity than non-Duchenne ones (reported as 3.11 vs 0.97; scale unclear in the extract). [SECONDARY] — [Frontiers review, 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01126/full)
- Virtual agents: a polite smile is modelled without cheek raise; AU6 is more present in amused than in polite or embarrassed smiles. [EXTRACT] — [AAMAS 2012 paper](https://ifaamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2012/papers/2A_5.pdf); [Ochs et al. draft](https://radoslawniewiadomski.github.io/papers/CP12_ochsetal_draft.pdf)
- Render failure modes reported: missing upper-face movement raises uncanniness (though not significantly for happiness), and exaggerated mouth movement raises it for happiness (pre-2018). [EXTRACT] — [Aalborg record](https://vbn.aau.dk/en/publications/facial-expression-of-emotion-and-perception-of-the-uncanny-valley/); [Springer chapter](https://www.springerprofessional.de/effect-of-emotion-and-articulation-of-speech-on-the-uncanny-vall/3759532). Poor mouth-corner topology gives the "uncanny MetaHuman smile". [SECONDARY] — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/drip-fit-for-a-king-realistic-game-ready-character-made-with-ue5-5). Teeth sliding rigidly behind lips read as off. [SECONDARY] — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/10/epic-shows-off-unreals-nearly-real-metahuman-3d-character-creator)
- Expression-dependent detail used by practitioners: tension-driven wrinkle maps and blood flow (Unity). [EXTRACT] — [Enemies page](https://unity.com/demos/enemies). Expression-dependent haemoglobin maps (Jimenez et al. 2010) (pre-2018). [EXTRACT] — [paper PDF listing](https://www.iryoku.com/skincolor/downloads/A-Practical-Appearance-Model-for-Dynamic-Facial-Color.pdf)

### Inferences
- Since AU6 intensity normally rises with AU12 intensity (Girard), a broad tooth-showing smile with relaxed, wide eyes is a combination real faces rarely produce; viewers do not need to "detect fakeness", they see an unusual configuration. The eye-region deformation must scale with the mouth.
- Checklist for a fixed whole-face smile, derived from the anatomy above: cheek volume displaced up and slightly inwards; lower lid pushed up and straightened so the eye opening narrows from below; a bulge and fine horizontal creases under the eye; radiating lines at the outer corner (strong in a middle-aged face); slight lowering of the outer brow skin; deepened nose-to-mouth fold that bends around the raised cheek; mouth corners that tuck into the cheek rather than float on it. The first four items have direct sources above; the last three rest on the unverified recall below.
- Typical render failures, inferred from the sources: the mouth is posed but the cheek and lid geometry are unchanged; folds are painted (albedo/normal) without volume change, so shading under directional light contradicts them; the smile is mirror-symmetric; skin colour and wrinkles do not respond to the pose.
- The Botox study is the best evidence that the outer-eye deformation itself, in still photographs, changes how genuine a smile looks.

### Gaps
- No source retrieved on tooth display in genuine smiles (how many teeth, gum line, lip-to-tooth occlusion shadow) or on how teeth rendering affects realism.
- Gunnery & Ruben's pooled effect size and number of studies not retrieved.
- A 2021 study on virtual characters reporting that smile asymmetry significantly affected perceived genuineness appeared in a search summary, but the source could not be pinned to a specific URL; treat as unverified.
- Unverified recall (FACS manual, Ekman, Friesen & Hager 2002; not seen this session): AU12 by itself also deepens the nasolabial furrow and raises the infraorbital triangle (cheek), and strong AU12 narrows the eyes and bags the lower lid; AU6 lowers the outer brow slightly and deepens the lower-lid furrow. This conflicts in part with the retrieved tables, which list furrow deepening only under AU10/AU11. Check the manual.

## 7. Ranked list of factors (synthesis)

### Takeaway
**This ranking is my synthesis, not a published result.** For a near-static portrait judged against a warm, softly lit photograph, the order of expected payoff is: lighting and photographic image formation first (it gates the two strongest measured cues), then eyes and eye region, then skin surface non-uniformity, then shape plausibility and cross-feature consistency, then whole-face smile coherence, then hair of all kinds, with further subsurface-scattering work and resolved micro-detail last.

### Cited Findings
- Cues with experimental support for real-vs-artificial judgements: corneal reflections and skin roughness (Vaitonytė 2021) [EXTRACT] — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/341dc5b8369044e98604654003db1446); eyes (Looser & Wheatley 2010; Fan 2014) [EXTRACT] — [paper PDF listing](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/celooser/files/looserwheatleypsychsci.pdf), [A*STAR repository](https://oar.a-star.edu.sg/communities-collections/articles/13118); shading over colour (Fan 2014) [EXTRACT] — [A*STAR repository](https://oar.a-star.edu.sg/communities-collections/articles/13118); shape when stylised (Zell 2015) [EXTRACT] — [Bielefeld record](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/record/2772349); feature-realism consistency (MacDorman & Chattopadhyay 2016, N = 548) [EXTRACT] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26435049/); portrait lighting on eeriness (Wisessing & McDonnell 2024) [EXTRACT] — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3675231.3675245); outer-eye smile deformation on perceived genuineness (Etcoff 2021) [EXTRACT] — [UCL Discovery PDF listing](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10120320/1/Etcoff%20et%20al%282021%29.pdf).
- Practitioner priorities: eyes "make or break" (3Lateral) [EXTRACT] — [MetaHuman spotlight](https://www.metahuman.com/spotlights/behind-the-scenes-on-blue-dot-metahuman-animator-brings-stunning-fidelity-to-cinematics); skin and eyes futile without tone mapping, depth of field, grain, bloom, anti-aliasing (Activision) [EXTRACT] — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1018270/Next-Generation-Character); specular micro-detail (USC ICT) [EXTRACT] — [Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v32i2pp335-344); peach fuzz "subtle but important" (Unity) [EXTRACT] — [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/news/introducing-enemies-the-latest-evolution-in-high-fidelity-digital-humans-from-unity).
- Cheap subsurface approximations already match physically based ones perceptually (Jimenez 2009) [EXTRACT] — [paper PDF listing](http://giga.cps.unizar.es/~diegog/ficheros/pdf_papers/TAP_Jimenez_LR.pdf).
- Local artefacts, not global statistics, drive realism judgements (Eurographics 2026) [EXTRACT] — [Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/items/1f506762-ef14-4f66-b9eb-115502d6bbc0).

### Inferences
Ranked synthesis for this head (current state in brackets). Evidence strength: strong = controlled experiments on the cue; moderate = one experiment or consistent practitioner reports; thin = indirect or assertion only.

1. **Lighting and photographic image formation** [flat, cool ambient]. Directional soft key plus environment light, real shadowing and occlusion, exposure and warm white balance, a filmic tone curve; then depth of field, grain, anti-aliasing. Rationale: shading outweighs colour (Fan 2014); lighting alone shifts eeriness/realism of a photoreal head (Wisessing 2024); corneal reflections and visible skin relief cannot exist without shaped lights; practitioners call asset work futile without it. Strength: moderate for lighting, thin for tone curve, depth of field and grain (no controlled study found). Ranked first because it gates items 2 and 3 and is the largest gap to the reference photograph, not because a study measured it as the largest effect.
2. **Eyes and eye region** [separate eye pieces]. Clear corneal reflection of the lights, wet tear line, soft lid contact shadow, lash shadows, correct limbus, sclera that is shaded and not white, lids that touch the globe. Strength: strong (Looser & Wheatley; Fan; Vaitonytė; MacDorman 2009) plus the only explicit practitioner "make or break". Dispute: one study puts skin on a par with eyes.
3. **Skin surface non-uniformity** [smooth, even]. Highlight breakup from meso/micro relief, pores via specular rather than albedo, fine wrinkles, colour variation from blood and pigment (red nose/cheeks/ears, paler forehead, mottling), age marks. Strength: strong for "over-smooth reads artificial" (Vaitonytė), moderate for the specific remedies (practitioner).
4. **Shape plausibility, age cues and consistency across features** [clean procedural, mirror-symmetric]. Middle-aged volume and folds, sub-millimetre asymmetry with a few millimetre-scale landmark offsets, and no single feature left at a lower realism level than the rest. Strength: strong that shape dominates when stylised (Zell) and that inconsistency causes eeriness (MacDorman); thin that symmetry itself hurts realism.
5. **Whole-face smile coherence** [fixed smile]. Cheek raise, eye narrowing from below, under-eye bunching, outer-corner lines, fold deepening, mouth corners embedded in the cheek, teeth shadowed by the lips. Strength: strong that eye-region deformation changes perceived genuineness (Etcoff; Gunnery & Ruben); thin on how much it changes real-vs-CG judgements; disputed as an emotion signal (Girard).
6. **Hair: eyebrows, eyelashes, scalp hair, stubble, vellus** [drawn eyebrows, no hair]. Strength: thin. No realism study found; support is practitioner tooling and the consistency argument (drawn brows on realistic skin is a realism mismatch). Could deserve a higher rank; the evidence simply is not there to say.
7. **Subsurface scattering refinements and thin-region translucency** [pre-integrated shader present]. Required by every practitioner, but approximations already test as equivalent, so further gains are likely small. Strength: moderate that it is necessary, moderate that more is low-yield.
8. **Resolved micro-detail** (individual pores as geometry, iris caustics, peach-fuzz geometry). Matters only in tight close-up; resolution had a small effect on real-vs-CG decisions. Strength: thin.
9. **Motion** (blinks, saccades, micro-movement), if the head is shown live rather than as a still. Not ranked against the others for a still comparison, but for a moving face the evidence that frozen or missing eye motion is unpleasant is moderate (McDonnell 2012, secondary), and smile effects are larger in video (Gunnery & Ruben).

Cross-cutting rule from the consistency and local-artefact findings: at each step, fix the single worst-looking feature; do not push one feature far ahead of the rest.

### Gaps
- No single study ranks these factors against each other; the order of 1-3 rests on combining different experiments with practitioner statements, and reasonable people could swap 1 and 2.
- The size of the lighting/tone-curve contribution relative to the asset is unmeasured in the literature found.
- Hair, symmetry, tooth rendering and translucency have no direct realism evidence; their ranks are the least certain.
- Full texts were unreachable in this session, so sample sizes and effect sizes are missing for most studies; allow these hosts (listed at the top) to verify before publication.
