## Skin colour from chromophores: spectral and RGB models of melanin and haemoglobin, their parameters, the published spatial distribution over a face, and how maps are authored without a scan

### Takeaway
The optics are fully published and scan-free: a two-layer melanin/haemoglobin model needs four to six scalar parameters, whose spectra and ranges are in open code and tables, and it reduces to a small lookup texture in real time (Jimenez 2010 did exactly that). What is not published is a reusable map of blood and melanin over a face: every paper that shows such maps measured them on a subject, so a scan-free head must author them from regional statistics (L\*a\*b\* by site, erythema ranking by site) and anatomy rules.

### Inferences
- The scan-free recipe that the sources support is: per-vertex (or low-resolution map) melanin and haemoglobin fractions → a precomputed 2D lookup (melanin × haemoglobin → RGB albedo, optionally a second for scattering radius) → the six-Gaussian or a Burley-type profile for subsurface blur. Jimenez 2010 shows this is real time with negligible overhead; the lookup can be generated at load time from the Jacques formulas with a two-layer diffusion or Kubelka-Munk solve (about 64×64 entries × 30 wavelengths) in well under a second in a worker; a Monte Carlo version would take minutes and is better done offline in the project's own code.
- Because blood distribution is low-frequency, the project's existing per-vertex attributes are an adequate carrier for melanin, haemoglobin and oxygenation; only freckles, moles and capillary mottling need a higher-frequency procedural term in the shader.
- A defensible authored blood map from the statistics above: raise haemoglobin on cheeks, nose and perinasal area, chin and ears; keep the forehead between cheek and chin; lower it on the eyelids' thin skin but lower oxygenation there (bluish), and give lips a thin epidermis with little melanin. The ranking is sourced; the magnitudes are not, so they must be tuned so the rendered L\*a\*b\* of forehead and cheek land inside the Xiao 2017 means ± SD.
- The Xiao 2017 standard deviations (L\* about 5, a\* about 3, b\* about 2.5 within one group) are a usable published prior for sampling individuals.
- BioSkin's decoder is trained purely on simulation of published optics, so it is class (a) in spirit, but its pretrained weights are third-party assets; under "our own code only" the table must be regenerated, not downloaded.

### Gaps
- No published, reusable "average face" haemoglobin or melanin map (as an image or as per-region fractions) was found; all maps shown in Donner 2008, Jimenez 2010, Gitlina 2020 and Aliaga 2023 belong to captured subjects.
- The exact parameter tables of Donner-Jensen 2006, Donner 2008 and BioSpec, and Jimenez 2010's measurement device, subject count and lookup construction, could not be read (hosts blocked); the [M] items above need checking.
- No numeric error (ΔE or spectral RMSE) between any of these models and measured skin could be retrieved; only the authors' qualitative statements.

## Pores and static microgeometry: procedural synthesis and the published statistics it needs

### Takeaway
The strongest scan-free method is the Disney graph model (pores as nodes, micro-wrinkles as edges, depth grown iteratively, rasterised by shader shape functions), which needs only a pore spacing, a direction field and about nine scalar parameters; a 2025 user study ranks it above the plain noise used in real-time faces but below the same model fitted to a photograph. Dermatology supplies the scales: follicle spacing near 0.5 mm, primary lines 20–100 µm deep, secondary lines 5–40 µm.

### Cited Findings
- (a when parameters are authored, c when fitted) Weiss, Moulin, Chandran, Zoss, Gotardo and Bradley 2023: pores are sampled on the skin as graph nodes; edges between them are candidate wrinkles; an iterative optimisation "inspired by pedestrian trail formation" assigns edge weights; the graph is turned into a displacement map by shape functions "implemented in graphics shaders"; it "can scale to millions of wrinkles" and targets about 16k² maps that image-based reconstruction cannot recover; shown both on reconstructions of real subjects and in an artist-driven workflow for fictional characters. [X] — [CGF 42(5), SGP 2023](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14904), [Disney PDF, host blocked](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/app/uploads/2023/07/GraphBasedSynthesisForSkinMicroWrinkles.pdf)
- (a) The model's parameters as tabulated by a later paper: φ average pore distance; β blending strength from wrinkles to pores; θ main wrinkle direction; μ orientation uniformity; δ influence of wrinkle length; σ contiguity; γ tolerance of wrinkle crossing; τ tolerance of similar wrinkles; ρ depth deposit per iteration. Each wrinkle starts at depth 0 and, with a probability that depends on those parameters and on its neighbours, is deepened by d ← d + Δt·(1−d)·ρ with Δt = 0.03; a pore's depth is max(d_max, d_max + β·(d_sum − d_max)) over its incident wrinkles. [P] — [Du, Wang and Wang, SIGGRAPH 2025 (FLEX), sections 3 and Table 1](https://icewired-yy.github.io/papers/face_micro_detail_synthesis/pdf/Facial%20Microscopic%20Structure%20Synthesis%20from%20a%20Single%20Unconstrained%20Image.pdf)
- (a) In the FLEX re-implementation pore positions are sampled from the pore distance, each pore node is connected to its six nearest neighbours to form the base graph, and wrinkle depth is shown after 0, 3, 5 and 7 iterations. [P] — [FLEX, section 4.3 and Fig. 4](https://doi.org/10.1145/3721238.3730760)
- (c) Fitting Weiss' parameters to a captured skin patch by particle swarm optimisation costs "more than 140 seconds" per patch because the simulation is not differentiable; FLEX's graph-neural-network version recovers them from a blurry photo patch in 2.35 s on an RTX 3090 (training 5 h and 18 h on 50 000 synthetic 256² patches each). [P] — [FLEX, sections 5 and 6.4](https://doi.org/10.1145/3721238.3730760)
- Comparison evidence: in FLEX's A/B user study (42 participants) the photo-fitted model won 462 comparisons, "Uniform" (the graph model with only pore distance recovered and fixed wrinkle parameters) 324, and "Noise" (the noise model of von der Pahlen et al. 2014, "commonly used in real-time face rendering") 138; Uniform beat Noise 162 to 48 and FLEX beat Uniform 216 to 162. [P] — [FLEX, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1145/3721238.3730760)
- Limits stated by FLEX: the model has only concave features; "certain facial regions (e.g., the nasal tip) exhibit convex structures caused by sebaceous filaments or fat deposits, which fall outside our model's representational scope"; and earlier wrinkle simulation was "empirical ... often providing a plausible appearance but not being realistic enough". [P] — [FLEX, sections 2.2 and 6.6](https://doi.org/10.1145/3721238.3730760)
- (b) Weiss et al. 2024, "Stylize My Wrinkles": the authors themselves say simulation "can lead to a synthetic look" while scanning gives "a more organic look" but only on small patches; they style-transfer scanned patch displacement onto simulated content, and provide a library of scanned patches plus preset simulation parameters for skin types. [X] — [Disney Research page](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/2024/06/07/stylize-my-wrinkles-bridging-the-gap-from-simulation-to-reality/), [CGF 43(2)](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf15048)
- (c plus b) Graham et al. 2013: adds microstructure to a mesostructure-level scan by texture synthesis from exemplar patches digitised at 10 µm with polarised gradient illumination; compares a 4K displacement with a 16K synthesised one; reports reflections "qualitatively more consistent with real photographs". [X] — [USC ICT project page](https://vgl.ict.usc.edu/Research/Microgeometry), [CGF 32(2)](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v32i2pp335-344)
- (a as a technique) Nagano et al. 2015: in vivo patches measured at 10 µm show stretching smooths and compression roughens; the neutral micro displacement is blurred along the stretch direction and sharpened along compression, on the GPU, at interactive rates on animated scanned faces. [X] — [USC ICT project page](https://vgl.ict.usc.edu/Research/SkinStretch)
- (a) Klintberg and Haapaoja, SIGGRAPH 2024 Talks: "a fully procedural approach to generate and visualise skin textures" as a Houdini SOP, with pores and wrinkles driven by stretch and compression per frame. [X] — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3641233.3665166)
- (a) A separate Houdini tool for procedural pores and micro wrinkles by Ramon Schauer exists (title only seen). [S] — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/a-houdini-tool-for-procedural-pores-and-micro-wrinkles)

Published statistics
- Microrelief depth: primary lines 20–100 µm (EEMCO guidance) or 30–100 µm; secondary lines 5–40 µm; a 2006 review allows up to about 200 µm for both. With age primary lines deepen, secondary lines become fewer, and the plateaus flatten and become more directional; mean primary depth is stable from birth to adulthood. [X] — [EEMCO guidance](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0926995998000944), [Gougeon et al. 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/srt.13190), [Quatresooz et al. 2006](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1467-2494.2006.00342.x)
- Enlarged (visible) pores: Flament et al. 2015 imaged the cheeks of 2 585 women with a polarised dermatoscope and a 250 µm detection threshold; density, size and coverage "vary much more with ethnicity than with age" and mostly plateau with age; Chinese women had very low density and size. A secondary source puts cheek density at about 10–90 per cm²; the paper's introduction is quoted with 200–300 per cm² for pores across the face. Unresolved conflict; the table could not be opened. [X]/[S] — [PubMed 25733918](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25733918/), [full text, host blocked](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4337418)
- Enlarged pores are more prominent on nose and forehead than on cheeks and increase with age, most between the thirties and forties (434 polarised images). [X] — [Jung et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29393550/)
- Follicle density (every follicle is a pore opening): forehead 292 follicles per cm² and vellus shaft diameter 16 µm, attributed to Otberg et al. 2004 by a later thesis [S]; vellus hairs 439 per cm² on the forehead (49 % growing) vs 85 per cm² on the back, cheek 416 ± 37 per cm² [X]/[S]. — [thesis citing Otberg](https://core.ac.uk/download/402586008.pdf), [Blume et al. 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1993141/)
- A noise model (von der Pahlen, Jimenez et al. 2014) is the common real-time baseline for facial micro detail. [P, as stated by FLEX] — [FLEX, section 6](https://doi.org/10.1145/3721238.3730760)

### Inferences
- Spacing follows from density: 292 per cm² gives one follicle per 0.34 mm² (about 0.58 mm apart), 439 per cm² about 0.48 mm; "enlarged" pores at 10–90 per cm² are 1–3 mm apart. So φ should be about 0.5 mm for the base graph, with a sparse second population of wider, deeper pores on nose, forehead and medial cheek.
- Assuming a face of roughly 500 cm² (my assumption, unsourced), the graph has 150 000–220 000 nodes and, at six neighbours each, about half a million candidate wrinkles; a few iterations of the depth rule over that graph is milliseconds to seconds in a worker. This agrees with the paper's "millions of wrinkles" for a whole head at 16k.
- The method fits the project's no-UV head unusually well: nodes can be sampled directly on the surface (Poisson-disc on the limit surface or in rest-space cells), the direction field θ and the regional parameters are natural per-vertex attributes, and rasterisation can go either into a few textures through any ad hoc projection or be evaluated in the fragment shader from rest-space position (nearest feature points give the pore dimple, the bisectors toward neighbours give the furrows).
- The Uniform-versus-Noise result (162 to 48) is the best published evidence that structured procedural micro detail without any person-specific fit already beats noise; the FLEX-versus-Uniform result (216 to 162) measures what spatially varying, person-fitted parameters add. A scan-free head can recover part of that second gap by authoring regional variation (nose, forehead, cheek, eyelid, lip) from the statistics above.
- Convex detail (sebaceous filaments on the nose tip, milia, fine scale) is absent from the graph model and would need a separate bump population.

### Gaps
- Weiss 2023's own numbers (pore sampling scheme and densities per region, iteration counts, generation time, hardware, how θ is obtained, the preset parameter sets) were not readable; neither were the per-ethnicity tables of Flament 2015.
- No published per-region table of micro-wrinkle direction on the face (the relation to Langer's or relaxed-skin-tension lines) was found; the task's "Li et al. on pore distributions" could not be identified.
- No sourced facial roughness value (Ra) and no sourced pore diameter or depth distribution were found.
- Per-region specular statistics (Weyrich et al. 2006 measured 149 subjects and say the statistics are public; a ten-region split is reported second-hand) could not be read — [project page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/wojciech/FaceAnalysis/). [X]

## Expression and age wrinkles without scans: strain-driven maps, buckling physics, where lines form, and numbers

### Takeaway
The run-time mechanism is old, cheap and scan-free (blend "compressed" and "stretched" wrinkle maps by local mesh strain; 96 KB in one implementation), but every published system that looks real took the wrinkle maps themselves from expression scans or a scan-trained generator. First-principles buckling gives the missing content in outline: wavelength scales linearly with the stiff layer's thickness and with the cube root of the stiffness ratio, which with published skin-layer values predicts sub-millimetre micro-wrinkles from the stratum corneum and centimetre-scale furrows from the dermis.

### Cited Findings
- (a as a mechanism) Oat 2007 (ATI, "Ruby: Whiteout"): two tangent-space wrinkle normal maps, one for the stretched and one for the compressed face, blended per region through masks and artist-animated scalar weights; HLSL given in the course notes. [X] — [SIGGRAPH 2007 course notes](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Oat%20-%20Wrinkles%20-%20Course%20notes%20(SIGGRAPH%202007).pdf)
- (a as a mechanism) Jimenez et al., "Practical and realistic facial wrinkles animation": extends Oat to more than two maps with partial-derivative normal maps; memory footprint 96 KB. [X] — [project page](https://www.iryoku.com/wrinkles)
- (b and c) Raman, Hewitt, Wood and Baltrušaitis, WACV 2023: "mesh tension" is computed per vertex at synthesis time and blends expanded and compressed albedo and displacement maps; the maps are pooled from posed-expression scans of each identity, and identities without expression scans borrow maps from those that have them; the base pipeline builds albedo and displacement from one neutral scan per identity; wrinkles appear for expressions absent from the scans; downstream vision tasks improve. [X] — [WACV open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2023/html/Raman_Mesh-Tension_Driven_Expression-Based_Wrinkles_for_Synthetic_Faces_WACV_2023_paper.html), [project page](https://microsoft.github.io/DynamicWrinkles/)
- (b and c) Li et al. 2020: from one neutral scan a network generates personalised blendshapes and dynamic albedo, specular and displacement maps; trained on "over 4,000 scans with pore-level details". [X] — [arXiv 2010.00560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00560)
- (a) Weiss, Chandran, Zoss and Bradley 2024, "Fast Dynamic Facial Wrinkles": micro-wrinkles get wider and shallower under stretch and deeper and narrower under compression; only wrinkles perpendicular to the stress axis deform ("a reservoir of skin"); the fast variant precomputes displacement maps binned by stretch direction and blends them interactively in a shader. [X] — [Disney Research page](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/2024/04/26/fast-dynamic-facial-wrinkles/), [EG 2024 short paper](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/egs20241016)
- (a) Rémillard and Kry 2013: high-resolution thin shells embedded in a coarse finite-element lattice with frequency-based constraints; a wavelength model takes skin thickness and the elastic properties of skin and underlying body and sets the constraint cutoff; resulting wavelengths match theory and high-resolution volumetric simulation. [X] — [project page](https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~kry/pubs/ets/index.html)
- (a) Thin-film buckling: the wrinkle wavelength depends on film thickness and the plane-strain moduli of film and substrate; in the thick-substrate regime it is linear in film thickness and independent of substrate thickness. [X] — [bilayer film preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.08624)
- (a) Published layer values for wrinkle models. Five-layer model: stratum corneum 10 µm at 5 MPa, living epidermis 70 µm at 5 kPa, papillary dermis 200 µm at 30 kPa, reticular dermis 1.2 mm at 100 kPa, hypodermis 1.5 mm at 20 kPa, Poisson ratio 0.49; an 11 % drop in stratum corneum moisture gives 25–85 % larger wrinkles. Literature range for stratum corneum modulus about 0.5–500 MPa. In vivo elastography: epidermis about 4 MPa (4–10 kHz), dermis about 40 kPa and hypodermis about 15 kPa (0.2–1 kHz). [X] — [Santoprete et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10155799/), [Rayleigh-wave OCE study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11878153/), [Flynn and McCormack multi-layer model](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021929009005806)
- (a) Limbert and Kuhl 2018: a 20 µm stratum corneum on a 2.6 mm substrate; microrelief dominates the micro-wrinkle pattern at moderate stiffness ratios, and above a ratio of about 100 other factors take over; ratios 1 to 600 span maximum to minimum humidity. [X] — [Soft Matter 14:1292](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29319711/), [humidity commentary](https://www.magonlinelibrary.com/doi/full/10.12968/joan.2018.7.4.204)
- (a) Ageing shifts buckling from the stratum corneum (young) to the epidermis (aged) and lengthens the wavelength; aged skin with sparse collagen wrinkles at smaller critical strain and larger wavelength. [X] — [J. Biomech. Sci. Eng. 2008](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jbse/3/3/3_3_368/_pdf)
- Measured depths (3D fringe projection, men and women): crow's feet and forehead lines appear first, at a mean wrinkle depth of 60–70 µm; glabellar lines develop in the mid-forties at about 180 µm; increases of about 100 µm at glabella and forehead and about 50 µm at crow's feet are reported. [X] — [fringe projection study](https://researchgate.net/publication/258851565_Quantification_of_Age-Related_Facial_Wrinkles_in_Men_and_Women_Using_a_Three-Dimensional_Fringe_Projection_Method_and_Validated_Assessment_Scales)
- Age course: deep wrinkles from about 30; crow's feet patterns formed by 35 and deepening after; rapid deepening from 40, plateau at 60; wrinkle area tracks severity better than depth. A clinical scale ties "moderate, visible" wrinkles to 1–2 mm depth. [X]/[S] — [profilometric method paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8664995_The_wrinkle_and_its_measurement_-_A_skin_surface_Profilometric_method), [crow's feet grading scale](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51434911_A_Validated_Grading_Scale_for_Crow's_Feet)
- A 2025 study measures wrinkling strain fields with digital image correlation per FACS action (title only seen). [S] — [Applied Sciences 15(12):6803](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/12/6803)

### Inferences
- Using the standard stiff-film formula λ = 2π·h·(Ē_f/(3·Ē_s))^(1/3) [M, not re-read here] with the values above: a 20 µm stratum corneum at stiffness ratio 20, 100 and 600 gives λ ≈ 0.24, 0.40 and 0.74 mm; a 10 µm layer at 5 MPa over 5 kPa gives ≈ 0.44 mm. These match the follicle spacing (about 0.5 mm), which fits Limbert and Kuhl's finding that microrelief sets the micro-wrinkle pattern.
- Treating the whole dermis (about 1.4 mm, 40–100 kPa) on hypodermis (15–20 kPa) as the film gives λ ≈ 8–10 mm, the right order for forehead furrow spacing; the stiffness ratio is only 3–5, where the formula is marginal, so this is an order-of-magnitude guide only. The matching critical strain ε_c = ¼·(3Ē_s/Ē_f)^(2/3) [M] is about 2 % for ratio 100 and about 18 % for ratio 5: micro-wrinkles appear at slight compression, furrows only at strong expression.
- A scan-free expression-wrinkle system can therefore be: (1) per-vertex compressive strain and its principal direction from the cage deformation; (2) furrow lines authored or generated perpendicular to the compression direction with spacing from the buckling estimate and position biased to anatomical creases; (3) depth as a function of strain beyond a critical value and of age (60–70 µm onset to millimetre-scale for static folds); (4) Oat-style blending at run time. All inputs are published numbers or the head's own geometry.
- Because the project bakes a fixed smile into the rest shape, strain must be measured against a relaxed reference, not the rest shape; otherwise crow's feet and nasolabial deepening that belong to the smile will be missing. This needs the neutral face as a second stored state or an analytic "un-smile".

### Gaps
- Raman's exact tension formula, the number of identities with expression scans, and texture resolutions were not retrievable.
- No sourced statement was retrieved for "expression lines form perpendicular to the muscle fibre direction"; it is standard anatomy but unverified here. No sourced numbers for nasolabial fold depth or crow's feet count, length and spacing were found.
- No published work was found that generates expression furrows purely from buckling physics on a face and compares them with measured wrinkles; Rémillard and Kry validate against theory and simulation, not against skin.

## Expression from anatomy: muscle- and physics-based face models, cost, real-time status, baking, and FACS displacement magnitudes

### Takeaway
Physics-based faces exist from 2005 to 2024, but every one takes its anatomy or its actuation from a person: MRI or a scan fitted with a template (Sifakis, Phace), performance capture (Srinivasan, Yang 2022/2023, Animatomy), or a large 3D face database (Yang 2024, SoftDECA). None was found that drives a procedural head with an authored muscle layout, and none publishes a real-time first-principles solve; real-time variants are neural approximations of an offline simulation. Smile magnitudes are published: the mouth corner travels about 9–16 mm.

### Cited Findings
- (c) Sifakis, Neverov and Fedkiw 2005: 32 transversely isotropic muscles in a quasistatic finite-element tetrahedral mesh built from volumetric data of a living subject; activations solved to track sparse markers. A related 2005 paper cites an 840 000-tetrahedron model. [X] — [paper PDF, host blocked](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~sifakis/papers/activations_siggraph_2005.pdf)
- (c, with a template) Ichim, Kadleček, Kavan and Pauly 2017 (Phace): expressions from minimising non-linear energies for passive flesh, active muscles and bone; a muscle activation model more flexible than fibre models; blendshape weights map to per-frame muscle activations. Timings not retrieved. [X] — [EPFL record](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/231436)
- (b/c) Srinivasan et al. 2021: a differentiable quasistatic Projective-Dynamics finite-element layer plus a decoder network that learns actuations from data "rather than from first principles". [X] — [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10281223)
- (c) Yang et al. 2022: an implicit neural map from material-space points to actuation, independent of mesh discretisation, extended to jaw kinematics, reproducing captured expressions. [X] — [arXiv 2401.14861](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.14861)
- (b/c) Yang et al. 2023, "An Implicit Physical Face Model Driven by Expression and Style": learns actuations across identities from a few performance-capture sequences; supports style transfer, collision handling, bone reshaping and paralysis. [X] — [arXiv 2401.15414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15414v1)
- (b) Yang et al. 2024, "Learning a Generalized Physical Face Model From Data": trained on a large 3D face dataset; fitted quickly to a single image or scan to give a ready-to-animate physical model. [X] — [Disney PDF, host blocked](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/app/uploads/2024/07/Learning-a-Generalized-Physical-Face-Model-From-Data-Paper.pdf)
- (b) Wagner, Schwanecke and Botsch 2023 (SoftDECA): a hypernetwork approximating a finite-element facial simulation across the DECA identity space; introduces a layered head model of skin, muscles and skull without intersections; SparseSoftDECA (2024) drives it from landmarks; the earlier "Neural Volumetric Blendshapes" "makes a physics-based model real-time capable with a neural network". No millisecond figure retrieved. [X] — [SoftDECA PDF](https://cg.cs.tu-dortmund.de/publications/2023-softdeca.pdf), [Neural Volumetric Blendshapes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.14784)
- (c) Animatomy (Wētā FX, SIGGRAPH Asia 2022): 178 contractile muscle-fibre curves, each with a unitless strain, parameterise the face; used on 12 hero characters and 5 generic transfers in "Avatar: The Way of Water"; per fxguide the curves are a spanning set of controls, not a flesh-and-muscle simulation. [X]/[S] — [Wētā paper PDF](https://www.wetafx.co.nz/assets/Uploads/PDFs/animatomy.pdf), [fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=287134)
- Smile magnitudes: mean lip-commissure displacement 9.2, 11.4, 13.5 and 16.0 mm for four increasing smile levels, at azimuth 44 ± 21° and elevation 37 ± 15°; sex and Caucasian/East-Asian differences about 1 mm. The record describes the data as BU-3DFE scans; one listing calls it motion capture. [X] — [PubMed 36171175](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36171175/)
- Older stereophotogrammetry of the normal smile: cheilion moves most, 16.6 mm in 3D at 31°; labiale superius 8.2 mm at 31°. [X] — [paper PDF](https://www.plasticsurgerygroup.co.uk/media/oume4vbf/quantitative-analysis-of-normal-smile-with-3d-stereophotogrammetry-an-aid-to-facial-reanimation.pdf)
- AU12 is the zygomaticus major pulling the lip corners sideways and up; AU6 is attributed to the orbital part of orbicularis oculi raising the infraorbital triangle and pushing cheek skin toward the eye; whether AU6 marks genuine amusement once intensity is controlled is disputed. [X] — [lip-corner dynamics study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5826373/), [Duchenne revisited](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2826128/), [Bayesian reanalysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32363090/)

### Inferences
- Under the project's rule, classes (b) and (c) cover the whole published physics-face literature; the rule-compliant route is the oldest idea in it: author fibre directions and attachment points on the procedural head from anatomy texts, run a quasistatic solve offline or at load time in the project's own code, and store the result as a corrective shape. That is conceptually what Phace and Sifakis do after their anatomy is in place, and a single baked smile needs one solve, not a real-time solver.
- The published magnitudes give a direct acceptance test for the baked smile: mouth corner displaced 9–16 mm along a direction about 30–45° above horizontal and about 45° outward, upper-lip midpoint about half of that.
- A volumetric solve also yields what a surface sculpt does not: volume-preserving cheek bulge, lower-lid push-up and the compressive strain field that should drive nasolabial and crow's feet depth.

### Gaps
- Per-frame or per-solve timings for Sifakis 2005, Phace, Srinivasan 2021 and the Yang papers, and the frame time of SoftDECA, were not retrievable.
- No millimetre values for AU6 (cheek and lower-lid displacement) were found; a kinematic Duchenne-smile study exists but its numbers were not visible.
- No paper was found that builds a muscle model on a procedural, non-scanned head, and none that reports baking such a simulation to blendshapes for a browser. Work from 2025–2026 could not be searched (budget exhausted).

## Asymmetry and individuality: published magnitudes of normal asymmetry and anthropometric statistics usable as "published numbers"

### Takeaway
Normal adult faces deviate from mirror symmetry by about 0.6–0.8 mm RMS over the surface, with local deviations above 2 mm in more than half of young adults, strongest in the lower face and at the nasal alae. Population means and standard deviations for the main head and face dimensions are public (NIOSH, ANSUR II) and are compatible with the rule, since they describe no specific person.

### Cited Findings
- Whole-surface asymmetry at rest: RMS deviation 0.80 ± 0.24 mm in 100 healthy volunteers; 0.63 mm (SD 0.16) in 350 adults by a global surface method. The pairing of each figure with its link is inferred from the titles in the search results, not confirmed in the papers. [X] — [3D photogrammetry baseline study](https://www.pubmed.gov/24406564/), [Symmetry 15(4):792](https://www2.mdpi.com/2073-8994/15/4/792)
- By surface projection in young adults (Raine cohort, 3dMD): more than 50 % of faces are more than 2 mm asymmetrical; 2–5 mm in about 52 % of women and 58 % of men; more than 5 mm in 7–8 %. [X] — [Murdoch research portal record](https://researchportal.murdoch.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Three-dimensional-assessment-of-facial-asymmetry-using/991005592651907891)
- Pattern: asymmetry is strongest in the lower jaw, weaker in the midface, weakest in the upper face; the chin tends to deviate left, tragus and gonion right. [X] — [Czech adults, 3D landmarks](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10399867/)
- In 80 young healthy white adults asymmetry was most evident at tragus and gonion and the right side of the face was larger. [X] — [Ferrario et al. 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7965306)
- Landmarks: alar and pogonion show the most horizontal asymmetry; alar asymmetry about 1.7 mm in women against a method error of 0.5 mm (85 adults); alar curvature mean 2.33 mm, with marked asymmetry also at cheilion and menton (60 adults). A gonion mean of 3.54 mm (range 0–12) appears in the same extract but its study is unclear. [X] — [landmark vs surface comparison](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258038229_Comparison_between_landmark_and_surface-based_three-dimensional_analyses_of_facial_asymmetry_in_adults), [Huang et al. 2013](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ocr.12010)
- NIOSH head-and-face survey (2003, 3 997 subjects, traditional measures plus laser scans): head breadth 153.0 mm (SD 6.0), interpupillary distance 64.5 (3.6), menton-sellion length 122.7 (7.0), bizygomatic breadth 143.5 (6.9); the sex split of these rows was not clear in the extract. A principal-component panel over ten facial dimensions defines five face-size classes (small, medium, large, long/narrow, short/wide) from the first two components; a 3D shape analysis reports ten components covering 64.1 % of variance. [X] — [Anthrotech final report](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/resource/11815/Anthrotech_report.pdf), [National Academies assessment](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/11815/chapter/6), [CDC summary](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/190020/cdc_190020_DS1.pdf)
- ANSUR II (secondary, internally inconsistent blog): male bizygomatic breadth about 142.6 mm; male menton-sellion 122.6 mm (5th–95th percentile 112–134); female interpupillary breadth 61.7 mm. [S] — [blog over the public ANSUR II file](https://realworldappeal.com/en/blog/male-face-measurements)

### Inferences
- A procedural asymmetry field consistent with the literature: a smooth, low-frequency vector field with zero mean, RMS about 0.6–0.8 mm, amplitude weighted toward jaw angle, chin and alae (1.5–3.5 mm locally) and small in the upper face, with a slight chin-left and right-side-larger bias. On the project's head this is a per-vertex offset on the cage, costing nothing at run time.
- The NIOSH standard deviations give a published sampling range for individuality (about 4–5 % of each dimension); correlations between dimensions are in the report's principal-component tables, which would let the project sample plausible combinations without any mesh.
- Statistical shape models distributed as meshes (Basel, FLAME, DECA) are both class (b) and "third-party meshes"; tabulated landmark statistics are not.

### Gaps
- The component loadings and variance split of the NIOSH principal components, Farkas' norms, and the official ANSUR II head tables could not be opened.
- No published covariance of asymmetry between features (for example whether nose and chin deviate together) was found, and nothing on asymmetry of expression (the smile) or of the soft features (brows, eyelid creases).

## Hair without assets: eyebrows, eyelashes, stubble and vellus from dermatology numbers, and strand rendering models

### Takeaway
Counts, diameters, lengths and growth rates are published well enough to grow lashes, brows and stubble procedurally; the fibre scattering model and its melanin coefficients are open and scan-free. Vellus hair (about 400 per cm² and 16–30 µm thick) is sub-pixel at portrait distance and is better treated as a grazing-angle scattering lobe, which Koenderink and Pont identify as the difference between skin and plastic.

### Cited Findings
- Eyelashes: 90–160 on the upper lid, 75–80 on the lower; growth stops near 12 mm. [X] — [StatPearls, Eyelash](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK537278/)
- Eyelash length about 7–8 mm upper and 5–6 mm lower. [S] — [Wikipedia, Eyelash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyelash)
- Eyelash shaft about 100 µm (range 0.05–0.20 mm), length 2–15 mm, 10–30 lashes per cm of lid margin (patent texts); growth 0.12–0.14 mm per day (review). [S] — [USPTO 11744348](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11744348), [Am J Clin Dermatol review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40257-022-00729-5)
- Eyebrows: terminal hair density about 134 ± 38 per cm² in unaffected control brows; thickness 0.01–0.09 mm. [X] — [burn reconstruction study with healthy contralateral brows](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10567046/)
- Eyebrows: 200–300 hairs per brow, hairs leave the skin at about 10–15° (clinic source); hypotrichosis patients average 34 µm shafts; density, diameter, pattern and cycle vary along the brow and the tail has the lowest density and diameter. [S]/[X] — [transplant clinic page](https://hair-transplant-clinics.co.uk/hair-follicle-density-in-eyebrow-transplants/), [Suchonwanit et al. 2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jocd.13167)
- Eyebrow direction: the head (medial end) is broadest with mostly vertical hairs; in the body the upper hairs point outward and down and the lower hairs outward and up, converging into a slight ridge; the tail narrows and feathers downward. [X]/[S] — [dermatology journal table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7982014/table/T1), [transplant clinic description](https://www.bernsteinmedical.com/?p=342)
- Beard: 20–80 follicles per cm² depending on area and ethnicity; growth 0.3–0.5 mm per day, about twice the eyebrow rate; densest on the upper lip. [X] — [The male beard hair and facial skin, Int J Cosmet Sci](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ics.12328)
- Beard by region (commercial site): upper cheek 30–80, lower cheek 20–40, upper lip 80–110, chin centre 70–120 hairs per cm²; growth 0.27–0.47 mm per day. A beard diameter of 0.08–0.12 mm comes only from a low-quality aggregator. [S] — [keratin.com](https://www.keratin.com/hair-biology/beard-hair-biology-structure-density-growth-rate/)
- Vellus: 439 per cm² on the forehead and 416 ± 37 on the cheek; growth 0.03 mm per day on the forehead; maximum length falls with age; diameter under 25–30 µm, forehead shaft 16 µm per a secondary citation of Otberg 2004. [X]/[S] — [Blume et al. 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1993141/), [thesis citing Otberg](https://core.ac.uk/download/402586008.pdf)
- (a) Koenderink and Pont 2003: a thin layer of sparse scatterers (hairs, dust) adds a "surface lobe" concentrated at grazing angles; it is "a neglected but often decisive cue" for skin, makes cheeks read as soft or peach-like, and without it skin looks like paper, glossy plastic or glass. [X] — [Machine Vision and Applications 14:260](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00138-002-0089-7)
- (a) Fibre scattering: Marschner et al. 2003 split reflection into R, TT and TRT, with cuticle tilt shifting R and TRT in opposite directions. [X] — [paper PDF](https://w1.graphics.cornell.edu/pubs/2003/MJC+03.pdf)
- (a) Chiang et al. 2016: energy-conserving near-field fibre model with a logistic azimuthal lobe and an albedo-to-absorption inversion fitted by simulation. Constants read in pbrt-v4: absorption per unit concentration, eumelanin σa = (0.419, 0.697, 1.37), pheomelanin σa = (0.187, 0.4, 1.05) (RGB); σa from a target colour c is (ln c / (5.969 − 0.215β + 2.532β² − 10.73β³ + 5.574β⁴ + 0.245β⁵))², β the azimuthal roughness. [X] for the paper, [C] for the constants — [CGF 35(4)](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf12830), [pbrt-v4 bxdfs.cpp](https://github.com/mmp/pbrt-v4/blob/master/src/pbrt/bxdfs.cpp)
- (a) Karis 2016: a real-time approximation of Marschner for games; some paths collapsed to single lobes, TRT treated most roughly, and the path-length absorption term is "an artistic approximation derived from photos". [X] — [SIGGRAPH 2016 course slides](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2016-shading-course/karis/s2016_pbs_epic_hair.pdf)
- (b/assets) Microsoft's synthetic faces use strand-level hair from a library of artist-made grooms. [X] — [Fake It Till You Make It](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/html/Wood_Fake_It_Till_You_Make_It_Face_Analysis_in_the_ICCV_2021_paper.html)

### Inferences
- Strand budgets from the numbers: about 170–240 lashes per eye; 200–700 hairs per brow (the per-brow count and the per-area density disagree by a factor of two to three); stubble at 20–120 per cm² over a beard area of a few hundred cm² is on the order of 10⁴ fibres, 0.3–0.5 mm long per day since shaving. All are small enough to generate in a worker at load and draw as instanced camera-facing ribbons or thin tubes.
- Growth rules that need no asset: lashes rooted in two to three rows along the lid margin, curved outward, with length tapering toward both canthi; brow hairs following the three-zone direction field at 10–15° to the skin; stubble following a smooth per-vertex direction field (downward on cheeks, converging at the chin, sideways on the upper lip) with per-follicle jitter.
- Vellus as geometry would be about 200 000 fibres of 16–30 µm, far below a pixel; a sheen/asperity lobe plus a sparse fringe of real fibres at the silhouette (cheek, jaw, ear, upper lip) buys the peach-fuzz cue at negligible cost. Stubble below about 1 mm likewise reads mostly as a darkening of albedo with a rough, anisotropic micro-normal; the follicle dots can reuse the pore graph's nodes.
- For a browser, the Karis-style three-lobe approximation with pbrt's melanin coefficients is the proven real-time choice; no per-fibre path tracing is needed for brows and lashes.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed healthy-population count per eyebrow, no measured lash curvature or angle distribution, and no published direction-field map for beard or vellus hair were found.
- Karis' numeric lobe shifts and roughness defaults, and any real-time strand-level-of-detail results (a 2025 EGSR paper was seen by title only), were not readable.
- Beard hair diameter and cross-section shape lack a reliable source here.

- (a element inside b) The associated patent describes scleral veins generated from procedural noise and a height map turned into a normal map. [S] — [US 10217265](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10217265)
- (b) Wood et al. 2016: an eye-region morphable model from registered high-quality head scans, with an eyeball "constructed from anatomical measurements and iris photos"; UnityEyes renders it in real time with approximated eyeball materials and procedural eyelid geometry. [X] — [ECCV 2016 paper page](https://perceptualui.org/publications/wood16_eccv/), [UnityEyes](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/rainbow/projects/unityeyes/)
- (a) Disney Animation's procedural eye work lists procedural iris generation by texture synthesis and Markov random fields as future work, not a result; artist tools build iris fibres and scleral veins from distorted wave and noise functions. [X]/[S] — [Pinskiy and Miller](https://media.disneyanimation.com/uploads/production/publication_asset/66/asset/realisticEyeMotion.pdf), [Blender procedural eyeball tutorial](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Blender_3D:_Noob_to_Pro/Advanced_Tutorials/Procedural_Eyeball_in_Cycles)
- (a) Enamel: refractive index 1.62 (hydroxyapatite crystal 1.651); scattering about 3 mm⁻¹ and absorption about 0.1 mm⁻¹ (Spitzer and ten Bosch), against 6.6 mm⁻¹ scattering at 633 nm (Zijp et al. 1995); absorption negligible in the visible to near infrared (Fried et al. 1995). [X] — [Groningen dissertation chapter](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/14515607/06_c6.pdf), [Zijp et al. 1995](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00220345950740121301), [Fried et al. 1995](https://opg.optica.org/ao/abstract.cfm?uri=ao-34-7-1278)
- (a) Dentin: absorption about 0.4 mm⁻¹, scattering about 3–20 mm⁻¹, Henyey-Greenstein g = 0.93 with about 2 % isotropic component; index about 1.50 (enamel 1.62 ± 0.02 by optical coherence tomography, a group index). [X] — [Groningen dissertation chapter](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/14515607/06_c6.pdf), [Fried et al. 1995](https://opg.optica.org/ao/abstract.cfm?uri=ao-34-7-1278)
- (a) Shade: VITA Classical tabs measure about L\* 56.2, a\* 8.0, b\* 14.6 for A3 and 43.1, 8.3, 14.9 for A4 in one older optics paper (values as returned by the search summary; the a\* looks high and needs checking); the most frequent natural shades in vivo are A1, A2, A3 and B3 (3D-Master 1M2, 2M1, 2M3); instruments disagree by ΔE00 2.2–5.0. [X] — [Applied Optics 49(3):422](https://opg.optica.org/ao/abstract.cfm?uri=ao-49-3-422), [scoping review of natural tooth colour](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0300571225001708), [spectrophotometer comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37921676/)

### Inferences
- A scan-free eye is: two spheres (sclera radius about 12 mm [M], cornea 7.8 mm) blended at a limbus about 11.8 mm across; refraction through index 1.376 over an anterior chamber of about 3 mm [M]; iris colour from a small melanin-driven lookup in the spirit of ILIT (anterior border layer and stromal melanin as two parameters, giving blue from scattering when melanin is low); radial fibre relief, crypts and collarette from authored noise; scleral veins from a branching or noise process fading toward the limbus. Geometry and colour rest on published numbers; fibre structure is the unsupported part.
- Teeth: a thin enamel shell (index 1.62, scattering 3–7 mm⁻¹, nearly no absorption) over yellow dentin (scattering to 20 mm⁻¹, absorption 0.4 mm⁻¹) explains why incisal edges are translucent and bluish and necks are yellower; in real time this reduces to a thickness-dependent blend between an enamel colour and a dentin colour plus a short subsurface profile, with the base colour pinned to a common shade (A2/A3).
- Because the project's eyes are blue, the iris is the case where pigment is nearly absent and stromal scattering sets the hue, which is exactly where a first-principles model is most defensible and a painted texture least.

### Gaps
- ILIT's parameter names, default values and validation against measured irides could not be read; no published statistics of iris pattern features (crypt count, collarette radius, fibre density) were found.
- No academic method for procedural scleral vasculature or limbus appearance with validation was found; limbus width, scleral radius, anterior chamber depth and pupil-size-versus-luminance formulas are [M] only.
- Gingival colour statistics, per-channel RGB coefficients for enamel and dentin, and tooth-size anthropometry were not retrieved.

## Honest ceiling: comparisons of procedural or synthetic faces with scanned ones, and expert statements

### Takeaway
No published head-to-head test of a fully procedural face against a scanned one at portrait distance was found. The indirect evidence is consistent: the best-known "synthetic" faces (Microsoft) still take textures, wrinkles and shape statistics from scans; the leading procedural micro-detail group (Disney) says its own simulation "can lead to a synthetic look" and adds scanned patches to fix it; and the one user study with numbers puts unfitted procedural detail clearly above noise and clearly below person-fitted detail.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft's pipeline combines "a procedurally generated parametric 3D face model with a library of hand-crafted assets" [X]; its claim is parity with real training data for landmark localisation and face parsing, not human-judged photorealism, and the released images are 512×512 [P, dataset README]. — [ICCV 2021 open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/html/Wood_Fake_It_Till_You_Make_It_Face_Analysis_in_the_ICCV_2021_paper.html), [dataset README](https://github.com/microsoft/FaceSynthetics)
- The same framework "builds albedo and displacement textures from a single neutral-expression scan per identity", and its wrinkle extension takes maps from posed-expression scans. [X] — [Raman et al. 2023 project page](https://microsoft.github.io/DynamicWrinkles/)
- [M] From memory of the 2021 paper, unverified here: the face shape model was learned from 511 scans, the albedo and displacement textures were cleaned from about 200 scans, and hair came from several hundred artist grooms. — [arXiv 2109.15102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.15102)
- Disney Research, 2024: "Simulation methods allow to highly customize the wrinkles on the face, but can lead to a synthetic look. Scanning methods can lead to a more organic look for the micro details, however these methods are only applicable to small skin patches." Their remedy mixes both. [X] — [Stylize My Wrinkles](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/2024/06/07/stylize-my-wrinkles-bridging-the-gap-from-simulation-to-reality/)
- SIGGRAPH 2025: past microscopic wrinkle simulation was "empirical ... often providing a plausible appearance but not being realistic enough"; in the A/B study, unfitted graph detail beat noise 162:48, photo-fitted detail beat unfitted 216:162. [P] — [FLEX](https://doi.org/10.1145/3721238.3730760)
- Measured parameters make the physical models match: with captured chromophore maps the spectral skin model gives "close matches to photographs"; with synthesised 16K microstructure, reflections are "qualitatively more consistent with real photographs" than a 4K scan alone. [X] — [Gitlina et al. 2020](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14055), [Graham et al. 2013](https://vgl.ict.usc.edu/Research/Microgeometry)
- Generating dynamic face assets without capturing each expression took a database of more than 4 000 pore-level scans. [X] — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00560)
- A fully procedural skin pipeline was presented to practitioners in 2024 (Houdini), so the direction is in production use for at least some assets. [X] — [SIGGRAPH 2024 Talks](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3641233.3665166)
- A scan vendor's claim that "true realism" needs scan-based textures is commercial and unsupported by data. [S] — [3D.sk blog](https://blog.3d.sk/2025/06/19/create-hyperrealistic-digital-humans-with-our-16-k-metahuman%E2%80%91ready-diffuse-head-texture/)
- A missing physical effect, not missing data, is named as what makes rendered skin look like plastic: asperity scattering. [X] — [Koenderink and Pont 2003](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00138-002-0089-7)

### Inferences
- What a scan supplies splits cleanly. (1) Optics and scales: fully replaceable by published models and numbers (skin colour, subsurface profile, fibre scattering, eye and tooth optics, pore spacing, line depths). (2) Regional variation: replaceable in part by authored maps guided by published rankings and statistics. (3) Idiosyncratic structure: the particular blotches, capillaries, moles, scars, the irregular way real pores cluster and real wrinkles branch, iris fibre patterns, and the exact form of the head. The literature gives no scan-free generator for (3) that authors themselves call realistic; it is where "synthetic look" comes from.
- At portrait distance the ceiling is set more by (3) and by form than by micro detail. A 24 cm face filling 70 % of a 1080-pixel-high view is about 0.3 mm per pixel (0.16 mm at 2160), so pores and primary lines are at or below one pixel and act mainly through the specular highlight's breakup, where structured procedural detail already beats noise. Colour mottling at 1–10 mm and shape at 1–50 mm are plainly resolved, and those are the least "published" parts.
- The most defensible reading of the evidence: a first-principles face can reach "convincing skin on a plausible person" in favourable light and at conversational distance, and will tend to read as too regular and too clean in a close portrait. No source measured this, and no source shows a fully procedural face passing as a photograph of a real person.
- Grey-zone items that would raise the ceiling but breach or strain the rule, listed so they are not hidden: BioSkin's pretrained decoder (trained on simulation, but third-party weights); Disney's preset simulation parameters fitted to captured patches (published numbers derived from scans of people); any 3D morphable model, DECA/FLAME-based physics, Bérard's parametric eye, Wood's eye-region model, Raman's wrinkle maps, Li's generator (all learned from scan databases); style transfer from scanned patches.

### Gaps
- No published perceptual study compares fully procedural faces with scanned faces at portrait distance; no statement from named practitioners (Debevec, Bradley, Hery and others) on whether procedural skin and form alone reach photorealism was retrieved, and film examples of fully procedural skin could not be confirmed.
- Whether MetaHuman-class real-time characters use any purely procedural skin layer was not checked (search budget exhausted).

## For each method: can it be a one-off bake at load time in a web worker or a GPU pass, stored in a few textures, and at what resolution?

### Takeaway
Almost everything in class (a) is a load-time bake or a pure shader term: the skin-colour lookup is a few kilobytes, blood and melanin fit in per-vertex attributes, the pore graph is a sub-second worker job, and expression wrinkles need two or three small maps plus a per-vertex strain. Only two items do not bake trivially: micro detail fine enough for close-ups (4k or more per face if stored, or evaluated procedurally instead), and a volumetric muscle solve (offline in the project's own tools, shipped as a shape).

### Cited Findings
- The pore-and-wrinkle graph is converted to a displacement map by shape functions written as graphics shaders, at about 16k² for film use. [X] — [Weiss et al. 2023](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14904)
- Dynamic micro-wrinkles can be approximated by precomputed displacement maps binned by stretch direction and blended in a shader at interactive rates. [X] — [Weiss et al. 2024, Fast Dynamic Facial Wrinkles](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/2024/04/26/fast-dynamic-facial-wrinkles/)
- Displacement-map convolution (blur along stretch, sharpen along compression) runs on the GPU interactively. [X] — [Nagano et al. 2015](https://vgl.ict.usc.edu/Research/SkinStretch)
- Wrinkle-map blending by masks and weights ran in real time in 2007, and a later variant needs 96 KB of memory. [X] — [Oat 2007](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Oat%20-%20Wrinkles%20-%20Course%20notes%20(SIGGRAPH%202007).pdf), [Jimenez et al.](https://www.iryoku.com/wrinkles)
- Dynamic skin colour from melanin and haemoglobin ran in real time with "negligible overhead". [X] — [Jimenez et al. 2010](https://www.iryoku.com/skincolor/)
- The six-Gaussian skin profile is evaluated per pixel in a real-time separable filter and can be "precomputed into a texture, for maximum performance". [P] — [SeparableSSS.h](https://github.com/iryoku/separable-sss/blob/master/SeparableSSS.h)
- A 4K displacement map does not carry skin microstructure; the comparison case used 16K. [X] — [Graham et al. 2013](https://vgl.ict.usc.edu/Research/Microgeometry)
- Iris light transport has been run interactively on a GPU; real-time hair uses a three-lobe approximation. [X] — [Kravchenko thesis](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/3ceb6f59-ae54-48dc-9323-5614d3611d44/full), [Karis 2016](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2016-shading-course/karis/s2016_pbs_epic_hair.pdf)

### Inferences

- The absence of UVs is not a blocker: every row either lives on vertices, is a small lookup, or can be evaluated from rest-space position. If textures are wanted for the micro detail, a load-time projection (for example a cube or octahedral map centred in the head, patched around ears and nostrils) is enough, because the generator samples the surface directly and does not care about seams.
- Resolution rule of thumb from the dermatology numbers: to draw a 250 µm pore as geometry needs texels of 60–125 µm, hence 2k–4k across a 20–25 cm face; at the 0.3 mm pixels of a 1080p portrait that detail is sub-pixel, so a 2k map with correctly filtered roughness is the sensible default and 4k only pays in close-ups or on 4K displays.
- Ranking by benefit per unit of work, as supported by the evidence above: (1) chromophore colour with regional blood variation and expression flush; (2) structured pore graph feeding specular breakup; (3) the grazing-angle vellus lobe; (4) strain-driven furrows with a relaxed reference state; (5) lashes and brows as real strands; (6) low-frequency asymmetry; (7) first-principles iris. The muscle solve is the most costly and the least supported by precedent.

### Gaps
- No timings were found for generating the pore graph or its 16k rasterisation, so the worker and GPU estimates are unverified; no source reports any of these bakes running in a browser.
- Whether per-vertex chromophore fields at the project's cage density are fine enough around lips, eyelids and nostrils was not assessable from the literature.
- Texture-compression behaviour of micro height fields (for example BC4/ASTC on pore maps) and its effect on specular aliasing were not researched.
