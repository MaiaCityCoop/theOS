# The engine today: how TheGame chooses detail (read in the code, 2026-11-16)

Source: the repository itself, at commit `29ce5a9`. Not web research. Every statement names the file it was read in.
The engine's own description is `TheGame/src/detail/README.md`.

## Takeaway

The engine already measures everything by **size on screen in pixels**, draws through **one heap on the GPU with
compute-packed instance lists**, and hides most changes by **moving vertices** (no dither). What is still written
per asset or per kind, and what Samuel wants gone, is: **which levels exist** (three different ways, one per make of
thing) and **the pixel sizes where they change**. Levels are chosen for a **whole object**, never for a part of it.

## The platform

- three.js 0.187, WebGPU renderer, TSL node shaders (`TheGame/package.json`). Compute passes, storage buffers.
- A WebGL 2 fallback exists but draws no rocks and no plants (`src/detail/README.md`, "What this does not yet give").
- Assets are generated by code in web workers at start-up and kept in the browser between starts
  (`src/scatter/recipes.worker.js`, `src/core/kept.js`). No offline tool, no art pipeline.
- Target: 60 frames a second as a hard gate, in Safari on Apple-silicon Macs and iPhones, within 8 GB
  (`src/core/governor.js`, `src/detail/README.md` "Memory").

## One measure for everything

- Size on screen: `px = size × pxK / distance / uLodInv`, with `pxK = height / (2 tan 34°)` (the walker's 68° lens) and
  `uLodInv` the governor's dial (`src/detail/index.js`, `src/core/governor.js`).
- `size` is the asset's largest side, or its height for a tree (`assets/kinds.js`: `sizeOf`).
- The governor holds 60 fps by first lowering the detail cap, then the pixel ratio (down to 0.6) (`src/core/governor.js`).

## Three makes of thing, three ways of having levels

| Make | Which levels exist | Where the pixel sizes are written | How a change is hidden |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Solids** (rocks, the stress stand's bodies, creatures) | A **ladder of eight**, the same for every kind (`assets/kit/ladder.js`): the full mesh (a rock: 96 squares an edge, 110,592 triangles; a test body 50,700 to 199,692), then balls of 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1 squares an edge (12,288; 3,072; 768; 192; 48; 12 triangles), then a dot of one triangle | `LADDER`: 400, 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 1.15 px | Every vertex also stores **where it lies on the next coarser mesh** (10 bits each way inside the level's `delta`) and that mesh's normal (`assets/kit/solid.js`). The vertex shader moves it there over the level's range of pixels (`src/scatter/drawer.js`: `t = 1 − smoothstep(lo, top, px)`), so the two meshes are one shape at the change |
| **Plants** (trees, shrubs) | **Written per species**: `levels: [{ P, most }]` and a `grow(q, …)` function with a table per level. Stone pine: puffs 140, 56, 20, 9; cards a puff 11, 6, 4, 3; card size 1.15, 2.6, 4.4, 6 m; tube sides 12, 7, 5, 3 (`assets/stonepine.js`). The kit adds a finest level (every sprig as its twigs) and two farthest (the crown as one solid body of 36 triangles, a speck of 8) (`assets/kit/tree.js`: `splayOf`, `farOf`) | per kind: tree `[260, 80, 34, 18, 7, 3]`, shrub `[140, 16, 5]`; `fine.px: 600` per species (`assets/kinds.js`) | Two levels are drawn at once over a band; the finer one's sprigs shrink to their feet one after another while the coarser one's grow, by a hash of each sprig's root; wood changes over whole, half way (`src/detail/handover.js`: `partGrown`) |
| **Made things** (the tent, the fire pit; later buildings, furniture) | **Written per asset by hand**: `detail.levels[].faces`. The bell tent: `bell(32)` with ropes, pegs and pole; `bell(16)`; `bell(10)`; `bell(6)` (`assets/belltent.js`) | per kind: shelter `[170, 45, 15, 5]`, default `[200, 60, 20, 6]` | None: "a thing of one piece changes over whole, half way" (`handover.js`). One frame it is one mesh, the next the other |

A solid's coarse levels are **the recipe evaluated again on a coarser grid**, with the noise frequencies that grid
cannot carry faded out (`assets/kit/rock.js`, `blob.js`: `carry`). That needs a shape that is a function over a
sphere: it cannot serve a building, a tent or a tree.

The grass is outside all of this: rings of blades by distance (`src/scatter/grass.js`).

## The far end

- Solids and made things end as **a dot**: one triangle of the kind's mean colour (`assets/kit/solid.js`: `dotMesh`).
- A plant's last level **sinks into the ground** over its last pixels (`handover.js`: `sinkInto`); where a stand has
  sunk, **the land wears its colour** (`src/terrain/material.js`, the stand's `cover`).
- "What is under a pixel is not drawn" (`src/detail/README.md`): of a million things about 135,000 to 180,000 are a
  pixel or more at once.
- An octahedral impostor (8 × 8 views, two 1024² atlases) exists for a placed asset in the library; at about 11 MB a
  kind it "does not scale to thousands of kinds" (`src/detail/impostor.js`, README).

## How it is drawn (what a new method would plug into)

- **The heap** (`src/scatter/heap.js`): every kind's meshes at every level in two GPU buffers (corners, vertices of 16
  or 32 bytes) and a table that says where each lies. Fine levels live in a pool of a few slots and stream in for the
  kinds near the camera; until one has come, the next coarser is drawn.
- **Tiers** (`src/scatter/tier.js`): a compute pass decides per cell what stands there and packs only those into a
  buffer; the draw reads its count from the GPU (an indirect draw).
- **Vertex pulling**: a draw's vertex stage reads its vertex from the heap by the kind's number, so one draw call
  draws a level of every kind (`heap.js`: `heapElem`). A draw is as long as its level's longest mesh; kinds are drawn
  by "class of size" (powers of two of their triangle count) once there are more than 32 (`drawer.js`).
- Measured cost noted there: "a vertex looked up by its corner is worked out six times" unless the level has a shared
  list of corners (the ladder's ball), which lets the GPU work each vertex out once.
- A temporal pass keeps the picture from frame to frame and jitters the camera by part of a pixel
  (`src/core/temporal.js`).

## What is per asset or per kind today

1. The ladder's eight pixel sizes and morph ranges (one list, but fixed steps of 4 to 9 times in triangles).
2. Each kind's list of pixel sizes (`assets/kinds.js`).
3. Each tree's tables per level, and its `most` (the room each level may take in the heap).
4. Each made thing's face lists per level.
