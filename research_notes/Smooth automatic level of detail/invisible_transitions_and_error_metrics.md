## 1. Screen-space error: the projection formula, the thresholds used in practice, and when a swap becomes visible

### Takeaway
Every error-driven engine examined decides from one stored number per level (an object-space error ε) projected to pixels as ρ = ε·H / (2·d·tan(fovy/2)) and compared with one threshold τ; τ is about 1 pixel wherever nothing else hides the swap (Nanite, Bevy, Godot), 2 to 3 pixels for terrain, and 16 for 3D Tiles, but these numbers are not comparable because ε is defined differently in each system. Practitioner evidence says a swap is safe below about one rendered pixel with a temporal filter, and that above it the eye catches two things: many triangles changing in the same frame, and shading (normals) changing.

### Cited Findings

**The formula, as implemented**
- [P] meshoptimizer's README gives the reference test: `float d = max(0, distance(camera_position, mesh_center) - mesh_radius); float e = d * (tan(camera_fovy / 2) * 2 / screen_height); // 1px in mesh space` and `bool lod_ok = e * lod_factor >= lod_error;` where "lod_factor can be 1 or can be adjusted for more or less aggressive LOD selection" — [meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md)
- [P] clusterlod.h projects a group's error as a fraction of screen height: `bounds.error / max(distance(bounds.center, camera_position) - bounds.radius, camera_znear) * (camera_proj * 0.5f)`, with `camera_proj` = `projection[1][1]` = cot(fovy/2), "multiply by screen height to get pixels", and "for simplicity, we ignore perspective distortion and use rotationally invariant projection size estimation" — [clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/demo/clusterlod.h)
- [P] CesiumJS: `error = (geometricError * height) / (distance * sseDenominator)`, then an optional fog-like term is subtracted (`dynamicScreenSpaceError`), then `error /= frameState.pixelRatio`; for orthographic views `error = geometricError / pixelSize` — [Cesium3DTile.js](https://github.com/CesiumGS/cesium/blob/main/packages/engine/Source/Scene/Cesium3DTile.js)
- [P] CesiumJS defines the quantity in words: "A tile's screen space error is roughly equivalent to the number of pixels wide that would be drawn if a sphere with a radius equal to the tile's geometric error were rendered at the tile's position." — [Cesium3DTileset.js](https://github.com/CesiumGS/cesium/blob/main/packages/engine/Source/Scene/Cesium3DTileset.js)
- [P] cesium-native computes the same value by pushing the point (0, geometricError, −distance, 1) through the projection matrix and multiplying the NDC difference by viewportHeight/2; its comment admits "That's not quite right: the distance argument is actually the slant distance to the tile, whereas view-space Z is perpendicular to the near plane." — [ViewState.cpp](https://github.com/CesiumGS/cesium-native/blob/main/Cesium3DTilesSelection/src/ViewState.cpp)
- [P] cesium-native measures distance to "the closest point on the tile's bounding volume"; inside the volume "the distance is treated as zero, and the SSE is effectively infinite"; with several views the largest SSE drives the decision — [cesium-native selection algorithm](https://github.com/CesiumGS/cesium-native/blob/main/doc/topics/selection-algorithm-details.md)
- [P] 3D Tiles: geometric error is "a nonnegative number that specifies the error, in meters" of "the tile's simplified representation of its source geometry"; it is used "to calculate the SSE introduced if this tile is rendered and its children are not"; "each successive level of children will have a smaller geometric error than its parent" — [3D Tiles specification](https://github.com/CesiumGS/3d-tiles/blob/main/specification/README.adoc)
- [P] OGRE's terrain attributes the formula to "W. de Boer 2000": `A = 1 / tan(fovy*0.5)`, `T = 2 * maxPixelError / vertRes`, `cFactor = A / T`; a tile level then "will transition downwards" at `distTransition = maxHeightDelta * cFactor` — [OgreTerrain.cpp](https://github.com/OGRECave/ogre/blob/master/Components/Terrain/src/OgreTerrain.cpp), [OgreTerrainQuadTreeNode.cpp](https://github.com/OGRECave/ogre/blob/master/Components/Terrain/src/OgreTerrainQuadTreeNode.cpp)
- [P] Godot walks the level list and stops at the first level whose error is too large: `float screen_size = s->lods[i].edge_length * p_model_scale / p_distance_threshold; if (screen_size > p_mesh_lod_threshold) { break; }` — [mesh_storage.h](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/servers/rendering/renderer_rd/storage_rd/mesh_storage.h)
- [P] Godot's inputs to that test: the threshold is `p_viewport->mesh_lod_threshold / float(p_viewport->size.width)`; the distance is multiplied by `get_lod_multiplier()` = `2 / columns[0][0]` and by `1.0 / scaling_3d_scale`, with the comment "take into account resolution scaling for the multiplier, since we have more leeway with quality degradation visibility. Conversely, allow upwards scaling, too, for increased mesh detail at high res." — [renderer_viewport.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/servers/rendering/renderer_viewport.cpp), [projection.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/core/math/projection.cpp), [renderer_scene_render_rd.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/servers/rendering/renderer_rd/renderer_scene_render_rd.cpp)
- [P] Godot's distance is from the main camera's position to the nearest point of the instance's bounding box (`Vector3 camera_position = ...main_cam_transform.origin; surface_distance = Vector3(0,0,0).max(aabb_min - camera_position).max(camera_position - aabb_max)`), also in shadow passes, which receive the main camera's transform, multiplier and threshold; the per-object `lod_bias` multiplies the error — [render_forward_clustered.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/servers/rendering/renderer_rd/forward_clustered/render_forward_clustered.cpp)
- [P] Bevy's meshlet test: error divided by `max(distance to bounding sphere - radius, znear)`, times `clip_from_view[1][1] * 0.5`, times viewport height, then `return projected_error < 1.0;` (code copied from meshoptimizer's demo) — [JMS55, Virtual Geometry in Bevy 0.15](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-11-14-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-15/)
- [P] NVIDIA uses the angular form: "the arcsine of the error divided by the distance to the closest point on the bounding sphere. A target threshold can be chosen based on a single pixel's FOV at the center of the projection - to keep any geometric error less than the size of a pixel. This avoids varying the threshold across the image" — [nv_cluster_lod_builder README](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/main/README.md)
- [P] Nexus (Batched Multi-Triangulation in WebGL): `var error = t.mesh.nerrors[n]/(t.currentResolution*dist); //in pixels`, where "resolution is how long is a pixel at distance 1", `dist` is the distance to the node's bounding sphere minus its radius (floored at 0.1), and the error is divided by 1000 for nodes outside the frustum — [nexus.js](https://github.com/cnr-isti-vclab/nexus/blob/master/html/js/nexus.js)
- [P] Ponchio's thesis on batched multiresolution, at second hand (quoted in the README of a WebGPU Nanite re-implementation; the thesis itself was not opened): "We use the screen projection error E which is simply the projection of the model space error λ on screen. In order to have a conservative estimate of the error, the closest point in the bounding volume of the patch should be used. [...] This metric provides an upper bound for the number of pixels by which a geometric picture will be displaced in the simplified mesh with respect to the original mesh. A user specified error threshold τ can then be simply expressed in pixels." — [Scthe/nanite-webgpu README](https://github.com/Scthe/nanite-webgpu/blob/master/README.md)
- [P] Hoppe's view-dependent code expresses the tolerance as a fraction of the window: "the screen-space tolerance in pixels is screen_thresh * min(nxpixels, nypixels) * 0.5f"; each vertex carries a uniform residual error, a directional residual error, a radius of influence and a back-face term `sin2alpha` for the refinement test — [SrMesh.h](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/libHh/SrMesh.h)

**Thresholds in practice**
- [P] Nanite, as summarised by meshoptimizer's author: "a cluster is replaced by a coarser cluster only if the resulting visual error is under 1 pixel (and the resulting switch is hidden by TAA or other temporal filters)" — [zeux, Billions of triangles in minutes (2025)](https://zeux.io/2025/09/30/billions-of-triangles-in-minutes/)
- [P] Bevy: "select the set of simplified meshlets such that at the distance we're viewing them from, they have less than 1 pixel of geometric difference from the original set of meshlets at LOD 0", while "accounting _only_ for geometric differences, and not taking into account material or lighting differences. Doing so is a _much_ harder problem." — [JMS55, Virtual Geometry in Bevy 0.14](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-06-09-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-14/)
- [P] Godot: `rendering/mesh_lod/lod_change/threshold_pixels` defaults to 1.0; "The default mesh LOD threshold of 1 pixel is tuned to look *perceptually* lossless; it provides a significant performance gain with an unnoticeable loss in quality." The only other value shown is `mesh_lod_threshold = 4.0`; "Higher camera FOV and lower viewport resolutions will make LOD selection more aggressive" — [Godot docs, mesh LOD](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/blob/master/tutorials/3d/mesh_lod.rst), [ProjectSettings.xml](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/doc/classes/ProjectSettings.xml)
- [P] Cesium 3D Tiles: `maximumScreenSpaceError=16`; terrain: `this.maximumScreenSpaceError = 2;` — [Cesium3DTileset.js](https://github.com/CesiumGS/cesium/blob/main/packages/engine/Source/Scene/Cesium3DTileset.js), [Globe.js](https://github.com/CesiumGS/cesium/blob/main/packages/engine/Source/Scene/Globe.js)
- [P] Cesium for Unreal explains why 16 and 2 are the same setting: "When a tileset uses the older layer.json / quantized-mesh format rather than 3D Tiles, this value is effectively divided by 8.0. So the default value of 16.0 corresponds to the standard value for quantized-mesh terrain of 2.0." — [Cesium3DTileset.h (cesium-unreal)](https://github.com/CesiumGS/cesium-unreal/blob/main/Source/CesiumRuntime/Public/Cesium3DTileset.h)
- [P] What a tile's error measures is left to whoever produced the tiles: "The geometric error is formulated based on a metric like point density, mesh or texture decimation, or another factor specific to that tileset." — [3D Tiles specification](https://github.com/CesiumGS/3d-tiles/blob/main/specification/README.adoc)
- [P] Cesium also relaxes the threshold on purpose: `dynamicScreenSpaceError=true` ("For street-level horizon views, use lower resolution tiles far from the camera", density 2.0e-4, factor 24.0, height falloff 0.25); `foveatedScreenSpaceError=true` (temporarily raises the error for tiles "around the edge of the screen", `foveatedTimeDelay=0.2` s "to wait after the camera stops moving"); `progressiveResolutionHeightFraction=0.3` — [Cesium3DTileset.js](https://github.com/CesiumGS/cesium/blob/main/packages/engine/Source/Scene/Cesium3DTileset.js)
- [P] 3DTilesRendererJS (a three.js-based 3D Tiles renderer): `this.errorTarget = 16.0;` — [TilesRendererBase.js](https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS/blob/master/src/core/renderer/tiles/TilesRendererBase.js)
- [P] Nexus runs at a higher error in the browser and ties it to frame rate: `var targetError = 2.0; //error won't go lower than this if we reach it`, `var maxError = 15; //error won't go over this even if fps is low`, `var minFps = 15;` each frame the current error is multiplied by 1.05 when `minFps/fps > 1.1` and by 0.95 when it is `< 0.9`, clamped between the two — [nexus.js](https://github.com/cnr-isti-vclab/nexus/blob/master/html/js/nexus.js)
- [P] OGRE terrain: default `mMaxPixelError(3.0)`. Its cheap distance (tile centre minus half the bounding radius) "will only use the max pixel error as a guide, the actual error will vary above and below that", whereas the ray/box distance "is guaranteed to give you at least the max pixel error or better, but will often give you more detail than you need" — [OgreTerrain.cpp](https://github.com/OGRECave/ogre/blob/master/Components/Terrain/src/OgreTerrain.cpp), [OgreTerrain.h](https://github.com/OGRECave/ogre/blob/master/Components/Terrain/include/OgreTerrain.h)
- [P] Hoppe's Grand Canyon fly-through runs with `-sr_screen_thresh 0.003846` together with face-count regulation (`-sr_regulatenf $nfaces -sr_fracvtrav .3333 -sr_gain .3333`, nfaces = 40000) — [view_gcanyon_interactive.sh](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/demos/view_gcanyon_interactive.sh)
- [P] A shipped console game (Blade II) used no error table at all: "num_tris = original_num_tris * global_scaling_factor / distance_from_camera [...] And that worked just fine, and was obviously a lot faster than some interpolated table-lookup." — [Tom Forsyth, Comparison of VIPM Methods (web version with later notes)](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/gem_vipm_webversion.html)
- [P] A mobile variant (Tencent "NanoMesh", SIGGRAPH 2024, as transcribed by a third party from slide 12) scales the threshold by device and distance: `threshold = projected_area * device_factor * decay_factor` — [Scthe/nanite-webgpu README](https://github.com/Scthe/nanite-webgpu/blob/master/README.md)
- [P] When the error ignores normals, authors compensate by lowering the threshold by hand: the same browser demo links its scenes with `nanite_errorthreshold=0.1` for a character and `0.5` to `1.0` for statues, and says "Normals on Jinx's face were a huge problem. In my app, I could just move the LOD error threshold slider to the left." — [Scthe/nanite-webgpu README](https://github.com/Scthe/nanite-webgpu/blob/master/README.md)
- [P] CDLOD is not error-driven: the range of each level is "two times larger than that of the previous one" so that triangle density on screen stays roughly even — [Strugar, CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)

**What experience says about visibility**
- [P] Sub-pixel plus a temporal filter is the stated condition for an unmorphed swap (see the Nanite summary above) — [zeux (2025)](https://zeux.io/2025/09/30/billions-of-triangles-in-minutes/)
- [P] Whole-level swaps are visible well before their per-triangle error suggests: "popping is annoyingly visible to the eye even for a relatively modest difference in the number of tris", and "You can't just say that each of the tris being changed have a small error, so changing 100 of them at the same time is similarly small - the human eye just does't work that way." — [Tom Forsyth, Comparison of VIPM Methods](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/gem_vipm_webversion.html)
- [P] Forsyth's rule of thumb for how far a whole-level swap must be pushed out: "If you have static LoD levels of 1500 and 1000 tris, you can't change from 1500 to 1000 tris at the same distance that VIPM changes from 1002 to 1000 tris. [...] from experience it's more like where the VIPM changes from 752 tris to 750." — [same](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/gem_vipm_webversion.html)
- [P] Changing a few triangles at a time needed nothing else, even with a slow camera: "No popping (geometric or lighting) was seen with VIPM, even when people deliberately looked for it and moved cameras around very slowly. I remain extremely skeptical that any form of complex vertex morphing or cross-fading is needed as long as you only change a few triangles as a time." — [same](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/gem_vipm_webversion.html)
- [P] Shading pops before shape does: "Lighting pop more noticeable than position pop in PM." and "vertex lighting can be very obvious with VIPM" — [Forsyth, Meltdown 2000 notes](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/Meltdown2000_notes.txt), [Meltdown 2001 notes](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/Meltdown2001_notes.txt)
- [P] The same was found in a cluster renderer: "visualizing the pixel positions was near-seamless as the LOD cut changed when you zoomed in or out. Pixel normals, however, had visible differences between LOD cuts." Feeding normals into the simplifier's error made them "near-seamless too" — [JMS55, Bevy 0.15](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-11-14-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-15/)
- [P] An unmorphed swap between meshes "of different triangle count and shape" produces "'popping' artifacts that appear if any vertex-based effect (such as vertex lighting) is used" — [Strugar, CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)
- [P] Sub-pixel cluster swaps are not always invisible in shipped titles: "if you've played recent games you might have noticed geometry popping in cutscenes now and again" — [zeux, Billions of triangles redux (2026)](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md)
- [S] A 2025 paper on peripheral level of detail argues that most current schemes rest on loose heuristics rather than threshold models, that sub-threshold control has not brought large speed-ups in interactive rendering, and that perception at and above threshold differs sharply — [Supra-threshold control of peripheral LOD, arXiv 2506.22583](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.22583)
- [S] A patent on per-object level of detail describes popping as picked up through spatial frequency and retinal velocity, and weaker during saccades; it gives no thresholds — [US 11941747](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11941747)

### Inferences
- All the variants above are the same expression, ρ = ε·H / (2·d·tan(fovy/2)) with H the viewport height in pixels and d the eye distance. They differ in three choices: the distance (centre, centre minus radius, nearest point of the box or sphere; the nearer the point, the more conservative), the axis (Godot uses width and the horizontal field of view), and which pixels count.
- "Which pixels" matters for a renderer at a pixel ratio of 1 or below. Godot multiplies the 3D resolution scale into the test, so its threshold is in rendered pixels and detail drops when rendering smaller. CesiumJS computes with the drawing-buffer height and then divides by the pixel ratio, so its threshold stays constant in device-independent pixels. A temporal filter can only hide what is sub-pixel in rendered pixels; what the eye resolves depends on angular size. The two only coincide at a pixel ratio of 1.
- Hoppe's demo value 0.003846 is 1/260, so by his own formula the tolerance is min(width, height)/520 pixels: 1 pixel in a 520-pixel window, about 2 at 1040.
- Pixel thresholds cannot be copied between systems. Cesium's own "divided by 8.0" note shows it within one product. meshoptimizer's ε is a normalised quadric distance (section 2), 3D Tiles' ε is "specific to that tileset", NVIDIA and Ponchio aim at a conservative maximum. This is the likely reason 16 is acceptable for 3D Tiles while 1 is the target for cluster renderers.
- Running at several pixels of error (the constraint in this project) is outside the regime in which any source calls an unmorphed swap safe. The Forsyth observations say what to remove in that regime: the simultaneity (spread the change over the approach, which is what a geomorph does) and the shading step (put normals into ε and move normals with the morph).
- A single automatic rule already exists in the form Godot uses: store ε per level at build time, keep one global τ in rendered pixels, let a per-object bias multiply ε. Per-kind lists of pixel breakpoints then reduce to per-asset ε values that the simplifier writes.

### Gaps
- Luebke et al., "Level of Detail for 3D Graphics" (2002) was not available; the formula above is sourced from implementations, not from the book.
- Neither Karis talk could be opened, so Nanite's own wording on the one-pixel target, on `r.Nanite.MaxPixelsPerEdge` and on the role of the temporal filter is not quoted; only third-party summaries are (recalled, unverified: the default edge target is 1 pixel and raising it is the documented quality-for-speed trade).
- No measurements were found of the pixel error at which a swap becomes visible for a still against a moving camera, or with against without temporal anti-aliasing. The two [S] items are the only perception sources, and neither was read.
- Unreal's and Unity's screen-size thresholds for static meshes, and Unity's newer automatic mesh LOD, were not reachable.

## 2. Error metrics for automatic simplification, and how seams are handled

### Takeaway
The working metric everywhere is the Garland and Heckbert quadric with Hoppe's attribute extension; what a simplifier reports is an approximate, normalised distance that also folds in weighted attribute error, not a guaranteed bound, and it has to be accumulated and forced to grow from level to level. Seams are kept either by topology (a seam vertex may only slide along its seam, and both sides move together) or, in meshoptimizer's newer permissive mode, by cost with selected seams protected.

### Cited Findings

**What the error is**
- [P] meshoptimizer's simplifier lists its basis: Garland and Heckbert 1997, Garland 1999, Lindstrom 2000, Hoppe 1999 ("New Quadric Metric for Simplifying Meshes with Appearance Attributes") and Hoppe and Marschner 2000 — [simplifier.cpp](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/src/simplifier.cpp)
- [P] "Target error is an approximate measure of the deviation from the original mesh using distance normalized to `[0..1]` range (e.g. `1e-2f` means that simplifier will try to maintain the error to be below 1% of the mesh extents)." — [meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md)
- [P] Relative by default, absolute on request: `meshopt_simplifyScale` "Returns the error scaling factor used by the simplifier to convert between absolute and relative extents"; "Relative error returned by meshopt_simplify via result_error must be *multiplied* by the scaling factor to get absolute error"; `meshopt_SimplifyErrorAbsolute` "changes the error metric from relative to absolute both for the input error limit as well as for the resulting error" — [meshoptimizer.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/src/meshoptimizer.h), [README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md)
- [P] In the code the scale is the largest side of the bounding box; each triangle's plane quadric is weighted by `sqrtf(area)` ("so that the error is scaled linearly; this tends to improve silhouettes"); the position error is the quadric value divided by the accumulated weight; the reported value is the largest accepted collapse error, square-rooted ("result_error is quadratic; we need to remap it back to linear"). With the sparse flag "errors will be computed relative to the subset extent", which is why the README recommends absolute errors there — [simplifier.cpp](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/src/simplifier.cpp)
- [P] The error is meant for selection: it "can be used to choose the correct level of detail based on screen size or solid angle" — [meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md)
- [P] NVIDIA describes the same quantity as "an approximate measure of the object-space distance between the decimated mesh and the original high-resolution mesh" and notes that in its builder "Inaccuracies from decimating vertex attributes such as normals and UVs are currently ignored." — [nv_cluster_lod_builder README](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/main/README.md)
- [P] Industry use since 2001: "Many people including myself have had excellent results with Garland and Heckbert's Quadric Error Metrics [GarlandHeckbert97], and the further refinements introduced by Hoppe [Hoppe99] for vertex attributes such as colour, texture co-ordinate and so on." — [Tom Forsyth, Comparison of VIPM Methods](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/gem_vipm_webversion.html)

**Attributes in the error**
- [P] "Both the target error and the resulting error combine positional error and attribute error, so the error can be used to control the LOD while taking attribute quality into account, assuming carefully chosen weights." — [meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md)
- [P] Weights: "For normalized attributes like normals and vertex colors, a weight around 1.0 is usually appropriate; internally, a change of `1/weight` in attribute value over a distance `d` is approximately equivalent to a change of `d` in position." Texture coordinates are optional ("simplification generally preserves texture quality reasonably well by default"), with "a weight of around 10-100" or `1/sqrt(average UV area)` — [meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md)
- [P] Hoppe's metric in one sentence by the library's author: it "models attribute error by extrapolating each attribute to any point in 3D space, and computing the squared difference between that and the target, weighted by triangle area", and because the extrapolation ignores the valid range of an attribute it can report "unrealistically large error" — [zeux (2026)](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md)
- [P] The fix taken from Unreal Engine: "their simplifier clamps the computed error to the accumulated triangle area [...] removing the triangle outright should have the error equal to the triangle's area; any combination of attributes on the triangle's vertices can't possibly make the visual impact worse." It shipped as `meshopt_SimplifyErrorClamped`, "recommended when using attribute aware simplification if error values are used for LOD selection"; on the Zorah scene the geometry streamed at the same threshold fell from 996 MB to 678 MB — [zeux (2026)](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md), [meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md)
- [F] Godot switched its selection error to include attributes in 2025: the metric "now incorporates attribute error", so that levels "that preserve the shape well but preserve attributes poorly are selected at a further distance cutoff"; vertex colours are "completely uncorrelated from positions" — [godot PR 106562](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/106562)
- [P] Godot's weights are 1.0 for the three normal components and 1.0 for colour when present — [importer_mesh.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/scene/resources/3d/importer_mesh.cpp)
- [P] A projected position-only error was judged insufficient by an implementer: "It's not a great metric for Nanite. I think that other vertex attributes have to be part of this function too." — [Scthe/nanite-webgpu README](https://github.com/Scthe/nanite-webgpu/blob/master/README.md)

**Error across levels**
- [P] "When generating a LOD chain, you can either re-simplify each LOD from the original mesh or use the previous LOD as the starting point for the next level. The latter approach is more efficient and produces smoother visual transitions between LOD levels while preserving mesh attributes better. With this method, resulting error values from previous levels should be accumulated for LOD selection." — [meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md)
- [P] Godot halves the triangle count per level and accumulates with an enforced growth: `current_error = MAX(current_error * 1.5f, step_error);` because "on coarse LODs, we may start getting *smaller* relative error compared to the previous LOD. To make sure the error is monotonic and strictly increasing, and to limit the switching (pop) distance, we ensure the error grows by an arbitrary factor each iteration." It stops at a relative error of 1.0 and stores `lod.distance = MAX(current_error * scale, CMP_EPSILON2)` — [importer_mesh.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/scene/resources/3d/importer_mesh.cpp)
- [F] The reasons given for simplifying from the previous level: "It's significantly faster", "attribute quadrics reduce the quality of attribute preservation the more they are accumulated", and "if a feature is reduced in a higher LOD, it will stay reduced or get reduced more significantly in lower LODs"; a 100-model import went from 25 s to 17 s — [godot PR 110027](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/110027)
- [P] In a cluster hierarchy: `bounds.error = std::max(bounds.error * config.simplify_error_merge_previous, error) + error * config.simplify_error_merge_additive;` commented "enforce error monotonicity (with an optional hierarchical factor to separate transitions more)"; group bounds are merged from the children because "it is incorrect to use the precise bounds of the merged or simplified mesh, because this may violate monotonicity" — [clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/demo/clusterlod.h)
- [P] "the angular error, or whatever metric is compared to a threshold, must never decrease with each level of decimation" — [nv_cluster_lod_builder README](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/main/README.md)
- [P] Bevy forces "the group error to be atleast as large as all of its constituent meshlet's individual errors", asserts monotonic errors and bounds, and simplifies with an unlimited target error ("no point in capping it for continuous LOD, gives better simplification results") — [from_mesh.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/from_mesh.rs), [JMS55, Bevy 0.15](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-11-14-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-15/)

**Seams, borders, faceted meshes**
- [P] Default behaviour: "The algorithm follows the topology of the original mesh in an attempt to preserve attribute seams, borders and overall appearance. For meshes with inconsistent topology or many seams, such as faceted meshes, it can result in simplifier getting "stuck" and not being able to simplify the mesh fully." — [meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md)
- [P] The mechanism: vertices are classed as manifold, border, seam ("on an attribute seam with exactly two attribute seam edges"), complex, fringe or locked; "border/seam vertices can collapse onto border/seam respectively, or locked"; "for seam collapses we need to move the seam pair together"; border edges get an extra edge quadric of weight 10 and seam edges 0.5 ("we try hard to maintain border edge geometry; seam edges can move more freely") — [simplifier.cpp](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/src/simplifier.cpp)
- [P] Flat shading is the worst case: "This is especially problematic for meshes with faceted normals (flat shading), as the simplifier may not be able to reduce the triangle count at all. The `meshopt_SimplifyPermissive` option relaxes these restrictions, allowing the simplifier to collapse vertices across attribute discontinuities when the resulting error is acceptable"; chosen seams are kept with `meshopt_SimplifyVertex_Protect`, found by comparing vertices that share a position — [meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md)
- [P] In permissive mode "it's important that attributes with discontinuities are given to the simplifier for attribute-based weighting, and that the output error is used during LOD selection; simply using a fixed triangle count budget does not guarantee reasonable appearance"; the mode was marked stable in meshoptimizer 1.3 (2026) — [zeux (2026)](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md)
- [F] The older remedy for faceted input: "faceted shading so normals create attribute discontinuities that restrict simplification" and "Sufficiently aggressive welding fixes this." — [zeux in bevy discussion 14998](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/discussions/14998)
- [P] Godot welds before simplifying: vertices at one position are merged when UVs, UV2, colour and tangent sign match and the normals are within the Normal Merge Angle (import default 20 degrees); their normals are averaged for the metric, and the level's indices are mapped back onto original vertices afterwards — [importer_mesh.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/scene/resources/3d/importer_mesh.cpp), [resource_importer_scene.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/editor/import/3d/resource_importer_scene.cpp)
- [P] The fallback that ignores topology, `meshopt_simplifySloppy`, "doesn't preserve attribute seams or borders" and gives "worse geometric quality and poor attribute quality"; clusterlod amplifies the error of groups that went through it by 2.0, and on Zorah it is taken "for ~0.01% of simplified groups" — [meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md), [clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/demo/clusterlod.h), [zeux (2026)](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md)
- [P] Borders as cut lines: `meshopt_SimplifyLockBorder` "can be useful for simplifying mesh subsets independently, so that the LODs can be combined without introducing cracks"; Godot sets it to "Lock geometric boundary in case the mesh is composed of multiple material subsets" and selects a level per surface — [meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md), [importer_mesh.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/scene/resources/3d/importer_mesh.cpp)
- [P] Cluster builders group and lock by position, not by attribute: Bevy decided to "Consider only the vertex position (and ignore things like UV seams) when determining meshlet groups" and to "Manually lock only vertices belonging to meshlet group borders" — [JMS55, Bevy 0.15](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-11-14-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-15/)

**Moving vertices, silhouettes, aggregate detail**
- [P] By default the simplified index buffer "references vertices from the original vertex buffer"; `meshopt_simplifyWithUpdate` instead "updates vertex positions and attributes in place", normals and colours "should be renormalized or clamped" afterwards, and unique vertices per level come "at a cost of ~doubling vertex memory used" — [meshoptimizer.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/src/meshoptimizer.h), [README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md)
- [P] Silhouettes in Hoppe's work: the refinement test stores a back-face region term per vertex next to the two error terms, and a separate paper is summarised as "Silhouette clipping [...] Efficient computation of mesh silhouette, used to clip coarse geometry." — [SrMesh.h](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/libHh/SrMesh.h), [Mesh-processing-library README](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/README.md)
- [P] A distance metric fails on foliage: once "every leaf has been simplified to a single triangle, the only possible next step the simplifier can take is to remove individual leaves", and because "every cluster looks approximately the same as any other cluster, has approximately the same error", the whole hedge erodes at once ("coordinated omission"); the cause is "loss of visible area, which leads to loss of coverage" — [zeux (2026)](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md)
- [P] Unreal's answer (Preserve Area) and meshoptimizer's variant: after simplifying a group "they compute the total surface area before and after simplification, and restore the area by taking the boundary edges of the resulting geometry, and expanding the vertex positions outwards"; meshoptimizer's `simplify_dilate_borders` redistributes only boundary area and is "most useful for foliage, should not be enabled everywhere"; "for card-based trees expanding the card boundary edges may result in odd looking branches from far away if they were baked into the texture" — [zeux (2026)](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md), [clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/demo/clusterlod.h)

### Inferences
- meshoptimizer's ε is a weighted, normalised quadric distance, so it is closer to an average than to a worst case. Treating it as "pixels of displacement" works at τ = 1 with headroom; at several pixels the difference between this and the true maximum deviation (silhouettes, thin parts) will show first, so either a safety factor or an explicit maximum-distance check per level is needed.
- Because `meshopt_simplify` returns only an index buffer over the original vertices, the coarse level's vertices are a subset of the fine level's. The natural morph target of a removed vertex is the surviving vertex it collapsed into. The public signatures have no output for that mapping, so an engine that wants exact collapse targets has to reconstruct them or patch the library. A closest-point target (the "place on the next coarser mesh") makes the fully morphed fine mesh only approximately equal to the coarse one.
- For flat-faced assets the topological mode cannot work, since every vertex is a seam. The two routes in the sources are welding by position before simplifying (Godot) and permissive mode with normals as weighted attributes (meshoptimizer 1.3). In both, the normal term must be in ε, otherwise a facet that tilts across a large screen area is scored as a small positional change.
- The enforced growth factor per level (1.5 in Godot, `simplify_error_merge_previous` in clusterlod) is the knob that spaces transitions apart. Section 4 shows it is also what makes room for a morph window.
- For card trees the right error is coverage, not distance. The discrete remedy (fewer, larger cards per level, with the area preserved) is the per-level form of the part-by-part swap this project already does.

### Gaps
- Garland and Heckbert 1997, Hoppe 1999 and Cohen, Olano and Manocha 1998 could not be opened. Recalled, unverified: the quadric is the sum of squared distances to the planes of the triangles around a vertex, held as a symmetric 4x4 matrix whose minimiser gives the new position, with extra perpendicular planes penalising movement of open boundaries; Hoppe's version measures attribute error by correspondence in 3D and stores attributes per wedge so seams survive; appearance-preserving simplification moves colour and normals into maps on the simplified mesh and bounds the "texture deviation", the 3D distance a texel moves, so the error of the mapped appearance can be stated in pixels.
- Nothing on Hausdorff distance was verified: no source read here states how any production simplifier relates its reported error to a one- or two-sided Hausdorff distance.
- Nanite's own error metric (how attributes enter, how it is made conservative) was not readable; only the area clamp is known, through Kapoulkine's account.

## 3. Geomorphing: progressive meshes, vertex-shader morphs from stored targets, CDLOD, Progressive Buffers

### Takeaway
A geomorph makes the finer mesh slide into the shape of the coarser one before the swap, so the swap itself changes nothing on screen; it costs a stored target per vertex (or none when the target can be computed, as on a grid) and no extra triangles, and its conditions are that every pass uses the same morph factor and that shading attributes move too. Terrain engines still ship it (CDLOD, OGRE, a current Godot terrain); for arbitrary meshes the one cluster-based design found, Progressive Buffers, is known here only through a one-slide summary.

### Cited Findings

**Hoppe's geomorph**
- [P] Definition in Hoppe's code: "A geomorph is a mesh which is able to smoothly transition between two endstates. It is a mesh together with a list of vertices and wedges that change and their endstates. It can be evaluated over a continuous blend parameter 0 <= alpha <= 1. It is created by applying a sequence of vsplits to a PM iterator." — [PMesh.h](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/libHh/PMesh.h)
- [P] What is interpolated: the vertex attribute is the position; the wedge attributes are normal, colour and texture coordinate, each with a pair of end states — [PMesh.h](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/libHh/PMesh.h)
- [P] The cost of duplicated vertices is named: a simple (wedge-free) mesh "is quicker to render since there is one less level of indirection (no wedges)", "but, it may be more expensive to geomorph since duplicated vertex positions must be interpolated separately" — [PMesh.h](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/libHh/PMesh.h)
- [P] Hoppe's summaries of the line of work: progressive meshes are an "Efficient, lossless, continuous-resolution representation of surface triangulations"; the 1998 terrain paper is "Visually smooth adaptation of mesh refinement using cascaded temporal geomorphs"; the code exposes `set_refine_morph_time` and `set_coarsen_morph_time` ("0 = disable") and keeps a per-vertex countdown ("time - 1 is left to achieve the goal") with "The increment to add during the morph", so these morphs run over a set number of update steps rather than over distance — [Mesh-processing-library README](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/README.md), [SrMesh.h](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/libHh/SrMesh.h)
- [P] His demo builds geomorphs between meshes of 10, 20, 40, ... 20000 faces, each level about twice the last — [create_geomorphs.sh](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/demos/create_geomorphs.sh)

**CDLOD (Strugar 2010)**
- [P] The principle: "the algorithm used to transition between LOD levels completely transforms the mesh of a higher level into the lower detailed one before the actual swap occurs. This ensures a perfectly smooth transition with no seams or artifacts." — [CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)
- [P] The morph is per vertex, not per chunk: "each vertex is morphed individually based on its own LOD metric unlike the method in [Ulrich 02], where the morph is performed per-node (per-chunk)"; "every block of eight triangles from the more detailed mesh is smoothly morphed into the corresponding block of two triangles on the less detailed mesh by gradual enlargement of the two triangles and reduction of the remaining six triangles into degenerate triangles that are not rasterized" — [CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)
- [P] No stored target is needed on a grid: `float2 fracPart = frac( gridPos.xy * g_gridDim.xy * 0.5 ) * 2.0 / g_gridDim.xy; return vertex.xy - fracPart * g_quadScale.xy * morphK;` then the height is sampled at the moved position — [CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)
- [P] The window: "This morph area usually covers the last 15%-30% of every LOD range." — [CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)
- [P] The condition against cracks: the distance used for the morph "can be an approximation as long as the approximating function is consistent over the whole dataset domain. This is necessary since, in order to prevent seams, the vertices on the node's grid mesh edges must remain exactly the same as the ones on the neighbouring nodes." — [CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)
- [P] The failure mode: "a single quadtree node can only support transition between two LOD layers. This limits the minimum possible viewing range, or the minimum quadtree depth"; when ranges are too tight for the node size the problem "can appear in the form of seams between LOD levels"; shrinking the morph area helps "but that can make the transition between levels noticeable" — [CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)
- [P] What it replaced: earlier chunk and clipmap methods need "additional connecting ("stitching") strips between different LOD levels", which "can cause various issues such as artifacts when rendering shadow maps; unwanted overdraw" — [CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)
- [P] Carrying it to precomputed meshes is sketched by the author: simplify a tessellated mesh per level, split it into the quadtree, and "Each vertex can contain additional morph data so that during the LOD transition some vertices can be moved into neighbouring ones in the vertex shader, eliminating triangles and effectively morphing the more detailed LOD mesh into the less detailed one"; the downsides are "the lengthy data pre-processing step, more difficult mesh compression" — [CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)

**Stored-target morphs in shipping open-source terrain**
- [P] OGRE stores one float per vertex: the delta "is a measure at a given vertex of by how much vertically a vertex will have to move to reach the point at which it will be removed in the next lower LOD" — [OgreTerrain.h](https://github.com/OGRECave/ogre/blob/master/Components/Terrain/include/OgreTerrain.h)
- [P] OGRE's factor is per tile and distance-driven: "fade from 0 to 1 in the last 25% of the distance" of the level's own range; the shader receives the factor and the target level, which "selectively applies the morph just to the vertices which would drop out at this LOD, even while using the single shared vertex data"; the terrain also keeps skirts, "used to hide terrain cracks" — [OgreTerrainQuadTreeNode.cpp](https://github.com/OGRECave/ogre/blob/master/Components/Terrain/src/OgreTerrainQuadTreeNode.cpp), [OgreTerrain.h](https://github.com/OGRECave/ogre/blob/master/Components/Terrain/include/OgreTerrain.h)
- [P] A current clipmap terrain for Godot morphs per vertex from a consistent function of position: "Geomorph vertex across clipmap LODs, set end and start for linear height interpolate", with `vertex_lerp = smoothstep(0.0, 1.0, (max_xz * inv_scale - _mesh_size - 4.0) / (_mesh_size - 4.0))` moving both the horizontal position and the height (`mix` of the heights at the fine and the coarse position) — [Terrain3D main.glsl](https://github.com/TokisanGames/Terrain3D/blob/main/src/shaders/main.glsl)

**Progressive Buffers (Sander and Mitchell 2005)**
- [S] As summarised from Karis's HPG 2022 slides: "Progressive buffers geomorph based on distance. If every vertex knows where it will move to in the next simplified level, it can move there before the next level is chosen." — [Journey to Nanite (slides)](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf)
- [S] The drawback named there: "Because the geomorphing is based purely on distance there is an assumption that everything in a LOD band is loaded." — [Journey to Nanite (slides)](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf)

**Normals, shading, shadows**
- [P] Shadows must see the same mesh: "When rendering a shadow map or a similar effect, the frustum cull is based on the shadow camera, but the actual LOD selection should still be based on the main camera settings. This is done to avoid rendering artifacts that would be caused by difference in the terrain mesh resulting from two different LOD functions" — [CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)
- [P] Godot does this for discrete levels: the level is chosen from the main camera's position in every pass, shadow passes included — [render_forward_clustered.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/servers/rendering/renderer_rd/forward_clustered/render_forward_clustered.cpp)
- [P] Terrain morphs sidestep normals by lighting from a map: CDLOD's lit datasets use "normals from the high resolution normalmap texture", and OGRE can "Request a terrain-wide normal map" — [CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf), [OgreTerrain.h](https://github.com/OGRECave/ogre/blob/master/Components/Terrain/include/OgreTerrain.h)
- [P] The same remedy for meshes, from 2000: "Bumpmapping: Stops VIPM lighting pops." — [Forsyth, Meltdown 2000 notes](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/Meltdown2000_notes.txt)
- [P] A continuous morph composes with further subdivision: "Since CDLOD performs the transition between LOD layers using smooth continuous morph of triangles with no discontinuities or T junctions, additional subdivision can easily be applied on top" — [CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)

### Inferences
- Cost. The finer level is on screen up to its swap distance with or without a morph, so a geomorph adds no triangles. It adds vertex data (nothing on a grid, one float in OGRE, a full target position plus target normal and texture coordinate in the general case) and a few vertex-shader operations. A cross-fade, by contrast, draws two levels (section 5). The practical gain of the morph is that τ can be raised for the same visibility.
- Window. A morph over the last fraction f of the distance to the swap equals an error-ratio window m = 1/(1 − f): OGRE's 25% is m ≈ 1.33, CDLOD's 15 to 30% is m ≈ 1.18 to 1.43. In error terms: draw level i while the coarser level's projected error ρ(i+1) is above τ, and morph as ρ(i+1) falls from m·τ to τ. Windows of successive levels do not overlap if ε grows by at least m per level, which Godot's factor of 1.5 already guarantees.
- Exactness. With collapse targets (each fine vertex goes to the vertex it collapsed into) the fully morphed fine mesh is the coarse mesh triangle for triangle plus zero-area triangles, as in CDLOD's eight-into-two description, so the swap is exactly invisible. With closest-point targets it is only approximately so.
- Distance-driven against time-driven. A morph that is a function of camera position (CDLOD, OGRE, Terrain3D) is stateless and reversible: nothing moves while the camera is still, it cannot flicker at a threshold, and it needs no hysteresis. Hoppe's temporal geomorphs and timed fades keep moving after the camera stops.
- Shading. Positions alone are not enough unless lighting comes from a map: per-vertex normals, colours and texture coordinates have to be interpolated to their targets too (Hoppe's wedge end states), and duplicated seam vertices must receive targets that keep the two sides together.
- Flat-faced objects. If face normals are derived from the geometry in the fragment stage, a position-only morph rotates each surviving facet continuously into the coarse facet while the removed triangles shrink to nothing, so the same morph applies. If face normals are stored on duplicated vertices, each needs the coarse facet's normal as its target.
- Motion vectors. A temporal anti-aliasing pass that reprojects with camera motion only will not know about morph motion; the previous frame's morph factor has to enter the velocity computation or the morphing surface will smear slightly. No source addresses this; it follows from how reprojection works.
- At several pixels of error the morph removes the discontinuity but not the movement: vertices travel up to about τ pixels across the window. During camera motion that is hidden in the image motion; with a still camera a distance-driven morph is frozen. The exposed case is a very slow approach.

### Gaps
- The Progressive Buffers paper could not be opened, so its data layout and its constraint on neighbouring clusters are not verified. Recalled, unverified: clusters are stored as static vertex and index buffers per level; each vertex carries its fine attributes and those of its parent in the next coarser level; the weight is computed per vertex from distance in the vertex shader, so a vertex shared by two clusters gets the same weight in both; levels are assigned in distance bands sized against the cluster radius so that adjacent clusters differ by at most one level and a cluster is fully morphed before it is swapped; texture levels are blended the same way.
- Hoppe's 1996 and 1998 papers were not opened; the description above comes from his published code and one-line summaries.
- Ulrich's chunked LOD and the geometry clipmap papers were read only through CDLOD's account of them.
- No source was found on how a geomorph interacts with temporal anti-aliasing, with per-vertex ambient occlusion, or with shadow maps beyond the main-camera rule.

## 4. Is geomorphing compatible with cluster hierarchies and DAGs (Batched Multi-Triangulation, Nanite-style meshlets)?

### Takeaway
Nothing was found that morphs between meshlet or cluster-DAG levels: no paper, post, thread or implementation, in the two web searches that ran before the search budget was exhausted and in direct look-ups of the meshoptimizer, Bevy and Godot repositories and of the Kapoulkine and JMS55 blogs. The published cluster systems hide the swap instead (error under a pixel plus a temporal filter). The structure does not forbid a morph: group borders are locked and a whole group switches together, which leaves one extra condition, derived below, that a group must not start morphing until every finer group feeding it has switched.

### Cited Findings

**What was looked for and not found**
- [S] An extended web search for meshlet and cluster level-of-detail geomorphing ("meshlet LOD geomorphing cluster hierarchy Nanite morph between LOD levels") returned Nanite re-implementation write-ups and no description of morphing between cluster levels — [search result: Recreating Nanite, LOD generation](https://blog.jglrxavpok.eu/2024/01/19/recreating-nanite-lod-generation.html), [search result: Nanite at Home](https://knnyism.com/posts/nanite/)
- [F] meshoptimizer's repository: the queries "geomorph" in issues, and "geomorph" and "popping" in discussions, return nothing; "morph" returns six threads, all on other subjects (morph targets, compression, remeshing) — [meshoptimizer discussions](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/discussions?discussions_q=geomorph)
- [F] The long thread in which Kapoulkine and JMS55 worked out DAG building contains, per the fetch summary, no discussion of popping, geomorphing, blending or temporal anti-aliasing; the Bevy thread on meshlet simplification does not either; Bevy's issue tracker has no result for "geomorph" — [meshoptimizer discussion 750](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/discussions/750), [bevy discussion 14998](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/discussions/14998)
- [P] clusterlod.h, the reference builder now also used by NVIDIA's sample, outputs clusters, groups, bounds and errors and a cut rule; it has no morph target, no collapse map and no transition parameter — [clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/demo/clusterlod.h)
- [P] JMS55's three virtual-geometry posts and Kapoulkine's 2025 and 2026 posts contain no mention of morphing between levels — [JMS55, Bevy 0.14](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-06-09-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-14/), [zeux (2026)](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md)

**What the cluster systems rely on instead**
- [P] "the transitions between LODs can be virtually imperceptible and extremely smooth, without extra rendering work. Traditional LODs typically have to hide transitions with crossfaded opacity between two levels." — [JMS55, Bevy 0.14](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-06-09-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-14/)
- [P] The swap is "hidden by TAA or other temporal filters" at under one pixel of error — [zeux (2025)](https://zeux.io/2025/09/30/billions-of-triangles-in-minutes/)
- [S] Karis's history of Nanite lists distance-based geomorphing in Progressive Buffers as a step on the way and names its assumption that "everything in a LOD band is loaded" as the drawback — [Journey to Nanite (slides)](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf)
- [S] The 2021 Nanite talk states that cracks form when independent clusters make different decisions and that the fix is to group clusters so they decide together; a write-up gives groups of 8 to 32 clusters, each decimated to half its triangles and split into 4 to 16 new clusters — [Nanite: A Deep Dive (slides)](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf), [The Candid Startup](https://www.thecandidstartup.org/2023/04/03/nanite-graphics-pipeline.html)
- [S] Kapoulkine tried a simpler two-level scheme (discrete levels, each split into meshlets, plus a second level per meshlet); the summary reports a more gradual transition but still a full-mesh pop at the end, and irregular intermediate levels on cluster boundaries — [zeuxcg on X](https://x.com/zeuxcg/status/1810841187433205817)

**The structural facts a morph would have to respect**
- [P] Borders are fixed for one step and released at the next: watertight transitions "require borders that match on both sides, and are obtained by keeping the border of the triangle edges fixed during decimation. Since these edges do not change, successive iterations of decimation must choose different borders and fix new edges to let the old ones decimate"; new groups "must instead be allowed to cross any border, and in fact encouraged to" — [nv_cluster_lod_builder README](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/main/README.md)
- [P] A group switches as one: "for LOD transitions to work, the entire group must change LOD at the same time, so all clusters in a group must share the same values" — [nv_cluster_lod_builder README](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/main/README.md)
- [P] The rate of change is bounded: the relationships "don't skip levels", and "LOD transitions may only happen across group borders, which places a limit on the rate of LOD change" — [nv_cluster_lod_builder README](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/main/README.md)
- [P] The cut is a pair of independent tests: a cluster is drawn if the simplified error of its own group "is over error threshold" and the cluster is original geometry "*or*" the simplified error of the group it was refined from "is at or under error threshold"; this needs monotonic errors and nested bounds — [clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/demo/clusterlod.h)
- [P] Without that, selecting in parallel gives overlaps: "two clusters that represent the same surface being drawn at once. This can happen when the bounding sphere of a decimated group is so far from the camera that its conservative angular error is smaller than a group's angular error that it was decimated from"; the fix is to "make bounding spheres bound generating geometry too" — [nv_cluster_lod_builder README](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/main/README.md)
- [P] A knob to space the levels apart already exists: `simplify_error_merge_previous`, "an optional hierarchical factor to separate transitions more" — [clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/demo/clusterlod.h)
- [P] Locking is done on positions shared between groups, so duplicated seam vertices lock together — [from_mesh.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/crates/bevy_pbr/src/meshlet/from_mesh.rs)
- [P] The terrain analogue of the missing condition: a node "can only support transition between two LOD layers", and violating the range constraint shows "in the form of seams between LOD levels" — [CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)

### Inferences
These follow from the cited structure; none is stated by a source.
- Within one step the borders take care of themselves. A group's border vertices are locked, so they are the same points in the group's fine clusters and in its simplified clusters. Morphing a group's interior toward its simplified version therefore never moves a vertex shared with a neighbouring group, whatever state that neighbour is in.
- The factor must be one value per group, taken from the same error and bounds that drive the cut, because the group switches as a whole. A per-vertex factor would have to be a consistent function of position (CDLOD's condition), which a per-group error test is not.
- The new condition comes from the regrouping. A border that was locked between groups G and H at level k lies in the interior of a group G′ at level k+1, where it is free to move. If G′ began to morph toward level k+2 while H was still drawn at level k, the clusters that came from G would pull that shared border away from H's unmoved fine clusters, and a crack would open. So G′ may only start morphing once every group feeding it has completed its own switch.
- That condition can be met with stored errors alone. Let G′ morph while its projected simplified error lies between τ and m·τ. No feeding group is still fine in that window if the projected error of G′ is at least m times that of each feeding group. With nested bounds (the parent's nearest point is never farther than the child's) this holds whenever the object-space error grows by at least m along every edge of the DAG. That is `simplify_error_merge_previous` ≥ m in clusterlod, or Godot's 1.5 in a chain. The same inequality makes the swap exact: at the moment a feeding group switches, G′ is still unmorphed.
- Storage. Each vertex of a cluster needs its target in the simplified group. With subset-placement simplification that is the index of a surviving vertex of the same group (borders map to themselves); clusters that store their own vertex copies (as NVIDIA's sample does) would carry a second position and normal instead. The builders do not export this map today.
- Residency. The finer clusters and their targets must be loaded for the whole window; this is the drawback Karis is reported to name for distance-band morphing. A renderer that keeps whole objects resident does not have it.
- Rendering path. A visibility buffer or a software rasteriser must apply the same morph in every pass that reads positions (depth, shadow, shading reconstruction), which is extra state per group in the cluster shader.
- Whole-object levels, the present design of this project, are the degenerate case with one group per level: the condition reduces to "ε grows by at least m per level", and no border bookkeeping is needed. The DAG condition only matters if parts of one mesh are to sit at different levels.
- Godot's per-surface selection with locked surface borders is a shipped example of the first inference at the granularity of materials: neighbouring surfaces pick levels independently and stay closed because the border never moves.

### Gaps
- The absence is weaker than it looks: only two web searches on this question ran before the turn's search budget was exhausted, and X, Mastodon, Reddit, forum threads, the Unreal source and the Batched Multi-Triangulation and Adaptive TetraPuzzles papers could not be opened. Whether Cignoni, Ponchio and colleagues discuss geomorphing for their patch hierarchies is not known here. A follow-up should search those papers, Karis's posts and the terms "meshlet geomorph", "cluster LOD geomorphing" and "continuous LOD morph nanite" again.
- Why Epic did not morph is known only from a search summary of one slide (residency). Other reasons that are plausible but unsourced: twice the position data per cluster against Nanite's compression, and the software rasteriser's inner loop.
- No implementation was found to confirm the inequality derived above in practice.

## 5. Alternatives: alpha and dithered cross-fades, hysteresis, screen-space blending

### Takeaway
A cross-fade hides any swap regardless of how the two levels relate, at the price of drawing both for the length of the fade. Engines implement it either with alpha blending, which moves the object into the transparent pass (Godot's built-in fade), or as a screen-door dither with complementary patterns, which stays in the opaque pass (Unity, Bevy, Cesium for Unreal); the dither shows as noise that a temporal filter softens only if the pattern does not change over time. Hysteresis does not hide a swap; it only stops it repeating.

### Cited Findings
- [P] The cost of alpha blending two levels: "pixel fillrate is increased dramatically. Not only is the object being drawn (and animated and lit and so on) twice, but both passes (yes, both, not just one – think about it) need to use alpha-blending, which is more expensive than normal rendering. And if the object normally has alpha-blended parts, then things can get a bit tricky" — [Tom Forsyth, Comparison of VIPM Methods](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/gem_vipm_webversion.html)
- [P] Screen-door is the older and better form: flight simulators "actually used screen-door "transparency", which works much better and you don't have to fiddle with the Z-buffer to get things to work properly. These days you could do the same by using MSAA with a stipple pattern on the samples - similar to using the alpha-to-coverage trick. Either way, it's rendering the object twice." — [same](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/gem_vipm_webversion.html)
- [P] Godot's three modes for manual levels: Disabled "Uses hysteresis to switch between LOD levels instantly [...] provides the best performance as it doesn't force rendering to become transparent"; Self and Dependencies use alpha blending, and each "forces transparent rendering on the object during its fade transition, so it has a performance impact" — [Godot docs, visibility ranges](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/blob/master/tutorials/3d/visibility_ranges.rst)
- [P] Godot on dithering instead: "Higher performance, as dithering transparency is faster to render compared to alpha blending" and "No visual glitches due to transparency sorting issues during LOD transitions"; "The downside of dithering is that a "noisy" pattern is visible during LOD fade transitions. This may not be as noticeable at higher viewport resolutions or when temporal antialiasing is enabled." — [Godot docs, visibility ranges](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/blob/master/tutorials/3d/visibility_ranges.rst)
- [F] In the Godot proposal for a built-in dither fade: "two consecutive LODs have to be rendered at once", and a cross-fade dither "works well" if the pattern is inverted on the two levels — [godot-proposals 5240](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/5240)
- [P] Unity's implementation makes the two patterns complementary by the sign of one factor: "LOD0 must use this function with ditherFactor 1..0 / LOD1 must use this function with ditherFactor -1..0", `float f = ditherFactor - CopySign(p, ditherFactor); clip(f);` with the note "This preserves the symmetry s.t. if LOD 0 has f = x, LOD 1 has f = -x." — [Unity SRP Core, Common.hlsl](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.core/ShaderLibrary/Common.hlsl)
- [P] Unity on the pattern and temporal anti-aliasing: "Generate a spatially varying pattern. Unfortunately, varying the pattern with time confuses the TAA, increasing the amount of noise." The Universal pipeline samples a screen-space dither texture the same way — [Common.hlsl](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.core/ShaderLibrary/Common.hlsl), [LODCrossFade.hlsl](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.universal/ShaderLibrary/LODCrossFade.hlsl)
- [P] Bevy's visibility ranges: "To avoid an artifact known as *popping* between levels, each level has a *margin*, within which the object transitions gradually from invisible to visible using a dithering effect"; the end margin of one level must equal the start margin of the next, and "smooth crossfades between LOD levels require that all LODs of a mesh be at *precisely* the same position" — [Bevy visibility/range.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/crates/bevy_camera/src/visibility/range.rs)
- [P] Timed fades for streamed tiles: Cesium for Unreal can "Use a dithering effect when transitioning between tiles of different LODs" over `LodTransitionLength = 0.5f` seconds, and "When this is set to true, Frustrum Culling and Fog Culling are always disabled" — [Cesium3DTileset.h (cesium-unreal)](https://github.com/CesiumGS/cesium-unreal/blob/main/Source/CesiumRuntime/Public/Cesium3DTileset.h)
- [P] The same in a three.js-based renderer: a plugin "overrides material shaders to fade tile geometry in and out as tile LODs change, preventing pop-in", with `fadeDuration = 250` ms and `maximumFadeOutTiles = 50` ("If exceeded, tiles pop instead of fading") — [3DTilesRendererJS plugins API](https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS/blob/master/src/three/plugins/API.md)
- [P] Hysteresis in three.js: `addLevel( object, distance = 0, hysteresis = 0 )`, "Threshold used to avoid flickering at LOD boundaries, as a fraction of distance"; the level's switch distance is reduced by that fraction while the level is the visible one — [three.js LOD.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/objects/LOD.js)
- [P] Hysteresis in Godot's manual levels: the margins prevent "LODs from popping back and forth too quickly when the camera is moving" at the boundary — [Godot docs, visibility ranges](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/blob/master/tutorials/3d/visibility_ranges.rst)
- [P] Forsyth's verdict on cross-fading against incremental change: "it's rendering the object twice. I just can't see that being a win over VIPM." — [Comparison of VIPM Methods](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/gem_vipm_webversion.html)

### Inferences
- A cross-fade is the only method here that needs no correspondence between the two levels, which is why it suits swaps a morph cannot express: a mesh to an impostor, cards to fewer cards, a group of objects to one merged object.
- Its cost is the overlap: for a fade spanning a fraction f of each level's range, both levels are drawn over that fraction. With complementary dither the pixel-shading cost stays near one level because each pixel survives in only one of them, but vertex and raster work is doubled, and depth pre-passes and shadow maps need the same clip.
- The artefacts are the dither noise (worse at low resolution, which is this project's case), a brief stipple edge on silhouettes, and for timed fades motion that continues after the camera stops. Unity's comment says to keep the pattern fixed in screen space under a temporal filter.
- Timed fades need hysteresis as well, or a camera hovering at a threshold replays the fade; distance-driven fades with matching margins (Bevy) do not.
- Hysteresis alone is the cheapest tool and the only one for assets that swap at once, but it trades flicker for a level that is wrong on one side of the band.

### Gaps
- Unreal's dithered LOD transition and Unity's LOD Group documentation could not be opened (recalled, unverified: both fade over time or over a transition width with a screen-door mask, and Unreal offers a dither that is varied per frame for use with temporal anti-aliasing, which is the opposite of Unity's comment above; the two were not reconciled).
- The screen-space blending papers could not be opened (recalled, unverified: Giegl and Wimmer, "Unpopping", 2007, render one level opaque while the other blends so that the pair never becomes see-through; Scherzer and Wimmer, 2008, alternate the two levels over successive frames and blend with the previous frame so that only one level is drawn per frame).
- Nothing was found on hashed alpha or stochastic transparency as a transition method beyond the mention in the Godot proposal.

## 6. Temporal stability: accumulation that hides sub-pixel swaps, and flicker at a threshold

### Takeaway
A temporal filter averages jittered frames, so a change smaller than a rendered pixel is blended in over several frames instead of appearing in one; this is the stated reason unmorphed cluster swaps pass. Flicker at a threshold is prevented in three ways in the sources: a hysteresis band, a selection that is a pure function of the camera with a morph or sub-pixel error on top, and, for budgets driven by frame rate, a dead band and a clamp.

### Cited Findings
- [P] How the filter works: "Temporal antialiasing works by *converging* the result of previously rendered frames into a single, high-quality frame. This is a continuous process that works by jittering the position of all vertices in the scene every frame. This jittering is done to capture sub-pixel detail" — [Godot docs, 3D antialiasing](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/blob/master/tutorials/3d/3d_antialiasing.rst)
- [P] Its side effects: "TAA introduces a small amount of blur when enabled in still scenes, but this blurring effect becomes more pronounced when the camera is moving. Another downside of TAA is that it can exhibit *ghosting* artifacts behind moving objects. Rendering at a higher framerate will allow TAA to converge faster" — [same](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/blob/master/tutorials/3d/3d_antialiasing.rst)
- [P] The dependence of cluster swaps on it: the switch at under a pixel "is hidden by TAA or other temporal filters" — [zeux (2025)](https://zeux.io/2025/09/30/billions-of-triangles-in-minutes/)
- [P] A pattern that changes every frame works against it: "varying the pattern with time confuses the TAA, increasing the amount of noise" — [Unity SRP Core, Common.hlsl](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.core/ShaderLibrary/Common.hlsl)
- [P] Godot's automatic levels have no hysteresis and no transition: the selection is the plain comparison shown in section 1, re-evaluated each frame from the camera position — [mesh_storage.h](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/servers/rendering/renderer_rd/storage_rd/mesh_storage.h)
- [P] The cluster cut is likewise stateless: two threshold tests per cluster on stored errors and bounds — [clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/demo/clusterlod.h)
- [P] Hysteresis where the swap is visible: three.js's fraction of distance and Godot's margins (section 5) — [three.js LOD.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/objects/LOD.js), [Godot docs, visibility ranges](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/blob/master/tutorials/3d/visibility_ranges.rst)
- [P] Spacing levels at build time limits how often a threshold is crossed: Godot grows the error by 1.5 per level "to limit the switching (pop) distance" — [importer_mesh.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/scene/resources/3d/importer_mesh.cpp)
- [P] A frame-rate-driven budget with a dead band: Nexus changes its error by 5% per frame only when the frame-rate ratio leaves the band 0.9 to 1.1, and clamps it to 2 to 15 pixels — [nexus.js](https://github.com/cnr-isti-vclab/nexus/blob/master/html/js/nexus.js)
- [P] A warning against that kind of feedback: "Attempts were made to have the engine auto-throttle itself and sit at 30Hz, but I have never got that sort of feedback mechanism to work well in any engine I've tried it in - framerates depend on too many different things, and you can get oscillations or pointless ugliness that way. Having it key off an independent feature (rather than a feedback from the framerate) seems to give far more predictability." — [Tom Forsyth, Comparison of VIPM Methods](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/gem_vipm_webversion.html)
- [P] Hoppe's terrain demo regulates towards a face count rather than a frame time (`-sr_regulatenf $nfaces` with nfaces = 40000, `-sr_gain .3333`, `-sr_fracvtrav .3333`; read from the flag names: regulate number of faces, gain, fraction of vertices traversed) and smooths each change with a timed geomorph — [view_gcanyon_interactive.sh](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/demos/view_gcanyon_interactive.sh), [SrMesh.h](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/libHh/SrMesh.h)
- [P] Camera motion is used to gate work, not quality: Cesium waits `foveatedTimeDelay=0.2` s "after the camera stops moving before deferred tiles start loading in" and skips requests for tiles "that will likely be unused when they come back because of the camera's movement" — [Cesium3DTileset.js](https://github.com/CesiumGS/cesium/blob/main/packages/engine/Source/Scene/Cesium3DTileset.js)
- [P] Streaming must never show a hole while waiting: cesium-native keeps the detailed tiles when zooming out until the coarser one is loaded ("Ancestor Meets SSE"), because otherwise part of the model would "suddenly vanish, only to reappear a moment later" — [cesium-native selection algorithm](https://github.com/CesiumGS/cesium-native/blob/main/doc/topics/selection-algorithm-details.md)

### Inferences
- The accumulation argument only covers error measured in rendered pixels, and only what the filter keeps in its history. At several pixels the change is larger than the jitter footprint; the filter then blends a visible step over a few frames (a short dissolve), it does not hide it. Where the filter rejects or clamps history that no longer matches the new frame, even that dissolve may be cut short.
- With a distance-driven morph the image is a continuous function of the camera, so there is nothing to flicker and no hysteresis is needed; the index swap at the end of the window changes no pixel. Hysteresis is then only needed for assets that still swap at once.
- A dynamic τ (to hold 60 frames per second) moves every morph a little at once when it changes. Under a morph this is a continuous deformation rather than a wave of pops, which makes a slowly varying budget more tolerable than in a discrete system; Forsyth's warning still applies to the controller, so a dead band, a slow rate and a clamp (as in Nexus), or a driver other than frame time, are indicated.
- Level selection should use the unjittered camera so the jitter itself cannot cross a threshold; no source says this, it follows from the selection being a threshold test.

### Gaps
- Karis's own account of how temporal accumulation hides cluster swaps, and whether Nanite adds any hysteresis to the cut, could not be read.
- Unreal's per-mesh hysteresis setting was not reachable (recalled, unverified: a hysteresis value applied when moving from a complex to a simpler level).
- No source quantifies how many pixels of change a temporal filter with a given history weight can absorb.

## 7. Shading continuity across levels

### Takeaway
A coarser level changes shading in three ways the positional error does not see: normals shift, detail that used to scatter light is gone (so highlights sharpen and sparkle), and coverage drops. The practices found are to score normals in the simplifier's error, to keep shading detail in normal maps that every level shares, to widen roughness from the variance of normals (on screen and in texture mips), and to restore lost area on foliage.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Shading is the first thing to pop: "Lighting pop more noticeable than position pop in PM." — [Forsyth, Meltdown 2000 notes](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/Meltdown2000_notes.txt)
- [P] Detail in a map survives geometry changes: "Bumpmapping: Stops VIPM lighting pops." — [same](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/Meltdown2000_notes.txt)
- [P] Material changes must be faded before the geometric swap: at low detail "Changes happen at VIPM level changes / Need to have 'faded' fancy effects by then, so no abrupt visible change." — [Forsyth, Meltdown 2001 notes](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/Meltdown2001_notes.txt)
- [P] Scoring normals fixes visible normal steps in a cluster renderer: "the deformation error the simplifier outputs (which we feed directly into the LOD cut selection shader) accounts for not only position deformation, but also normal deformation"; still open: "I'm not currently accounting for UV coordinate deformation, and the weights I chose for position vs normal influence are completely arbitrary" — [JMS55, Bevy 0.15](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-11-14-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-15/)
- [P] The general difficulty: "pre-calculating an error amount that perfectly accounts for every aspect of human perception, for meshes with arbitrary materials, is a _really_ hard problem. The best we can do is spend time tweaking heuristics" — [same](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-11-14-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-15/)
- [P] Simplifiers that keep original vertices keep original normals at the survivors; those that move vertices must repair them: attributes "that have specific constraints like normals and colors should be renormalized or clamped after the function returns new data" — [meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md)
- [F] Rebuilding level normals by casting rays at the original mesh was tried and removed in Godot: "raycasting generally reduces normal quality compared to native simplifier results", and it cost vertex memory and import time — [godot PR 98620](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/98620)
- [P] Widening roughness from the screen-space variance of the normal (Godot's roughness limiter, citing Tokuyoshi and Kaplanyan's "Improved Geometric Specular Antialiasing"): `variance = amount * (dot(dndu, dndu) + dot(dndv, dndv)); kernelRoughness2 = min(2.0 * variance, limit); roughness = sqrt(min(1.0, roughness2 + kernelRoughness2))`, on by default with amount 0.25 and limit 0.18 — [scene_forward_clustered.glsl](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/servers/rendering/renderer_rd/shaders/forward_clustered/scene_forward_clustered.glsl), [ProjectSettings.xml](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/doc/classes/ProjectSettings.xml)
- [P] Its stated reach: it "works best on detailed geometry", and helps "to an extent, though not as much as enabling" temporal anti-aliasing — [Godot docs, 3D antialiasing](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/blob/master/tutorials/3d/3d_antialiasing.rst), [ProjectSettings.xml](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/doc/classes/ProjectSettings.xml)
- [P] The same in Filament: `specularAntiAliasing` "Reduces specular aliasing and preserves the shape of specular highlights as an object moves away from the camera", with a screen-space variance (default 0.15) and a clamping threshold (default 0.2) — [Filament Materials guide](https://github.com/google/filament/blob/main/docs/Materials.md.html)
- [P] Widening roughness in texture mips from the normal map (Godot's importer): for each texel of each mip the normals under its footprint are averaged, r is the length of the average, and if r < 1 then `kappa = (3r - r^3) / (1 - r^2)`, `variance = 0.25 / kappa`, `roughness = sqrt(roughness^2 + min(3 * variance, 0.4^2))` — [image.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/core/io/image.cpp)
- [P] Its limit: "Limiting roughness on import only helps reduce specular aliasing within textures, not the aliasing that occurs on geometry edges on detailed meshes." — [Godot docs, 3D antialiasing](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/blob/master/tutorials/3d/3d_antialiasing.rst)
- [P] Per-vertex colour data needs its own protection: it "may be necessary to preserve vertex colors when textures are not used in the first place", and Godot now passes colours to the simplifier with weight 1.0 — [meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md), [importer_mesh.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/scene/resources/3d/importer_mesh.cpp)
- [P] Coverage is brightness for foliage: simplification "leads to loss of visible area, which leads to loss of coverage: a simplified mesh covers fewer pixels far away"; restoring area by dilating the survivors "retains coverage by redistributing surface area to surviving triangles; the result is that each successive layer of the cluster DAG gets fewer, bigger, leaves" — [zeux (2026)](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md)
- [P] Its limit, and where the field is heading: Unreal's own documentation says the setting "should be enabled on all foliage meshes and nothing else"; "ideally what you want here is a filterable representation, and triangle meshes are anything but", hence Epic's voxel-based foliage — [zeux (2026)](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md)
- [P] An image-driven alternative to geometric metrics exists as published code: "Differentiable rasterization applied to 3D model simplification tasks, as described in the paper: Appearance-Driven Automatic 3D Model Simplification" (Hasselgren, Munkberg, Lehtinen, Aittala and Laine, Eurographics Symposium on Rendering 2021); only the repository's front page was read, not the paper — [NVlabs/nvdiffmodeling](https://github.com/NVlabs/nvdiffmodeling)

### Inferences
- The step in brightness or glossiness at a swap is the same phenomenon as specular aliasing: geometry that used to spread the highlight is replaced by a flatter surface with the same roughness. A screen-space limiter reacts to the normal variation that is still in the mesh, so it helps the fine level and does nothing for the coarse one. The level-to-level fix is to bake the lost variation into the coarse level, either as wider roughness per vertex or per texel, or as a normal map carried by every level.
- With a geomorph the normal targets make the shading change continuous, but the end state is still the coarse level's shading. Whether the end state matches is decided by the metric (normals in ε) and by the roughness correction, not by the morph.
- Baked normal maps and vertex normals behave differently across levels. A tangent-space map moves with the interpolated tangent frame, so it inherits any change in vertex normals; an object-space map, or lighting detail stored independently of the frame (as terrain does), is unaffected by simplification. For small assets with few texels the vertex normal dominates and must be in ε.
- Ambient occlusion stored per vertex is an attribute like colour: give it a weight in the simplifier and a morph target. Occlusion baked for the fine mesh into a texture stays consistent across levels by construction; recomputing it per level introduces a brightness step.
- For trees, keeping total card area constant per level (fewer, larger cards) is the brightness-preserving version of removing cards, and can be made continuous by scaling cards during the approach rather than at the swap.

### Gaps
- Toksvig's "Mipmapping Normal Maps" and Olano and Baker's LEAN mapping could not be opened (recalled, unverified: Toksvig shortens the averaged, unnormalised normal in each mip and uses its length to lower the specular power, f = |N| / (|N| + s(1 − |N|)); LEAN stores the first and second moments of the normal's slope so that linear filtering yields a per-pixel covariance that is added to the base roughness, giving anisotropic widening). Godot's importer above is a verified implementation of the same idea with a different variance estimate.
- Cohen et al.'s appearance-preserving simplification could not be opened (see the recollection in section 2).
- No source was found that deals with keeping ambient occlusion consistent across levels, with baked normal maps against vertex normals as such, or with roughness corrections stored per level; the corresponding inferences are unsupported by a direct citation.
- Recalled, unverified: Cook et al., "Stochastic Simplification of Aggregate Detail" (2007), removes elements at random, enlarges the rest to keep area, and corrects contrast; it is the standard reference for the foliage case.

## 8. Why fully continuous view-dependent LOD fell out of use on GPUs, why cluster granularity won, and what "continuous" means today

### Takeaway
Per-vertex view-dependent refinement lost because it spends CPU time and complex data structures to save triangles that GPUs draw for less than the bookkeeping costs, and because it needs buffers that change every frame; fixed batches (chunks, patches, clusters) with a cheap, parallel, independent test per batch keep the GPU fed. "Continuous" now means a selection that varies smoothly over space in cluster-sized steps, with each step an unmorphed swap small enough to be hidden, not a mesh that changes continuously.

### Cited Findings
- [P] The argument as made for terrain in 2010: "there is little benefit of having an algorithm that produces optimal triangle distribution on the CPU if it cannot provide enough triangles for the hardware graphics pipeline or if it uses too much CPU processing power. The API, driver, and OS layer between the CPU and GPU is also a common bottleneck [...] Therefore, even a simplistic GPU-based approach can be faster and provide better visual results than those complex approaches executed on the CPU and formerly considered optimal." — [Strugar, CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)
- [P] The same for objects in 2001: view-dependent progressive meshing "is significantly more complex and requires some fairly extensive data structures to achieve good efficiency"; view-independent progressive meshes had "beaten off VDPM and static LoD methods for the most visual bang for the CPU buck (though it is worth noting that VDPM methods are still challengers for large landscapes, especially regular-height-field ones)" — [Tom Forsyth, Comparison of VIPM Methods](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/gem_vipm_webversion.html)
- [P] What made a method viable was not touching vertices on the CPU: with sliding-window VIPM "no runtime modification is made to the index or vertex lists" and there is "almost zero CPU use to change level of detail"; an option that rewrites vertices per instance is dismissed because it "requires the CPU to copy around vertex data when doing collapses/splits". The game shipped with it: "The runtime code was very simple - five lines of code with each DrawPrim call." — [same](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/gem_vipm_webversion.html)
- [P] Hoppe's own follow-up was about fitting GPUs: "Efficient implementation of progressive meshes [...] Progressive mesh data structures compatible with GPU vertex buffers." — [Mesh-processing-library README](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/README.md)
- [P] His view-dependent implementation shows the per-frame state that had to be maintained: linked lists of active vertices and active faces, a refinement record per vertex, an `adapt_refinement(int nvtraverse)` step that can be limited to part of the vertices each frame (the terrain demo passes `-sr_fracvtrav .3333`), and morph countdowns — [SrMesh.h](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/libHh/SrMesh.h), [view_gcanyon_interactive.sh](https://github.com/hhoppe/Mesh-processing-library/blob/main/demos/view_gcanyon_interactive.sh)
- [P] Why the unit became the cluster: "Groups are made from clusters of triangle rather than just triangles for performance reasons. [...] Whole triangle clusters are swapped in and out at runtime for detail transitions." — [nv_cluster_lod_builder README](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/main/README.md)
- [P] Why the test became independent per cluster: an exact graph cut "would be challenging to do quickly and in parallel on a GPU. We ideally want to test whether to render a cluster independently", which monotonic errors and nested bounds make possible — [same](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/main/README.md)
- [P] The lineage NVIDIA cites: "A pyramidal data structure for triangle-based surface description" (1989), "On Levels of Detail in Terrains" (1995), "Efficient Implementation of Multi-Triangulations" (1998), "Visualization of Large Terrains Made Easy" (2001), "Batched Multi Triangulation" (2005), then the Nanite talk (2021) — [same](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/main/README.md)
- [P] What "continuous" means in that line: "Continuous LOD allows for fine-grained control over geometric detail within a mesh, compared to traditional discrete LOD. Clusters of triangles are carefully precomputed by decimating the original mesh in a way that they can be seamlessly combined across different LOD levels." — [same](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/main/README.md)
- [P] The same, from two implementers: "part of the mesh can be low resolution, part of the mesh can be high resolution, and a third part can be somewhere in between - all at the time same time, all on a very granular level"; "The goal of the DAG is not to "use fewer triangles for far objects". The goal is to have a consistent 1 pixel == 1 triangle across the entire screen. [...] It allows you to "sample" geometry at any detail level you choose." — [JMS55, Bevy 0.14](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-06-09-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-14/), [Scthe/nanite-webgpu README](https://github.com/Scthe/nanite-webgpu/blob/master/README.md)
- [P] The other meaning, in terrain: CDLOD is continuous because "the LOD function is the same across the whole rendered mesh and is based on the precise three-dimensional distance", with a morph between levels — [Strugar, CDLOD paper](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)
- [P] Cluster hierarchies are not treated as the settled answer by the author of the library most engines use: "Should all engines embrace clustered level of detail? I'm still not sure. There's a place for hierarchical continuous LOD but it's not obvious to me that this is a vital, or the final, step; triangles continue to be suboptimal in many respects"; "runtime efficiency of these systems is often worse than potential alternatives we could have been building, and on-disk impact is non-trivial" — [zeux (2026)](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md)
- [P] They also depend on reaching very small triangles cheaply: "if you want pixel-sized triangles (the main selling point for most people), you **need** a software rasterizer" — [Scthe/nanite-webgpu README](https://github.com/Scthe/nanite-webgpu/blob/master/README.md)
- [F] Bevy's author states there is "no plan to support older GPUs" for its cluster renderer and treats mobile as out of scope — [bevy discussion 14998](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/discussions/14998)
- [P] Discrete automatic levels remain a first-class path in 2026 and benefit from the same simplifier work: "all of simplification changes are quite helpful with or without the cluster hierarchy; and even the foliage "hacks" can be applied to discrete LOD pipelines with similarly good results" — [zeux (2026)](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md)
- [S] Karis's history of Nanite is reported to walk through progressive meshes and distance-based geomorphing in Progressive Buffers before arriving at grouped clusters with locked boundaries — [Journey to Nanite (slides)](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf)

### Inferences
- There are two separate properties that the word "continuous" has covered: adaptivity over space (how finely the detail can vary across a mesh) and continuity over time (whether a change of level is a step or a slide). Hoppe's view-dependent meshes had both, per vertex. Cluster DAGs keep the first at cluster granularity and give up the second, relying on the step being under a pixel. CDLOD and Progressive Buffers keep the second: they select at chunk or cluster granularity and evaluate the morph per vertex.
- The cluster approach therefore assumes the error can be driven below a pixel everywhere. Where a renderer cannot afford that triangle density (no software rasteriser, a mobile GPU, 60 frames per second in a browser), the steps become visible and the temporal property has to be supplied again by a morph or a fade. This is the position this project is in.
- For objects that fit on screen as a whole, spatial adaptivity inside one mesh buys little, and a chain of whole-object levels with a geomorph gives temporal continuity at a fraction of the machinery. A cluster hierarchy pays off for meshes much larger than their distance to the camera (terrain, buildings, large rocks), which is where section 4's condition would matter.
- The 2001 argument against per-vertex refinement was CPU time per frame; on a GPU-driven path the remaining argument is data layout, since static, compressible, streamable buffers are what clusters provide and what a per-vertex scheme cannot.

### Gaps
- Luebke et al. (2002) and the GPU implementations of view-dependent progressive meshes by Hu, Sander and Hoppe (2009, 2010) could not be opened, so the academic account of the decline is missing (recalled, unverified: those papers moved refinement to the GPU with relaxed dependencies and still used geomorphs).
- Karis's own reasons for rejecting each earlier approach are known only through a search summary.
- Jonathan Blow's 2002 "Rendering Level-of-Detail Forecast" and Ulrich's chunked LOD paper, both usually cited for the turn towards static batches, were not reachable.
