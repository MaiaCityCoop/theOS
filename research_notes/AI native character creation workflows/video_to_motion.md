## 1. Video generation models as of November 2026

### Takeaway
The field moved a full generation since mid-2026: Sora is gone (API removed 24 September 2026), and the current flagships are Kling 4.0 (October 2026, 3 to 30 s clips, up to 10 reference images plus saved "elements"), Seedance 2.5, Wan 3.0, MiniMax Hailuo H3, Luma Ray 3.2, Google Veo 3.1 alongside the newer Gemini Omni Flash, and Runway Gen-4.5 with Act-Two. Kling 4.0, Seedance 2.5, Wan 3.0, Hailuo H3 and Gemini Omni Flash are callable through fal.ai with reference-image inputs, and Luma Ray 3.2 with first/last frames and keyframes; published per-second prices sit between roughly $0.05 and $0.40. The only open-weight models whose licences I could read are Wan 2.2 (Apache-2.0) and HunyuanVideo 1.5 (a Tencent licence that excludes the EU, UK and South Korea).

**Quality of human motion, measured independently**
- [EXTRACT-S] [INDEPENDENT] HumanScore (Stanford, April 2026, NeurIPS 2026 poster) scored 13 video models on 51 motion types with a biomechanics hierarchy (anatomical, kinematic, kinetic correctness). Per a summary of the paper, Seedance 1.0 Pro Fast and HunyuanVideo 1.5 co-lead at 91.1, Kling 2.5 Turbo Pro 90.8; real videos score 94.3. ### Inferences
- For a character shoot, the decisive features are (a) multi-image identity references, (b) a seed, and (c) clip length long enough for one action with rest poses at both ends. On those, Kling 4.0 (elements with frontal plus side views, 30 s, keyframes) and Seedance 2.5 (30 references, 30 s, seed, cheap 480p drafts) are the best fits visible in the schemas; Luma Ray 3.2's `loop` flag is the only explicit walk-cycle aid I found; MiniMax H3 Max `camera-controls` is the only explicit orbit control.
- The benchmarks tested model versions one or two generations behind today's (Seedance 1.0, Kling 2.5). No independent human-motion score exists yet for Kling 4.0, Seedance 2.5, Wan 3.0 or Hailuo H3.
- Because fal's default resolution for Kling 4.0 is 1080p and motion extraction does not benefit from pixels beyond 720p to 1080p, setting `resolution` explicitly is a direct cost lever (a third cheaper at 720p).
- Open-weight choices are narrow for an EU-based project: Wan 2.2 is Apache-2.0; the Tencent licences exclude the EU, UK and South Korea by their own text.

### Gaps
- No vendor terms of service were readable. Commercial-use rights for outputs of Kling, Seedance, Veo, Runway, Luma, Hailuo and for fal as intermediary are unverified.

## 2. Is generated video good enough as a source for motion capture?

### Takeaway
Yes for body motion on a narrow kind of shot, and only with a correction layer: the one documented practitioner pipeline (September 2026) turns Seedance clips into clean animation on Mixamo characters, but it demands a locked camera, full body in frame, a T-pose at start and end, no occlusion, and it rebuilds foot contact, drift, fingers and extremes itself because the estimator gets them wrong. Generated video also does not follow its prompt reliably, so the agent must retarget what was generated, not what was asked for.

### Cited Findings
- [READ] [INDEPENDENT practitioner] `mixamo-llm-mocap` (MIT, about 450 stars, updated September) "turns any locked-camera video, filmed or AI-generated, into a clean FK animation on any Mixamo character", with every stage scriptable "enough that an AI agent can run the whole loop". Its showcase is a 10 s AI-generated fight clip (nine punches, a slip, a high side kick) and a two-fighter clip. — [mixamo-llm-mocap README](https://github.com/squall01337/mixamo-llm-mocap)
- [READ] The author's rules for a generated "plate": camera locked, full body in frame every frame, even light, 1080p (720p minimum), 24 to 30 fps, about 10 s; T-pose held about 1 s at start and end, square to camera; chest and hips facing camera unless a turn is the motion ("turns are retargetable; occlusion is not"); every moving limb visible; no props, tight clothing, bare feet; one continuous take on the spot; reuse the same first-frame still (image-to-video) across clips. The shipped plates "came from Seedance and an earlier image-to-video tool". — [docs/PROMPTING.md](https://github.com/squall01337/mixamo-llm-mocap/blob/main/docs/PROMPTING.md)
- [READ] "Every plate so far deviated from its prompt somewhere: a left-knee jump delivered as right-knee, an extended kick delivered as a knee snap, an opening T-pose delivered as an A-pose." The rule: "Prompt for what you want; retarget what you got", and regenerate if a must-see beat is hidden, because "a hidden foot cannot be retargeted, and regeneration is cheaper than a wasted pass". — [docs/PROMPTING.md](https://github.com/squall01337/mixamo-llm-mocap/blob/main/docs/PROMPTING.md)
- [READ] Measured failures of the estimator (GVHMR) on these plates: world trajectory drifts 0.3 m in depth, 0.4 m sideways and 5 to 15 cm vertically over a take that ends where it began; the support foot was moved at up to 2 m/s and sunk 4 to 10 cm into the floor while the video shows it fixed; the network's foot-contact confidence "can be confidently wrong" (a foot sliding 0.2 m with contact confidence near 1); a kick apex was placed 0.10 to 0.18 m below where 2D keypoints saw the ankle ("a regressor flattens extremes"); an arm behind the torso "came back as garbage"; feet carry a steady 0 to 12 degree toes-up tilt. — [docs/PITFALLS.md](https://github.com/squall01337/mixamo-llm-mocap/blob/main/docs/PITFALLS.md)
- [READ] Hands and face from a full-body shot: at 1280x720 a hand is 30 to 50 px and a hand detector run on the whole frame finds nothing usable; cropped around the wrist and upscaled to 256 px, the hand is found on 83 to 100 % of frames. The face spans about 25 px at 4.7 m; a 3 px detection error is a 10 degree head turn. — [docs/PITFALLS.md](https://github.com/squall01337/mixamo-llm-mocap/blob/main/docs/PITFALLS.md)
- [READ] The pipeline's corrections: refine the estimate on the clip's own 2D keypoints, add heel and toe keypoints, plant feet from four signals (estimator contact, floor, image stillness, physics), remove height drift continuously and horizontal drift per footfall, then a numeric QA gate (exploded bones, hip pops, foot skate, root drift) and a frame-by-frame comparison against the source video. "QA passing is not done": a rendered side-by-side review remains part of the loop. — [mixamo-llm-mocap README](https://github.com/squall01337/mixamo-llm-mocap); [docs/PITFALLS.md](https://github.com/squall01337/mixamo-llm-mocap/blob/main/docs/PITFALLS.md)
- [READ] Two characters in one generated clip work only if they never cross or swap sides of frame (tracker ids swap on contact), silhouettes do not overlap, and clothing colours differ. — [docs/PROMPTING.md](https://github.com/squall01337/mixamo-llm-mocap/blob/main/docs/PROMPTING.md)
- [EXTRACT-P] [INDEPENDENT] HumanScore finds a "significant gap between generated and real motions" with temporal jitter, anatomically implausible poses and motion drift as recurrent failures (best models 91.1 against 94.3 for real video). — [HumanScore](https://cs.stanford.edu/~xtiange/projects/humanscore/)
- [EXTRACT-P] [INDEPENDENT] Even on real video, state-of-the-art recovery "like GVHMR fail[s] to capture complex 3D foot movement" (FootMR, March 2026), and video mocap "lack[s] dynamics modeling, leading to floating and foot sliding" (ICCV 2025 physical restoration paper, which cut a foot-sliding metric from 12.26 to 0.78). — [FootMR, arXiv 2603.09681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09681); [Physical motion restoration, arXiv 2412.17377](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17377v1)
- [EXTRACT-S] [VENDOR] Vendor guidance for any video mocap matches the practitioner's rules: single performer, full body in frame, stable camera, one continuous shot, limited occlusion (Uthana); phone on a tripod, full body framed (Cascadeur). — [Uthana guide](https://uthana.com/resources/best-ai-motion-capture-tools); [Cascadeur video mocap notes](https://lecture2-3d3.pages.dev/en/docs/cascadeur/cascadeur-video-mocap/)
- [EXTRACT-S] A reviewer's test clip design for judging a mocap tool: three steps, a turn, a planted foot, a reach across the body, and return; the walk shows sliding and the reach tests occlusion. — [WaveSpeed DeepMotion review](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/3d-model-access/deepmotion-review/)

### Inferences
- The failure list has two layers that stack: the video model's own errors (wrong limb, wrong move, pose drift, jitter) and the estimator's errors on any monocular video (depth drift, sliding support foot, flattened extremes, lost occluded limbs). Limb swaps in particular are avoided by never trusting the prompt or a still frame for which limb moved.
- Shot types that work: locked camera, frontal or perpendicular-to-action view, plain studio with a floor grid, whole body with headroom for the tallest moment, action on the spot, about 10 s, one action per clip, T-pose bookends. Shot types that do not: walking through the frame with a moving camera, spins, clinches, anything where a limb passes behind the torso.
- Walk cycles are the weak case: the practitioner's own contract says "no walking" for plates. Locomotion is better taken from text-to-motion or a motion library (section 5) than from generated video.
- Hands and face need their own close shots (or zoomed crops); a full-body plate at 720p does not carry them.

### Gaps
- No source measured motion extracted from generated video against ground truth; the evidence is one practitioner's documented pipeline plus general benchmarks. No test of Move.ai, DeepMotion or Flow Studio on generated video was found, nor any vendor statement on accepting AI-generated input.
- No evidence for clips from Kling 4.0, Wan 3.0 or Hailuo H3 specifically.
- I recall a 2025 paper, "Animating the Uncaptured: Humanoid Mesh Animation with Video Diffusion Models", that uses generated video as a motion source; the search did not find it and I could not verify it this session.

## 4. Facial animation from video or audio

### Takeaway
Two free, permissively licensed routes exist: MediaPipe Face Landmarker (Apache-2.0, runs in the browser) gives 51 ARKit-named blendshape scores plus a neutral channel and a head pose matrix from any video, with no tongue channel and reduced accuracy on small or poorly lit faces; NVIDIA Audio2Face-3D (open-sourced, MIT SDK, open-model weights) gives ARKit blendshapes with jaw, tongue and eye motion from audio but needs an NVIDIA GPU. The character needs ARKit-named morph targets, eyes that can rotate, and a jaw; neither route is available on fal.

### Cited Findings
- [READ] MediaPipe's blendshape list has 52 entries: index 0 is `_neutral`, followed by 51 ARKit-style names from `browDownLeft` to `noseSneerRight`, including `cheekPuff`, `jawOpen`, `jawForward`, `jawLeft/Right`, the eight `eyeLook*` and the `mouth*` set. There is no `tongueOut`. — [mediapipe face_landmarker.py](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/mediapipe/tasks/python/vision/face_landmarker.py)
- [READ] Versions and licence: `mediapipe` 1.2.3 on PyPI and `@mediapipe/tasks-vision` 1.2.3 on npm, both published 11 November 2026, Apache-2.0. — [mediapipe on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/mediapipe/); [@mediapipe/tasks-vision on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@mediapipe/tasks-vision)
- [READ] three.js ships a working reference: `webgl_morphtargets_webcam` loads Face Landmarker with `outputFaceBlendshapes: true`, maps each category name through a table onto `morphTargetDictionary` of the Face Cap model (`facecap.glb`) and writes scores into `morphTargetInfluences`; because the eye meshes have no morphs it converts the `eyeLook*` scores into eye rotations; it applies MediaPipe's facial transformation matrix as the head pose; the `tongueOut` mapping is commented out as having no source. The example still pins tasks-vision 0.10.35. — [three.js example source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/webgl_morphtargets_webcam.html)
- [EXTRACT-P] [VENDOR] Google's Blendshape V2 model card: blendshapes are predicted from FaceMesh landmarks, so limits of the landmark model carry over, and accuracy drops with poor light, noise, motion or face overlap. Google publishes no accuracy benchmark for the task. — [Model Card Blendshape V2 (mirror)](https://dcmpx.remotevs.com/com/googleapis/storage/SL/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20Blendshape%20V2.pdf); [Banuba comparison](https://www.banuba.com/blog/banuba-face-ar-sdk-vs-mediapipe)
- [EXTRACT-P] [INDEPENDENT] A 2026 paper notes ARKit blendshapes rely on the iPhone's depth/IR sensor system, which "does not match less powerful systems like standard webcams". The underlying Google method ("Blendshapes GHUM") predicts the same 52-name set from a single RGB image in real time. — [arXiv 2601.08234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08234); [arXiv 2309.05782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05782)
- [EXTRACT-S] Tools that turn a video file into ARKit curves with MediaPipe: "Face Landmark Link" records ARKit data from recorded or live 2D video and writes CSV compatible with the Live Link Face importer of the Faceit Blender add-on. — [Faceit documentation](https://faceit-doc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/mocap_general/)
- [READ] NVIDIA Audio2Face-3D is open: SDK in C++ and Python under MIT, training framework v1.0 under Apache, Maya plugin v2.0 and Unreal Engine 5.5/5.6 plugin v2.5 under MIT; pretrained models (regression v2.3 "Mark", v2.3.1 "Claire" and "James"; diffusion v3.0) under the NVIDIA Open Model licence; Audio2Emotion models under a custom licence (use with Audio2Face only); the sample training data is evaluation-only. — [Audio2Face-3D](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Audio2Face-3D)
- [READ] [VENDOR] Audio2Face-3D generates "synchronized motion of the jaw, tongue, and eyes" and skin deformation from pre-recorded or streamed audio, and drives a character "through direct mesh deformations, joint transformations, or blend shape weights"; the microservice outputs ARKit blendshapes and can infer or accept emotion. — [Audio2Face-3D](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Audio2Face-3D); [Audio2Face-3D-Samples](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Audio2Face-3D-Samples)
- [READ] The Audio2Face-3D SDK needs Windows 10/11 or Ubuntu 20.04+, an NVIDIA GPU with CUDA 12.8+, TensorRT 10.13+, about 4 GB of GPU memory. — [Audio2Face-3D-SDK](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Audio2Face-3D-SDK)
- [EXTRACT-S] Wav2ARKit: an ONNX model that takes 16 kHz audio and outputs 52 ARKit blendshapes at 30 fps on CPU. Seen only through a mirror extract; licence unknown. — [wav2arkit_cpu (mirror)](https://hug-proxy.pages.dev/myned-ai/wav2arkit_cpu)
- [READ] MHR, the rig under SAM 3D Body, carries 72 facial expression parameters, and fal's SAM 3D Body endpoint can return "shape, pose, expression" per person. These are MHR expression coefficients, not ARKit names. — [MHR](https://github.com/facebookresearch/MHR); [fal-js endpoints.ts](https://github.com/fal-ai/fal-js/blob/main/libs/client/src/types/endpoints.ts)
- [READ] On fal, the text "blendshape", "arkit" and "audio2face" does not occur anywhere in the endpoint type map; the lip-sync endpoints there (`fal-ai/sync-lipsync/v3`, `veed/lipsync/v2`, `fal-ai/kling-video/lipsync/...`, `fal-ai/pixverse/lipsync`, `fal-ai/heygen/v3/lipsync/...`) output video. — [fal-js endpoints.ts](https://github.com/fal-ai/fal-js/blob/main/libs/client/src/types/endpoints.ts)

### Inferences
- What the character must have to receive either route: morph targets named after the ARKit set (51 are driven by MediaPipe; `tongueOut` only by audio-driven or iPhone sources); two eye bones or eye meshes that can be rotated from the eight `eyeLook*` values (or eye-look morphs); a jaw that opens through the `jawOpen` morph or a jaw joint, with lower teeth and tongue following it; a head/neck joint to take the head pose matrix. This is exactly the structure of the three.js Face Cap example.
- A generated full-body clip cannot carry the face (about 25 px in section 2). The face needs its own generated close-up (speaking, smiling) processed by MediaPipe, or it should be driven from audio.
- For speech, audio-to-face is the more robust route for an invented character: text-to-speech audio is clean, while lip motion in generated video is itself synthetic and may not be phonetically right.
- Running Audio2Face-3D needs an NVIDIA GPU machine or a custom deployment; MediaPipe runs on CPU, in Node or in the browser, so an agent can run it anywhere.

### Gaps
- No independent accuracy comparison of MediaPipe blendshapes against Apple's Live Link Face / ARKit capture was found.
- Apple Live Link Face and Epic's MetaHuman Animator (which I recall accepting ordinary video since 2025) were not covered by any source in this session; whether they can process a generated video file is unverified.
- The licence text of the NVIDIA Open Model licence, and whether Audio2Face-3D v3.0 output can be redistributed as baked animation in a web game, were not read.
- Quality of Audio2Face-3D or Wav2ARKit on non-English speech: not found.

## 5. Text-to-motion and motion libraries (the routes that skip video)

### Takeaway
Text-to-motion became a practical alternative in 2026: Tencent HY-Motion 1.0 is on fal for about $0.08 per clip of up to 12 s as FBX, NVIDIA's Kimodo (Apache-2.0 code, open-model weights, trained on 700 hours of licensed studio mocap) adds keyframe, path and end-effector constraints with BVH export, and Uthana sells text-to-motion, looping locomotion, stitching and download onto your own auto-rigged character through a documented API. For standard locomotion these are cleaner than motion pulled from generated video; Mixamo remains usable in games but its files may not be redistributed as assets.

- Quality against mocap: Kimodo and Uthana are trained on studio mocap and output contact-aware motion, so for walking, turning, idling and gesturing they start from physically plausible data, which generated-video extraction does not. ## 6. Retargeting onto a custom skeleton

### Takeaway
Retargeting is the step with no hosted API on fal and the one where most visible errors arise. three.js's own `SkeletonUtils.retargetClip` copies world rotations bone by bone and moves only the hip; it has no rest-pose solver, no IK and no foot contact, so it works between rigs that share a rest pose and breaks on different proportions unless per-bone offsets are hand-authored. The robust route is to retarget offline (Blender with Auto-Rig Pro, the Rokoko plugin or a scripted rotation transfer) and ship the result as glTF animation on the game's own skeleton.

### Cited Findings
- [READ] three.js is at 0.187.0 on npm. `SkeletonUtils.retarget(target, source, options)` resets the target to its bind pose, then for every target bone finds the mapped source bone (`names` map or `getBoneName` callback), takes the source bone's world matrix, strips scale, and writes that rotation to the target bone; only the bone named in `hip` receives position, multiplied by a scalar `scale` and `hipInfluence`; with `preserveBonePositions` (default true) all other bones keep their own positions. `localOffsets` lets the caller multiply a fixed matrix per bone. `retargetClip` samples this at the clip's fps and writes quaternion tracks plus one hip position track; `useFirstFramePosition` and `trim` are the only other controls. — [SkeletonUtils.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/jsm/utils/SkeletonUtils.js); [three on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/three)
- [READ] The official example that retargets between two Mixamo-named rigs (`webgpu_animation_retargeting`, Soldier to Michelle) needs a hand-written table of per-bone offset matrices (45 degree and 180 degree rotations for shoulders, arms, legs, feet, toes) and a full bone-name map; the second example retargets a Mixamo FBX onto a Ready Player Me GLB with only a name function, a hip name and a 0.01 scale. — [webgpu_animation_retargeting.html](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/webgpu_animation_retargeting.html); [webgpu_animation_retargeting_readyplayer.html](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/webgpu_animation_retargeting_readyplayer.html)
- [EXTRACT-S] three.js forum threads report inverted feet and backward hands with Mixamo skeletons, poor results on a model "with significantly different body proportion", and a 2024 thread titled "Fixing SkeletonUtils retarget() and retargetClip() functions" in which a user "was not able to get good results retargeting between two different models"; a forum reply notes retargeting is needed only when bones differ. — [inverted feet thread](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/skeletonutils-retarget-doesnt-work-with-mixamorig-skeleton-inverted-feet-backward-hands/54892); [fixing thread](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/fixing-skeletonutils-retarget-and-retargetclip-functions/65149); [differing height thread](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/applying-animations-to-character-models-of-differing-height/39801)
- [EXTRACT-S] A May 2026 browser-game devlog needed a bone-name map plus a bind-pose alignment so both rigs share a reference orientation before retargeting worked across proportions. — [Cinevva devlog](https://app.cinevva.com/blog/2026-05-01-open-world-browser-part-17-animations-and-search)
- [EXTRACT-S] Blender tools: Auto-Rig Pro's Remap retargets BVH or FBX onto "any type of armature" with presets for Mixamo, Rokoko and Xsens and root/IK bone marking (price reported as $40 or $50); the Blender Extensions add-on "Retarget" 5.0.1 (22 May 2026) calls itself a complete solution with custom-rig support; one blog claims Rokoko's developers recommend Auto-Rig Pro over their own retargeter. — [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/auto-rig-pro-alternatives); [MoCap Online guide](https://mocaponline.com/blogs/mocap-news/blender-animation-addons-guide); [Blender Extensions: Retarget](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/retarget/versions/)
- [READ] The Rokoko Blender plugin is MIT and includes retargeting between rigs; it requires the character in T-pose and a matching hand pose for finger retargeting. The Mwni add-on maps bones manually, needs a rest-pose alignment step, and offers a foot/hand position correction for sliding caused by anatomical differences. — [rokoko-studio-live-blender](https://github.com/Rokoko/rokoko-studio-live-blender); [blender-animation-retargeting](https://github.com/Mwni/blender-animation-retargeting)
- [READ] What the practitioner pipeline found breaks, and its fixes: positions cannot carry twist, so transfer joint rotations and align rest poses "only where they differ in pose (limbs), never where the skeletons differ in proportion (spine, neck, head, collars)"; matching the performer's stance width breaks the legs because the rig's hip joints are 18 cm apart against the performer's 11 to 13 cm ("the width is the rig's, keep it"); a "Hand" bone's position is the wrist; linear resampling from 24 to 30 fps puts a velocity kink at every source frame; two retargeted characters collide where the performers did not, because game characters are thicker than humans. — [docs/PITFALLS.md](https://github.com/squall01337/mixamo-llm-mocap/blob/main/docs/PITFALLS.md)
- [READ] NVIDIA's SOMA Retargeter (Apache-2.0, beta) retargets SOMA-skeleton BVH to humanoid robots with scaling, per-frame IK, contact stabilisation and foot-plant correction; it expects the SOMA hierarchy and outputs robot CSV, so it is not a character retargeter. — [soma-retargeter](https://github.com/NVIDIA/soma-retargeter)
- [READ] Services that retarget for you: Uthana returns any motion on an uploaded, auto-rigged character as GLB / FBX / BVH; Meshy rigging on fal returns preset animations already on the character it rigged. — [uthana on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/uthana/); [fal-js endpoints.ts](https://github.com/fal-ai/fal-js/blob/main/libs/client/src/types/endpoints.ts)

### Inferences
- Three things break, in this order of visibility: foot contact (leg-length and hip-width differences turn planted feet into sliding feet unless an IK or hip-height pass re-plants them), proportions (hands miss the face or body, arms pass through a thicker torso), and fingers (they need matching rest hand poses and are often absent in the source).
- For a three.js game the cheap, reliable design is to do no retargeting in the browser: retarget once offline onto the game skeleton, bake to glTF animation clips with identical bone names, and play them with `AnimationMixer`. Runtime `retargetClip` is only safe when source and target share rest pose and bone orientations.
- If the custom skeleton is designed to be a superset of a common convention (Mixamo-style names and T-pose, or the SOMA 77-joint layout), most sources map by name and the offset tables disappear.

### Gaps
- No source compared Blender retargeters on SMPL / SOMA / MHR sources, on foot contact or on fingers.
- Whether any current tool retargets MHR or SOMA motion directly onto arbitrary game rigs (rather than robots) was not found; SOMA-X mentions pose inversion and conversion utilities whose scope I did not read.
- glTF-specific pitfalls (animation sampling, morph-target animation alongside skeletal tracks) were not researched within the budget.

## 7. Using video to help rigging itself

### Takeaway
No production tool infers a skeleton or skin weights from a video of a character. The research that exists works the other way round (rig a static mesh, then use generated video to drive it) or learns rigs from 3D deformation sequences; "animate anyone"-style models such as Wan-Animate, Kling motion control and Runway Act-Two produce only pixels. In production, rigging still comes from static-mesh auto-riggers.

### Cited Findings
- [READ] Puppeteer (NeurIPS 2025 spotlight, ByteDance Seed and NTU): given a 3D object it generates a skeleton and skinning weights, "then animates the rigged model with video guidance through a differentiable optimization pipeline"; rigged meshes export to FBX since September 2025; code and checkpoints released with an Apache-2.0 licence file. — [Puppeteer](https://github.com/Seed3D/Puppeteer)
- [EXTRACT-P] Puppeteer's animation stage renders the mesh, feeds a frame and a text prompt to a text-to-video model and transfers the resulting 2D motion to the rig by optimisation: the video comes after rigging. — [arXiv 2508.10898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10898)
- [EXTRACT-P] AnimaX (June 2025): jointly generates multi-view video and 2D pose sequences, recovers 3D joint positions and joint angles by inverse kinematics, and drives an already rigged mesh of arbitrary skeleton; trained on about 160,000 rigged sequences; about 6 minutes per run. — [arXiv 2506.19851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19851)
- [READ] AnimaX's repository still lists "release code and model" as not done. — [anima-x](https://github.com/anima-x/anima-x)
- [EXTRACT-P] RigMo (January 2026) learns rig and motion together from per-vertex deformation sequences, decoding explicit bones and skinning weights; SkinTokens / TokenRig (February 2026) generates skeleton and skinning jointly from a static model; G-Skin (August 2026) optimises skinning weights for a given skeleton with a generative visual prior; a May 2026 paper generates meshes that are rigged from the start. None starts from a video of the character. — [RigMo, arXiv 2601.06378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06378); [SkinTokens, arXiv 2602.04805](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04805); [G-Skin, arXiv 2608.01726](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.01726); [arXiv 2605.13129](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.13129)
- [READ] UniRig (VAST, MIT) rigs static meshes. — [UniRig](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/UniRig)
- [READ] 2D performance transfer, available as APIs: Wan2.2-Animate (open, Apache-2.0) and, on fal, `fal-ai/wan/v2.2-14b/animate/move`, `fal-ai/kling-video/v3/pro/motion-control`, `moonvalley/marey/motion-transfer` and `pose-transfer`. All take a character image plus a driving video and return video. — [Wan2.2](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2); [fal-js endpoints.ts](https://github.com/fal-ai/fal-js/blob/main/libs/client/src/types/endpoints.ts)
- [READ] The nearest production building block on fal is static-mesh rigging: `fal-ai/meshy/rigging` requires "a humanoid character with clearly defined limbs" and a height in metres. — [fal-js endpoints.ts](https://github.com/fal-ai/fal-js/blob/main/libs/client/src/types/endpoints.ts)

### Inferences
- Video can still help rigging indirectly, as a test rather than a source: a generated clip of the character bending elbows, knees and shoulders shows how the surface should deform, and a vision-language agent can compare renders of the rigged mesh in the same poses against those frames to judge skin weights. I found no tool that does this; it is a workflow idea, not a product.
- 2D "animate anyone" models are useful one step earlier: they can turn a library or text-generated motion (rendered on a proxy) into a video of the invented character performing it, which gives appearance reference for deformation and clothing, but they never produce joint data.

### Gaps
- I found no paper or tool that takes monocular or multi-view video of a generated character and outputs a skeleton plus skin weights. Searches for "4D reconstruction of generated characters" returned only the works above; the budget did not allow a deeper pass on 4D Gaussian methods.
- Production readiness of Puppeteer on human characters with clothing: no independent test found.

## 8. End-to-end cost and time per minute of animation; what a vision-language agent can automate

### Takeaway
On published rates, one minute of generated source video costs roughly $6 to $14 before re-rolls, extraction costs from a few cents (local open model) to about $4.50 (Flow Studio) per minute, and text-to-motion costs about $0.40 to $6 per minute; the dominant real cost is re-rolling and review. The whole loop is automatable by an agent: every step has an API or CLI, and the documented practitioner pipeline already runs with numeric QA gates and rendered side-by-side reviews.

### Cited Findings
- [EXTRACT-S] Video generation per second: Kling 4.0 on fal $0.157 (720p) / $0.235 (1080p); Veo 3.1 on fal $0.20 without audio, Fast $0.10; Gemini Omni Flash about $0.10 to $0.125; Runway Gen-4.5 $0.12; Runway Act-Two $0.05. — [aireiter on fal Kling prices](https://aireiter.com/blog/kling-4-0-api-pricing-fal-cost-vs-seedance-vidu); [fal veo3.1](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/veo3.1/llms.txt); [fal gemini-omni-flash](https://fal.ai/models/google/gemini-omni-flash/llms.txt); [Activepieces on Runway](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/runway-api-pricing-per-second-for-every-video-model)
- [EXTRACT-P] Flow Studio AI mocap: 10 credits per second; Standard plan $45 for 6,000 credits. — [Autodesk compare page](https://www.autodesk.com/eu/products/flow-studio/compare); [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/08/autodesk-cuts-prices-of-flow-studio-subscriptions/)
- [EXTRACT-P] fal: Hunyuan Motion $0.08 per generation (up to 12 s); SAM 3D Body $0.02 "per unit". Uthana text-to-motion 3.0 $0.10 per second. — [fal hunyuan-motion](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/hunyuan-motion/llms.txt); [fal sam-3 3d-body](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/sam-3/3d-body); [Uthana pricing](https://uthana.com/pricing)

- [READ] FlowHMR's README gives each step as a prompt to "paste into your coding agent"; Uthana's client is written to be shared by "applications, scripts, and MCP servers" and warns agents not to auto-resubmit uncertain mutations; HY-Motion ships SSAE, a vision-language-model-based metric for whether a generated motion matches its text. — [FlowHMR](https://github.com/flowhmr/flowhmr); [uthana on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/uthana/); [HY-Motion-1.0](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HY-Motion-1.0)
- [EXTRACT-S] Kling's release notes say its MCP server and CLI support Kling 4.0. — [Creaa tracker](https://creaa.ai/blog/kling-4)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic from the rates above, per minute of footage or motion (single take, no re-rolls): Kling 4.0 via fal $9.42 (720p) or $14.10 (1080p); Veo 3.1 Fast via fal $6, standard without audio $12; Runway Gen-4.5 $7.20; Flow Studio mocap $4.50 (60 s x 10 credits x $45/6,000); Uthana text-to-motion $6; fal Hunyuan Motion about $0.40 (five 12 s clips); fal SAM 3D Body run on every frame about $36 at 30 fps or $14.40 at 12 fps, if one "unit" is one image (unverified).
- Route totals for one finished minute, assuming every clip is generated three times before one is accepted (the practitioner reports that every plate deviated from its prompt): generated video plus Flow Studio about $33 to $47; generated video plus a local open extractor about $28 to $42 plus GPU time of roughly 3 minutes per minute of video; text-to-motion about $1 to $18. The multiplier of three is my assumption, not a measured rate.
- A minute of game animation is a lot: a playable character typically needs a set of short clips (idle, walk, run, turn, a few gestures) of 2 to 10 s each, so a full starter set is on the order of one minute and tens of dollars by any route.
- What an agent can do unattended: write prompts from a shot list; call generation APIs with fixed seeds and reference images; reject clips by rule (camera moved, body cropped, missing T-pose bookends) from 2D keypoints; run extraction; run numeric checks (foot skate, root drift, joint limits, jitter, loop closure); render the retargeted character beside the source and judge the side-by-side with vision; re-roll on failure. What still benefits from a human: taste (does the walk suit the character), and the cases where eye and numbers disagree.
- Time per minute of animation is dominated by generation queues and review loops, not by extraction. No source gave wall-clock generation times for the current video models.

### Gaps
- No practitioner published an end-to-end cost or time per finished minute. The totals above are my arithmetic on list prices of mixed reliability.
- Move.ai's per-second price in dollars could not be established (credit rate known, credit price not).
- GPU rental prices were not researched; local-inference cost is therefore stated only as time.

### Inferences

**A fal-only motion recipe**
1. *Locomotion and stock actions, no video.* Rig the generated character with `fal-ai/meshy/rigging` and request walk, run, idle, turn and gesture presets with `/multi-animation` (10 per call). They come back already on the character as GLB. For anything the 697 presets lack, call `fal-ai/hunyuan-motion` with a text prompt and get an FBX of up to 12 s for about $0.08. This is the only route that is fal from end to end.
2. *Performance-specific motion, with video.* Generate the clip on fal with `kling/v4/reference-to-video` (character as an element with front and side views) or `bytedance/seedance-2.5/us/reference-to-video`, set to 720p, about 10 s, following the plate rules in section 2. Then fal stops: the only 3D pose endpoint is per-image. Calling `fal-ai/sam-3/3d-body` on every frame yields MHR pose parameters without temporal smoothing, world trajectory or foot contact, at a likely $0.02 per frame; turning that into usable animation needs your own smoothing, floor and foot-lock code, which is the hard part the open video models already solve.
3. *Face.* fal can make the speech audio (several text-to-speech endpoints are in the type map) and a talking close-up video, but it returns no blendshape curves. Run MediaPipe Face Landmarker on the close-up locally or in the browser, or run Audio2Face-3D on the audio on an NVIDIA GPU.
4. *Retarget and export.* Not on fal. Meshy's presets need none; Hunyuan Motion's FBX and any extracted motion must be retargeted to the game skeleton in headless Blender or accepted only if the game skeleton matches the source rig.

**Where fal stops, honestly**
- Temporal video-to-motion: needs Uthana's API (video-to-motion 3.0, download on your own auto-rigged character), a commercial web service (Flow Studio, Move.ai, without a confirmed self-serve API), or a local run of GEM-X / FlowHMR.
- Facial animation data: needs MediaPipe (free, anywhere) or Audio2Face-3D (GPU).
- Retargeting, foot-contact repair, loop closing, glTF baking: need Blender or own code.
- Keeping "fal as the one API" is still possible by deploying those pieces as a private fal app (for example GEM-X plus a headless Blender retarget behind one endpoint). That moves the work from integration to maintenance and is subject to each model's licence; I did not verify GPU types, pricing or access conditions for fal's serverless product.
- Licence caution: `fal-ai/hunyuan-motion` runs a model whose licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea, including for outputs (section 5).

### Gaps
- fal's model catalogue, pricing page and terms could not be opened. Prices for most rows of the table are missing, and "per unit" for SAM 3D Body is not defined in what I saw.
- I could not confirm whether fal hosts further motion endpoints that are absent from the type map (the Veo case shows this can happen), nor what fal's serverless deployment costs or whether it is open to all accounts.
- The rig and skeleton delivered by Meshy rigging and by Hunyuan Motion's FBX on fal (joint names, fingers, face bones) were not documented in the sources read, so how well they map to a custom game skeleton is untested.
