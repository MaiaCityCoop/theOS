# The Tribe

The best principles for growing a tribe: how a community finds its people, brings them together and keeps them. They are kept abstract, as a reference, to be mapped onto the game and the villages later. Written with Samuel on 18 November 2026 (intent 0040), in our own words. Source in the artifact library: `the-worst-way-to-build-a-tribe`.

## Overview

**A tribe grows from people, purpose and an engine, never from land first.** Every community that lasts runs three things from its first day:

- **Culture:** a clear way of life that people choose.
- **Income:** it pays its own way.
- **A way in:** new people keep finding it, testing it and staying.

Land, buildings and infrastructure come after these three, and they come faster because of them.

**The engine is signal, filter, gather:** a vision said out loud draws people; a commitment shows who is serious; living and working side by side shows who stays.

## Rules & Limitations

### The principles

1. **Signal before soil.** Say the big vision out loud, every day, in public, before there is anything to stand on. People come for the vision, then build the place.
2. **Filter by commitment.** The right people show themselves by what they put in: time, money, work. A small real cost filters better than any form.
3. **Gather in proximity, with a purpose.** Relationships form when people live and work side by side, and show who will go the distance. Living together without a shared project is only sharing a flat.
4. **People and income first, land later.** The order decides everything. A place without income and without new people stays half-built, however beautiful the land.
5. **Build shoulder to shoulder.** The deepest bonds come from making something together. A gathering is good; a build day is better.
6. **A clear way of life.** Name what is sacred, how people treat each other, what a day looks like. Without an ethos, a new neighbourhood is just housing.
7. **Clear structures over endless consensus.** Clear roles, clear rights and decisions that end free people to build. Flat consensus without structure breeds drama and stalls.
8. **Culture before buildings.** Gatherings, events and stays on a place find the people and the money before the first building does.
9. **Low-risk ways in.** People, families above all, join when they can keep their job and school, try a season, then stay.
10. **Proven tribes attract land.** Owners of empty land look for living communities. A tribe whose engine runs is offered places to grow.

### The ways, ranked

The known ways of building a community, from strongest to weakest. Every way rises a rank when it is fed by a strong public signal and a filter.

| Way | Rank | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The residency funnel**: signal, filter, gather | **S** | Culture, income and recruiting in one engine; the best guests are invited to stay |
| **Build in public around a big vision** | **S** | The vision itself draws the people and the money |
| **Culture first, then land** | **A** (S with a strong signal) | People, partners and investors gather around events before the buildings |
| **A shared mission**: a coop, a guild, a business | **A** (S with a strong signal) | Bonds made by working together; deep buy-in |
| **Shared housing with a purpose** | **A** | People show who they are by living together |
| **Friends moving into one neighbourhood** | **A** | Uses the roads, schools and jobs that already exist; low risk for families |
| **A new neighbourhood from scratch** | **A−** with a strong team, else **B** | Lasting, but slow; needs a way of life |
| **Local gatherings alone** | **B** | Friends, but no shared life afterwards |
| **An online community alone** | **D** | Contact without proximity |
| **The old eco-village model**: sacrifice, patronage, flat consensus | **D** | Endures at best, rarely grows |
| **Buying raw land first** | **F** | Capital sunk, no income, no people |

## History

Ten years of shared houses, eco-villages, raw-land projects and the first startup cities show the same lesson: the communities that grew solved money and people first. The ones that started with land, sacrifice or consensus alone rarely grew past their founders.

## Practitioners

Anyone who grows a community on purpose: whoever sends the signal, whoever sets the filter, and whoever hosts the gathering.

## Impact on Society

If a tribe can be grown on purpose, belonging stops being luck. Anyone with a vision can find their people, live beside them and build with them.
