# The Citizen

**The hero of this serial is the viewer.** Not Samuel, not Maia: the person who comes into the system. The reader of today's episode, the player who joined last night, the one who wins the first real dome, the thousand who leave their old lives for the first village. Everyone who builds the city, in the end, arriving one at a time.

This is why the serial is written in the second person. "You" is not a stylistic choice; it is the protagonist.

They are not one person, so this file is the role rather than a biography. Each dome's winner and each founding settler is a real, named human who fills it, and the episodes belong to them.

## Who they are when they arrive

Someone living a safe life and calling it responsible. Not unhappy enough to act and not satisfied enough to look away. They suspect they are capable of something considerable, and they know exactly what a small-minded room does to anyone who reaches, so they have made themselves a reasonable size. Every choice defensible on its own, and the sum of them a life they did not pick.

Nobody did this to them, which is what makes it land rather than offend.

## What they want, and what stands in the way

- **Want.** To build something larger than the life they have, with people who want the same, and to own a piece of it.
- **Obstacle.** The safe life they would have to leave, and a world that punishes whoever reaches. The fear is not of failing. It is of their own greatness, and of being judged for it.
- **Stake.** The city is built in public by the people who walk in; every day it stands a little more because they did.

## What changes for them

Not their courage. **The room.** Where they came from, reaching was punished and shrinking was sensible; here, stepping up to the thing you already know is right is the norm, and playing it safe is the exception — the odd choice you would have to explain. They never have to endure the judgment, because they have walked into somewhere that does not issue it.

So the feeling at the end is vindication, not conversion. They were not wrong to be afraid and they have not become someone else. **They knew they were right all along**: right about the size of the thing, right that the room would punish it. The room was the only part that was ever wrong.

## Their arc, which every one of them walks

Watcher → walking in → player in [the twin](../worldbuilding/locations/the-twin.md) → winner of something real → [founding settler](../glossary/terms/founding-settler.md) → owner of a city.

Each step is there so that nobody is ever more than one step from the next one, and the first is walking in. See [the jump](../glossary/terms/the-jump.md).

## What they do to Maia

They shape her. Every citizen who arrives shapes [Maia](maia.md) further, with what they intend, what they have taste for, and what they will not accept. A city ends with a mayor made out of everyone who built it. They are not users of a system; they are what the system is made of.

And she pushes every one of them, with no exceptions and no contempt, and never lets them take the easy route.

## Voice

Theirs, not written for them. When a citizen or a settler is on the page they are a real person quoted in their own words, the same rule that holds for Samuel. The second-person narration speaks **to** them; it never speaks **as** them about things they did not say.
