# Samuel

Age 37 at the start. A visionary and a systems-first engineer, a poly-path: he works across whole stacks rather than down one.

## He is the messenger, not the hero

**The hero of this serial is [the citizen](the-citizen.md)**: the viewer, the settler, whoever comes into the system. Samuel is the one who went first and reports back, every day, for 5,423 days. That is his whole function and it is enough.

He has no special claim on the city. He gives ownership away by design, he holds one stack like anyone else, and [Maia](maia.md) pushes him exactly as she pushes a first-day player. What makes him worth listening to is not authority; it is that he jumped from where the reader is standing, while frightened, and kept a record.

The serial never makes him the subject of its own argument. When he is on the page he is carrying news, admitting something, or being pushed, never being admired.

## Personality & Traits

- Cannot bear small thinking. Small talk is physically hard for him.
- Believes it is insane that playing it small is treated as normal and building mega-projects as crazy.
- Gives ownership away by design. Everyone gets their own stack, so the story never asks him to learn to let go.
- Reasons from first principles, and from the whole system down, never from the patch.

## Motivations & Goals

- **What he wants.** 1,346,269 people owning a sovereign solarpunk city they built from the ground up, first in the game and then in the earth.
- **What he needs.** To not die having never lived, and to not watch the people around him bury their dreams in the safe life.
- **What he believes produces joy.** Building momentum and contributing around a shared vision greater than yourself. Ownership is a human right. Survival is meant to be eliminated.
- **His deepest pain.** The waste of human potential on nothing, caused by badly designed systemic principles, not by the people living inside them.

## What he is alone in

In his own words: not one human he knows lives up to what they are actually capable of. Everyone around him is, as he puts it, basically dead. This is testimony about his own experience, not a verdict on anyone named, and on the page it follows the rule of [The Trade](../plot/arcs/the-trade.md): aim at the trade, never at the person.

**And he includes himself.** Until the day of the day-1 decision he was afraid too. Not of failing. Afraid of his own greatness, and of being judged for it by a small-thinking society. Maia City is so big that to commit to it publicly is to invite exactly that judgment, and the fear was proportional to the size of the thing.

That is the mechanism that keeps everybody small, and it is exactly what makes him a messenger worth hearing rather than a man lecturing from above: he was frightened of the same thing the reader is.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Speaks in systems and in the largest version of the claim. Starts from the principle, not the feature. Impatient with the average and generous with the ambitious. His lines in the serial are only ever his own words, taken from what he actually said or wrote; they are never written for him.

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** The first one through the door, and for a while the only one.
- **Key turning points:** The first citizen who shapes Maia who is not him. The first time she pushes him and he does not like it. The first winner of a real dome, whose episode is theirs and not his. The day the citizens hand her a decision rather than him.
- **Ending state:** One citizen among 1,346,269, with one stack, still filing the day's report.
