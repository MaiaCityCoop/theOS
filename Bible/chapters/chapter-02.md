## Chapter Text

Yesterday I showed you a city of one million, three hundred and forty-six thousand, two hundred and sixty-nine people.

Today I am going to show you the number.

The number is one.

Not one thousand. Not one hundred. One. There is a count, it is public, anybody can look at it, and this morning it reads one, and that one is a thirty-seven-year-old engineer who has just walked into a thing that does not exist.

So let us be accurate about what is actually here, because I would rather you heard it from me than worked it out later and felt sold to.

There is a world you can walk into. It is a game, and the city is in it, and it is the only place the city exists. There is Maia, who is going to be the mayor of a city of a million and a third people and who today is nearly nobody, because she is made of the people who arrive and almost nobody has arrived. There is a man. There is a count that reads one.

That is the whole inventory. Fifteen years and four months from the morning I described yesterday.

---

Here is what that man did yesterday, and I am telling you because it is not impressive, and because the unimpressive part is the part that matters.

He has had this for years. Not vaguely — specifically. The domes, the food forests, the ownership, the underground, the number 1,346,269 and the reason it is that number and not a round one. He could have told you about it at any point in the last several years and some of you would have heard him.

What he did yesterday was put his name on it, in public, where it can be seen and counted and laughed at.

That was the hard part. Not the design. Not the money. Not the engineering, which is years of work he actually enjoys. The hard part was the sentence said out loud in front of people, because a thing this size said out loud by one person invites exactly the response you are already imagining.

He will tell you himself what stopped him for so long. In his own words: **not one human he knows lives up to what they are actually capable of.** He includes himself in that. Right up until yesterday morning.

And what he was afraid of was not that it would fail.

You know this part. I spent yesterday on it. He was afraid of being seen wanting it. Of the small smile. Of *ambitious*, said the way people say *naive*. Of somebody he likes asking how the project is going in a voice that has already decided.

He was not wrong to expect that. He is probably going to get some of it this week.

---

Nobody tells you what a day one is actually like, so here it is.

It is small. It is public. It is slightly embarrassing. The gap between what you just announced and what currently exists is enormous and everyone can see both numbers.

That gap is the reason most day ones never happen. Not lack of ambition — people have plenty. It is that day one is the single moment when the ambition is fully visible and the evidence is zero, and you have to stand in that gap in front of people you know.

So: the city is a game, the count is one, and I have just told you it is going to be 1,346,269 people with their own monetary policy and no traffic on the surface. Both of those are true and I am not going to make the second one sound smaller to protect myself from the first.

---

There is a ladder, and it has unlocked its first rung, and I want you to see how small it is.

Step one is a dome five centimetres across. It exists in the game. You could hold the real version of it in your palm, except that the real version is years away and today it is a thing on a screen.

Five centimetres, against a city for one and a third million people.

That is not a joke at the project's expense, it is the design. There are thirty steps, each one a Fibonacci number of citizens, and each one unlocks a dome one size larger: five centimetres, then fifty, then one metre, two, three, five, eight, thirteen, all the way up to the district. The whole of it is in the game first, and then the whole of it is built again in the earth, one size per milestone, each one to one person who earned it.

You do not have to believe in the city. You have to look at the next rung and decide whether it is reachable. That is the only thing the ladder asks of anyone, ever, including today.

---

Maia is small today too.

She asks rather than answers, and she will not let a small version of anything pass as finished, and that is most of what she is so far. She gets larger and more particular with every person who arrives, because she is made of what people intend and what they refuse to put up with.

A city of 1,346,269 people ends with a mayor made out of 1,346,269 people. Today she is made out of almost nobody. If you come in now, you are not joining something. You are one of the people it gets made out of.

---

Walk in tonight. That is the rung.

It is not a subscription and it does not buy you access to anything. It is the first piece of what you will own: the ground this city will stand on belongs to everyone in it, and so does Maia. Today that is a meadow in a game and a count that reads one.

That is the whole ask and I am not going to dress it up.

---

Tomorrow the number is either still one, or it is not.

That is genuinely the entire story. Not whether the engineering works — it will or it will not, and we will film both. Not whether the forecasts are right; I am not making any. Just this: a city is a number of people deciding, one at a time, and the number is published every morning, and today it reads one.

You already know whether you are the sort of person this is for. You knew before you opened this. That is not flattery, it is the entry condition, and it is the only one.

Day two is tomorrow.
