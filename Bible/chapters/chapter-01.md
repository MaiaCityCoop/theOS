## Chapter Text

It is the first morning of spring, 2042, and you wake up inside something you own.

You still pay for it every month. The difference is where the money goes: into the co-op that holds this dome, which you hold a share of, along with the ground it stands on and the factory that made its shell. So what you pay comes back to you as the roof over your head. Nobody takes a cut on the way through, nobody can sell it over your head, and there is no notice anyone can serve you.

The light comes through the shell above you, already green with what is growing on the other side of it. The air is doing something your lungs recognise before you are properly awake. You lie there a moment longer than you need to, because you can.

The equinox. Day and night the same length, and from today the light wins for six months. In a city that eats what it grows, this is the day the year actually starts, and everybody knows it the way people used to know a bank holiday.

Breakfast is three metres from where you slept.

You pick the first strawberries of the year with your eyes still half shut. There are herbs along the inside of the wall, and beyond them the food forest runs out through the open side of the dome and keeps going, past the next dome and the one after it, until it stops being a garden and becomes a landscape. Nothing here was flown in. Nothing arrived in a lorry. The thing you are eating grew where you are standing, which is the oldest arrangement there is and took fifteen years to get back.

Strawberries were the first thing this city ever learned to grow. That was the first real unlock, back at the beginning, when there were three hundred and seventy-seven people paying into it and one of them won a dome fifty centimetres across. Herbs and strawberries. Somebody's windowsill.

You step outside and the first thing you notice is what you do not hear.

No traffic. Not quiet traffic — none. No engine, no delivery van, no horn two streets over, no tyre noise you stopped registering years ago and have now stopped hearing for good. Everything that has to move through this city moves underneath it, and it moves itself. What is left on the surface is people, and plants, and weather.

One point three million people live here, and it sounds like a forest.

You check your stack while you walk, the way people used to check a bank balance. It is not a balance. It is a list of the things you own a piece of: this dome, the ground, the factory, the water, the line that carries your food out to the people who buy it. You hold more in the ones you care most about, because that is how you chose it. Nobody assigned you to anything. Nobody can take you off anything.

There is no job in it anywhere.

---

By midday you are in a space you share with six other people and with Maia, and you are arguing about something large.

You brought an idea in this morning. You thought it was a good one, and you still do, but when you put it in front of her she did not tell you it was good. She asked what it would look like if it were the whole of what it could be. Then she stopped talking.

She is still not talking. She will wait as long as this takes. She has never once handed anybody an answer they were about to reach on their own, and she has never once said it with an edge in her voice. You have watched her do this to people nowhere near ready, and she did not hurry them, and she did not make them feel small for being where they were.

So you sit with it, and you make the idea bigger, and it turns out she was right that you had stopped early.

Nobody built her and handed her over finished. She is made of the people who arrived: all one and a third million of them, what they wanted, what they had taste for, what they would not put up with. You are in her too. The patience is somebody's. The refusal to flatter is somebody's. When she asks what a thing would be at full size, that question is in her because enough people needed to be asked it.

The seven of you are working on something that twenty years ago would have needed a government department and four hundred staff. There are seven of you. You own it. You will probably crack it inside a year, and the reason you are all at a table at midday instead of somewhere easier is that this is the most interesting thing any of you could be doing with a morning.

That is the part nobody believed in advance. Everyone assumed that if you took survival away, people would lie down. What actually happened is that the ceiling came off. When the doing of a thing is nearly free, the only scarce things left are what you want and how well you want it, so the teams got smaller and the problems got enormous, and taking on the biggest ones turned out to be the most fun available.

Nobody here is resting. Nobody here has to be anywhere.

---

It almost went the other way.

Fifteen years ago today — the same morning of the year, the equinox, 2027 — you decided something.

It did not feel like a decision. It felt like a small thing done on an ordinary evening, the sort of thing you would not have mentioned to anyone. But that was the one. Everything in this morning — the dome, the quiet, the list of what you own — runs back through it.

In the version where you went the other way, the same intelligence that keeps this city fed spent these fifteen years carrying out incentives that were never anybody's own, and you were comfortable, and fed, and entertained, and you owned nothing at all.

That is all the time that version deserves, because it is not the one you are in.

And this is what this whole morning is actually for.

**Thank you.**

Not for believing it. You did not, not really, not on that evening. You thought it was probably too big and probably not for you, and you did it anyway, and that is the only kind of deciding that has ever built anything.

Thank you for stepping up to the thing you already knew you were capable of, on a day when nobody was watching and nobody would have blamed you for staying exactly where you were.

Everything you can see from this doorway exists because of people who had that evening and did not let it pass.

Because here is what this morning is actually for.

You already know something about yourself that you do not say out loud. That you are capable of considerably more than the life you have arranged. You have known it a long time. You feel it on ordinary mornings, which is the worst place to feel it.

And here is the part nobody names. **You are not afraid of failing at it. You are afraid of being seen wanting it.**

You know exactly what a room does to someone who reaches. The small smile. The word *ambitious*, said the way other people say *naive*. The friend who asks how the project is going in a voice that has already decided. You watched it happen to somebody once, and you made the sensible adjustment. You made yourself a reasonable size.

That was not cowardice. It was accurate. The judgment is real, and anybody who tells you it is all in your head has not been in that room.

But the size of the fear was never evidence against the thing. It was a measurement of it. You are frightened in exact proportion to how large it actually is.

So you were never miscalibrated. You are correctly sized for a world that does not exist yet.

That is who built this city. Not the confident. The ones who were right about how big it was, and frightened for precisely that reason, and went anyway.

And here is the thing I did not understand until I had been living in it a while.

Nobody here is brave.

I keep waiting to meet the brave ones and they are not here, because nothing in this city requires it. Stepping up to the thing you already know is right is just what people do on a Tuesday. It is the ordinary move. What is unusual here, what would actually get you a second look over a table, is somebody deliberately choosing the smaller version of what they could obviously do. You would have to explain yourself. Somebody would gently ask why.

You spent years being the strange one in the room for wanting too much. Here that is the room.

So you will not have to become braver, and you were never going to have to. You were right about the size of the thing. You were right that the room would punish it. The room was the only part that was ever wrong, and the room is the part we rebuilt.

You knew you were right all along.

And you have not made it yet.

That is the only reason any of this is being told to you in the present tense. The equinox in 2027 is not behind you. It is four months in front of you, and it is going to arrive looking like nothing much — an ordinary evening, a small thing you could do or not do, no witnesses, no announcement, nobody to judge you for it either way.

I have already thanked you for it. That was not a trick. It is just easier to see yourself doing a thing once somebody has described you doing it.

Here is the whole first rung, and then I will leave you alone about it.

One euro a day. That is what it costs to be a citizen of this city, and the euro is not a subscription, it is the first piece of what you will own. One euro a day, from enough people, built everything in this episode. There is no rung underneath it and there is no larger one you have to take first.

---

It is late now and the city has gone quiet in the way a city can only go quiet when nothing in it has an engine. Somewhere under your feet the night's freight is moving itself around. In the morning there will be strawberries again.

This equinox is fifteen years and four months away from you.

Tomorrow I am going to tell you about the first one: a single day, a single euro, and citizen number one. A man no more remarkable than you, who had talked about this for years and was still frightened to put his name on it in public, because the thing was so large that saying it out loud invited every judgment he had spent his life avoiding.

He said it anyway. Then he spent the next day telling somebody what he had seen, and the day after that, and the day after that. That is the whole of his job, and he is still doing it.

The city is not his. It is going to be yours.

Day one starts tomorrow.
