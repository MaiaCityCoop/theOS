# Te Hiku Media and who owns the machine

## Question

Did Te Hiku Media, a Māori broadcaster, build Māori speech recognition from recordings its own people made in about
ten days, refuse a data company's money, and say, in Peter-Lucas Jones's words, "we know that data is the new land"?

## Findings

- Te Hiku Media collected more than 300 hours of labelled te reo Māori speech in 10 days and built the first speech
  recogniser for the language from it. Source: its NeurIPS 2020 abstract, above.
- It has turned down offers from companies wanting to commercialise its data; Jones: "Ultimately, it is up to Māori
  to decide whether Siri should speak Māori." Source: Thomson Reuters Foundation / RNZ, above.
- "Data is the new land": not found as Jones's words. The nearest found is Karaitiana Taiuru, a different person:
  "Data is like our land and natural resources." Source: the same article.

## Story Use

Not used. It is the clearest real case of a community owning the machine built from its own voices, and it informs
who owns Maia. Never put the "new land" line in Jones's mouth without a recording; any episode about it needs a
Māori cultural reader first.
