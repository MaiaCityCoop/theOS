# Abundism

The economy Maia City runs on, and Samuel's name for the transition out of capitalism as we have it: a post-AGI, post-labour economy where nobody has to survive, so nobody sells their potential to stay alive.

## Overview

Under abundism, machines produce the necessities and people own the machines. Income does not come from selling your hours; it comes from ownership in the things you believe in, held through shared co-ops ([the stack](the-stack.md)). Robots run the dome factories around the clock, the domes grow the food year-round, and the result is that survival stops being a question anyone has to answer.

This is not a hand-out. Nobody is given a wage for existing. Everybody holds a share of the productive capacity itself, which is a different thing and the whole point.

## What people actually do all day

**Nobody lies in a hammock.** This is the single most common misreading of a post-labour economy, and the city is the opposite of it.

When execution is nearly free, the scarce things are intent and taste, and the ceiling on ambition comes off. So the trend runs one way: **smaller teams take on larger challenges.** A handful of people with [Maia](../../characters/maia.md) and the dome factories behind them can take on what used to need a corporation or a state. The unit of ambition shrinks in headcount and grows enormously in scope.

Each challenge is a specific co-op, and every citizen who cares about it holds stakes in it. You are not assigned to one and you are not stuck in one; you hold stakes in the challenges you believe in, and you can hold several. Ownership and contribution are the same act.

So the day is not empty and it is not leisure. It is a small group of people, who all own the thing, taking on something that would have been called impossible, because nothing is stopping them and [the push](the-push.md) makes sure they do not aim low. Taking on the world's biggest challenges becomes the enjoyable thing to do, which is the part that sounds least believable until survival is gone and you watch it happen.

## Rules & Limitations

- **No traditional work.** There is no selling of labour to survive. What replaces it is ownership and contribution to visions you chose.
- **Ownership is a human right**, not a reward for having arrived early.
- **Abundance is produced, not declared.** The domes, factories and underground have to actually work; the economy is downstream of the engineering.
- **The city has its own monetary policy.** It is sovereign in money as well as in government.
- **It does not abolish scarcity of everything**, only of what survival requires. Attention, taste, trust and the willingness to jump stay scarce, which is where the story's conflict lives.
- **Its own failure mode is softness.** A person with nothing required of them can stop growing, and a city of a million who stopped is the dystopia reached by the comfortable route. [The push](the-push.md) is the answer to that, and it is the only thing standing in its way.

## History

The window for it opens once machines can produce more than people need, and narrows as ownership of those machines settles. That settling is happening this decade, while work is still a lever an ordinary person can pull to buy in.

The serial puts no date on it. A borrowed forecast owes receipts the project does not control, and one that passes unmet fails in public. What is true needs no date, and the thing actually measured is [the count](a-euro-a-day.md).

## Impact on Society

The failure case is the one the serial is written against: abundance arrives, the machines carry out incentives that were never the person's own, and people are fed, entertained and owned. The success case is the same abundance with the ownership in the right hands, which is the only difference between the two futures and the thing the city is built to demonstrate.
