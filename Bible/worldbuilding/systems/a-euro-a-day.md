# A Euro a Day

**A paying citizen in [the twin](../locations/the-twin.md) is one euro a day.** That is how the city is paid for, and it is the whole funding mechanism.

It is also the serial's most arresting true sentence: it takes a euro a day to build a city.

## Overview

No investors and no state. The city is built by the people who will live in it, a euro at a time, and the euro is what makes them a citizen rather than a customer. Ownership starts at the first one, which is why this is a rung on the [jump](../../glossary/terms/the-jump.md) ladder below all the others: an evening, a trip, your old life, everything, and underneath them all, a euro.

Because every citizen pays the same euro, **the citizen count and the daily revenue are the same number.** One figure measures the audience, the funding and the progress at once, which is why the seasons are anchored to it.

## Rules & Limitations

- **One euro, one day, one citizen.** No tiers, no premium, nobody worth more than anybody else. A city of equal owners cannot be funded by unequal ones.
- **The euro buys a stake, not a service.** It is the first contribution to [the stack](the-stack.md), which is what separates this from a subscription.
- **The count is public and it is the score.** The season's goal is a number anyone can check, and a season that does not reach it does not end early for the story's convenience.
- **Growth at the golden ratio is the rate the target needs.** 31 Fibonacci steps from one citizen reaches 1,346,269, and 5,423 days divided by 31 steps is 175 days a step.

## The budget is what has been collected, never a projection

A step's build budget is **half of the euros actually collected to that day**. Annual revenue is the wrong figure, badly so at the start: at 5 citizens the yearly run-rate reads €1,825 while the money in hand is €38.

Collected euros are the citizen count added up over every day it has been paid, so the pot depends on how long the lower rungs were held. With exponential growth it lands on a simple rule: **the pot is always a fixed number of days of current revenue.**

| Growth rate | The pot is |
|-------------|------------|
| Doubling each month (the early months) | **43 days** of current daily revenue |
| Golden ratio per 175 days (the long-run pace) | **364 days** of current daily revenue |

So the faster you are still growing, the smaller the pot is next to the revenue, because most of the citizens arrived recently. The annual figure only becomes a fair estimate once growth has slowed to the long-run pace.

The early months, at 10, 25, 50, 100, 250 and 500 citizens:

Each rung is a Fibonacci number of paying citizens, and daily revenue in euros equals the count.

Thirty distinct steps from 1 to 1,346,269; the rows above are every second one, for reading. **Step 30 is 1,346,269, the real target, and "1.3 million" is that number rounded down for saying out loud.**

At the top the city earns about half a billion euros a year from its own citizens, at a euro a day each.

**The first twelve steps fall inside the first 147 days**, at the early growth rate, with €8,479 collected by the end of them. That is why phase one happens in the game. **Step 13 is the hinge**: 377 citizens on day 165, €14,078 collected, the complete dome ladder standing in the twin, and the first real 50 cm dome delivered to one winner. See [the ladder](the-ladder.md).

Each milestone also opens **a new produce category** the city sells, so the euro is not the only income as the ladder climbs.

## History

The first euro and the first citizen are the same event, on day 1.

## Impact on Society

It settles who the city belongs to before a single dome exists. A city funded by investors owes them; a city funded by a euro a day from the people who will live in it owes nobody else. That is [the stack](the-stack.md) starting on day 1 rather than at the end.

For the reader it collapses the distance between watching and owning to one euro, which is the smallest jump the project can ask for and the one that makes every later rung reachable.

## Open

- **What the euro buys in the game**, concretely, on the day someone pays it.
- **Growth after month 6.** The early curve roughly doubles each month. Beyond it the pace is unknown, and every budget on [the ladder](the-ladder.md) moves with it.
- **What a city for 1.3 million actually costs.** Nothing yet establishes it. Until it is researched and sourced, the serial may state the euro, the count, the collected total and the yearly revenue, and must not claim that the euro alone builds the city. The produce categories and the stakes citizens hold are the other two income routes, and neither is costed yet.
- How a citizen who cannot pay a euro a day joins, which decides whether this is a movement or a club.
