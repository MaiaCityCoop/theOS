# The Stack

How ownership works. Every citizen gets their own stack: their share of everything the city runs on, held through shared co-ops rather than granted by an employer, a landlord or a state.

## Overview

The city owns no citizen and no citizen is a tenant. Housing, food production, factories, logistics, energy and the digital layer are each held by a co-op whose owners are the people who use and build them. You hold ownership in what you believe in, and that ownership is the thing that replaces a wage.

Samuel holds one stack like anyone else, by design from the first day. It is not a sacrifice he has to be talked into: a city that depends on one founder holding control is not the thing being built, and he is its messenger rather than its owner.

## Rules & Limitations

- **Everyone gets their own stack.** By design, not by negotiation.
- **It starts at the first euro.** A paying citizen in the twin pays [a euro a day](a-euro-a-day.md), and that euro buys a stake rather than a service, which is what makes it the beginning of a stack instead of a subscription.
- **Co-ops, not shares in a company.** Owners are users and builders, so control follows participation rather than capital.
- **Ownership in what you believe in.** You choose which co-ops you hold, which is how taste and conviction become economic signals.
- **Rent does not disappear; it changes direction.** A citizen still pays every month for the dome they live in. It goes into the co-op that holds it, and they hold a share of that co-op, so what they pay comes back as the roof over their head rather than out to a landlord. Saying "no rent" is wrong and the serial never says it.
- **A co-op is a challenge, not a job.** Each one is a specific thing a small group of people is taking on, and every citizen who cares about it holds stakes in it. You can hold several. Nobody is assigned and nobody is stuck, so contribution and ownership are the same act. See the day's actual shape in [abundism](abundism.md).
- **No traditional work**, so contribution cannot be coerced by need. It has to be wanted.
- **The founder holds no more than anyone else** by the end. Samuel's arc is the test of whether that actually happens.

## Practitioners

Every citizen. The founding settlers of [the first village](../locations/the-first-village.md) are the first to hold a stack in something real rather than in the twin.

## Impact on Society

This is what makes the difference between the two futures concrete. In one, somebody else owns the machines and you receive what they decide to give. In the other, you own a piece of them and what you receive is yours. The serial's entire argument reduces to: own something, before the day work is worth nothing.

## Open

- What a stack is worth, and whether it can be sold, lent or lost.
- How the first stacks are issued to people who joined in the twin and own nothing yet.
