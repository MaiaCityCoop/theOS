# The Stack

How ownership works. Every citizen gets their own stack: their share of everything the city runs on, held together
with every other citizen rather than granted by an employer, a landlord or a state.

## What is fixed

Two things hold whatever else is learned on the way, and the serial can say them plainly from day 1:

- **The Maia City Coop owns all the land, which means every citizen does.** Every square metre, from the first one.
  Not Samuel, not a founder, not an investor, and never "for now". Nobody's land is somebody's land, so nobody can
  pull it from under anyone else.
- **Maia is owned by everyone too.** The intelligence the city runs on belongs to the citizens it pushes, the way a
  dome does. What a citizen brings to her stays theirs. This is the answer, inside the city, to the only open question
  about abundance: who owns the machines.

## What is still being worked out

**How the coop is governed, how a stake is held, and how the city's currency works** are not fixed, on purpose. The
models are being learned and iterated, and will come back into the Bible with true citizenship. Until then an episode
says what is fixed above and does not describe the mechanics.

## Rules & Limitations

- **Everyone gets their own stack.** By design, not by negotiation.
- **Samuel holds one stack like anyone else**, from the first day. A city that depends on one founder holding control
  is not the thing being built, and he is its messenger rather than its owner.
- **Ownership in what you believe in.** A citizen chooses which challenges they put themselves into, which is how
  taste and conviction become what moves the city.
- **A co-op is a challenge, not a job.** Each one is a specific thing a small group of people is taking on, and every
  citizen who cares about it holds a stake in it. Nobody is assigned and nobody is stuck, so contribution and
  ownership are the same act. See [abundism](abundism.md).
- **No traditional work**, so contribution cannot be coerced by need. It has to be wanted.

## Practitioners

Every citizen. The founding settlers of [the first village](../locations/the-first-village.md) are the first to stand
on the coop's land in the real world rather than in the twin.

## Impact on Society

This is what makes the difference between the two futures concrete. In one, somebody else owns the ground and the
machines, and you receive what they decide to give. In the other, you own them together with everyone you live
beside, and what you receive is yours. The serial's argument reduces to: own something, before the day work is worth
nothing.

## Open

- The coop's legal form, its jurisdiction and how it is governed: to come back with true citizenship.
- How the city's currency and a stake's value work: the same.
