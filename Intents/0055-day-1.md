## The article: what we learned about LOD and far views

Written for whoever builds plants and distance again on top of day 1. Everything named here is in the archive tag.

### What we built, in order

1. **Seven LOD schemes, grown one at a time.** Grass thinned by rings of chunks, GPU grass in doubling cells, a baked
   lawn, patch quads, per-asset impostors, near and far ground, and sphere crowns for Earth's trees. Each had its own
   measure (rings, cells, tiles, squares, altitude) and its own hand-set numbers, about twenty in all, two of them
   copied by hand between Rust and the shaders.
2. **Intent 0050 tried to replace them with one ladder:** every asset a recipe that grows a master; its reduced
   meshes, far view and lawn as bakes belonging to the asset; one measure (size on screen against one dial) and one
   switch. Most of its parts were built and merged: the asset contract, bakes linked from the asset doc, one far view
   per asset with variants, a baker run by the kernel when an asset changes, one source for what Rust and shaders
   share, far views baked as SmartImposter does, masters swaying in the wind. The last part, putting the worlds onto
   the ladder, was written but never merged.

### What worked

- **Bake once, belong to the asset.** A far view is a pure function of the recipe, the bake's settings, its shaders
  and the bake's own code. Keyed on those and linked from the asset doc, it is read back without growing the asset
  again. Putting the bake code's source into the key removed the version number someone had to remember to raise.
- **One far view per asset, not per seed.** At far-view distance one individual cannot be told from another. A seed
  picks one of a few baked variants and gets its own turn, scale and tint. One variant is enough to start.
- **Weather at runtime, not in the bake.** Bake what weather cannot change (colour, normal, depth, coverage, sky
  visibility, a wind weight per texel) and apply wind, and later snow and season, as uniforms with the same function
  the full mesh uses. The far copy then sways like its mesh at any strength.
- **A baker outside the engine.** A kernel plugin that watches asset docs and runs the engine's `--bake` in the
  background kept baking out of the game's start and out of its frame.
- **One list of generators with one signature,** the recipe's size meaning one thing for every kind.

### What failed, and why

- **Our first impostor bake was a simplification, and it showed.** 128 px frames for every size (a 12 m tree got
  10 cm texels for 8 cm leaves), one sample per texel, a hard cut at half coverage. A crown is mostly under half
  covered per texel, so the tree's far view was a dark trunk with the crown gone. The lesson Samuel drew: reuse the
  method that is proven at scale exactly, do not run our own experiment.
- **SmartImposter's method is a whole, not a menu.** Ported faithfully (12×12 views, 3×3 sub-samples for coverage,
  5.5 cm texels scaled by the plant's size and capped at 448 px, cropped frames, coverage-weighted mips, a march
  through the baked depth, one of the three nearest views picked per pixel), the tree read as the same tree. But the
  method assumes three things we did not have:
  - **accumulation over frames** (TAA or DLSS): the per-pixel random picks are meant to average out; without it the
    far view is speckled, worst on small things seen close;
  - **compressed atlases and streaming:** uncompressed, one variant of one tree took about 220 MB (four took 890 MB);
    its 3.25 bytes a texel and a small always-resident base atlas are what make hundreds of species fit;
  - **ray-traced self-shadowing and photographed source assets:** its trees look real because its meshes do.
- **A far view can only be as good as its mesh.** Our procedural tree had plain leaf shapes; the far view's leaves
  came out darker and denser than the mesh's because at distance the mesh's own leaves fall between pixels. Matching
  the two is a property of both, not of the bake.
- **Small things do not want an impostor.** By the size rule a clover or a pebble gets 32–64 px frames: fine at its
  switch distance, soft and noisy anywhere nearer. Small plants go from mesh to a patch or the ground; the ladder's
  steps must follow from size, and for small sizes the impostor step is simply absent.
- **Memory follows what is visible here, not what exists.** A thousand species is a disk and streaming question
  (tens of GB compressed, fetched by hash when a place needs them), with only the current place's few dozen species
  resident. That only holds once compression and residency exist; we had neither.
- **Runtime-adaptive LOD cost us the walk before (intent 0038).** Levels built at runtime and stores sized by reading
  the GPU back made the old game stutter. What stayed stable was levels made once, fixed caps, a stateless choice.

### What it cost to build this way

- **Building many layers before one worked end to end.** Seven schemes, then a ladder to replace them, then a port of
  another engine's bake, each started before the one below was seen working on screen. The far view was first looked
  at by a person hours after it was "done".
- **Checks that pass are not pictures.** Tests and compiles passed while the tree's far view was a stump, a shader
  had two entry points, and a hidden window never quit. Every visual thing needs a picture taken by the tool that
  builds it, compared with its mesh, before it is called done.
- **Parallel agents on one large crate.** Every change rebuilt and linked one 224 MB binary (1 to 2 minutes), builds
  queued behind each other, stale outputs filled the disk twice, and two branches that both edited `place.rs` could
  not be merged by text. Small modules (intent 0054) and structured docs (JSON as Loro maps, done; docs in SQLite,
  intent 0053) are the answers already planned.

### If we build distance again

1. One asset, on screen, with a person looking, before a second asset or a second level.
2. Take SmartImposter's method whole or not at all: its bake, its temporal accumulation, its compression and its
   residency, in that order of dependence. Budget memory per species before baking the first tree.
3. Decide each asset's steps from its size. Far views for trees and bushes; patches and ground for the small.
4. Levels made once, fixed caps, one measure, one switch. Nothing adaptive at runtime without a measured win.
5. The far view is judged against its own mesh in the same picture, by brightness and silhouette, every time.
