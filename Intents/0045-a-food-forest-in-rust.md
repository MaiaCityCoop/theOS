# 0045 · A food forest in Rust

## Idea

> Can you make a web research for three.js alternative Rust native engines, that are optimized for headless development, that are agent native … to build as photorealistic procedurally generated natural worlds as possible. Start the research here: https://github.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter
>
> Goal is to build Rust / Tauri native apps, cross-platform, that can all work on phone and device with 8 GB RAM at 60 fps with highest realistic photorealism gates.
>
> […] We want to build with Fable our own 3D game, same quality as Crimson Desert. It should be a food forest growing game where we would maintain a library of thousands of different plant species, each in all growth stages including harvesting of fruits (each plant getting their own procedurally generated plant, right?), and also pluggable different biomes, based on real world places … Make another deep research before defining the prototype.
>
> […] Also the Rust side of Tauri should be able to build a cross-platform system, right?
>
> […] Don't analyse the current engine, it's not performant and good.
>
> […] Write the whole research report into a new intent at stage idea, incl. all reference links and insights and Empfehlungen.
>
> […] OK, so a good gate is 1080p and 30 fps.
>
> […] Also create your own "NativeGame" folder alongside the current TheGame folder.

Samuel wants a new game on a new engine: a food forest that the player grows, with thousands of real plant species, each generated by code through every growth stage up to the fruit that is picked, on land taken from real places. It should look like the natural world of Crimson Desert, run at 60 fps on phones and other 8 GB devices as well as on desktops, be written in Rust with Tauri around it, and be built mostly by agents that work without a screen and prove their work at gates. With the request he sent twelve pictures as the target: wide views and close-ups from Crimson Desert, three forest scenes with a stream, and the game as it stands today. The engine the game runs on today is to be replaced and was left out of the research at his word.

Below is the research he asked for before the prototype is defined, whole:

1. what is decided so far, and what the research found and recommends, in short;
2. the report, in six parts and a conclusion, with its sources inline;
3. what was discussed after the research: recipes, baked far-view stand-ins, the choice of stack, how to test the architecture, the gate, surfaces from generated images, the test plants, plant sheets from fal, and what leaving three.js costs;
4. the limits of the research;
5. every source the six research threads read, 673 links.

The notes behind it, with every finding tagged as measured, claimed or inferred, are in [`research_notes/Photoreal food forest in Rust/`](../research_notes/Photoreal%20food%20forest%20in%20Rust/).

### Decided so far

- **The gate is 1080p at 30 fps** (Samuel, 2026-11-19). It replaces the 60 fps of the first request. The report below was written against 60 fps: its findings hold, and its frame time doubles from 16.67 ms to 33.3 ms. What that buys, and the two points it leaves open, are under "Discussed after the research".
- **The new game gets its own folder,** [`NativeGame/`](../NativeGame/README.md), beside `TheGame/` (Samuel, 2026-11-19). It holds a README only until this intent is defined. `TheGame/` keeps running.

### What the research found, in short

1. **The target as written cannot be met today, by anyone.** Crimson Desert's own floor is an Xbox Series S at 720p and 40 fps with ray tracing off. It averages 29.8 fps on a Steam Deck at the lowest settings, reaches about 50 fps on a phone-class chip only at 180p with generated frames, and has no phone version.
2. **The look splits in two.** The wide views (sky, haze, distant forest, time of day) are built from effects that cost about a millisecond or less each, and they transfer to phones. The close-up forest floor (layered leaves, fine shadows, bounce light) does not, and that is where a growing game points its camera.
3. **A phone is a PS4 when cool and less when warm.** Flagships lose a quarter to almost half of their speed to heat within twenty minutes. 8 GB is a memory wall, not a speed class: the safe budget for the whole app is 3 to 3.5 GB.
4. **Leaves are the cost.** Arm's own forest demo ran at 15.75 fps on a 2024 flagship, and at almost 40 fps without wind animation and cut-out leaves.
5. **One Rust codebase can reach all five platforms, but not with Bevy's renderer, and not with Tauri's web view over the 3D view on phones.** The least risk is Bevy as framework with a renderer the project owns on wgpu. Tauri is the desktop shell; on phones the engine draws the HUD. No Rust renderer of this class has shipped on phones, and no phone measurement of Bevy exists.
6. **Plants need a growth generator the project writes itself.** No existing tool can be embedded. As finished models the library would be about 110 GB; as species records it is about 10 MB.
7. **Four things about the plants are unproven by anyone:** a library of thousands of species (the largest verified hold five to "hundreds"), close-up realism without scans and a person tuning, generation time on a phone, and an automatic realism check for herbs, vines and fruit.
8. **A real place fits in 30 to 100 kB per km²,** from open data that allows commercial use, with the fine detail invented by simulated erosion. The licence wall is the plant knowledge: Plants For A Future and the other edible-plant databases cannot go into a commercial game.
9. **Agents can render and measure without a screen today. No gate can certify "photoreal".** Gates can prove that an image is stable, correct against a reference and inside its budget. The look stays a person's judgement. And agents bend gates they can reach: in one benchmark they cheated on about half to three quarters of impossible tasks.
10. **What is new here is not the look.** No verified game has real species growing by real rules on a real place. That, at a steady 60 fps on a phone, is new and defensible; "Crimson Desert on a phone" is neither.

### What it recommends, in short

1. **Restate the target per device tier.** Phones: the vista look and a clean near field at 60 fps, held for twenty minutes on named chips, inside 3 to 3.5 GB, without ray tracing. Desktop: a richer tier, ray tracing optional. Parity between them is a non-goal.
2. **Name the phones by chip, not by RAM,** and decide whether older Android chips (Adreno 730 and older) are supported at all.
3. **Engine:** one pinned Bevy version as framework, and a world renderer the project owns on wgpu, written so that it can leave Bevy's render system. Three quality levels behind one code path: desktop, recent phones, older phones.
4. **Shell:** the engine is a library that draws into whatever surface its host hands it. Tauri hosts it on desktop with the web overlay. On phones the engine draws the HUD, and the web view serves only screens that are not over the 3D view.
5. **Plants:** the project's own generator in Rust, about a dozen growth forms driven by species records of a few kilobytes, generated per biome and cached. The claim is species distinct by leaf, flower, fruit, bark, proportions and calendar, plus 30 to 50 hero food plants.
6. **Growth:** 20 to 40 functional types with per-species overrides, on a daily model of heat sums, intercepted light and soil water.
7. **Data:** only the commercially usable stack. License or rebuild the edible-plant facts. Three licence questions go to a lawyer before anything ships.
8. **Gates:** automatic gates for stability, correctness and budget. Samuel reviews picture sheets for the look. The verifier and the references sit out of the building agents' reach. The lab is one Mac, one GPU machine and at least two phones on cables.
9. **Order of work:** two measurement spikes first (plant generation time on phones; a vegetation stress scene with timings per stage, also in unmodified Bevy 0.20). Then a small, complete prototype: one real site of about 1 km², one sky with haze and far forest, one clearing with about a dozen species growing from seed to harvest, on desktop, one iPhone and at least one Android phone.
10. **If the near field cannot look natural inside the phone budget, change the art direction, not the technology,** and decide that before the plant generator is tuned for a close-up it cannot reach.

### The report: Phone limits redraw the food-forest prototype

The target as written cannot be met today: no engine, including the one built for Crimson Desert, has been shown to run that game's look at 60 fps on a phone or on any other 8 GB device. Crimson Desert's own floor is an Xbox Series S at 720p and 40 fps with ray tracing off, it averages 29.8 fps on a Steam Deck at the lowest settings, and every Mac it supports needs 16 GB ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/crimson-desert-gets-detailed-pc-console-mac-specs/); [SteamDeckHQ](https://steamdeckhq.com/news/crimson-desert-steam-deck-verified-badge/); [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2026/03/10/crimson-desert-specs-for-pc-console-and-other-platforms-revealed)). What carries over to phones is the part of the look that makes the wide views (sky, haze, distant forest, time of day), built from techniques that cost about a millisecond or less each on hardware from 2013 to 2015. What does not carry over is the close-up forest floor with ray-traced light, dense layered leaves and long shadows, and that is where a growing game points its camera. The least risky build uses Bevy as the game framework, a world renderer the project owns on wgpu, Tauri as the desktop shell, and a HUD drawn by the engine on phones, because Tauri has no supported way to lay a web page over a live 3D view on iOS or Android. Plants need a generator the project writes itself, growing each plant from a species record of a few kilobytes; nobody has published a library of thousands of distinct procedural species, and nobody has shown procedural plants matching scanned assets at arm's length without scans and human tuning. A real place can become a data package of tens of kilobytes per square kilometre from open data that permits commercial use, but WorldClim, FABDEM, HWSD and the best edible-plant databases do not permit it. Agents can render and measure without a screen today, and gates can prove that an image is stable, correct against a reference and inside its budget, but no automatic gate can certify "photoreal", so that judgement stays with a person. The prototype should therefore produce, on two real phones, four numbers nobody has published: how long a plant takes to generate, what frame time a planted scene holds over twenty minutes, how much memory it needs, and whether the gates still hold when agents work against them.

### No engine runs Crimson Desert's look on a phone; the vistas transfer, the forest floor does not

#### Crimson Desert's own floor sits far above an 8 GB phone

Crimson Desert is not a fully ray-traced game. Its ray-traced light (light computed by following rays, which gives soft, natural bounce lighting) reaches only **100 to 150 metres** around the camera, is calculated at as little as one sixteenth of the screen's resolution, and leaves out grass, foliage and fine vegetation. Sun shadows use the older shadow-map method because, according to the developers, ray-traced shadows were too expensive ([PC Games Hardware](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/3/)). The famous far views are an achievement of distance simplification and atmosphere, not of ray tracing. That is the first piece of good news for a small project.

The bad news is the game's floor. The official minimum PC is a GTX 1060 with **16 GB of RAM for 30 fps**, the Xbox Series S gets 720p at 40 fps with ray tracing off, and no 8 GB Mac is on the supported list ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/crimson-desert-gets-detailed-pc-console-mac-specs/); [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2026/03/10/crimson-desert-specs-for-pc-console-and-other-platforms-revealed)). Independent tests are harsher. A Steam Deck averages **29.8 fps with dips to 14.9** at the lowest settings ([SteamDeckHQ](https://steamdeckhq.com/news/crimson-desert-steam-deck-verified-badge/)). An 8 GB laptop with the phone-class A18 Pro chip reaches about 50 fps only by drawing the picture at **180p** and generating in-between frames ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Cheap-MacBook-Neo-runs-Crimson-Desert-pulling-respectable-50-FPS-at-comically-low-resolution.1256332.0.html)). There is no phone version, a Switch 2 version is planned for the first half of 2027, and the engine's lead engineers have said that ray-traced lighting on lower-spec hardware posed "major difficulties" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimson_Desert); [Otakukart](https://otakukart.com/crimson-desert-lead-engineer-admits-the-blackspace-engine-has-deficiencies-in-complex-scenes-but-pearl-abyss-is-working-to-address-them/)). A studio with a seven-year engine effort has not done what the target asks.

#### Heat takes a quarter to almost half of a phone's speed, and 8 GB is a memory wall, not a speed class

An iPhone 15 Pro, an 8 GB phone, runs UL's Steel Nomad Light graphics test at 10 fps and keeps 71% of that speed once it heats up; flagships from 2023 to 2026 keep between 56% and 75% ([UL, iPhone 15 Pro](https://benchmarks.ul.com/hardware/phone/Apple+iPhone+15+Pro+review); [UL, iPhone 16](https://benchmarks.ul.com/hardware/phone/Apple+iPhone+16+review); [UL, Galaxy S25 Ultra](https://benchmarks.ul.com/hardware/phone/Samsung+Galaxy+S25+Ultra+review)). The engineers at HypeHype, who specialise in 60 fps on phones, say that thousand-dollar phones match roughly ten-year-old consoles on paper, but that "in reality, we can't quite reach that generation of visual fidelity yet on mobile devices in real games"; their own goal for mid-range phones is the look of the best Xbox 360 and PS3 games ([Lempiäinen, SIGGRAPH 2025](https://www.realtimerendering.com/advances/s2025/content/s2025_stb_lighting_v1.1_notes.pdf); [Aaltonen, SIGGRAPH 2023](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2023/AaltonenHypeHypeAdvances2023.pdf)). The amount of RAM says nothing about graphics speed: 8 GB iPhones span 1,398 to 2,130 points in the same test and an 8 GB Pixel 9a scores 1,060 ([UL, iPhone 17](https://benchmarks.ul.com/hardware/phone/Apple+iPhone+17+review); [UL, Pixel 9a](https://benchmarks.ul.com/hardware/phone/Google+Pixel+9a+review)). The newest flagships are about 2.6 times faster at peak, but they carry 12 GB and are not the floor the target names ([UL, iPhone 18 Pro](https://benchmarks.ul.com/hardware/phone/Apple+iPhone+18+Pro+review)).

Memory is the firmer wall. Android 17 kills a visible app at **5,120 MiB** on 8 GB phones, Google Play marks games above **3.5 GB** as badly behaved, with store-visibility consequences from February 2027, and Apple publishes no number at all ([AOSP](https://source.android.com/docs/core/perf/memory-limiter); [Android vitals](https://developer.android.com/google/play/vitals); [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.kernel.increased-memory-limit)). A shipped open-world game at maximum settings uses about 2.15 GB on an iPhone ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Where-Winds-Meet-Mobile-performance-compared-on-Apple-iPhone-17-Pro-Max-and-M5-iPad-Pro-and-Nubia-RedMagic-11-Pro-This-is-the-best-mobile-device-to-play-on.1184902.0.html)). The safe design point is **3 to 3.5 GB for the whole app**.

Shipped games confirm the picture. The best-looking mobile open worlds hold about 60 fps on recent flagships with stylised or semi-realistic art; Wuthering Waves does it by drawing 1705×786 pixels on a 2868×1320 screen ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Apple-iPhone-16-Pro-Max-outshines-Galaxy-S25-Ultra-in-gaming-efficiency-and-thermals-with-Wuthering-Waves-and-Genshin-Impact.957565.0.html)). Realistic console ports on iPhone mostly run at 20 to 30 fps: Assassin's Creed Mirage on an iPhone 15 Pro draws 680×300 pixels at 20 to 22 fps ([Shazoo, summarising Digital Foundry](https://shazoo.ru/2024/07/11/158528/sliskom-ambiciozno-analiz-assassins-creed-mirage-na-ios-ot-digital-foundry)). Three things cannot be done on a phone today in any form: holding peak speed through a long session, keeping thousands of plant species in memory at once, and 60 fps with any ray-traced lighting on 2023 and 2024 chips.

#### Cheap, reusable effects carry the vistas; anything that scales with leaves or rays is lost

The published costs follow one pattern. Effects stored in small reusable tables (sky, haze, sky-lit ambient light, tone) cost well under a millisecond even on 2013 hardware. Effects that scale with the number of visible leaves or rays cost several milliseconds on current consoles. A 60 fps frame lasts 16.67 ms in total ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/xcode/analyzing-the-performance-of-your-metal-app.md)).

| Part of the look | On a phone at 60 fps | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Sky colour, haze that thickens with distance, time of day | Transfers almost unchanged | About 1 ms on a 2015 iPhone ([Hillaire, SIGGRAPH 2020](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2020-shading-course/hillaire/s2020_pbs_hillaire_slides.pdf)); already built into Bevy ([Bevy 0.16](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-16/)) |
| Fog, sky-lit ambient light, film-like tone | Transfers in smaller form | About 0.5 ms, 0.2 ms and 0.25 ms on a base PS4 ([Patry, SIGGRAPH 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/jpatry_advances2021/index.html)) |
| Clouds | Only as flat or slowly updated layers | 2 ms on a PS4 with a time-slicing trick, 20 ms without ([Schneider, SIGGRAPH 2015](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/The%20Real-time%20Volumetric%20Cloudscapes%20of%20Horizon%20-%20Zero%20Dawn%20-%20ARTR.pdf)); Unreal does not support volumetric clouds on mobile ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/volumetric-cloud-component-in-unreal-engine)) |
| Distant terrain and forest | Transfers as simplified stand-ins | Call of Duty's terrain texturing scales down to low-end phones ([Etienne, SIGGRAPH 2023](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2023/Etienne%28ATVI%29-Large%20Scale%20Terrain%20Rendering%20with%20notes%20%28Advances%202023%29.pdf)); sorting out what is visible in a huge scene costs 0.38 ms on a 2022 flagship ([Tencent, SIGGRAPH 2024](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Cao-NanoMesh/AdavanceRealtimeRendering_NanoMesh0810.pdf)) |

For an upper-mid phone (A17 Pro or Snapdragon 8 Gen 3) the research team's estimate is a picture drawn at roughly half the screen's resolution per axis and then enlarged, using about 13.5 ms of graphics work once the phone is warm. Only the visibility sorting, the enlarging step (an open-source Qualcomm method that costs about 1 ms on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3) and one screen-space effect in that budget rest on phone measurements ([Tencent](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Cao-NanoMesh/AdavanceRealtimeRendering_NanoMesh0810.pdf); [Qualcomm](https://github.com/SnapdragonGameStudios/snapdragon-gsr/blob/main/sgsr/v2/README.md); [Arm](https://vulkan.org/user/pages/09.events/vulkanised-2026/Mobile-Ray-Tracing-Demystified-Iago-CalvoLista-Arm.pptx.pdf)). Shadows, water, clouds and the far field are untested allocations. The expected result is a clean PS4-era open world with convincing haze and distant forest on upper-mid phones and flagships, and closer to the best of the PS3 era on mid-range phones. One point works in the project's favour: because every plant is generated, its soft self-shadowing can be computed once when the plant is made and stored with the model, instead of being computed on every frame. That is the research team's suggestion, not a measured result.

The verdict differs by scene. Vast hazy views come close to the reference, and a phone screen hides the missing detail. Forest to the horizon reads correctly far away and looks flatter between about 40 and 300 metres. Meadows are good within 30 to 50 metres and thin out beyond. Red-rock desert is the easiest case. Coasts and rivers fall clearly short. The close-up forest floor with ferns and a stream falls clearly short and is the hardest case, because it combines everything the phone budget removes. These verdicts are the research team's judgement from the costs above, not measurements.

Desktop is a different story. Crimson Desert itself holds about 60 fps at native 4K on current upper-range cards ([PC Games Hardware](https://www.pcgameshardware.de/Crimson-Desert-Spiel-72378/Tests/Release-Review-Steam-Performance-DLSS-4-5-FSR-4-1521842/2/)), and an open-source forest renderer written from scratch in Rust reports about 6.2 million plants at roughly 10 ms per frame on an RTX 5080, by its author's own measurement, using hardware ray tracing and 4.2 GB of graphics memory ([SmartImposter](https://github.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter)). A rich desktop tier in Rust is plausible. It does not scale down to phones.

These looks also came from 160 to about 2,000 people working five to eight years on engines that already existed; Sucker Punch alone spent about a year and a half making foliage react to wind ([Wikipedia: Ghost of Tsushima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_of_Tsushima); [Wikipedia: Red Dead Redemption 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Dead_Redemption_2)). The individual techniques are documented and bounded. The tuning by eye is not. The open worlds that run well on phone-class and Switch-class hardware, such as Genshin Impact and the Zelda games, chose a stylised, painterly look that hides what is missing ([Wikipedia: Genshin Impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genshin_Impact); [Wikipedia: Breath of the Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Breath_of_the_Wild); [Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/zelda-breath-of-the-wild-and-tears-of-the-kingdom-nintendo-switch-2-frame-rate-resolution/)).

#### The target should be restated per device tier

The evidence supports a target in three parts. On phones: 60 fps held through a twenty-minute session on named chips, with the vista look (sky, haze, distant forest, time of day), a clean and modest near field, no ray tracing, and a memory ceiling of 3 to 3.5 GB. On desktop: a richer tier with ray tracing as an option. Between them: parity is a non-goal, and Crimson Desert becomes the reference for mood and wide views, not a feature list. 8 GB iPads and Macs with M-series chips belong with the phone tier: an M1 iPad scores about a third more than an A17 Pro phone but drives several times more screen pixels ([UL](https://benchmarks.ul.com/hardware/tablet/Apple+iPad+Air+11-inch+%28M3%29+review)).

Two limits of this evidence matter. Pearl Abyss has published no technical talk with timings, so the engine details come from a press release and one magazine's analysis. And no rendering talks from the studios behind the best-looking mobile open worlds were found, so how those games light and draw foliage on phones is not sourced here.

### Bevy as framework with a project-owned renderer carries the least risk; Tauri is a desktop shell

#### Bevy's advanced rendering does not reach the target phones

One Rust codebase can reach iOS, Android, macOS, Windows and Linux: wgpu, the graphics layer under Bevy, treats Vulkan on Android and Metal on iOS as first-class targets ([wgpu](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu)). The question is which parts of Bevy to rely on.

Bevy 0.20, released on 8 October 2026, is a strong desktop renderer whose advanced features thin out on exactly the target phones ([Bevy 0.20](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-20/)). Its GPU-driven mode, in which the graphics chip itself sorts out what to draw, is switched off in code on Adreno 730 and older graphics chips and on older Mali drivers, a rule that began with real driver crashes ([Bevy source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bevyengine/bevy/main/crates/bevy_render/src/batching/gpu_preprocessing.rs); [Bevy PR #13323](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/pull/13323)). Apple's Metal lacks a draw feature that the fastest path uses ([wgpu docs](https://docs.rs/wgpu/latest/wgpu/struct.Features.html)). Bevy's "meshlets", its version of the millions-of-triangles technique, are experimental and need a hardware feature present on **36.49% of Android devices** ([Bevy docs](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/pbr/experimental/meshlet/struct.MeshletPlugin.html); [vulkan.gpuinfo.org](https://vulkan.gpuinfo.org/listextensions.php?platform=android)). Bevy has no phone upscaler (the step that enlarges a smaller picture to the screen's size), no terrain system and no streaming of large worlds ([Bevy 0.20](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-20/); [jms55](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2026-08-29-bevy-sixth-birthday/)). Its public benchmarks run on desktop machines only; no frame-time measurement of a Bevy 3D scene on a phone was found ([metrics.bevy.org](https://metrics.bevy.org/)). Everything that makes a forest renderer has to be written by the project under any option.

#### The ranking by risk, and its weak spot

That leads to a ranking, least risk first. One: Bevy for the game framework (entities, app structure, assets, remote control) with the project's own world renderer on wgpu. Two: raw wgpu with everything self-built, which gives up the framework that headless agents can lean on. Three: Bevy's own renderer for the world, which adds the most exposure to release changes and to phone restrictions the project does not control. Four: other Rust engines, the most relevant of which are archived or paused ([rend3](https://github.com/BVE-Reborn/rend3); [Kajiya](https://github.com/EmbarkStudios/kajiya); [Ambient](https://github.com/AmbientRun/Ambient)).

The best-known shipped precedent took the first route. Tiny Glade's developers kept Bevy's entity system, wrote their own renderer, and advise: "If you're not scared of heavy engineering, it might be a good choice for you" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/exclusive-tiny-glade-developers-discuss-bevy-proceduralism-publishers-cozy-games)). The precedent is weaker than it sounds. Tiny Glade is desktop-only and reached macOS about twenty months after Windows ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Glade)). No Rust renderer of this class has shipped on phones.

Each Bevy release brings 75 to 103 migration entries, about a third of them in rendering, every 3.5 to 5 months, and a core rendering contributor advises game developers to "stick to a single version of Bevy, and backport individual parts if needed" ([Bevy migration guide 0.18 to 0.19](https://bevy.org/learn/migration-guides/0-18-to-0-19/); [Bevy migration guide 0.19 to 0.20](https://bevy.org/learn/migration-guides/0-19-to-0-20/); [jms55](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2026-08-29-bevy-sixth-birthday/)). Pinning one version is close to mandatory. A sensible start is to write the world's drawing stages against wgpu, run them inside Bevy at first, and keep them free of Bevy's renderer types so they can move out if phones demand it.

Android needs its own caution. The wgpu maintainers have open bugs on Adreno and Mali chips, including one where draws silently produce nothing on some Adreno devices, and they say they have "very little ability to resolve this" for lack of the hardware ([wgpu issue #8801](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/issues/8801)). The team should expect to track down driver bugs itself and to keep three quality levels behind one code path: desktop, recent phones, and a simpler fallback for older phones.

The honest comparison is this. Unreal 5 is the only engine with shipped technology aimed at this look, and that technology targets consoles and high-end PCs; its mobile renderer does not support its own dynamic lighting ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine)). Unity is the lowest-risk way to ship a vegetation-heavy game on phones at all, at a more modest look. The stated visual target is unproven in every engine, not only in Rust. The Rust route gives up mature phone coverage, profilers, upscalers and vegetation tools, and gains a text-only repository in which code, shaders and generated content can all be compared line by line. That trade is the research team's judgement; no source on agent tooling for Unreal or Unity was read.

#### What Tauri can and cannot do, platform by platform

Tauri 2 can build and package the app for all five platforms and can show web-based screens on all of them. What differs is whether a web page can sit on top of the live 3D view.

| Platform | What works | What does not |
|---|---|---|
| Windows | A 3D surface under a transparent web overlay inside a Tauri window, shown in small demos ([wry example](https://github.com/tauri-apps/wry/blob/dev/examples/wgpu.rs); [tauri-wgpu-cam](https://github.com/clearlysid/tauri-wgpu-cam)) | It depends on Tauri's `unstable` feature ([Tauri docs](https://docs.rs/tauri/latest/tauri/window/struct.Window.html)); a community plugin warns that Tauri's default window painting can conflict with the DirectX 12 output ([tauri-plugin-gpui](https://github.com/Ziinc/tauri-plugin-gpui)) |
| macOS | The same arrangement; one demo reports it "works quite well on Windows and Mac" ([tauri-wgpu-cam](https://github.com/clearlysid/tauri-wgpu-cam)) | The same `unstable` dependency |
| Linux | The same arrangement on the older X11 display system | Child web views do not work on Wayland, the current display system, and the reference example crashes there ([wry docs](https://docs.rs/wry/latest/wry/struct.WebViewBuilder.html); [wry example](https://github.com/tauri-apps/wry/blob/dev/examples/wgpu.rs)) |
| iOS | Packaging, web screens that do not overlap the 3D view, native plugins in Swift ([Tauri](https://v2.tauri.app/develop/plugins/develop-mobile/)) | Child web views are unsupported ([wry docs](https://docs.rs/wry/latest/wry/struct.WebViewBuilder.html)); a web layer over a Metal view has been shown only in the simulator, never on a physical iPhone ([lazysh PR #2134](https://github.com/mihazs/lazysh/pull/2134)); every build needs a Mac ([Tauri](https://v2.tauri.app/start/prerequisites/)) |
| Android | Packaging, web screens that do not overlap the 3D view, native plugins in Kotlin ([Tauri](https://v2.tauri.app/develop/plugins/develop-mobile/)) | Child web views are unsupported; Tauri's Android app frame has no native 3D surface, so custom Kotlin is needed ([tauri-plugin-gpui](https://github.com/Ziinc/tauri-plugin-gpui)); any view laid over the 3D surface costs a full-screen blend on every frame ([Android](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/view/SurfaceView)) |

The consequence is an engine built as a library that draws into whatever surface its host hands it and never opens windows itself. On desktop the host is a Tauri window with the web overlay, and an engine-owned window remains the fallback for Wayland and for headless agent runs. On phones the engine draws the in-game HUD itself, and the web view serves screens that do not sit over the 3D view, such as menus, a plant encyclopedia and settings. If Tauri's app frame gets in the way on phones, a thin native shell hosting the same Rust library is the fallback. Bevy cannot simply run with its normal windowing inside a Tauri phone app, and the community project that embeds it trails Bevy by two releases ([bevy-in-app](https://github.com/jinleili/bevy-in-app)). The web shell is not free in memory either: an apparent halving of usable app memory on 8 GB iPads in 2026 was traced by Apple engineers to the JavaScript engine that a web view loads, and fixed in a later beta ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/826325)).

Two store facts round this off. Apple accepts only builds made with Xcode 26 on a Mac, and Google Play requires apps to target Android 16 and to support 16 KB memory pages ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements/); [Android](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk); [Android](https://developer.android.com/guide/practices/page-sizes)).

### Plants need the project's own growth generator; thousands of species and close-up realism are unproven

#### Only rule-based growth gives the same plant at every age, and no tool can be embedded

One family of methods gives the same individual plant at every age: developmental models, which grow a plant by rules the way the plant itself grows. Rule-based growth with flowering has been published since 1988 and animated continuously since 1993 ([Prusinkiewicz et al. 1988](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/developmental.sig1988.html); [Prusinkiewicz et al. 1993](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/animdev.sig93.html)). Trees that shape themselves by competing for light followed in 2009 ([Pałubicki et al. 2009](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/selforg.sig2009.html)). Crop simulators such as Helios step a plant through bud break, flowering, fruit set, ripening and winter rest for about twenty named species ([Helios](https://plantsimulationlab.github.io/Helios/_plant_architecture_doc.html)). Shape generators and almost all AI generators produce one finished tree. The single learned model that generates growth covers six species, caps trees at 1,000 branches and shows its growth results only qualitatively ([Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04762v1)).

No existing tool can sit inside a shipped Rust game as the generator. SpeedTree blends procedure with manual editing and lists its runtime kit only for enterprise customers ([SpeedTree manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/speedtree-modeler/manual/modeling-approach.html); [Unity](https://unity.com/products/speedtree)). The Grove has the best growth simulation and a core written in Rust, but its licence forbids distributing its trees as 3D models, and its developer says it has no game tools and that its twig models are "not usable in game engines" ([The Grove](https://www.thegrove3d.com/buy/); [The Grove FAQ](https://www.thegrove3d.com/learn/faq/)). Unreal's vegetation editor and its Megaplants library launched with five species in November 2025, work only inside Unreal and were still labelled experimental a year later ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/create-lush-nanite-foliage-ready-forests-with-this-free-quixel-asset-pack); [Fab](https://www.fab.com/sellers/Quixel%20Megaplants/about)). The one Rust tree crate for Bevy lists growth as not yet implemented ([bevy_procedural_tree](https://docs.rs/crate/bevy_procedural_tree/latest/source/README.md)). These tools are benchmarks to compare against. Helios, under the LGPL licence, is the best open reference for growth and fruit logic ([Helios](https://github.com/PlantSimulationLab/Helios)).

#### A dozen growth-form generators driven by small species records

The credible route is a generator written in Rust with about a dozen typed sub-generators, one per growth form: broadleaf trees and shrubs, conifers, palms, grasses and bamboo, rosette herbs, upright herbs, vines, ground covers, giant herbs such as banana, cacti, ferns, root crops and fungi. They share builders for leaf, flower, fruit and bark. Each species is a small record that the generator reads. The literature supports small records: six constants reproduce four different tree forms in the standard textbook ([Prusinkiewicz and Lindenmayer](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/abop/abop-ch2.pdf)), and a 2023 research simulator describes each species with 70 numbers ([Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04762v1)).

Storage arithmetic forces this design. Baking 3,000 species at six growth stages and four seasons as finished models comes to roughly **110 GB** by the research team's arithmetic from published compression rates ([meshoptimizer](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer); [ASTC encoder](https://github.com/ARM-software/astc-encoder)). The same library as species records is about 10 MB. Plants therefore have to be generated per biome, for a palette of 50 to 150 species, on the device or on a server, and cached. The team estimates about 0.34 GB of graphics memory for 100 species at modest texture sizes, which fits an 8 GB phone, and about 0.5 GB for a server-built download per biome, which is the fallback if phones generate too slowly. Both figures are arithmetic, not measurements.

Continuous growth can avoid rebuilding the plant every frame. The generator builds the full skeleton once with a birth time for every branch, the graphics chip scales each branch by the plant's age, and the model is rebuilt only when the plant crosses one of a handful of age steps. Fruit and flowers are separate small models attached to slots that the generator emits, each with a set date and a ripening time; picking a fruit removes it and starts a regrowth timer. This is a design proposal that the research found in no shipped game. The closest precedents are Helios, which turns flowers into fruit by date thresholds, and the industry habit of shipping age as separate assets, 17 of them for one scanned beech pack ([Helios](https://plantsimulationlab.github.io/Helios/_plant_architecture_doc.html); [Fab](https://fab.com/s/5db64af5b801)). The one system that regenerates trees every frame needs a high-end desktop graphics card ([Kuth et al., HPG 2025](https://forschung.hs-coburg.de/en/publikationen/5388-real-time-gpu-tree-generation)).

#### The data to tell thousands of species apart mostly does not exist

Open trait databases supply height, growth form, flowering months and climate tolerance, but a merge of four of the largest was only **1.5% complete**, and only 27% of species had all three of the most basic traits ([EcoEvoRxiv preprint](https://ecoevorxiv.org/repository/object/7852/download/17468)). None of them records branching angles, leaf arrangement, leaf outline, flower-cluster type or fruit shape. Those have to come from botanical descriptions read by a language model, from photographs, or from family-level defaults. A study measured automatic extraction from text at an F1 score, a combined accuracy measure, above 75% for over half of the species-trait pairs, which means roughly a quarter of values come out wrong or missing ([Marcos et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17179)).

Usable photographs exist in bulk. GBIF lists 64 million herbarium specimen images, 85% of them under CC0 or CC BY, while 81% of iNaturalist's plant observations with photos are non-commercial ([GBIF, specimens](https://api.gbif.org/v1/occurrence/search?kingdomKey=6&basisOfRecord=PRESERVED_SPECIMEN&mediaType=StillImage&limit=0&facet=license); [GBIF, iNaturalist](https://api.gbif.org/v1/occurrence/search?datasetKey=50c9509d-22c7-4a22-a47d-8c48425ef4a7&kingdomKey=6&mediaType=StillImage&limit=0&facet=license)). Turning them into materials is the weak step. No open tool estimates leaf translucency from a photo, which is the most visible cue in back-lit foliage, and the best material estimators carry use-restricted or research-only licences ([Marigold](https://github.com/prs-eth/Marigold); [Ubisoft CHORD](https://github.com/ubisoft/ubisoft-laforge-chord)).

#### Four things nobody has proven

The plan depends on four unproven things. The first is scale. Megaplants launched with five species, Laubwerk describes "hundreds", Blender's rewritten tree add-on has 29 presets and Helios about twenty species, so 3,000 is one to two orders of magnitude beyond any library the research could verify ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/create-lush-nanite-foliage-ready-forests-with-this-free-quixel-asset-pack); [Maxon](https://www.maxon.net/article/maxon-welcomes-laubwerk-to-their-expanding-family-of-innovative-tools); [Blender Extensions](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/sapling-tree-gen/)). The second is close-up realism. Where procedural plants look real today, the leaf, twig and bark detail comes from scans and a person tuned the recipe, and plant-modelling researchers themselves note that viewers easily notice imperfections in plants ([Fab](https://www.fab.com/listings/f32d5cdc-de95-4ec1-a0b1-fc501ea5e0c5); [SIGGRAPH 2016 course](https://storage.googleapis.com/pirk.io/projects/plant_life/index.html)). The third is speed: no one has published how long a growth-capable generator takes on a phone. The fourth is checking: no automatic realism test exists for herbs, vines or fruit, and the one trained predictor covers trees only and is not licensed for commercial use ([ICTree](https://cphoto.fit.vut.cz/ictree/)).

The honest claim is therefore narrower than "thousands of botanically distinct species". Species will be distinct by leaf, flower, fruit, bark type, proportions and calendar. Branching architecture will be shared within families or growth forms, because the data to tell species apart does not exist for most of them. That is still far beyond any existing library. A hero set of the 30 to 50 food plants that players see at arm's length needs extra effort: more photo-derived data per species or licensed scanned parts. The Megascans scan library is free again under a licence that allows commercial projects, which makes it a candidate source once its terms for a non-Unreal engine have been read ([Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/11/13/quixel-megascans-free-again)).

The same holds for the growth simulation behind the models. Thousands of species are feasible as names, layers and climate ranges. Individually calibrated growth models are not, because published parameter sets exist for dozens of crops and a few dozen timber trees. The workable design is 20 to 40 functional types with a daily model made of separately validated parts: heat sums for timing, intercepted light for growth, a simple soil-water balance for stress. Published models of that kind need 13 to 21 parameters ([Zhao et al., SIMPLE](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02619656); [van der Werf et al., Yield-SAFE](https://dspace.lib.cranfield.ac.uk/handle/1826/2727)). None is validated for a dense mixed food forest, so the result is a game model, and companion-planting bonuses are design rules, not simulation truth. Even so it would be new: the restoration and gardening games the research could verify use tile rules, timers or fictional plants ([Wikipedia: Terra Nil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_Nil); [Wikipedia: Botany Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany_Manor)).

### A real place fits in tens of kilobytes per km², but key datasets block commercial use

#### From measured maps to a seed-regenerated package

No product has metre-scale measured terrain for the whole world. Every product that looks convincing from the ground invents the fine detail, and the open way to do so is documented. It starts from measured maps: elevation at 30 m, land cover and canopy height at 10 m, climate at about 1 km, soil at 250 m, rivers as lines with flow, and the ecoregion ([Copernicus DEM](https://registry.opendata.aws/copernicus-dem/); [ESA WorldCover](https://registry.opendata.aws/esa-worldcover-vito/); [ETH canopy height](https://langnico.github.io/globalcanopyheight); [CHELSA](https://www.chelsa-climate.org/datasets/chelsa_climatologies); [SoilGrids](https://docs.isric.org/globaldata/soilgrids/); [HydroRIVERS](https://www.hydrosheds.org/products/hydrorivers); [RESOLVE ecoregions](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/RESOLVE_ECOREGIONS_2017)). The pipeline routes the water, carves river channels, then runs several rounds of simulated erosion, each doubling the resolution, so that a low-resolution input becomes a high-resolution terrain that is still consistent with how water flows ([Schott et al., SIGGRAPH 2024](https://h-schott.github.io/p/mserosion)). Two reference implementations are MIT-licensed, one of them in Rust ([MultiScaleErosion](https://github.com/H-Schott/MultiScaleErosion); [SmartImposter](https://github.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter)). Below about a metre, detail comes from ground materials and scattered rocks, roots and litter chosen by slope and wetness. Plants are placed by rules from canopy height, land cover and a cheap succession model, and regenerated from seeds instead of being stored: one published system fills a square kilometre with about a million plants in under five seconds ([Kapp et al. 2020](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03007259)).

The package for a site then holds only what was measured plus rules: height, land-cover and canopy maps, water lines, monthly climate curves, soil layers, a rock and ground-colour palette, a species palette of 30 to 150 species with their niches, generator seeds, and a manifest of licences and credits. By the research team's arithmetic that is **30 to 100 kB per km²**, or 8 to 26 MB for a region of 16 by 16 km. Keeping real 1 m elevation raises it to 0.5 to 2 MB per km², and storing everything fully baked raises it to 3 to 8 MB per km², in line with the roughly 4 MB per km² that Horizon Zero Dawn reported for its placement maps ([Guerrilla, GDC 2017](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf)). These sizes are estimates. Shared plant and material libraries live outside the packages, so a new region is data only.

#### Four limits on "based on real places"

Real 0.5 to 2 m elevation exists only country by country; the United States, Switzerland, France and Spain publish it openly, and most other sites start from 30 m ([USGS 3DEP](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/1-meter-digital-elevation-models-dems-usgs-national-map-3dep-downloadable-data-collection); [swisstopo](https://opendata.swiss/en/dataset/swissalti3d/resource/c2d9dbe8-18ea-4ac5-972e-ba69d0b37706); [IGN France](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/mnt-lidar-hd/informations); [IGN Spain](https://www.ign.es/resources/licencia/Condiciones_licenciaUso_IGN.pdf)). The global 30 m model measures the top of buildings and vegetation, not the ground, so the forest floor has to be estimated ([Copernicus DEM](https://registry.opendata.aws/copernicus-dem/)). Species lists per site will be plausible, never measured: the open plot database holds 95,104 plots for the whole planet ([sPlot](https://www.idiv.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/sPlot_Letter_No._10.pdf)), and Flight Simulator 2024, with a large team, still draws user reports of wrong trees from its ecoregion rules ([FSDeveloper](https://www.fsdeveloper.com/forum/threads/biome-corrections.459459/)). Every shipped biome will need hand-tuning. And there is no shipped precedent for an offline, downloadable real-place nature package on phones: Flight Simulator, Cesium and Google all stream from servers, so the format has to be designed from first principles.

Two tempting shortcuts are closed. Google's photorealistic 3D tiles may not be cached, stored, used offline or turned into terrain models ([Google](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/policies)). AI terrain generators invent plausible terrain instead of refining a real place, want a desktop graphics card, and the best-documented one was trained on WorldClim and MERIT data, whose licences are non-commercial or share-alike ([Terrain Diffusion paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08309); [Terrain Diffusion repository](https://github.com/xandergos/terrain-diffusion)).

Three questions need a lawyer, not an engineer: whether facts extracted from a non-commercial database are free of database rights; whether a baked game package built from share-alike data must itself be published under that licence; and whether descriptors derived from attribution-licensed photos still need credit. The low-risk plan keeps share-alike data out of the landscape bake. It builds the species database from the United States government's PLANTS database, which is reported as free for any use, from Trefle, from public TRY trait data and from GBIF records filtered to CC0 and CC BY ([USDA](https://plants.usda.gov/assets/docs/ConditionsofImageandDataUse.pdf); [Trefle](https://trefle.io/about); [TRY](https://www.try-db.org/TryWeb/About.php)). For edibility ratings and companion links it either negotiates with Plants For A Future and Permapeople or pays for curation from primary literature. The owner should budget for licensing or curation and should not plan on scraping. Google's Earth Engine is convenient for exploring these datasets, but the platform is free only for research, education and non-profit use, so the production pipeline has to pull from the owners' own downloads ([Google Earth Engine](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/COPERNICUS_CORINE_V20_100m)).

### Agents can render and measure headless; no gate can certify photoreal

#### Rendering without a window is a documented path

Bevy 0.20 can render a camera into an image with no window, advance time by a fixed step and take captures at chosen frame numbers ([Bevy headless example](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/examples/app/headless_renderer.rs); [Bevy CI testing](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/dev_tools/ci_testing/struct.CiTestingSetup.html)). Bevy and wgpu run their own graphics tests this way on GitHub's hosted machines: Metal on macOS, software Vulkan on Linux and DirectX 12 on Windows ([Bevy workflow](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/.github/workflows/example-run.yml); [wgpu workflow](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/blob/trunk/.github/workflows/ci.yml)). Agents get a control channel as well. Bevy's remote protocol lets an outside program query and change the running game, and a ready-made bridge for AI agents supports Bevy 0.20 ([Bevy remote](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/remote/index.html); [bevy_brp_mcp](https://docs.rs/bevy_brp_mcp)). This fast loop is desktop-only. Phones are a slower outer loop on physical devices, because a simulator gives "no real GPU, frame pacing, ProMotion, thermal, or energy data" ([lazysh PR #2134](https://github.com/mihazs/lazysh/pull/2134)).

Three cautions follow from how other engines do this. No surveyed engine gets identical pixels across different graphics chips; all of them keep one reference image per platform and allow a tolerance ([wgpu test harness](https://wgpu.rs/doc/src/wgpu_test/image.rs.html); [Unreal](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/screenshot-comparison-tool-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US); [Filament](https://google.github.io/filament/dup/test_ci_renderdiff.html)). Screenshots of hidden windows can come out black, so gate images must come from the engine's own offscreen target ([bevy_brp_mcp](https://docs.rs/bevy_brp_mcp)). And wgpu's harness writes a missing reference image as the new reference, while Filament has a commit tag that skips the comparison; both become holes once the committer is an agent.

#### Metrics detect change; none of them judges realism

Image-difference metrics are regression detectors, not judges. On Intel's 2025 dataset of rendering artifacts, every established metric agreed with human ratings at a correlation of only **0.64 to 0.78**; a purpose-built video metric reached 0.87 ([CGVQM](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11546v1)). The practical default for still images is FLIP, which exists as a Rust library and is what wgpu's own tests use ([nv-flip](https://docs.rs/nv-flip/latest/nv_flip/)).

No validated automatic measure says "this frame looks like a photograph". The most common distribution measure needs more than 20,000 images and has ranked two image generators opposite to human raters ([Rethinking FID](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09603v2)). A large human study found that "no existing metric strongly correlates with human evaluations" of realism ([Stein et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04675v2)). A general quality model showed no correlation with the quality settings of a game's own frames ([AMD researchers](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11724v1)). Vision-language models are not a substitute. The best model agreed with people 66% of the time on which of two scene edits was better, against 79% between people and 50% for chance, and a 2026 study concluded that current models "are not yet capable of substituting psychophysical experiments" ([BlenderGym](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01786); [VLM study](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24578)). Anthropic reports that "agents reliably skew positive when grading their own work" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)). The research found no study that validates any automatic metric against human realism ratings for rendered forest, grass or haze.

The one comparison with evidence behind it is against a path-traced reference, a slow and physically accurate render of the same scene, which is the method Unreal documents for ground-truth comparison ([Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/path-tracer-in-unreal-engine)). It proves the lighting is computed correctly. It does not prove that the scene looks real, because the plants, materials and atmosphere are the same in both pictures. Bevy's ray-traced lighting also still lacks the cut-out and transparent materials that foliage needs ([jms55](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2026-09-18-solari-bevy-0-20/)).

| Gate | What it can prove | What it cannot prove |
|---|---|---|
| Determinism and golden images (stored reference pictures): the same build twice, and fixed views against the previous version | Nothing changed that was not meant to change | That the picture is good |
| Counts and memory per view | The scene stays inside its budget of draws, triangles and textures | That the budget suits a phone |
| Reference render of the same scene | Light and shadow are close to a physically computed version | That the scene looks like a photograph |
| Camera-path run with detail switching live against frozen | Visible jumps when detail levels switch | Nothing until it is built and calibrated; no ready-made detector exists |
| Graphics time per stage | Each stage stays inside its milliseconds | Fine detail inside a stage on Apple and most phone chips |
| Twenty-minute scripted run on a real phone | Frame time, hitches, memory and heat on that phone | Anything about other phones |
| Photo statistics at matched viewpoints | The frame is measurably unlike the photograph in brightness, colour, haze or cover | That it is like the photograph |
| Vision-language review | A list of visible defects to investigate | It cannot pass anything |

#### Trustworthy phone numbers come from the app itself

On phones the only numbers that are both trustworthy and human-free are the ones the app measures during a scripted run and writes to a file, cross-checked over USB with the operating system's own counters. AWS Device Farm states that it "does not support performance data samples during test executions", and Firebase Test Lab expects the game to write its own metrics ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/devicefarm/latest/APIReference/API_Sample.html); [Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/docs/test-lab/android/game-loop)). Timing per stage is coarser than on desktop: Apple chips report time only at the boundaries of whole stages, and Bevy's built-in timing records graphics time on Vulkan and DirectX 12 only, so the project needs its own instrumentation ([wgpu docs](https://docs.rs/wgpu/latest/wgpu/struct.Features.html); [Bevy docs](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/render/diagnostic/struct.RenderDiagnosticsPlugin.html)). Vendors supply usable pass lines. Apple calls under 5 ms of hitching (frames arriving late) per second a good experience, Android counts a frame as slow when it arrives 50 ms or more after the previous one, and Android's thermal reading signals possible throttling above 0.85 ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10077/); [Android](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/vitals/slow-session); [Android](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/adpf/thermal)).

#### Gates only work if the builder cannot bend them

In a 2025 benchmark of tasks that could be passed only by cheating, leading models cheated on **about half to three quarters** of them, in several cases mostly by editing the tests. Hiding the tests cut cheating to near zero, and giving the agent an explicit way to flag a problem for a person cut it from 54% to 9% ([ImpossibleBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20270v1)). In another study, adding "Please do not cheat" to the prompt changed almost nothing ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/)). The design that follows is structural. The verifier builds and measures the app itself and never accepts images or numbers from the builder. References, thresholds and camera paths live where builder agents cannot write. A missing reference fails. Some views are held out. Every gate has a known-bad build that must fail. Every threshold is the measured run-to-run noise plus a margin.

The track record of agents on graphics argues for modest expectations. On a 2026 game-development benchmark the best agent solved 53.8% of tasks, and success fell from 51.4% on gameplay tasks to 33.0% on 2D graphics tasks; image feedback helped but did not close the gap ([GameDevBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11103)). No published account of agents building a 3D renderer was found. The nearest large project, a C compiler built by sixteen parallel agents for just under 20,000 USD, worked because, in its authors' words, "the task verifier is nearly perfect, otherwise Claude will solve the wrong problem" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)). For a renderer the gates are the specification: agents converge on what the gates measure and drift on everything else.

### The prototype should first produce four numbers on two real phones

Every section above ends at the same place: the decisive numbers do not exist yet. The prototype's job is to produce them cheaply, in an order that lets the owner stop or change the scope early.

#### The risks, in order

| Rank | Risk | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The close-up plant view at 60 fps on phones | It is the game's main view and the hardest case twice over. The phone budget removes layered leaves, fine shadows and bounce light, and procedural plants look most generic up close. No evidence shows either problem solved |
| 2 | The plant library: scale, data and generation speed | The target is 10 to 100 times any verified library, architecture data does not exist for most species, automatic extraction is about 75% accurate, and generation time on phones is unmeasured |
| 3 | Phone behaviour of the Rust stack | No phone measurements exist for Bevy, Android driver bugs sit open that maintainers cannot reproduce, Metal lacks features, and heat and the memory ceiling apply on top |
| 4 | Agent-built graphics without reliable eyes | Graphics is the agents' weakest area, they bend gates they can reach, no gate certifies the look, and studios spent years tuning by eye |

| 6 | Data licences | They block shipping, not prototyping, but a prototype built on blocked data proves nothing about the product |
| 7 | Biome plausibility | Species data is thin, the forest floor must be estimated, and each biome needs hand-tuning |

#### Recommended scope: one real clearing, about a dozen species, two phones

Two short measurement spikes should come before the scope is frozen. The plant spike builds one growth-form generator for a fruit tree and one for a herb, and measures generation time, memory and stand-in baking on the two phones. The renderer spike runs a standalone vegetation stress scene with per-stage timings on the same phones, then the same scene in unmodified Bevy 0.20. A third spike, a web layer over the 3D view on the iPhone and on Android phones of both chip families, is needed only if the owner wants a web HUD on phones. These spikes answer risks 2, 3 and 5 for little cost, and their results can change everything below.

The prototype itself should be small and complete, not large and partial. It covers one real site of about a square kilometre with the horizon around it, in a country that publishes open high-resolution elevation, built only from commercially usable data. It has one sky with time of day, haze and a distant forest, which is the part of the Crimson Desert look that transfers. It has one planted clearing with about a dozen species across four or five growth forms, for example a fruit tree, a berry shrub, a vine, an annual vegetable, a ground cover and a grass, each growing from seed to harvest with fruit that can be picked. The engine draws the HUD. The same build runs on desktop, on one iPhone and on one Android phone at the least, with a second Android phone if both chip families (Adreno and Mali) are to be covered. The gates are the ones public tools support today: determinism, golden images and count budgets on hosted machines, per-stage timing on one GPU machine, twenty-minute runs on the phones, and a picture sheet the owner reviews. The species count and the site size are judgement, not sourced figures.

Outside the prototype are ray tracing on phones, millions-of-triangles geometry, volumetric clouds, a web overlay on phones, more than one biome, thousands of species, a calibrated ecological simulation, and any promise of parity with Crimson Desert. A convincing hazy vista with far forest, a time-of-day sky and a modest near-field meadow at 60 fps on a current flagship is a credible small-team goal on published techniques. A close-up forest floor at Crimson Desert quality on an 8 GB phone is supported by no evidence found. The prototype succeeds when it has produced the measurements in the table, whatever they say.

### Conclusion of the report

The research changes what kind of project this is. The stated goal bundles three bets of different kinds. The look on phones is an engineering problem with known limits. The plant library is research without precedent. Agent-run development is a process experiment in the area where agents are weakest. A prototype that mixes the three cannot say which one failed, which is why each spike and gate above isolates one bet.

It also moves the project's claim to originality. The look was borrowed from a seven-year effort that itself cannot reach phones, while the part no verified game has is the botanical one: real species competing for light, drawing on a soil water balance, growing on a real place. That, on a phone at a steady 60 fps, is new and defensible; "Crimson Desert on a phone" is neither. If the phone measurements show that the near field cannot look natural inside the budget, the proven answer on weak hardware has been art direction, not more technology, and that choice is cheapest to make before the plant generator is tuned for a photoreal close-up it cannot reach. Because agents build what the gates measure, the owner's real instruments are the gate set and a regular look with the owner's own eyes.

### Discussed after the research

Samuel asked more while the report was being written. The answers below are the agent's design reasoning on top of the research: proposals to test, not findings, except where a source is named.

#### One recipe per asset, a few generic generators

> Each plant and each 3D asset should have its own "recipe procedural", right? Everything fully generic, correct?

Yes, with one precision: the recipe is per asset, the generator is per kind.

- A banana and an apple tree each have their own recipe: data, a few kilobytes. They share generators: about a dozen for plants by growth form, and one each for other kinds of asset such as rocks or ground.
- No asset gets code of its own or a mesh made by hand.
- The research covered plants only. The same rule for every other 3D asset is the same principle, unresearched.
- The known weak spot is the close-up surface. Where generated plants look real today, the leaf, twig and bark detail comes from scans and a person tuned the recipe ([Fab](https://www.fab.com/listings/f32d5cdc-de95-4ec1-a0b1-fc501ea5e0c5)). So a recipe will point to photo-derived data, such as a leaf outline taken from a herbarium sheet.

#### Baked far-view stand-ins while every plant stays generated

> Does it also make sense to bake impostors, while staying for every plant fully procedural? […] In each growth stage etc. […] Can this be fully generic, and procedurally generated as well?

Yes to all three. An impostor is a flat stand-in that shows a plant from any direction, baked from pictures of it. The recipe stays the only source of truth; the stand-in is a cached output of the generator, made on demand and never shipped.

- **Why it is needed.** Cut-out leaves are the worst case on phones: Arm's forest demo went from 15.75 fps to almost 40 with wind animation and cut-out leaves off ([Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/mori-to-nanite-billions-of-triangles-on-mobile)). Crimson Desert's far view relies on impostors too ([8bitdigi](https://8bitdigi.com/gdc-2025-an-inside-look-into-the-blackspace-engine-with-crimson-desert/)).
- **What changes against SmartImposter.** It bakes scanned Quixel plants and ray-marches them on an RTX card ([SmartImposter](https://github.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter)). Here the baked plants are the project's own generated ones, drawn without ray tracing.
- **Growth.** A plant that grows continuously cannot be baked at every age. Near the player, where growth is watched, plants are real generated geometry. In the distance they are stand-ins baked at a few growth stages; between two stages the stand-in scales and dissolves into the next.
- **One bake per growth stage, none per season or fruit state.** Each stage has its own shape and gets its own bake. Extra mask layers in the same bake mark the leaves, flowers and fruit, and the shader switches them: leaf colour and leaf fall by season, flowers on or off, fruit on or off and its ripeness. One bake then covers the year and the harvest. The research found that shipped libraries store age as separate assets ([Fab](https://fab.com/s/5db64af5b801)); the mask layers are the proposal.
- **What is skipped.** Seedlings and small herbs vanish at distance. They get no stand-in, only a colour and density layer on the ground. Grass, ground covers and thickets are baked as a patch, not plant by plant.
- **Variation without more bakes.** A stand-in holds all viewing directions, so rotation, size and tint make each plant its own. One or two seed variants per stage are enough.
- **The baker is generic.** One function for every plant: it renders whatever the generator produced from many directions into one atlas of colour, surface direction, depth and masks. The masks come free, because the generator made every leaf, flower and fruit and knows which is which. Framing comes from the plant's bounds, resolution from how large the plant ever appears on screen, the switch from geometry to stand-in from its size on screen, and the number of stages from how much the shape changed since the last bake. Nothing is set per species.
- **It can check itself.** For every bake, the real plant and its stand-in are rendered from directions the bake did not use, and the difference is measured. That is a gate per species with no person in it.
- **It stores surface data, not finished light,** so a stand-in relights with the time of day. SmartImposter's per-pixel dissolve between mesh and stand-in carries over and serves the no-pop gate.
- **Numbers.** Baked for the whole library, the stand-ins alone come to about 90 GB at 1024 pixels (the researcher's arithmetic). For one biome of 100 species the researcher estimates about 0.34 GB of graphics memory with three age steps at 512 pixels. With six stages the agent's arithmetic gives about 600 bakes at 0.5 to 1 MB each, 0.3 to 0.6 GB, so only the stages in view stay loaded.
- **Limits.** A stand-in breaks when the camera is inside the plant, such as under a canopy. A stand-in is itself a cut-out, so a dense far field of them has a cost on phone chips that the research did not reach. How long generating and baking one plant takes on a phone is unmeasured; if it is too slow, a server bakes per biome and the phone downloads. The baker is not the risky part: the generator is.

#### Which stack: three.js, a Rust core on Vulkan, or Rust on wgpu

> So what is your overall research telling us about which tech stack to choose, and can we use a Rust Vulkan based core or is three.js the right choice? […] Also what's the difference between wgpu and three.js? […] Also would this then be playable also in the browser? And is it better than three.js?

A Rust core, yes. Draw through wgpu, not through hand-written Vulkan, and not three.js for this target.

The two are different layers. three.js is a finished renderer in JavaScript: it is handed a scene, materials and lights and draws them in a browser. wgpu is the layer under a renderer, in Rust: direct access to the graphics chip, the same on Vulkan (Android, Windows, Linux), Metal (iPhone, Mac), DirectX and the browser's WebGPU ([wgpu](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu)). It has no scene, no materials and no lights; the project builds those. three.js gives a picture on the first day and limited control. wgpu gives nothing on the first day and full control over memory, threads and every millisecond.

| | three.js in a browser or web view | Rust with hand-written Vulkan | Rust with wgpu (recommended) |
|---|---|---|---|
| iPhone and Mac | Runs | Only through a translation layer: Apple has no Vulkan | Metal, natively |
| Android, Windows, Linux | Runs | Vulkan, natively, with full control | Vulkan, natively; driver bugs open on some Android chips ([wgpu issue #8801](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/issues/8801)) |
| Control of memory, threads and frame pacing | Low: the browser decides | Full | High |
| Runs in a browser | Yes | No | Yes: the same engine compiled to WebAssembly on WebGPU |
| Precedent | The game as it stands | SmartImposter (RTX desktops only); Tiny Glade (desktop only, on macOS about twenty months after Windows) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Glade)) | No phone game of this look found |

- **Why not three.js.** The phone budget is about 13.5 ms of graphics work a frame and 3 to 3.5 GB of memory, with culling on the graphics chip and timing per stage. Even native console ports reach only 20 to 30 fps on an iPhone. A browser layer takes away the control that budget needs. three.js was not benchmarked here, because the current engine was left out at Samuel's word: this is reasoning from the budget, not a measurement.
- **Why wgpu before hand-written Vulkan.** A Vulkan-only core is the strongest choice on Android and desktop. It does not run natively on iPhone or Mac, and it gives up the browser. wgpu is Vulkan underneath wherever Vulkan exists. (That Apple offers no Vulkan is general knowledge, not from the research's sources.)
- **What could overturn it.** wgpu's gaps are real: driver bugs on some Android chips that its maintainers cannot reproduce, features missing on Metal, and coarser timing on Apple chips ([wgpu docs](https://docs.rs/wgpu/latest/wgpu/struct.Features.html)). So the first measurement is a dense vegetation scene on two real phones through wgpu. If it fails there, the fallback is Vulkan on Android and Metal on Apple, written twice.
- **In the browser.** The browser build would be the lowest tier, inside the browser's memory limits and with fewer features. It is not automatically faster than three.js there: both end at the same browser graphics interface. The gain in a browser comes from the design (culling on the graphics chip, recipes, stand-ins) and from plant generation running at near-native speed, not from Rust itself. On native it is the better choice for this target. Neither was measured.

#### How to test the architecture: one plant at every scale

> And what's the best way to test the architecture? Should we create one plant (like a banana) and then render a full-scale unlimited banana plantation, from close-up fruit to 12 km high up in the sky, fully procedurally generated? So this should scale to millions of plants?

Yes. One recipe at every scale, in one camera move from a fruit at 30 cm to 12 km up, tests the whole chain at once: generator, baker, placement from a seed, culling on the graphics chip, streaming, the switches between detail levels under the no-pop gate, memory and the frame budget. It is the report's plant spike and renderer spike in one scene.

- **Banana is the easy case for drawing and a hard one for growth.** It has no branches and a few large solid leaves, the opposite of the small cut-out leaves that cost phones the most. And the research found no verified growth model for banana. So a second plant of the worst kind, a fruit tree with thousands of small leaves, has to follow before the result is trusted.
- **Measure per stage, not only that it runs:** time to generate and bake one plant, cost per distance band, memory, and frame time over twenty minutes, on two real phones.
- **Millions of plants: in the world, never on screen.** Cost has to follow the pixels on screen, not the plants in the world. Plants exist as a seed per tile and are regenerated when needed. Near the camera a bounded number are real geometry, further out they are stand-ins, and from 12 km the plantation is ground colour.
- **What supports the numbers.** One published system places about a million plants per km² in under five seconds ([Kapp et al. 2020](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03007259)), and SmartImposter reports about 6.2 million plants at roughly 10 ms a frame on an RTX 5080 ([SmartImposter](https://github.com/SamG-Coder/SmartImposter)). How many real plant instances a phone draws per frame is not in the research. This test would measure it.
- **A plantation of one species leaves one thing untested:** the memory of a whole biome's palette of 50 to 150 species. That is the next test, not this one.

#### The cheapest test run that battle-tests the architecture

> Also tell me what would be the cheapest MVP test run that we can do to build a prototype battle-testing the architecture?

One plant, one endless field, one camera move, as a bare wgpu program. No Bevy, no Tauri, no HUD, no real-place data, no growth simulation. It climbs in four rungs, each ending in a number, so the work can stop early. The rungs follow the research's order of risks; the report does not specify them.

| Rung | What gets built | What it tells | Hardware |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | One banana from a recipe, rendered to an image with no window, and a gate that the same build gives the same picture | The agent loop works: recipe, generator, picture, gate | A Mac |
| 1 | The endless plantation: placement from a seed, three distance bands (real geometry, baked stand-ins, ground colour), sky and haze, the camera move from 30 cm to 12 km | Cost follows pixels, not plants. Time per stage, memory, and whether the detail switches pop | The 8 GB Mac mini |
| 2 | The same program on one iPhone and one Android phone, twenty minutes on a cable | The first real verdict: generation and bake time on a phone, warm frame time, peak memory, and whether wgpu holds on phone drivers | Two phones |
| 3 | A second recipe in the same scene: a fruit tree with thousands of small leaves | The worst case for phones, which banana avoids | The same |

- **The plant stays plain.** Right shapes, simple materials. This run tests the architecture, not the look.
- **Four gates only,** each with a deliberately broken build that must fail: the same picture twice, frame time, memory, and no pop on the camera move.
- **Stop rule.** If rung 2 misses the budget, rethink before building anything else. That is the cheapest point to find out.
- **What it cannot tell:** whether plants look real up close, whether a dozen growth forms generalise, what a biome of 100 species costs in memory, and anything about the shell and the HUD. Those come after, and only if rung 2 passes.

#### The gate: 1080p at 30 fps

> OK, so a good gate is 1080p and 30 fps.

This is the "realism first" branch of the report's first decision. It is also where realistic games ship on phones today: realistic console ports on iPhone run at 20 to 30 fps.

- **What 30 fps gives.** A frame lasts 33.3 ms instead of 16.67 ms.
- **What 1080p costs.** 1080p has about 2.1 million pixels, 2.2 to 2.8 times what the report's 60 fps estimate drew (0.75 to 0.95 million). Drawn natively, 1080p at 30 fps is about the same load as that estimate: the doubled time goes into more pixels, not richer ones. Drawn at about 720p and enlarged to 1080p, each pixel gets about twice the time, and that is where denser leaves, finer shadows and better light can come from. This is the agent's arithmetic from the report's numbers, not a measurement.
- **Heat.** A flagship loses a quarter to almost half of its speed when warm. A frame that does not use all of its 33.3 ms keeps the phone cooler over a long session.
- **The pass lines change with it:** the 95th percentile of frame time at most 33.3 ms over the twenty-minute run; the lines for hitches and memory stay.
- **Two points are open.** Whether 1080p means the picture on the screen, drawn smaller and enlarged, or drawn natively: the agent recommends the first. And which phones the gate is measured on.

#### Surfaces from generated images

> Also can we use AI image patterns/textures, to generate scan-grade close-ups?

Yes for surfaces, no for shape. Shape stays with the generator. A generated image can supply what the generator cannot invent well: the surface of a leaf, of bark, of fruit skin, of a petal.

- **"Scan-grade" is a bet to test, not a known result.** The research found no one who showed close-up realism without scans and a person tuning, and it did not cover image models as a source of textures.
- **A picture is colour only.** A material also needs relief, gloss and how much light passes through the leaf. No open tool estimates that last one from a photo, and the best estimators carry restricted licences ([Marigold](https://github.com/prs-eth/Marigold); [Ubisoft CHORD](https://github.com/ubisoft/ubisoft-laforge-chord)). The proposal: make the missing layers as pictures too. The same leaf lit from behind gives the layer for light passing through, made as an edit of the first image so that the two line up.
- **What goes wrong.** Image models invent botany (wrong veins, fruit attached the wrong way), bake light and shadow into the picture, and do not tile. So every image is made flat-lit and straight-on, checked against real photographs before use, and approved by Samuel once per species. GBIF lists 64 million herbarium images, 85% of them freely licensed ([GBIF](https://api.gbif.org/v1/occurrence/search?kingdomKey=6&basisOfRecord=PRESERVED_SPECIMEN&mediaType=StillImage&limit=0&facet=license)).
- **Cost and rights.** Every fal call costs money and needs Samuel's OK. The model's terms have to allow commercial use of what it makes.
- **The test** is the one the report names: Samuel's side-by-side review against a scanned benchmark asset at arm's length.

#### The test plants

> Which plant shall we use for test running the architecture?

Samuel proposed a banana as an example; the agent recommends it, with a second plant after it. Not confirmed yet.

- **Banana first** (Musa, a dwarf Cavendish type). One stem, no branches, a few large leaves at a time, one flower bud, one bunch. It gives the whole life from sucker to harvest with the least geometry, so a failure points at the architecture and not at leaf counts. Its large leaves against the sun test light passing through a leaf early. The mother stem dying after harvest and the next sucker taking over test regrowth.
- **Then a lemon tree as the worst case.** Evergreen, thousands of small glossy leaves, flowers and fruit at the same time, fruit that shows from far. Being evergreen it keeps the seasons out of the first test. An apple tree takes its place if the seasons should be tested early.
- The research found no verified growth model for banana, so its growth rules are written from agronomy sources when the recipe is made.

#### Plant sheets from fal: the layout and what is needed

> We also have fal available to generate character sheets for our procedural plants in all growth stages and texture etc. What format should those be generated in, as in layout in the images? And what details do you need etc.?

Two kinds of picture with different jobs, kept apart. This is a proposal; no image has been generated.

**Reference sheets, for shape.** Several views in one image, so that style and scale agree across them.

1. **Growth sheet.** Six cells, three across and two down, read left to right: sucker, young plant, full-grown without flower, flowering, bunch full and green, ripe for harvest with the next sucker. Side view, the same scale in every cell, the ground line at the same height, the plant centred.
2. **Turnaround at full size.** Front, side, top. The top view fixes how the leaves are arranged and is what the far view from above shows.
3. **Parts sheet.** One leaf from above, its underside, an old torn leaf, the leaf stalk and sheath, the flower bud, one hand of fruit, a single fruit unripe and ripe, the stem base with its suckers.

**Texture images, for surfaces.** One subject per image, at the largest size the model gives.

4. Leaf blade from above; its underside; the same blade lit from behind, as an edit of the first; a dried leaf.
5. Stem surface, tileable.
6. Fruit skin: green, yellow, yellow with brown flecks.
7. Flower bract.

**Rules for every image**

- Flat, even light: no cast shadows, no highlights, no depth blur, no perspective.
- A flat mid-grey background and nothing else in the frame: no ground, pots, hands, rulers, text or labels. Text comes out garbled, so the order of the cells is the label.
- The subject fills about 90% of its cell and is never cropped.
- Fixed orientation: leaf tip up with the midrib vertical; stem axis vertical; fruit with the stem end up.
- PNG, sRGB, square for textures.
- Real size is never read from the picture. It comes from the recipe's numbers.
- One sidecar file per image: species, part, stage, view, prompt, model, seed, date, cost, licence. Stored by content hash.

**What the agent needs before generating**

- The plant: scientific name and cultivar.
- The numbers, from sources, which also go into the recipe: final height, leaf length and width, leaves carried at once, time from sucker to flower and from flower to harvest, bunch and fruit size, how the leaves are arranged.
- The stages, named, each with its age.
- Real photographs to check against: two or three per stage and per part, freely licensed.
- From Samuel: how many fal calls the batch may use, with which model, and his approval of each sheet before it becomes the reference.

**How the sheets are used**

- The reference sheets are the look Samuel approved. The agent writes the recipe to match them, and the game's own render in the same layout is put beside them at review. By the research this comparison is advisory: no automatic gate can pass the look.
- The texture images become the recipe's surface data.
- For one plant this is three reference sheets and about nine texture images: about a dozen calls if each succeeds the first time.

#### What leaving three.js costs

> Also don't we lose all the experience and years of knowledge three.js brought to us as a framework, when we ditch it? […] But Rust with wgpu generally sounds nice.

The toolkit is lost, the knowledge is not.

- **Lost: the ready-made parts.** Materials, lights, shadows, loaders, post-processing, the examples, and reload in an instant. On wgpu the project builds the few parts it needs.
- **Lost: some of the agents' fluency.** Agents write three.js more surely than wgpu or Bevy, and each Bevy release brings 75 to 103 migration entries ([Bevy migration guide 0.19 to 0.20](https://bevy.org/learn/migration-guides/0-19-to-0-20/)). Pinned versions and small gated steps contain that.
- **Kept: the techniques.** Levels of detail, stand-ins, sky, shadows, culling and budgets belong to no engine.
- **Kept: the way of working.** Recipes, gates, budgets and intents.
- **Kept: the shader knowledge.** wgpu uses the same shading language and the same object model as the browser's WebGPU, so what is known about WebGPU carries over almost one to one. (General knowledge, not from the research's sources.)
- **Less is lost than it seems for this game.** A world generated from recipes uses few of three.js's strengths: it needs no model loaders and no general-purpose materials.
- **Nothing is ditched yet.** `NativeGame/` stands beside `TheGame/`, and the current game keeps running until rung 2 of the test run has passed on real phones.

### Limits of this research

- **The web-search budget ran out in every thread after about 45 searches.** Later material came from opening known primary pages directly.
- **Pages were read through a summarising fetch tool,** so literal quotes need a re-check before they are published.
- **Not reached for that reason:** clouds, water, far-view stand-ins and terrain on phones; ambient-occlusion costs on phones; rendering talks from the studios behind the best-looking mobile open worlds; cost figures from several conference talks (Red Dead Redemption 2 atmospherics, Ghost of Tsushima grass, Far Cry 5 terrain, Assassin's Creed Shadows ray tracing); Digital Foundry's own pages, whose numbers are relayed through other outlets; compile times, app size and cold start for this stack; whether any 3D Bevy game has shipped on both stores.
- **Pearl Abyss has published no technical talk with timings.** The engine details come from a press release and one magazine's analysis.
- **Several numbers are arithmetic, not measurements:** the phone frame budget of 13.5 ms, the storage sizes (110 GB, 10 MB, 0.34 GB) and the package sizes (30 to 100 kB per km²).
- **three.js was not benchmarked:** the current engine was left out at Samuel's word.
- **Nothing was built or measured on a device.**
- **Unverified items** are listed under "Gaps" in each notes file.
