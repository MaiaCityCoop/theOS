# 0036 · A real body, its movement and its clothes

## Idea

> Start with the proper body phase … so the complete body with good movements, and the garment as well, like nice
> natural garment.

> Isn't it possible to push procedurally generated code to full realism?

Samuel wants the person to read as a real person: the whole body, moving well, in natural clothes. Intent 0031 listed
what is not real yet (points 1–25). This intent fixes points 1–22 in the person (`characters/kit/human.js`,
`characters/settler.js`, `characters/kit/avatar.js`): form, skin and face shape, hair, clothes, skeleton and skinning,
movement. Points 23–25 (shadows and the shade where parts touch) are intent 0032's.

**The direction (decided with Samuel):** the person stays generated by our own code: no meshes, scans or captured
motion from anyone else. What is known about real people comes in as numbers, each table small and on its own, each
number cited: body measures by sex and age (public anthropometric surveys), published gait and joint-angle curves,
anatomical proportions. Samuel may still veto the tables; they can be taken out without touching the code that uses
them.

**The aim:** a body, movement and clothes that read as a real person at the library's distance (about 4 m, the whole
body) and in the world. The face in a close-up is the hardest part: its form first (skull, jaw, eyelids, real eyeballs,
a mouth that is an opening), the skin's fine detail later.

**The gates (Samuel's):** 60 fps and 4K, always. The canvas stays at the screen's own pixel ratio; a frame where people
are drawn must not get slower, and a cost hidden behind the governor lowering the inside resolution does not count.
Phones get levels of detail: many people will stand in the world one day, so far ones must be cheap.

## Define

Samuel asked for this work outright, and the brief holds no surprise beyond what he asked, so it is built straight
away, in three rounds, each its own pull request that leaves the person at least as good as before.

**Round 1: the body**

1. **Form from anatomy, not tubes.** The body generated from bones (landmarks), muscles and fat (volumes) and the skin
   over them: collarbones, shoulder blades, chest, buttocks, kneecaps, ankle bones, the muscles of the arms and legs;
   the limbs flow into the trunk as one surface, nothing passing through anything.
2. **Hands and feet.** Five fingers with knuckles and nails; feet with toes, inside the shoes.
3. **The head.** Skull, jaw, chin, cheekbones, brow; eyelids; eyeballs with iris, pupil and cornea; a mouth that is an
   opening; ears and a nose with their form.
4. **Variety by the avatar's numbers:** age, sex and build join the avatar (`characters/kit/avatar.js`, its copy in
   `API/src/avatar.js`, `API/test/api.test.js`); avatars kept before still load.
5. **The skeleton:** fingers, toes, jaw, eyes and eyelids, a spine of several bones, twist bones; four bones per point
   of the skin, skinned so that elbows, knees and shoulders keep their volume (the choice of method, and why, in Build).
6. **Levels of detail**, from a close-up to a dot, the far ones cheap enough for many people.
7. The three moves (walk, idle, wave) and the clothes (shirt, trousers, belt, shoes, hair) still there, at least as
   good as before; the next rounds replace them.

**Round 2: movement**

8. Quaternion blending; idle, walk, jog and run blended by speed, the stride matched to the speed so the feet never
   slide; the feet set on slopes and steps (IK); starting, stopping, turning by stepping; the continuous speed of the
   rig and the joystick (intent 0030) wherever a person walks.
9. Life: breathing in the chest, the weight shifting, blinking, eyes and head that look at things (the camera, in the
   library); hair and flesh that move by themselves.

**Round 3: garments**

10. Shirt, trousers, belt and shoes as their own surfaces over the body: thickness, seams, a collar that stands, cuffs
    and hems; drape and folds that follow the pose; the world's own baked cloth (linen, denim, leather).
11. Hair as strands or cards, with volume and a silhouette.

**Checks** (every round)

| # | Check | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The person from the front, side and back, at rest and mid-move | stills in the library (`library.html?character=944dc52a-5094-480c-9396-5ceedbbf03b1`) |
| 2 | The face and the hands, close | stills |
| 3 | Variety | stills of people made from different avatars (age, sex, build) |
| 4 | Frame time where people are drawn | main against the branch, several runs each, alternating: the frame's median and 95th percentile, draws, the governor's inside ratio; in the library with the Settler, in `me.html`, in the world. And the person alone: the same view drawn with only the person and with nothing, timed on the GPU (`?gputime`) |
| 5 | Both sites build | `vite build` in `TheGame/` and `OS/` |
| 6 | The API keeps the same rules as the game | `cd API && bun test`, when the avatar changes |
| 7 | Nothing else in the picture changes | stills of the world on main and on the branch, compared pixel by pixel |

**Out of scope**

- Shadows on and from the person, and the shade where parts touch: intent 0032.
- The skin's fine detail (pores, freckles, light through the skin's thickness) beyond what is there now.

## Build

**Round 1, first attempt (2026-11-16), turned down.** A body as a field of about 280 blended solids (eggs and rods
for bones, muscles and fat, set on a skeleton of 68 bones), drawn out as one surface region by region (the face, the
hands and the head finer than the body, joined where they meet), with the clothes and the hair as shells over it
(`characters/kit/field.js`, `body.js`, `measures.js`; nothing in the game uses them yet). Seen only in previews drawn
outside the game. Samuel: "This looks absolute catastrophic." What was wrong, as seen:

- Every solid showed as its own lump: muscles as eggs stuck on rods, nothing running from bone to bone and thinning
  into tendon, no layer of skin pulling them into one surface.
- The trunk a barrel (too deep and wide at the waist and hips, no taper), the chest and breasts eggs stuck on, the
  shoulder blades plates, the buttocks two balls; narrow sloping shoulders, thin even arms meeting the trunk as
  tubes, a thick neck; small lumpy hands, blobs for feet.
- The face: the eye sockets holes with the eyeballs bulging out of them, a nose of a rod and a ball, nostrils
  punched into the front of the face, lips like a sausage, a flat face with a receding chin; ears like crumpled blobs.
- The hair a helmet; the body through the shirt and trousers.
- The cuts at the wrists gave several rims instead of one; the middle level held 61 000 points (twice what it should)
  and took 2.6 s to build.

**Round 1 again, as Samuel asked:** "let's go for naked first without hair and muscular body (clothes come later)".
The skeleton and its proportions first, checked from the front and the side; muscles as shapes from their origin to
their insertion, thinning into tendons; a skin over them; the face from the skull up; each part looked at alone
before it is joined. A muscular man first, smooth at the groin as an anatomy figure is; women and other builds after.

**Round 1, second attempt (2026-11-16), set aside.** A muscular man of reference (1.80 m) with the muscles laid as
strands from their origin to their insertion, each end carried by its own bone (`characters/kit/anatomy.js`; the strand
and a profiled tube added to `field.js`). Seen in previews only: the muscles floated apart with gaps between them, and the
broad flat ones (the back's) stood off the ribs as wings in the arms-out pose. Muscles cannot be laid well without the
bones they lie on.

**Round 1, as Samuel then asked:** "do the bones completely correct first, and then the anatomically correct muscles
and fat afterwards, each part, bones, fat and muscles, correctly coloured … as choreographed basis." In layers, each
on the one below, each looked at alone before the next:

1. The bones, bone-coloured: each in its own shape and size from the measures (skull and jaw, the spine's vertebrae,
   ribs and breastbone, collarbones and shoulder blades, the pelvis, the long bones, kneecaps, the hands' and feet's
   bones), meeting at their real joints: the skeleton the person moves by.
2. The muscles, red: each from its origin on a bone to its insertion on another, at its size.
3. The fat, yellow: where it lies, more or less by build, sex and age.
4. The skin over all of it.

**The bones (2026-11-16), as Samuel asked: "the skeleton now to be anatomically absolut correct and photorealistic -
feel free to research skeletons reference images as well"; "once you got all the bones correct you can try to rig it
correctly"; "ensure the skull is correct".**

- **What it is built from.** Each bone is now a field of its own (never blended with its neighbour), written in one
  file a region (`characters/kit/skeleton/`: skull, spine, chest, pelvis, arm, hand, leg, foot) with the rig and the
  kit they share; a point is written once, for the left side of the man of reference, and mirrored. Two solids were
  added for real bone shapes (`field.js`): a loft (a shaft with its section given along it: three-sided where the
  bone is, its end closed as a dome where it is a head or a crown) and a plate (a blade drawn by its outline, bent,
  thick at its rim).
- **What it is measured against.** The plates of Gray's Anatomy (1918, public domain, from Wikimedia Commons), about
  fifty of them, kept outside the repository: they are looked at, and for the skull read on a grid; nothing of them
  is traced into a mesh. The numbers they gave are written where they are used, with the figure they came from.
- **The skull**, from fig. 188 (side) and 190 (front): the braincase a loft whose breadth, profile and plan are given
  level by level, its crown a true dome; the face one shaped mass whose front passes through the rims of the eye
  sockets, with the sockets (four-sided funnels, inner walls straight back, outer slanting in), the nose's opening
  and cavity and the palate cut into it; the bony nose, the cheekbones' arches, the mastoids, the styloids, the
  condyles; the lower jaw as a blade with its head, coronoid and notch on a body round the arch; thirty-two teeth each
  its own piece, standing where the ones before them end; the hyoid; the seams (sutures) as lines for whoever draws
  it. Laid over the two plates at their own scale and tilt, its outline, sockets, nose, row of teeth and jaw fall on
  theirs within a few millimetres; seen from the front its jaw is wider than the plate's at the angles (the plate is
  drawn in perspective, the check is not).
- **The other regions** were each rebuilt by a helper session of mine working in its own file against its own
  plates, all at once: the leg and foot (femur, kneecap, tibia, fibula, seven ankle bones, metatarsals, toes), the
  spine and chest (twenty-four vertebrae each in its region's shape with an open canal, the discs, twelve pairs of
  ribs, their cartilages, the breastbone), the pelvis and the arm and hand.
- **Found on the way.** The pictures I had judged every body by, since the first attempt, were drawn inside out: my
  preview drew the faces turned away from the eye, so a hollow showed as its far inner wall (convex shapes looked
  nearly right, which hid it). Fixed; every earlier still of mine was wrong in this way. The chest stood 4 cm too far
  forward (its inlet twice as deep as a man's): set back, and the collarbone's joint with it. The eyes' joints moved
  12 mm forward and the jaw's hinge to the jaw's heads, where this skull has them.
- **Where bones meet.** Laid one by one, neighbours stood in one another by up to a centimetre at their joints (the
  talus in the heel bone, each toe bone in the next, the ribs' heads in the vertebrae). Now the hollow side of every
  joint is carved by the round side, half a millimetre off it (`skeleton.js`, a list of which bone gives way to
  which): the foot's bones, the wrist's, the vertebrae's small joints, the hip's and shoulder's sockets fit round
  their neighbours. Checked by asking every bone's points how deep they lie in every other bone: standing, nothing
  passes through anything by more than 1.5 mm.
- **The rig** (`skeleton/pose.js`): a joint where each pair of bones really meets, 90 of them: one for every vertebra
  (at the disc under it), the atlas turning on the axis' peg and the skull nodding in the atlas' cups, the jaw's hinge
  through its two heads, the collarbone on the breastbone, the blade on the collarbone, the shoulder, the elbow's
  hinge, the radius turning about the ulna (the forearm's twist), the wrist, each finger joint, the hip, the knee,
  the kneecap riding round the femur's end and sinking into its groove, the ankle, each toe at its own knuckle. A pose carries every
  bone whole by the joint that holds it. Plain moves (a bend of the loins, an arm raised, a knee bent) are shared
  out the way a body shares them: a bend of the loins mostly in its two lowest joints, half of the head's turn in
  the atlas, a third of an arm's raise by the blade; each move is held within a body's range (the knee to 150°, the
  jaw to 30°, the loins' twist to 8°: the ranges clinicians measure by, in a table of their own). Seen walking,
  reaching, with the elbow and the knee bent, the forearm turned (the radius crosses the ulna) and a fist; walking,
  nothing in the whole skeleton passes through anything by more than 2 mm; reaching with one elbow bent 100° and a
  fist, only that elbow does (the ulna's beak 2.8 mm into the humerus).
- **The measures that moved.** The arm's joints: the elbow 3 cm higher and the wrist 1 cm (the humerus had come out
  37 cm, a man's is 34), the shoulder 14 mm further back (so the blade lies on the ribs 35° forwards of the frontal
  plane, its inner border 7.5 cm from the middle); the fingers 15% longer and the palm shorter. As built: humerus
  343 mm, radius 272, ulna 298, collarbone 157, the hand 190 to the middle finger's tip.
  The pelvis: its front upper spines and the pubis' front stand 58 mm before the hip joints (not 68 to 72, which would
  have left the pelvis too shallow from the promontory to the pubis), its back upper spines level with the sacrum's
  second piece.
- **Not there yet.** The bones are not in the game: they are seen in stills drawn outside it (a camera of my own:
  soft shadows, the shade of hollows from the field, a bone's mottling). No fine relief yet (muscle lines, grooves,
  most small holes); the wrist's bones are rounded blocks, the skull's base is simplified. A loft cut flat at its end
  can leave holes in the surface near it (the regions round or cut their ends to avoid it), and the hip bones' and
  the sacrum's surfaces have a few edges shared by more than two triangles (thin places): both to be found. In the
  rig the breastbone follows one vertebra (so a bent chest pulls the ribs' front ends off it), the ribs do not yet
  rise with a breath, the shoulder blade swings on the collarbone's end without yet gliding on the ribs (with the arm
  raised high its lower corner stands off them), and the thumb's part in a fist is rough. The fingers' middle bones
  come out some 4 mm long for their shares of a finger's length.

**The skeleton in the library (2026-11-16), as Samuel asked: "Did you add the skeleton and it's rigged moves to our
lib?"** It was not there (every still till then was drawn outside the game). Now it is.

- **What is there.** A second character beside the Settler: `characters/skeleton.js` (a recipe with its own uid and its
  own measure), built by `characters/kit/bones.js`. It stands on the library's stage in the world's light as every
  surface does (the shading contract of intent 0032: the sun past the clouds and the shadow map, the sky's light as
  much as each hollow leaves of it, a little gloss, more on a tooth), with its card: 90 joints, 262 pieces, its moves,
  its rig drawn over it on request.
- **Grown in the page, off its thread.** The bones' fields are drawn out as surfaces by workers
  (`skeleton/grow.worker.js`, `skeleton/mesh.js`), a bone at a time: first a quick surface (8 mm cells, 450 000
  triangles, no shade in its hollows), then the full one (3.5 mm cells, 999 000 triangles; 5.5 mm on a phone or a
  machine of four threads or fewer, 637 000), each point with the way it faces, what it is and how open it stands. A
  bone with a thin blade (the shoulder blade, the hip bone's wing) is drawn with cells half the blade's thickness: at
  cells nearer its own thickness it came out pricked with holes. The page keeps both surfaces in the browser (under a
  digest of the bones' fields and of the rules they are drawn by: other bones, grown again), so a second visit should
  have them at once; that second visit I have not seen. One thread takes 9 s for the quick surface and 31 s (5.5 mm)
  or 48 s (3.5 mm) for the full one; the workers share it out. The page's own thread carries none of the bones' code:
  the game's start names 18.7 kB more script than main's (7.1 kB compressed: the stage kit); the rig's part (8.4 kB)
  comes when a skeleton is first made, the bones' code (84.6 kB) only in the workers.
- **One surface, carried whole.** All bones are one mesh; each point is held by one joint alone (bone does not
  bend), so the 90 joints move it exactly as the pose says (`skeleton/pose.js`).
- **The moves**, all on the rig's plain moves and within a body's ranges: Stand (the weight going from foot to foot,
  the pelvis tipping over upright legs), Walk, Wave, Reach, and Every joint (each joint in turn through its range:
  the spine bent, leaned and twisted, the head and the jaw, the arms raised and turned, elbows, forearms, wrists and
  fists, a hip and its knee, the other hip turned, its ankle and toes). A move runs from its own beginning.
- **The walk.** A stride's joint angles are a table of their own (`GAIT`: Winter 1991, Perry 1992, as remembered),
  then fitted to this skeleton's legs, because as remembered they did not carry a body: the leading leg reached 5 cm
  less far down than the trailing one at the heel's strike (gait analysis gives the hip against a pelvis tipped some
  10° forwards), so the front foot landed late and flat while the back one rolled onto its toes; and the standing
  sole went back under the body at anything from 0.8 to 2 m/s within one step. Fitted: both feet reach the ground
  together at the step's change, the heel strikes with the toes up 17°, the foot lies flat from a twelfth to a third
  of the stride, rolls over its ball and leaves at six tenths; its sole goes back at 1.1 to 1.5 m/s. The body is
  carried as far as its standing soles go back under it (it has no speed of its own to slide on), the hips let down
  till the lowest sole touches, where the body's sole will be (the heel's bone stands 17 mm above the ground, on a
  pad that is not there yet). On the stage's round the pelvis turns over the standing foot: that leg is turned back
  in its hip and let out by as much as keeps the foot in place, half of the round's turn left to the foot itself.
  Measured in the page frame by frame: 1.25 m/s (1.07 to 1.54 within a stride), the hips rising and falling 23 mm,
  a standing foot's big-toe joint moving 2.5 to 5 cm through its half second on the ground (its roll and its turn on
  the round in that), never more than 8 mm in one frame. Before the fitting and the footing it slid 12 to 14 cm a
  step. Two ways of holding the foot along the way as well (a pace found as it walks; the leg drawn back or
  forwards) ran away or swung, and are not in it.
- **In the rig, changed.** The upper arm's and the thigh's own turn (in and out) are about the bone's own length
  wherever the limb is raised to (before: about the upright, right only for a hanging limb); the pelvis has a move
  of its own (`hips`).
- **In the library, changed.** A character need not wear anything: its card says how it is made, gives its own
  figures and a note while it grows. With a person on the stage the camera comes as near as 45 cm (it stopped at
  3 m), a drag up or down moves what it looks at from the feet to the crown, and the lens is stopped down as it
  comes close; at 3 m and beyond nothing changed. I have not tried this with a hand on the mouse. The registry's
  versions follow a kit that is loaded when first wanted or run in a worker (no other recipe's version moved).
- **With main (intents 0034 and 0035).** The skeleton has its own measure (`bun tools/scale.js`: it stands 1.774 m,
  within a grown person's 1.6 to 1.8; the man it is the skeleton of stands 1.80 m, said as its own choice); the run
  waits for a character that is grown after it is made. The body's tables (`kit/measures.js`) read where the eyes,
  shoulders, hip, knee and ankle stand from the reference body (`world/scale.js`), and keep the rest of the figure's
  shares themselves.
- **Not there yet.** The Settler and the game still stand on the old body: only the Skeleton uses the new rig. Its
  shadow on the ground is faint: the world's shadow map has 6 cm texels at the library's distance, wider than most
  bones. No levels of detail by distance yet. The walk is still a table played forwards: its angles do not answer
  the ground (no leg solved from where its foot stands: round 2); on the round the inner leg is let out up to 13 cm
  and turned up to 16° to keep its foot; while both feet are down each slides a little (the body goes with both).
  Wave turns the body on the spot with the feet sliding under it, as the Settler's does. A first visit grows the
  quick surface for the list's small picture even if the skeleton is never chosen.

**The muscles (2026-11-16), as Samuel asked: "Body should do muscles next."** The second layer, on the bones, in the
library beside the Skeleton; the Settler, the game and `me.html` stay on their body.

- **What is there.** A third character, Muscles (`characters/muscles.js`, its own uid and measure): the skeleton with
  206 muscles on it (103 a side), drawn as 406 strands; the same rig, the same five moves, the same card (its joints,
  its muscles, its faces).
- **The table** (`characters/kit/muscles/table.js`): each muscle from its origin to its insertion through the points
  it passes (over a joint, round a bone), with its volume and how much of it is tendon at each end. The trunk's back
  (trapezius, latissimus with its white fascia, rhomboids, levator scapulae, erector spinae), the shoulder (deltoid,
  the rotator cuff, teres major), the chest and belly (pectoralis major and minor, serratus, rectus abdominis with its
  three tendinous lines, external oblique), the neck (sternocleidomastoid, scalenes, splenius, semispinalis, the hyoid
  muscles), the face (temporalis, masseter, the scalp's frontal and back bellies and its tendon sheet, the rings round
  the eye and the mouth, the zygomatics, the levators and depressors of the lips, mentalis, buccinator, nasalis,
  procerus, corrugator), the arm (biceps' two heads, brachialis, coracobrachialis, triceps' three, anconeus), the
  forearm (pronator teres, the wrist's and fingers' flexors and extensors with a tendon to each finger, brachioradialis,
  the thumb's long muscles), the hand (thenar, adductor of the thumb, the dorsal interossei, hypothenar), the hip
  (the three glutei, tensor fasciae latae, the iliotibial band, psoas, iliacus, pectineus), the thigh (the adductors,
  gracilis, sartorius, the four quadriceps and the patellar ligament, the hamstrings), the leg (gastrocnemius' two
  heads, soleus, tibialis anterior and posterior, the long extensors with a tendon to each toe, flexor hallucis, the
  fibulares) and the foot (extensor digitorum brevis, the abductors of the big and little toes, flexor digitorum
  brevis). Attachments from Gray's Anatomy (1918), as the bones; the volumes of the legs from Handsfield et al. (2014)
  and of the arms and shoulders from Holzbaur et al. (2007), **as remembered**, to be checked against the papers; the
  trunk's and neck's estimated from their cross-sections, the face's from their size on Gray's plates. Each muscle
  names its source. The man trains: every volume a fifth over the studies' means (`BUILD`, said where it is set).
- **Where they are fastened** (`characters/kit/muscles/landmarks.js`): 124 points of the bones (the inion, the
  mastoid, the greater tubercle, the medial epicondyle, the ASIS, the ischial tuberosity, the tibial tuberosity, …),
  each found on the grown bones as the point of its bone furthest in the direction its name says, and written down
  once. As a muscle is drawn, its ends are set onto its bone's surface again (down the bone's own field).
- **Drawn out** (`characters/kit/muscles/build.js`), in the workers that grow the bones, in about 3 s, kept by the
  browser as the bones are: each strand a curve through its points (Catmull-Rom), with a frame at each place along
  it; its section an oval, full on its outside, flatter against what it lies on, as large as its share of the volume
  asks and thinning into its tendons. A broad muscle (the trapezius, the latissimus, the pectoralis, the deltoid, the
  obliques, the gluteus maximus, …) is one sheet woven between its strands, a point of it a share of the way from one
  strand's curve to the next: side by side as strands they read as shingles, not as a sheet. Standing, each strand is
  moved out of the bones and of the layers laid before it (three: on the bone, the middle, under the skin) down the
  slope of what it is in; how open each point stands (the shade of the hollows between muscles) is found then too.
- **Moving with the rig** (`characters/kit/muscles/flesh.js`): every frame each strand's points are carried by their
  joints into a texture (a row a strand) and the shader finds each point of the surface on its curve again; a belly
  swells as its strand shortens and thins as it lengthens, its section scaled by the square root of how much shorter
  its fleshy part stands than at rest (its tendons keep their length), so its volume stays what it was; and the
  strand slides over the bones, carried at its ends and at the points it passes, not glued along its length.
  Measured on the CPU with the same numbers: bending the elbow, the hip and the knee, the swell runs from 0.72 to 1.34.
- **The look**: muscle red, darker where its fibres part, a wet sheen; tendon and aponeurosis a pearl white, glossier;
  fine fibres along each muscle; in the world's light as the bones are.
- **Levels of detail and cost.** The muscles have 153 000 triangles near and 42 000 far (every other ring and every
  other point round, the same points), the far level from 7 m. The bones under them stand at a coarser full
  fineness than the Skeleton's (5.5 mm cells, 637 000 triangles; 8 mm with the shade of their hollows on a phone or
  a machine of four threads or fewer, about 450 000), so the Muscles character has fewer triangles than the Skeleton
  at its own fineness: 790 000 against 999 000, and on a four-thread machine about 600 000 against 637 000. Counted,
  not timed (Propose). The game's start names one more small recipe; the muscles' page code (4.3 kB, 2.1 kB
  compressed) comes when a body with muscles is first made, their table and builder only in the workers (the
  workers' script grew to 130 kB).
- **Only for their own character.** The muscles are laid and drawn when the Muscles character is chosen: not for the
  list's small picture (which shows its bones) and not in the measuring run (which measures its bones, 1.774 m). The
  Settler, the Skeleton, the game and `me.html` draw nothing new.
- **How it was seen.** In stills drawn outside the game (a small rasteriser of my own, the bones' quick surface and
  the muscles' points, standing and posed with the rig's own numbers and the shader's formulas run on the CPU): front,
  side and back, the torso, the hips, the legs, the arms, the head; and bent at the elbow, the hip, the knee and the
  loins. Not in the library: see Propose.
- **On the way.** Broad muscles first drawn as strands side by side read as shingles and loose ribbons, the front of
  the trunk as stripes: woven into sheets since. Lifted off what lay under them only along their own outside, some
  stood 5 to 6 cm off the bone: now moved down the slope of what they are in. The thigh's vasti as thin sheets made
  the thigh 10 cm deep from the side: now strands of their own. The gluteus maximus as a sheet first crumpled into a
  shell, then as four strands read as sausages: a sheet again, its points nearer the bone.
- **Not there yet.** The face's muscles are thin as wires (true to their size, but they read as wires without a skin);
  the masseter and the temporalis are plates with hard edges; the tops of the thighs bulge out past the hips; the
  rectus' tendinous lines run in a V, not across; the semitendinosus kinks a little. A muscle's tendons stretch with
  its belly (only its section keeps the volume, not its tendons' length); its frame and its shade of the hollows are
  the standing one's; muscles of the same layer may pass into one another, and in a deep bend any may pass into a
