# 0033 · A real body, its movement and its clothes

## Idea

> Start with the proper body phase … so the complete body with good movements, and the garment as well, like nice
> natural garment.

> Isn't it possible to push procedurally generated code to full realism?

Samuel wants the person to read as a real person: the whole body, moving well, in natural clothes. Intent 0031 listed
what is not real yet (points 1–25). This intent fixes points 1–22 in the person (`characters/kit/human.js`,
`characters/settler.js`, `characters/kit/avatar.js`): form, skin and face shape, hair, clothes, skeleton and skinning,
movement. Points 23–25 (shadows and the shade where parts touch) are intent 0032's.

**The direction (decided with Samuel):** the person stays generated by our own code: no meshes, scans or captured
motion from anyone else. What is known about real people comes in as numbers, each table small and on its own, each
number cited: body measures by sex and age (public anthropometric surveys), published gait and joint-angle curves,
anatomical proportions. Samuel may still veto the tables; they can be taken out without touching the code that uses
them.

**The aim:** a body, movement and clothes that read as a real person at the library's distance (about 4 m, the whole
body) and in the world. The face in a close-up is the hardest part: its form first (skull, jaw, eyelids, real eyeballs,
a mouth that is an opening), the skin's fine detail later.

**The gates (Samuel's):** 60 fps and 4K, always. The canvas stays at the screen's own pixel ratio; a frame where people
are drawn must not get slower, and a cost hidden behind the governor lowering the inside resolution does not count.
Phones get levels of detail: many people will stand in the world one day, so far ones must be cheap.

## Define

Samuel asked for this work outright, and the brief holds no surprise beyond what he asked, so it is built straight
away, in three rounds, each its own pull request that leaves the person at least as good as before.

**Round 1: the body**

1. **Form from anatomy, not tubes.** The body generated from bones (landmarks), muscles and fat (volumes) and the skin
   over them: collarbones, shoulder blades, chest, buttocks, kneecaps, ankle bones, the muscles of the arms and legs;
   the limbs flow into the trunk as one surface, nothing passing through anything.
2. **Hands and feet.** Five fingers with knuckles and nails; feet with toes, inside the shoes.
3. **The head.** Skull, jaw, chin, cheekbones, brow; eyelids; eyeballs with iris, pupil and cornea; a mouth that is an
   opening; ears and a nose with their form.
4. **Variety by the avatar's numbers:** age, sex and build join the avatar (`characters/kit/avatar.js`, its copy in
   `API/src/avatar.js`, `API/test/api.test.js`); avatars kept before still load.
5. **The skeleton:** fingers, toes, jaw, eyes and eyelids, a spine of several bones, twist bones; four bones per point
   of the skin, skinned so that elbows, knees and shoulders keep their volume (the choice of method, and why, in Build).
6. **Levels of detail**, from a close-up to a dot, the far ones cheap enough for many people.
7. The three moves (walk, idle, wave) and the clothes (shirt, trousers, belt, shoes, hair) still there, at least as
   good as before; the next rounds replace them.

**Round 2: movement**

8. Quaternion blending; idle, walk, jog and run blended by speed, the stride matched to the speed so the feet never
   slide; the feet set on slopes and steps (IK); starting, stopping, turning by stepping; the continuous speed of the
   rig and the joystick (intent 0030) wherever a person walks.
9. Life: breathing in the chest, the weight shifting, blinking, eyes and head that look at things (the camera, in the
   library); hair and flesh that move by themselves.

**Round 3: garments**

10. Shirt, trousers, belt and shoes as their own surfaces over the body: thickness, seams, a collar that stands, cuffs
    and hems; drape and folds that follow the pose; the world's own baked cloth (linen, denim, leather).
11. Hair as strands or cards, with volume and a silhouette.

**Checks** (every round)

| # | Check | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The person from the front, side and back, at rest and mid-move | stills in the library (`library.html?character=944dc52a-5094-480c-9396-5ceedbbf03b1`) |
| 2 | The face and the hands, close | stills |
| 3 | Variety | stills of people made from different avatars (age, sex, build) |
| 4 | Frame time where people are drawn | main against the branch, several runs each, alternating: the frame's median and 95th percentile, draws, the governor's inside ratio; in the library with the Settler, in `me.html`, in the world. And the person alone: the same view drawn with only the person and with nothing, timed on the GPU (`?gputime`) |
| 5 | Both sites build | `vite build` in `TheGame/` and `OS/` |
| 6 | The API keeps the same rules as the game | `cd API && bun test`, when the avatar changes |
| 7 | Nothing else in the picture changes | stills of the world on main and on the branch, compared pixel by pixel |

**Out of scope**

- Shadows on and from the person, and the shade where parts touch: intent 0032.
- The skin's fine detail (pores, freckles, light through the skin's thickness) beyond what is there now.

## Build

**Round 1, first attempt (2026-11-16), turned down.** A body as a field of about 280 blended solids (eggs and rods
for bones, muscles and fat, set on a skeleton of 68 bones), drawn out as one surface region by region (the face, the
hands and the head finer than the body, joined where they meet), with the clothes and the hair as shells over it
(`characters/kit/field.js`, `body.js`, `measures.js`; nothing in the game uses them yet). Seen only in previews drawn
outside the game. Samuel: "This looks absolute catastrophic." What was wrong, as seen:

- Every solid showed as its own lump: muscles as eggs stuck on rods, nothing running from bone to bone and thinning
  into tendon, no layer of skin pulling them into one surface.
- The trunk a barrel (too deep and wide at the waist and hips, no taper), the chest and breasts eggs stuck on, the
  shoulder blades plates, the buttocks two balls; narrow sloping shoulders, thin even arms meeting the trunk as
  tubes, a thick neck; small lumpy hands, blobs for feet.
- The face: the eye sockets holes with the eyeballs bulging out of them, a nose of a rod and a ball, nostrils
  punched into the front of the face, lips like a sausage, a flat face with a receding chin; ears like crumpled blobs.
- The hair a helmet; the body through the shirt and trousers.
- The cuts at the wrists gave several rims instead of one; the middle level held 61 000 points (twice what it should)
  and took 2.6 s to build.

**Round 1 again, as Samuel asked:** "let's go for naked first without hair and muscular body (clothes come later)".
The skeleton and its proportions first, checked from the front and the side; muscles as shapes from their origin to
their insertion, thinning into tendons; a skin over them; the face from the skull up; each part looked at alone
before it is joined. A muscular man first, smooth at the groin as an anatomy figure is; women and other builds after.

**Round 1, second attempt (2026-11-16), set aside.** A muscular man of reference (1.80 m) with the muscles laid as
strands from their origin to their insertion, each end carried by its own bone (`characters/kit/anatomy.js`; the strand
and a profiled tube added to `field.js`). Seen in previews only: the muscles floated apart with gaps between them, and the
broad flat ones (the back's) stood off the ribs as wings in the arms-out pose. Muscles cannot be laid well without the
bones they lie on.

**Round 1, as Samuel then asked:** "do the bones completely correct first, and then the anatomically correct muscles
and fat afterwards, each part, bones, fat and muscles, correctly coloured … as choreographed basis." In layers, each
on the one below, each looked at alone before the next:

1. The bones, bone-coloured: each in its own shape and size from the measures (skull and jaw, the spine's vertebrae,
   ribs and breastbone, collarbones and shoulder blades, the pelvis, the long bones, kneecaps, the hands' and feet's
   bones), meeting at their real joints: the skeleton the person moves by.
2. The muscles, red: each from its origin on a bone to its insertion on another, at its size.
3. The fat, yellow: where it lies, more or less by build, sex and age.
4. The skin over all of it.

**The bones (2026-11-16), as Samuel asked: "the skeleton now to be anatomically absolut correct and photorealistic -
feel free to research skeletons reference images as well"; "once you got all the bones correct you can try to rig it
correctly"; "ensure the skull is correct".**

- **What it is built from.** Each bone is now a field of its own (never blended with its neighbour), written in one
  file a region (`characters/kit/skeleton/`: skull, spine, chest, pelvis, arm, hand, leg, foot) with the rig and the
  kit they share; a point is written once, for the left side of the man of reference, and mirrored. Two solids were
  added for real bone shapes (`field.js`): a loft (a shaft with its section given along it: three-sided where the
  bone is, its end closed as a dome where it is a head or a crown) and a plate (a blade drawn by its outline, bent,
  thick at its rim).
- **What it is measured against.** The plates of Gray's Anatomy (1918, public domain, from Wikimedia Commons), about
  fifty of them, kept outside the repository: they are looked at, and for the skull read on a grid; nothing of them
  is traced into a mesh. The numbers they gave are written where they are used, with the figure they came from.
- **The skull**, from fig. 188 (side) and 190 (front): the braincase a loft whose breadth, profile and plan are given
  level by level, its crown a true dome; the face one shaped mass whose front passes through the rims of the eye
  sockets, with the sockets (four-sided funnels, inner walls straight back, outer slanting in), the nose's opening
  and cavity and the palate cut into it; the bony nose, the cheekbones' arches, the mastoids, the styloids, the
  condyles; the lower jaw as a blade with its head, coronoid and notch on a body round the arch; thirty-two teeth each
  its own piece, standing where the ones before them end; the hyoid; the seams (sutures) as lines for whoever draws
  it. Laid over the two plates at their own scale and tilt, its outline, sockets, nose, row of teeth and jaw fall on
  theirs within a few millimetres; seen from the front its jaw is wider than the plate's at the angles (the plate is
  drawn in perspective, the check is not).
- **The other regions** were each rebuilt by a helper session of mine working in its own file against its own
  plates, all at once: the leg and foot (femur, kneecap, tibia, fibula, seven ankle bones, metatarsals, toes), the
  spine and chest (twenty-four vertebrae each in its region's shape with an open canal, the discs, twelve pairs of
  ribs, their cartilages, the breastbone), the pelvis and the arm and hand.
- **Found on the way.** The pictures I had judged every body by, since the first attempt, were drawn inside out: my
  preview drew the faces turned away from the eye, so a hollow showed as its far inner wall (convex shapes looked
  nearly right, which hid it). Fixed; every earlier still of mine was wrong in this way. The chest stood 4 cm too far
  forward (its inlet twice as deep as a man's): set back, and the collarbone's joint with it. The eyes' joints moved
  12 mm forward and the jaw's hinge to the jaw's heads, where this skull has them.
- **Where bones meet.** Laid one by one, neighbours stood in one another by up to a centimetre at their joints (the
  talus in the heel bone, each toe bone in the next, the ribs' heads in the vertebrae). Now the hollow side of every
  joint is carved by the round side, half a millimetre off it (`skeleton.js`, a list of which bone gives way to
  which): the foot's bones, the wrist's, the vertebrae's small joints, the hip's and shoulder's sockets fit round
  their neighbours. Checked by asking every bone's points how deep they lie in every other bone: standing, nothing
  passes through anything by more than 1.5 mm.
- **The rig** (`skeleton/pose.js`): a joint where each pair of bones really meets, 90 of them: one for every vertebra
  (at the disc under it), the atlas turning on the axis' peg and the skull nodding in the atlas' cups, the jaw's hinge
  through its two heads, the collarbone on the breastbone, the blade on the collarbone, the shoulder, the elbow's
  hinge, the radius turning about the ulna (the forearm's twist), the wrist, each finger joint, the hip, the knee,
  the kneecap riding round the femur's end and sinking into its groove, the ankle, each toe at its own knuckle. A pose carries every
  bone whole by the joint that holds it. Plain moves (a bend of the loins, an arm raised, a knee bent) are shared
  out the way a body shares them: a bend of the loins mostly in its two lowest joints, half of the head's turn in
  the atlas, a third of an arm's raise by the blade; each move is held within a body's range (the knee to 150°, the
  jaw to 30°, the loins' twist to 8°: the ranges clinicians measure by, in a table of their own). Seen walking,
  reaching, with the elbow and the knee bent, the forearm turned (the radius crosses the ulna) and a fist; walking,
  nothing in the legs, the spine or the skull passes through anything by more than 2 mm.
- **The measures that moved.** The arm's joints: the elbow 3 cm higher and the wrist 1 cm (the humerus had come out
  37 cm, a man's is 34), the shoulder 14 mm further back (so the blade lies on the ribs at its own angle); the
  fingers 15% longer and the palm shorter (the hand is 19 cm to the middle finger's tip).
  The pelvis: its front upper spines and the pubis' front stand 58 mm before the hip joints (not 68 to 72, which would
  have left the pelvis too shallow from the promontory to the pubis), its back upper spines level with the sacrum's
  second piece.
- **Not there yet.** The bones are not in the game: they are seen in stills drawn outside it (a camera of my own:
  soft shadows, the shade of hollows from the field, a bone's mottling). No fine relief yet (muscle lines, grooves,
  most small holes); the wrist's bones are rounded blocks, the skull's base is simplified. A loft cut flat at its end
  can leave holes in the surface near it (the regions round or cut their ends to avoid it), and the hip bones' and
  the sacrum's surfaces have a few edges shared by more than two triangles (thin places): both to be found. In the
  rig the breastbone follows one vertebra (so a bent chest pulls the ribs' front ends off it), the ribs do not yet
  rise with a breath, and the thumb's part in a fist is rough.
