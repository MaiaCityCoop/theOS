# Keep the head, borrow real skin

**The most realistic face a browser can afford is the industry's own recipe, cut down to what is lawful and to what the project already owns: keep the project's parametric head as the geometry that ships, light it like a portrait and give it real eyes first, put texture coordinates on its cage, and then borrow the two things that numbers alone have never been shown to supply, measured skin relief and sculpted expression shapes.** The relief can come from the Lee Perry-Smith head scan, which is licensed CC BY 3.0 and may ship in a commercial web game with a credit; the shapes from a permissively licensed set such as ICT-FaceKit (MIT) or Meta's MHR (Apache-2.0); colour, scattering, eyes, teeth, brows, lashes and stubble come from published optics and dermatology, in the project's own code. A learned or Gaussian-splat head is the wrong road for this game: it cannot be lit by the game's sun without a light-stage capture of a real person, it is soft at close range, it has no animated path in three.js r187, and every leading method is closed to commercial use. **No female counterpart to the scan could be verified**: every scan of a woman that was found is restricted to non-commercial use, so the female variant is the same parametric head with female numbers and the same borrowed fine relief, with a commissioned or purchased capture as the later upgrade. Adopting the scanned man's whole face is lawful as to copyright but not recommended, because it puts a real man's likeness on a nude game body, has no equal for a woman, and gives an avatar builder nothing to adjust. That builder can sit on top, and its reliable form is known: landmarks found on the device and an optimiser over the head's own numbers for shape, colour statistics for tone, and a vision-language model as a bounded critic of what has no landmark. The order of work follows the perception research, in which light, eyes and uneven skin decide whether a face is taken for a photograph and the weakest feature sets the verdict. A browser can carry all of it (a block-compressed map set costs 13 to 21 MiB at 2K and 53 to 85 MiB at 4K), but **no source gives a browser timing for any part**, so a measuring harness and a control render of the scan in the project's own renderer come before everything else. Most of what follows rests on search extracts, not on sources read in full; the licence texts and the three.js code are the exceptions.

**How to read the evidence.** The research environment could open GitHub, the npm registry and PyPI, and almost nothing else. Blocked, among others: arxiv.org, dl.acm.org, the Eurographics library, PubMed and PMC, vgl.ict.usc.edu, iryoku.com, gdcvault.com, every Epic and Unity site, threejs.org and its forum, webkit.org, polyhaven.com, sketchfab.com, flame.is.tue.mpg.de, creativecommons.org, every scan vendor and wikipedia.org; the shared search budget also ran out before several planned checks. Each claim below therefore carries a mark. *Read*: the source itself was opened, which here means a file on GitHub or in a package: licence files, READMEs, the three.js r187 code, the WebGPU specification, Unity's HDRP documentation, Creative Commons' legal code in its own repository. *Read in part*: the file was opened and only the relevant lines were read. *Measured*: counted on a downloaded file. *Extract*: known only through the search tool's digest of the page, wording and numbers unchecked. *Secondary*: a press article, blog or later paper describing the primary, itself seen only as an extract. *Recalled*: from a researcher's memory, unverified. *Computed*: arithmetic on cited numbers. *Inference*: the researchers' or this report's reasoning. *First-hand*: read in the project's own files for this report. Three warnings follow from that. **Everything in the perception section is an extract or secondary; no paper there was read.** Unreal's shader constants were read from a copy of Epic's source of unknown engine version that could not be re-fetched. And every licence verdict reports what a text says and is not legal advice. The six notes are beside this report: [what makes a face read real](../research_notes/Photoreal%20browser%20face%20first%20principles/what_makes_a_face_read_real.md), [industry pipeline](../research_notes/Photoreal%20browser%20face%20first%20principles/industry_face_pipeline.md), [browser feasibility](../research_notes/Photoreal%20browser%20face%20first%20principles/browser_feasibility_and_budgets.md), [scan-free methods](../research_notes/Photoreal%20browser%20face%20first%20principles/scan_free_first_principles_methods.md), [neural and Gaussian heads](../research_notes/Photoreal%20browser%20face%20first%20principles/neural_and_gaussian_head_avatars.md), [open assets and licences](../research_notes/Photoreal%20browser%20face%20first%20principles/open_head_assets_and_licences.md).

## Light, eyes and uneven skin decide, and the weakest feature sets the verdict

### The two measured tells of a computer-made face are both on the head today

One study tested directly what separates a virtual face from a photograph and then proved it by doing the damage to real photographs. Vaitonytė and colleagues found by image analysis that virtual faces "had smoother skin and a reduced number of corneal reflections"; when they smoothed the skin and removed the corneal reflections in photographs of real people, speeded real-or-not decisions shifted, and a third experiment "confirmed the causal role of both features" ([DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/341dc5b8369044e98604654003db1446), extract; sample and effect sizes not retrieved). Older work agrees about the eyes: on doll-to-human morphs "the eyes are disproportionately informative" for judging a face alive ([Looser and Wheatley 2010](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/celooser/files/looserwheatleypsychsci.pdf), extract), and in Fan's experiments the eyes were the face part with the strongest influence on realism, with shading counting for more than colour ([A*STAR repository](https://oar.a-star.edu.sg/communities-collections/articles/13118), extract). One reported study dissents and puts skin level with the eyes ([ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/06/140610152745.htm), secondary), so "eyes and skin together" is the safe reading. The project's head has both tells at once, an even surface and, under flat ambient light, nothing distinct for a cornea to reflect (the note's inference; the state of the head is first-hand from intent 0040).

### Unequal realism is what reads as eerie, so features must rise together

What produces unease is not low realism but realism that differs between parts. MacDorman and colleagues found that "a mismatch in the size and texture of the eyes and face was especially prone to make a character eerie" and that wrong proportions disturbed more on photoreal faces ([IU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/e8fb6458-ddad-4067-b877-1207d720ba0c), extract); a study of **548 people** found that lowering the consistency of realism between feature groups made faces eerier and colder ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26435049/), extract). Zell and colleagues found that "shape is the dominant factor when rating realism" and that realistic materials do not raise the realism of stylised shapes ([Bielefeld record](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/record/2772349), extract). A meta-analysis of 72 studies puts the uncanny-valley effect at Hedges' g = 1.01, largest for distorted faces at 1.46 ([Cardiff record](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/148120/), extract), and a 2026 short paper finds that localised artefacts, which global image statistics miss, drive human judgements of generated faces ([Eurographics library](https://diglib.eg.org/items/1f506762-ef14-4f66-b9eb-115502d6bbc0), extract). The working rule that follows is the strongest single result for planning: **at each step repair the worst-looking feature, and never push one feature far ahead of the rest**. Photoreal skin under drawn eyebrows is the mismatch case the experiments describe.

### Light gates both tells, though nobody has measured its share

Corneal reflections are by definition images of the light sources ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s41235-022-00445-y), extract), and skin relief shows only where light rakes it, so flat ambient light removes the two strongest measured cues whatever the asset. That is the argument for fixing light first, and it is an argument, not a measurement: the one controlled experiment found that portrait lighting patterns alone changed eeriness ratings of a photoreal virtual human, with frontal, Paramount and Rembrandt lighting all keeping eeriness low ([Wisessing and McDonnell 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3675231.3675245), extract), and no source quantifies how much of realism is light and how much asset. Practitioners assert the camera side flatly. Activision's 2013 session abstract says efforts toward photoreal characters are futile without subsurface scattering, realistic eye shading, depth of field, film grain, plausible bloom, film-like tone mapping and accurate anti-aliasing ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1018270/Next-Generation-Character), extract), and Naughty Dog rebuilt its eyes after finding they looked right "only in certain lighting scenarios and camera angles", starting from photographs of real eyes under several lights ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/the-last-of-us-2-is-so-detailed-naughty-dog-had-to-overhaul-their-rendering-to-get-characters-eyes-perfect), secondary). No controlled study of image-based lighting, a filmic curve, depth of field or grain on face realism was found; for those the evidence is assertion only.

### Fine detail works through the highlight, and scattering is already good enough

Practitioners place skin realism in the highlight. Measured skin microstructure at 10 micrometres "allows for greater realism in facial renderings, including more accurate reproduction of skin's specular reflection effects" ([Graham et al. 2013](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v32i2pp335-344), extract), and Unity calls facial peach fuzz "subtle but important" for the shading of skin ([Unity](https://unity.com/blog/news/introducing-enemies-the-latest-evolution-in-high-fidelity-digital-humans-from-unity), extract). The arithmetic says why pores need not be seen as dots: a face 150 mm wide across 800 pixels is about 0.2 mm a pixel, at or above the size of a pore, so even in a portrait pores act by breaking up the highlight (computed, with those assumptions). Resolution had only a small effect on telling computer-made portraits from photographs ([Mader, Banks and Farid 2017](https://farid.berkeley.edu/downloads/publications/perception17.pdf), extract). On scattering, a forced-choice test found a cheap screen-space approximation to have "as high visual realism as a previously developed physically-based shader", independent of distance and light position ([Jimenez et al. 2009](http://giga.cps.unizar.es/~diegog/ficheros/pdf_papers/TAP_Jimenez_LR.pdf), extract). The inference is that more work on the scattering model yields little next to surface detail and colour variation. Every flagship face in the sources starts from a scan of a real person, so shape, asymmetry and texture arrive for free, and none of the sources describes reaching a photograph with a procedural, mirror-symmetric head; that absence is evidence about practice, not proof of impossibility.

### A whole-face smile is a cheek and lid deformation, with published sizes

A smile of the whole face is the lip-corner pull together with the cheek raise: the cheek mass moves up and inward, pushing skin toward the eye and narrowing it from below, with lines at the outer corner ([Waller et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2826128/), extract). The best causal evidence that the outer-eye deformation itself changes how a still smile reads is a study of the same patients before and after botulinum toxin paralysed that muscle: smiles with it active were rated "more felt, spontaneous, intense, and happier" ([Etcoff et al. 2021](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10120320/1/Etcoff%20et%20al%282021%29.pdf), extract). Whether the marker signals felt emotion is disputed, since controlling for smile intensity "substantially reduced" the link ([Girard et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7193529), extract); for a render the point is perceptual, a broad smile over relaxed, wide eyes being a combination real faces rarely make. Published magnitudes give an acceptance test for the smile baked into the rest shape: the mouth corner travels **9.2 to 16.0 mm** across four smile levels ([PubMed 36171175](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36171175/), extract). Reported render failures are a posed mouth over unchanged cheeks and lids, exaggerated mouth movement, which raised uncanniness for happy characters ([Springer chapter](https://www.springerprofessional.de/effect-of-emotion-and-articulation-of-speech-on-the-uncanny-vall/3759532), extract), poor edge flow at the mouth corners ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/drip-fit-for-a-king-realistic-game-ready-character-made-with-ue5-5), secondary), and teeth that slide rigidly behind the lips ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/10/epic-shows-off-unreals-nearly-real-metahuman-3d-character-creator), secondary).

### Symmetry and hair: thin evidence, moderate rank

Real faces deviate from their mirror image by about 0.6 to 0.8 mm over the whole surface in healthy adults, with single landmarks differing by up to about 3 mm before clinicians call it visible ([PubMed 38637251](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38637251); [Huang et al. 2013](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ocr.12010), extracts; the mapping of each figure to its paper is uncertain). No study compares a symmetric rendered face with an asymmetric version of itself on a real-or-rendered judgement, and one study of virtual characters reports mixed results ([EPFL](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/227463/files/postprint_evaluating-sensitivity-virtual-characters-facial-asymmetry.pdf), extract). The evidence supports "an over-regular surface reads as artificial" far better than "symmetry is a realism killer". For hair no realism study was found at all; removing eyebrows from familiar faces hurt recognition more than removing the eyes ([Sadr et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12729380/), extract), and the case for real brows and lashes otherwise rests on the consistency rule.

### The ranking

The table is the researchers' synthesis for a near-static portrait judged against a warm, softly lit photograph. **It is not a published result**: no study ranks these factors against each other, sample and effect sizes are missing for most of the studies, and reasonable readers could swap the first two rows.

| Rank | Factor | The head today (first-hand) | Strength of evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Light and photographic image formation: a soft directional key over an environment, real shadows, exposure and white balance, a filmic curve, then depth of field, grain and anti-aliasing | Flat, cool ambient light | Moderate for lighting (one 2024 experiment, shading over colour); thin for curve, depth of field and grain (assertion only). Ranked first because it gates rows 2 and 3, not because an experiment measured it largest |
| 2 | Eyes and eye region: a clear corneal reflection, wet tear line, lid contact shadow, lash shadows, a correct limbus, a shaded sclera | Separate eye pieces behind lids | Strong (several experiments; the only practitioner "makes or breaks"); one dissent puts skin level |
| 3 | Uneven skin surface: highlight broken by relief, pores through the highlight, fine lines, colour varied by blood and pigment | Smooth and even; pores and folds drawn in the shader | Strong that over-smooth reads artificial; moderate for the remedies (practitioners) |
| 4 | Plausible shape, age cues and equal realism across features | Clean, authored, close to its own mirror | Strong that shape dominates when stylised and that inconsistency is eerie; thin that symmetry itself hurts |
| 5 | A coherent whole-face smile | One smile baked into the rest shape | Strong for perceived genuineness; thin for real-or-rendered judgements |
| 6 | Hair of every kind: brows, lashes, scalp, stubble, vellus | Brows drawn, no hair | Thin: no realism study found; could deserve a higher rank |
| 7 | Further scattering work, light through thin parts | A pre-integrated model is present | Moderate that scattering is necessary and that more of it is low-yield |
| 8 | Resolved micro-detail: pores as geometry, iris caustics | Not applicable yet | Thin; matters only in tight close-up |
| 9 | Motion: blinks, gaze, micro-movement | None; no blendshapes | Moderate, for a live face only ([McDonnell et al. as summarised](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.17803), secondary) |

## Studios scan one person and spend the result on maps and shapes, not triangles

### The scan is enormous; what ships is small

A hero head begins as a polarised light-stage or cross-polarised photogrammetry capture of a neutral face and a set of expressions. Activision and USC's Digital Ira scanned its actor in **30 expressions at 0.1 mm geometry with 4K diffuse and specular maps for each**, kept 8 of them, and ran the result as bone animation on a mesh of about 4,000 polygons at 180 frames a second on a 2012 graphics card ([Digital Ira poster](https://vgl.ict.usc.edu/Research/DigitalIra/digitalIraPoster.pdf), extract; [Jimenez's blog](https://www.iryoku.com/?p=1639), secondary). Epic's guide figures for a MetaHuman head are 24,000 vertices at the finest level falling to 130 at the coarsest ([Epic, LOD specifications](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/metahuman/platform-support-and-lod-specifications-for-metahumans), extract). The project's head, at 204,776 triangles near on 106,304 points (first-hand), is already several times denser than either. The gap to the industry is therefore not in the mesh. The scan's value at run time lies in its maps and its expression deltas.

The maps are where resolution is spent, and unevenly. Epic's 2017 and 2018 digital humans "all use 4K texture resolution produced from facial scans", with diffuse, roughness, specular, scatter and normal maps and several normal maps blended for medium and micro detail ([Unreal 4.26 documentation](https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.26/Resources/Showcases/DigitalHumans), extract); MetaHuman's top tier bakes to 8K, its mobile ceiling is 2048, and its expression-driven maps are stored as deltas at only "512x512 by default for basecolor and 1K by default for normal" ([Epic, materials and textures](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-materials-and-textures), extract). Scans do not reach pore scale by themselves: USC compares a scanned 4K displacement map with a synthesised 16K one built from patches measured at 10 micrometres ([USC ICT](https://vgl.ict.usc.edu/Research/Microgeometry/), extract), Epic blends further normal maps over the scan's for micro detail, and Unity's team found that 4D processing smoothed pore detail away and had to add animated micro detail back ([Unity](https://unity.com/kr/blog/technology/making-of-the-heretic-digital-human-character-gawain), extract). The pattern a small project can copy is one static high-frequency layer, a tiling micro layer, and low-resolution expression deltas.

### Expression is a hybrid, and its fine layer is cheap

Shipped rigs mix joints with corrective shapes and scale them down by distance. A MetaHuman head carries 713 joints and **669 blendshapes that exist only at the finest level**, with animated maps at the two finest ([Epic, LOD specifications](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/metahuman/platform-support-and-lod-specifications-for-metahumans), extract); Ryse ran 260 facial joints with 230 corrective shapes ([fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=49486), secondary); Epic's "Meet Mike" used about 750 shapes on about 440,000 triangles, three quarters of them hair ([fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=195972), secondary). Apple's ARKit blend-shape coefficients, which third-party references count as 52, are the common interface on the capture side ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arfaceanchor/blendshapelocation), extract). The fine layer costs little. Animated wrinkle maps began as two extra normal maps, one squashed and one stretched, blended by masks and weights ([Oat 2007](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Oat%20-%20Wrinkles%20-%20Course%20notes%20(SIGGRAPH%202007).pdf), extract); a later variant needs 96 KB ([Jimenez et al.](https://www.iryoku.com/wrinkles), extract); Unity applies its wrinkle maps by measured skin tension ([Unity Digital Human package](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/com.unity.demoteam.digital-human), read) and says of its later demo that tension-driven blood flow and wrinkle maps remove the need for a facial rig for fine detail ([Unity, Enemies](https://unity.com/demos/enemies), secondary); and expression-dependent blood colour from melanin and haemoglobin maps adds "negligible overhead" ([Jimenez et al. 2010](https://www.iryoku.com/skincolor/), extract).

### The shading stack is light; hair and correctives are the heavy parts

Three diffuse models ship. Pre-integrated scattering is a lookup inside the lighting function with no blur pass ([Penner 2011](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/penner-preintegrated-skin-rendering-siggraph-2011-advances-in-realtime-rendering-course/13966747), extract). Separable screen-space scattering is two passes of 11, 17 or 25 taps ([SeparableSSS.h](https://github.com/iryoku/separable-sss/blob/master/SeparableSSS.h), read) and cost **0.39 ms on a PlayStation 4 at 1080p** in portrait shots ([Activision technical report](https://www.activision.com/cdn/research/Separable_Subsurface_Scattering_Expanded_Technical_Report_NEW.pdf), extract). Burley's normalised diffusion is sampled in screen space with 20, 40 or 80 samples in Unity ([HDRP documentation](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition/Documentation~/skin-and-diffusive-surfaces-subsurface-scattering.md), read) and up to 64 in Unreal ([SubsurfaceBurleyNormalized.ush](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/release/Engine/Shaders/Private/SubsurfaceBurleyNormalized.ush), read from an unverified copy), where it "requires Temporal Anti-Aliasing to display properly" ([Unreal documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/subsurface-profile-shading-model-in-unreal-engine), extract). All pair it with two highlight lobes; Unreal's defaults are roughness multipliers of 0.75 and 1.30 mixed at 0.85 ([SubsurfaceProfile.h](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/release/Engine/Source/Runtime/Engine/Classes/Engine/SubsurfaceProfile.h), read from an unverified copy). The project's shader already has the cheapest of the three diffuse models and both lobes. None of these figures is for a browser or a phone.

The standard eye is a wet cornea over iris and sclera with refraction, a dark limbus, a caustic on the iris and scattering in the sclera; Unity grades three eye models by cost and names the caustic one "the most resource intensive" ([HDRP eye material](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition/Documentation~/snippets/shader-properties/surface-options/eye-material-type.md), read). The lid line gets its own treatment, in Unity a "custom pass for cross-material normal buffer blur (tearline)" and "custom marker-driven occlusion for eyes and teeth" ([Unity Digital Human package](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/com.unity.demoteam.digital-human), read). Hair is the largest geometry cost at every tier: 50,000 strands for a MetaHuman hairstyle and 10,000 for facial hair at the finest level, cards below ([Epic, LOD specifications](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/metahuman/platform-support-and-lod-specifications-for-metahumans), extract). Showcases light a head as a portrait, an environment map on a sky dome with lights that can be turned about the subject ([Epic, custom lighting](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/working-with-additional-actors-for-custom-metahuman-lighting-scenarios-in-unreal-engine), extract), and in both engines temporal anti-aliasing is load-bearing, since it hides the sampled scattering's noise as well as the hair's (the note's inference from the documentation above).

| Budget | Mesh | Expression | Maps | Speed | How known |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MetaHuman head, current documentation | 24,000 vertices finest, 130 coarsest | 713 joints, 669 blendshapes (finest level only) | Up to 8192 on desktop, 2048 on phones; expression deltas 512 and 1K | Not stated | Extract |
| Epic "Meet Mike", 2017, desktop VR | About 440,000 triangles, 75 % hair | About 750 blendshapes | 4K from scans | 90 frames a second in VR | Secondary |
| Digital Ira, 2013 | About 4,000 polygons, bone-driven | 8 of 30 scanned expressions | 4K per expression at capture | 180 frames a second on a GeForce GTX 680 | Extract and secondary |
| Ryse hero, 2013, console | 85,000 polygons | 260 facial joints, 230 correctives | 512 texels a metre | Rendered at 1600 by 900 | Secondary |

No studio in the sources ranks what contributes most; the best evidence is what Epic switches off first: blendshapes after the finest level, strands after the second, animated maps from the third, and subsurface scattering at the Medium quality setting ([Epic, materials and textures](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-materials-and-textures), extract). Read as costs, the cheap adoptions are the second highlight lobe, colour deltas for blood, wrinkle normal deltas, pre-integrated scattering, a simple iris caustic with limbal darkening, and lid-line occlusion without extra meshes; the expensive ones are strand hair, hundreds of correctives and a sampled diffusion pass (the note's reading).

## A browser can carry the stack, and nobody has published what it costs

### The three.js world adopted the delivery, and its realistic head is this same scan

As of 18 November 2026 no open, locally rendered WebGPU head was found that combines diffusion-profile skin, refractive eyes and hair. The pieces exist apart. An open pull request renders the Lee Perry-Smith head with a Burley screen-space diffusion pass, 32 taps by default, and is not merged into r187 ([three.js PR 33761](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/pull/33761), read). A WebGPU project draws about 108,000 hair strands on the same scan ([hair-fur-sim](https://github.com/evanlask/hair-fur-sim/blob/main/README.md), read). An open pipeline brings MetaHumans into three.js with 51 ARKit shapes, textures capped at 1,024 pixels, hair as cards and plain PBR skin without scattering, about 40 MB a character, "Tested in Safari on iPhone X" ([metahuman-to-glb](https://github.com/creategamecharacters/metahuman-to-glb/blob/main/README.md), read). A procedural WebGPU portrait describes itself as "not a scan or a claim of photorealism" ([DERMIS](https://github.com/SamG-Coder/dermis-cuda/blob/main/README.md), read). r187 has no eye, hair or wrinkle example among its 621 ([files.json](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/examples/files.json), read), and Ready Player Me, the usual source of browser avatars, shut its services on 31 January 2026 ([Genies](https://genies.com/blog/ready-player-me-shutdown), extract from a competitor's blog). A head that does scattering, two lobes, wrinkle maps, a refractive eye and real hairs in one forward pass would lead every open example found, on technique if not yet on asset.

### Maps: 2K for phones, 4K for desktops, always block-compressed

Any JPEG, PNG or WebP becomes RGBA8 once uploaded. Khronos's own worked example grows from 13 MB of files to 96 MB of GPU memory, against 21 MB when the same textures are shipped as KTX2 ([KTX artist guide](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/3D-Formats-Guidelines/blob/main/KTXArtistGuide.md), read). r187's `KTX2Loader` transcodes in up to four web workers with a 527 KB WebAssembly transcoder and sends UASTC files to ASTC 4×4 or BC7 at 8 bits a pixel ([KTX2Loader.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/examples/jsm/loaders/KTX2Loader.js), read). The memory of a four-map face set with mip chains follows by arithmetic.

| How a four-map set (colour, normal, roughness, displacement) is stored | 2K | 4K | 8K |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Four RGBA8 textures, which is what four image files become | 85 MiB | 341 MiB | 1,365 MiB |
| All four at 8 bits a pixel (UASTC to ASTC 4×4 or BC7) | 21 MiB | 85 MiB | 341 MiB |
| Colour and normal at 8 bits, roughness and displacement at 4 | 16 MiB | 64 MiB | 256 MiB |
| As above with colour at 4 bits on phones | 13 MiB | 53 MiB | 213 MiB |

These are computed from the formats' bit rates. Against the only published budgets, VRChat's 10 to 40 MB of textures for a mobile avatar ([VRChat creator docs](https://github.com/vrchat-community/creator-docs/blob/main/Docs/docs/avatars/avatar-performance-ranking-system.md), read) and the 1K cap of the MetaHuman web pipeline, a phone face fits at 2K only when block-compressed and 4K is a desktop or flagship setting. No vendor publishes a memory limit for a browser tab; WebKit bug reports describe iOS killing the page when a process passes its limit ([WebKit bug 300782](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=300782), extract), so the tolerance has to be found on the target phones. Displacement need not be a run-time texture at all: its coarse part can be applied to vertices at build and the rest folded into the normal map. No source that could be opened gives transcode times, and four 4K maps are about 5.6 million blocks (computed).

### Maps made from code at load time are practical, with three traps

r187 can bake a procedural shader into texture space on the GPU: its new `LightMapper` draws a mesh with its texture coordinates as screen positions and dilates the borders ([LightMapper.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/examples/jsm/lighting/LightMapper.js), read), and a 4K bake is the pixel count of about eight 1080p frames (computed). The traps are that render targets default to RGBA half float, 171 MiB for one 4K map with mips unless the type is set to 8-bit (computed from the read source), that compute baking into a storage texture has no WebGL 2 fallback, and that the run-time unwrapper packs 23 to 41 % looser than a dedicated tool and has no timing above 53,400 triangles ([three.js PR 34944](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/pull/34944), read in the earlier session). A head built by the project's own code should emit its texture coordinates with the mesh. Baked maps can be block-compressed in the browser with spark.js, whose licence is "free for non-commercial use, or until your product reaches $100,000 in lifetime revenue" ([spark.js](https://github.com/ludicon/spark.js/blob/main/README.md), read), a product decision; maps that derive from a fixed donor can simply ship as KTX2 files. On per-pixel noise against textures no measurement exists, but the argument is one-sided: a texture is prefiltered by its mip chain, while the one browser project that evaluates all skin detail per pixel must filter by pixel footprint and still accumulates up to 24 samples for a still view ([DERMIS](https://github.com/SamG-Coder/dermis-cuda/blob/main/README.md), read).

### Expression shapes belong in the project's own buffers, not in three.js morph targets

r187 stores each morph target as one layer of a 32-bit float array texture and loops over every target for every vertex in every pass ([Morph.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/nodes/accessors/Morph.js#L88-L199), read). For this head that is about **3.3 MB a target with normals and about 170 MB for the 52 ARKit shapes** (computed for roughly 103,000 vertices), outside any phone budget; and because the head's vertices come from its own position function, the built-in result would be discarded anyway. The buildable forms are sparse deltas in a storage buffer applied in the once-per-frame compute pass, or PlayCanvas's method of blending the active targets into one texture when weights change, which also works on WebGL 2 ([PlayCanvas morph-instance.js](https://github.com/playcanvas/engine/blob/main/src/scene/morph-instance.js), read). What cheaper routes lose is documented by the MetaHuman web pipeline: baking the rig to bones and 51 shapes drops fine correctives worth "~5-10mm" ([metahuman-to-glb](https://github.com/creategamecharacters/metahuman-to-glb/blob/main/README.md), read). Wrinkle maps need no engine support in a hand-written fragment node: a few weight uniforms, region masks in the channels of one texture, and one extra normal-map sample for each wrinkle set, at 5.3 MiB for a compressed 2K map (the note's recipe, derived from how TSL works, with no reference implementation found).

### Scattering stays pre-integrated; hair and anti-aliasing must be chosen together

For one forward pass with a hand-written fragment node, pre-integrated scattering is the only option that adds no pass, no attachment and no dependence on temporal anti-aliasing. The screen-space route in the open pull request needs an extra render-target attachment that a hand-written fragment node must write itself, 16 to 32 taps on every skin pixel and, at low tap counts, a temporal resolve ([three.js PR 33761](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/pull/33761/files), read); Babylon.js warns of its own equivalent that "smaller GPUs won't necessarily have the resources to run this effect" ([Babylon.js documentation](https://github.com/BabylonJS/Documentation/blob/master/content/features/featuresDeepDive/materials/using/masterPBR.md), read). It is a desktop-only refinement to judge last. Light through ears and nostrils is separate and cheap, a per-vertex thickness with an exponential falloff, done in under 20 lines in one open project ([hair-fur-sim SkinMaterial.ts](https://github.com/evanlask/hair-fur-sim/blob/main/src/examples/SkinMaterial.ts), read).

Real strands run in browsers. One raw-WebGPU project measures 1.29 million strand particles drawn as ribbons in about 6 ms on an unnamed Apple Silicon chip at 1280 by 769 ([HairyMess](https://github.com/patrickFuerst/hairymess_web/blob/main/README.md), read; the author's own figure), and software-rasterised hair holds under 10 ms a frame on an RTX 3060 ([Frostbitten hair](https://github.com/Scthe/frostbitten-hair-webgpu/blob/master/README.md), read). Brows, lashes and stubble are a small fraction of such loads (computed). No strand timing exists for any phone, and the one phone-tested viewer uses cards. The choice of coverage is tied to the choice of anti-aliasing by r187 itself: alpha-to-coverage needs 4× multisampling ([WebGPUPipelineUtils.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/renderers/webgpu/utils/WebGPUPipelineUtils.js#L212), read), the temporal resolvers need multisampling off ([TRAANode.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/examples/jsm/tsl/display/TRAANode.js), read in the earlier session), and a hand-written fragment node gets neither the built-in alpha test nor the alpha hash and must discard itself.

### Eyes must be written from the literature; light and tone are already in the box

No eye shader exists for three.js under WebGPU. The MetaHuman web viewer's "refractive" eye traces no refracted ray ([viewer.js](https://github.com/creategamecharacters/metahuman-to-glb/blob/main/5.7/native-glb/stages/04-webview-build/templates/viewer.js), read) and draws its eye-occlusion mesh "as a flat 40% dark layer over the whole eye" ([README](https://github.com/creategamecharacters/metahuman-to-glb/blob/main/README.md), read), and DERMIS states that its eyes "do not implement physical corneal refraction". The node language has `refract`, parallax helpers and ordinary analytic maths, so a refracting cornea over an iris plane with a dark limbus is a few dozen lines with no texture read (inference); the lid contact shadow, tear line and lash density are problems of geometry and masks, not of the renderer.

For light, r187 supplies GGX-prefiltered environment maps, nine-coefficient spherical harmonics that a hand-written node can evaluate directly, and a five-tap rotated-disk shadow filter, with variable-penumbra soft shadows absent under WebGPU but pluggable through `shadow.filterNode` ([ShadowNode.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/nodes/lighting/ShadowNode.js#L117), read). A nose shadow should widen with distance from the nose, so a hand-written soft shadow of 16 to 32 taps on face pixels and a short screen-space march for lids, nostrils and lip line are the project's own work (inference). Among tone curves Khronos PBR Neutral leaves colours between 0.08 and 0.8 exact apart from a constant and states "there are no hue shifts" ([Khronos](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/ToneMapping/blob/main/PBR_Neutral/README.md), read), while the game's own per-channel exponential curve compresses the largest channel most and pulls sunlit skin toward yellow-white (first-hand and computed in the earlier body research). No source compares tone curves on skin, so that is a test, not a pick.

### What exists as a measured number

There is no published per-frame cost of a skin shader, of a 200,000-triangle skinned mesh or of any morph-target count in three.js WebGPU on an integrated GPU or a phone. The usable device-named numbers are the hair figures above and three.js's per-object overhead, 20,000 separate meshes at about 15 frames a second against 60 in the old renderer on an M1 Pro ([three.js issue 30560](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/30560), read in the earlier session). GPU time can be measured with the optional `timestamp-query` feature, quantised to 100 microseconds unless Chrome's developer flag is set, and timestamp results "are not a good measure of performance" in isolation ([WebGPU Fundamentals](https://github.com/webgpu/webgpufundamentals/blob/main/webgpu/lessons/webgpu-timing.md), read); fixes to r187's timestamps for resumed, layered and nested passes were still open after its release ([PR 35339](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/pull/35339); [PR 35414](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/pull/35414), titles read). The dominant unknown is the fragment side: a face filling a third of a 2560 by 1440 frame is about 1.2 million shaded pixels (computed), and every map read, lobe and shadow tap multiplies by that.

## Of the three routes, the project's own head with borrowed skin and shapes wins

### Scan-free methods supply the optics and the scales, not the irregularity

What a scan supplies splits into three parts, and first principles cover two of them (the note's analysis). The optics and scales are fully published. Skin colour follows from melanin and haemoglobin through a two-layer model with four to six scalar parameters, whose spectra are in open code ([skinoptics](https://github.com/victorportog/skinoptics), read; the package is GPLv3, so the project should take the published formulas it cites and not its code), with plausible ranges of 0.005 to 0.40 for melanin and 0.001 to 0.0225 for haemoglobin listed in Meta's MIT-licensed BioSkin ([BioSkin](https://github.com/facebookresearch/BioSkin), read), and it ran in real time from base pigment maps with "negligible overhead" in 2010 ([Jimenez et al. 2010](https://www.iryoku.com/skincolor/), extract). The six-Gaussian scattering profile ([SeparableSSS.h](https://github.com/iryoku/separable-sss/blob/master/SeparableSSS.h), read), the fibre scattering of hair with its melanin coefficients ([pbrt-v4](https://github.com/mmp/pbrt-v4/blob/master/src/pbrt/bxdfs.cpp), read), the eye's geometry (corneal radius 7.7 to 7.8 mm and corneal index 1.376, [schematic eye paper](https://typeset.io/pdf/accommodation-dependent-model-of-the-human-eye-with-sq9k64hcgf.pdf); visible iris 11.8 mm, [entrance pupil model](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-45827-3); both extracts) and the optics of enamel over dentin ([Groningen dissertation](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/14515607/06_c6.pdf), extract) are likewise numbers anyone may use. Regional variation is covered in part: calibrated colour by site exists, with Caucasian forehead and cheek near L\* 59, a\* 12, b\* 15 and standard deviations of about 5, 3 and 2.5 ([Xiao 2017 table in skinoptics](https://github.com/victorportog/skinoptics), read), and nose, perinasal skin and cheeks are the most vascular units ([normative data at 16 sites](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281516544_Skin_characteristics_Normative_data_for_elasticity_erythema_melanin_and_thickness_at_16_different_anatomical_locations), extract). But **no reusable map of blood or melanin over a face has been published**; every such map shown in a paper was measured on a subject, so the fields must be authored from rankings and tuned until rendered cheek and forehead land inside the measured ranges.

For relief the best scan-free method is Disney's graph model, pores as nodes and micro-wrinkles as edges with depth grown iteratively ([Weiss et al. 2023](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14904), extract), which needs a pore spacing, a direction field and about nine parameters. Dermatology gives the scales: follicles about 0.5 mm apart on the forehead (computed from densities of 292 and 439 per cm²; [thesis citing Otberg](https://core.ac.uk/download/402586008.pdf), secondary; [Blume et al. 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1993141/), extract), primary lines 20 to 100 micrometres deep ([EEMCO guidance](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0926995998000944), extract). The one user study with numbers measures both what structure adds and what it lacks. Among 42 participants, the graph model with fixed parameters beat the noise "commonly used in real-time face rendering" by **162 comparisons to 48**, and the same model fitted to a photograph of the person beat the unfitted one by **216 to 162** ([FLEX, SIGGRAPH 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3721238.3730760), read). Structured procedural detail is clearly better than what the head has now, and measured, person-specific detail is clearly better again.

The third part is idiosyncratic structure: the particular blotches, capillaries, moles and scars, the irregular way real pores cluster and real lines branch, iris fibres, and the exact form of a head. The literature offers no scan-free generator for it that its own authors call realistic. Disney Research writes that simulation "can lead to a synthetic look" and mixes in scanned patches to cure it ([Disney Research 2024](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/2024/06/07/stylize-my-wrinkles-bridging-the-gap-from-simulation-to-reality/), extract); Microsoft's well-known synthetic faces still take their textures from a scan of each identity ([Raman et al. project page](https://microsoft.github.io/DynamicWrinkles/), extract); generating dynamic face assets without capturing each expression took a database of more than 4,000 pore-level scans ([Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00560), extract). Expression follows the same pattern: every physics-based face found, from 2005 to 2024, takes its anatomy or its actuation from a person or a scan database, and none was found that drives a procedural head from an authored muscle layout (all extracts). The honest ceiling of route (a), stated as the researchers' reading, since **no published test compares a fully procedural face with a scanned one**, is convincing skin on a plausible person in favourable light at conversational distance, and a face that reads as too regular and too clean in a close portrait. That is the distance intent 0040 already reported against the photograph.

### A learned or Gaussian head fails on light, range, engine and licence

The learned route was assessed against the game's needs and is the weaker choice on five of seven, with no workable answer on two.

| Need | What a learned or Gaussian head offers today | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Lit by the game's changing sun and sky | Only models trained on light-stage captures of real people relight well; Meta's universal prior was trained on "hundreds of high-quality multi-view human scans with controllable point lights" and is not released ([URAvatar page](https://github.com/junxuan-li/urgca-website/blob/HEAD/index.html), read). One-shot generators have lighting baked in. None handles the scene's local shadows | Fails without capture |
| Expressions, gaze, later speech | Standard, through FLAME or ARKit-style control | Meets |
| Sits on the procedural body in three.js r187 WebGPU | r187's `GaussianSplat` is a static renderer with no skinning, light or shadow code ([GaussianSplat.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/examples/jsm/objects/GaussianSplat.js), read in part); the engine with splat skinning is "built for THREE.js and WebGL2" ([Spark](https://github.com/sparkjsdev/spark/blob/HEAD/docs/docs/overview.md), read in part). Splats write no depth | Substantial custom work |
| Runs on desktops and phones | Non-relightable avatars report over 110 frames a second on a flagship phone ([LAM](https://github.com/aigc3d/LAM/blob/HEAD/README.md), read; no resolution stated); relightable ones are documented only on desktop cards or headsets | Only without relighting |
| A few centimetres to tens of metres | The model that claims pore detail decodes 1,048,576 Gaussians ([goliath rgca.py](https://github.com/facebookresearch/goliath/blob/HEAD/ca_code/models/rgca.py), read in part) from a dome capture; one-shot heads have 20,000 to 80,000 | Fails at the near end |

The practical web answer to relighting splats is itself revealing. PlayCanvas's documentation says "Gaussian splat scenes are captured with their lighting baked in" and relights them by lighting a proxy mesh and multiplying the result onto the splats ([PlayCanvas](https://github.com/playcanvas/developer-site/blob/HEAD/docs/user-manual/gaussian-splatting/building/relighting.md), read): the light is computed on a mesh. The frontier is moving back toward meshes too. Meta's phone-class hybrid decodes a face mesh with a texture and keeps only 16,384 Gaussians, three quarters of them on hair ([GPiCA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15711), extract), and the follow-up to the fastest one-image model outputs a textured mesh ([MeshLAM](https://github.com/meshlam/meshlam.github.io/blob/HEAD/index.html), read in part). No shipped game or web product was found that uses a Gaussian or neural head for an in-world character lit by its scene; what has shipped is telepresence. That is a negative result from a limited search, not proof. What is worth taking from this research is narrow: tension-driven wrinkle maps, fine geometry spent where meshes are weakest (lashes, brows, hairline), and a small learned imitator of the project's own builder as a fast forward model.

### The recommendation: the project's head, a donor's relief, a permissive shape set

The first-principles reading is this. A photograph of a face is light on a surface that has optics, scales and a history. Optics and scales are published and belong in the project's own code. History, meaning irregular relief, irregular colour and the trained shapes of a moving face, has been obtained in every credible source by measuring people or by sculptors. The useful question is therefore how to obtain history lawfully with the least damage to what the project owns, and what the project owns is exactly what a scan lacks: a head defined by a table of numbers, on one cage with the body and so without a neck seam, with eyes behind lids, teeth, ears, three levels of detail and a smile, rebuilt in about 30 seconds (first-hand). The industry's own step for joining a production head to a scan is wrapping and re-projection, and it applies in the direction that keeps the head.

**The recommended route is (b) in a narrow form, laid over (a).** The project's head stays the shipped geometry and the single source of identity. Its cage gains texture coordinates, because all of the industry's surface realism lives in maps and the head's points lie about a millimetre apart (computed, taking the head as a 9.5 cm sphere), which carries colour fields well and pores not at all. Light, eyes, pigment colour, scattering, teeth, hairs and the mechanism of wrinkles are built scan-free from published numbers. Two things are borrowed. The first is **measured relief from the Lee Perry-Smith scan**: its displacement, normal and specular maps, re-projected onto the project's texture coordinates through a fitted correspondence, as the medium-scale layer under a procedural pore layer, the same split of scan plus synthesised micro detail that Epic and USC use. The second is **an expression set**, transferred as per-vertex deltas from a permissively licensed head (MHR's 72 FACS shapes, ICT-FaceKit's 53 or the CC0 Face Units' 52) and checked against the published smile magnitudes; the project's own muscle solve remains possible for the one baked smile, but it is the costliest item with the least precedent. Route (c) is rejected.

Adopting the scanned man's whole face, form and colour as well, is the fastest way to one photoreal male still, and Samuel has offered it. The evidence weighs against shipping it. He is an identifiable real person, the licence is silent on likeness, and the game shows a nude body. No woman's scan of equal standing may ship, so the two sexes would differ in kind. A single static scan has no parameters for an avatar builder to adjust. The scan is a neutral face without eyes, teeth, hair or expression shapes, at 17,684 triangles, and from memory its eyelids are closed, so it would need the same wrapping plus an eye region rebuilt. And the gate is a photograph of a different man: the aim is a face that reads as photographed, not his face. His form still has two honest uses that carry no likeness into the game: as the **control** in the project's renderer, and as a **calibration target** to which the head's numbers are fitted, so that the residual shows in millimetres where the authored form departs from a real one.

The rule "our own code only" then has three positions, as it had for the body. Keep it whole and accept the ceiling of route (a). Lift it for one donor's relief and one permissive shape set, the recommendation, which brings no third-party identity into the game and adds one credit line and one licence notice. Or lift it for a whole head. The second position should also be earned by measurement, not assumed. The first stage of the plan renders the scan itself, untouched, in the project's own shader and light beside the project's head and the photograph: if the scan does not read as a photograph there, the gap is in light and shading and no asset will close it; if it does and the project's head does not, the difference is the asset, and a plain grey render of both separates form from surface. After the scan-free skin stage the same comparison shows whether borrowed relief still adds anything. The evidence predicts that it will, from the 216 to 162 result and Disney's own remark, but only this test measures it on this head.

## One man's scan may ship; no woman's could be verified

### The Lee Perry-Smith head: lawful with a credit, silent on likeness

The licence file in three.js reads in full that "Infinite, 3D Head Scan by Lee Perry-Smith is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 Unported License", is "Based on a work at www.triplegangers.com", and ends "Do what you want with the files, but always mention where you got them from" ([licence file](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/models/gltf/LeePerrySmith/LeePerrySmith_License.txt), read in full). The CC BY 3.0 legal code grants a worldwide, royalty-free, perpetual licence to reproduce, adapt and distribute, has no non-commercial restriction, and allows "such modifications as are technically necessary to exercise the rights in other media and formats" ([CC BY 3.0 legal code](https://github.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/blob/main/docs/licenses/by/3.0/legalcode.en.html), read). **The verdict from the texts is yes, with attribution**: commercial use, serving the files to browsers, keeping them in a public repository and modifying them are all inside the grant.

The three.js copy holds a mesh of 9,279 vertices and 17,684 triangles with texture coordinates, colour, specular and tangent-normal maps at 1,024 pixels, and a 4,096-pixel displacement map stored as an 8-bit JPEG ([three.js directory](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/dev/examples/models/gltf/LeePerrySmith), measured). Better source data appears to exist: an open WebGPU project loads the scan's colour and normal maps "at their full 4096²" ([hair-fur-sim](https://github.com/evanlask/hair-fur-sim/blob/main/src/examples/MulletCharacter.ts), read), and one search extract suggests the displacement original was 16-bit. The archive three.js credits for the model, casual-effects.com ([three.js example](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/webgl_materials_normalmap.html), read), and the scanner's own site could not be opened, so the original files, their resolutions and the archive's suggested credit must be fetched and checked before the donor stage. A 4K map across a whole head does not hold skin microstructure in any case, which is why the procedural pore layer remains necessary.

The duties are concrete. Each distributed copy must carry the licence or its address, the author's name, the title, the source address "to the extent reasonably practicable", and for an adaptation "a credit identifying the use of the Work in the Adaptation"; the credit "may be implemented in any reasonable manner" (same legal code, read). A web game distributes to every visitor, so the credit has to be reachable from the game and not only sit in the repository. The pattern the texts support is a credits page in the game, a licence file beside the asset, and the same line in the file's metadata (inference). A complete line would read: "Infinite, 3D Head Scan" by Lee Perry-Smith (www.triplegangers.com, www.ir-ltd.net), CC BY 3.0 Unported, re-projected to this project's texture layout. Converting to KTX2 or re-baking to other coordinates is permitted or is an adaptation that must be labelled as changed, but encrypting the files so that visitors cannot reuse them would meet the clause against "effective technological measures".

Likeness is a separate matter that the licence does not settle. The CC BY 3.0 text contains none of the words "publicity", "privacy", "personality" or "likeness" (measured on the text). It offers the work "AS-IS" without warranty of title, forbids action "prejudicial to the Original Author's honor or reputation" and forbids implying his endorsement. The scan is commonly said to be Lee Perry-Smith's own head (recalled, not confirmed), and it has been redistributed since 2010 in three.js and elsewhere with no reported objection, which suggests tolerance and is not a release. A recognisable real face on a nude game body is the use most likely to exceed what a person agreed to. **Using the scan only for what is not recognisable, pore relief and specular detail on a head whose form is the project's own, removes the likeness question and leaves only the duty of attribution.** No statute, case law or regulator guidance was retrieved; this needs a lawyer before launch.

### No scan of a woman with a usable licence was found

The answer to the question about a female counterpart is that none could be verified. Digital Emily 2 is "licensed only for non-commercial use" ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2015/06/download-digital-human-labs-digital-emily-2-model/), extract). Eisko's "Louise" is offered "for testing and evaluation purposes" in the vendor's wording as quoted by a third party ([3dart.it](https://3dart.it/en/3d-scan-head-model-download), extract), and press reports call its licence non-commercial ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=88250), extract). The free female head scan from 3D Scan Store and Ten24 is "not licensed for commercial use" ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2023/09/download-ten24s-free-hi-res-3d-scan-of-a-female-human-head/), extract). The research captures that include women are all non-commercial, FaceScape because "facial data involves the privacy of participants" ([FaceScape](https://github.com/zhuhao-nju/facescape), read). Sketchfab, the likeliest home of a CC BY or CC0 scan of a woman, could not be opened; each such upload would carry its own licence and its own unanswered likeness question. Paid scans from 3D Scan Store, TexturingXYZ, Triplegangers and RenderPeople are unclear, since no vendor licence could be read; the pattern in comparable licences is that use inside a product is allowed and redistribution of the files is not, which a browser game with a public repository meets on both hard points, so each vendor would have to confirm in writing.

What may lawfully ship for a woman is not a scan. Microsoft's Rocketbox library is MIT-licensed and holds 19 adult women and 21 adult men, with head colour, normal, wrinkle-normal and specular maps at 2,048 pixels on the one avatar measured ([Rocketbox](https://github.com/microsoft/Microsoft-Rocketbox), read; [Female_Adult_01](https://github.com/microsoft/Microsoft-Rocketbox/tree/master/Assets/Avatars/Adults/Female_Adult_01), measured); these are game-resolution characters from an older library. The MakeHuman family's painted skins are CC0, with a 2,048-pixel colour map and no relief map in the one example measured ([TalkingHead mpfb.glb](https://github.com/met4citizen/TalkingHead/blob/main/avatars/mpfb.glb), measured). Statistical heads produce female shapes without any texture: Google's GNM Head, Apache-2.0 since July 2026, samples identities "based on attributes like gender and ethnicity" from 253 identity and 383 expression components ([GNM shape README](https://github.com/google/gnm/blob/main/gnm/shape/README.md), read). None of these is a counterpart to a pore-level scan.

The recommendation's shape decides the matter rather than the missing asset. Because identity stays in the project's parametric head, a woman is that head with female numbers, checked against published anthropometry and, if Samuel allows shape statistics, against GNM or ICT-FaceKit samples, which depict no person. Her colour comes from the pigment model, her fine relief from the procedural pore layer with female parameters, and the donor's medium-scale relief only if look development shows that a man's relief reads rightly on her, which is a question for the eye and not for a licence. If it does not, the clean upgrade is a capture of a consenting woman under a written release, which is the project's own material and not a third party's, or a purchased scan whose vendor confirms web delivery in writing (inference).

Two patterns are worth keeping from the table. Public licences (CC0, CC BY, MIT, Apache-2.0) let the files themselves sit in a public repository and be served to browsers, while end-user agreements such as Epic's, Unity's and the vendors' typically allow only embedding in a product; an open-source project that ships a MetaHuman-derived avatar puts the concern plainly: "Many commercial 3D products generate avatars that cannot be used in public web apps, where the asset is delivered to the client and effectively becomes downloadable" ([TalkingHead](https://github.com/met4citizen/TalkingHead), read). And provenance has two tiers that matter to a project with a rule about scans: artist-made and permissive (MakeHuman, Face Units), and scan-derived but permissive and depicting no individual (ICT-FaceKit, GNM, MHR). The Lee Perry-Smith scan is the only verified source of measured skin relief under a licence that clearly allows shipping.

## The avatar builder: landmarks fit the shape, statistics set the tone, the model only criticises

### A deployed precedent exists, and it did not use a language model

Fitting a game head with a few hundred parameters to a photograph has a published, deployed precedent. NetEase's "Face-to-Parameter Translation" created characters from one photograph by optimising the game's own facial parameters, 264 for male and 310 for female characters; because the engine was not differentiable, the authors trained a small network to imitate it on 20,000 random pairs of parameters and renders and ran gradient descent through that, and the feature was reportedly used over a million times ([arXiv 1909.01064](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01064), extract). The measuring part runs in a browser today: MediaPipe's face mesh "estimates 468 3D face landmarks in real-time even on mobile devices" under Apache-2.0, and its successor task outputs blendshape scores and a head transform through a WebAssembly package ([MediaPipe](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/v0.10.9/docs/solutions/face_mesh.md), read in part). The research-grade fitters (DECA, MICA and the FLAME-based trackers) are non-commercial and cannot ship, but their benchmark shows the ceiling for one image: median scan-to-mesh error of about 0.9 to 1.1 mm on a neutral face ([NoW leaderboard](https://now.is.tue.mpg.de/nonmetricalevaluation.html), extract). Those figures are for learned regressors with strong priors; landmark fitting alone is weaker, above all in depth from a frontal picture.

### What a vision-language model in the loop can and cannot do

Loops in which a vision-language model edits 3D parameters by looking at renders exist as research systems. BlenderAlchemy has the model act as "edit generator" and "state evaluator", searching with a set breadth and depth (its shipped example runs four rounds of eight candidates), and one of its packaged demos edits face blendshape values ([BlenderAlchemy](https://github.com/ianhuang0630/BlenderAlchemyOfficial/blob/HEAD/README.md), read in part). A January 2026 agent alternates a generator role with a verifier that "examines rendered output from multiple viewpoints, identifies visual discrepancies, and provides feedback" ([VIGA](https://github.com/Fugtemypt123/VIGA/blob/HEAD/README.md), read in part). The benchmark built for exactly this task shape is the published caution: across 245 scenes "even the state-of-the-art VLM system struggles" with edits that are easy for human users ([BlenderGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01786), extract). No product was found in which a model iteratively tunes avatar identity against a photograph, a weak negative from a search that was cut short. So the answer to whether Fable can change the avatar from reference pictures is yes, as an editor and critic of the parameter table, and no, as the instrument that measures. A language model judges "brows too thin, face too long, looks older than the reference" well and metric distances poorly, and it is inconsistent between runs.

### The design that follows

The builder should have four layers, each doing what it is good at (the note's design, extended here). Shape comes from landmarks and silhouette: the user gives a frontal, a three-quarter and a profile picture with a neutral face, landmarks are found on the device, and an optimiser adjusts the head's numbers until the head's own named points land on them, held inside published ranges (the NIOSH survey of 3,997 people gives means and standard deviations for the main face dimensions; [Anthrotech report](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/resource/11815/Anthrotech_report.pdf), extract). Tone comes from colour statistics: skin is sampled in landmark-defined patches on cheek and forehead, the illuminant is estimated from the whole picture, and the result is mapped to the pigment model's melanin and haemoglobin; iris colour is sampled the same way. This is the part most easily wrong, because skin tone and scene light cannot be told apart in one photograph, a bias the TRUST work quantified ([TRUST](https://github.com/HavenFeng/TRUST/blob/HEAD/README.md), read in part), so the user must be able to correct it. Attributes without landmarks go to the language model as critic: it sees the render beside the reference and proposes bounded changes to named parameters (brow density, hairline, beard, age lines, lid crease), and a change is kept only if an objective score, landmark error or a perceptual distance on the render, does not get worse; the rounds are capped. The fourth layer is the result, which is only ever the parameter table.

Two engineering facts shape it. A 30-second rebuild makes search by full rebuilds impractical: BlenderAlchemy's example budget of 32 renders is 16 minutes in series (computed). The builder therefore needs a fast forward path, first a landmark-only evaluation that returns where a few dozen named points land without building the mesh, later an imitator network trained on the project's own renders as NetEase did, whose 20,000 samples would cost about 170 worker-hours at 30 seconds each (computed). And the photograph must never become the texture: it carries its own light, so the face would be lit twice, with painted shadows under the nose and a painted highlight, which is why even research pipelines normalise illumination and expression before extracting a texture ([FFHQ-UV](https://github.com/csbhr/FFHQ-UV/blob/HEAD/README.md), read in part); it would also carry the pictured person's exact likeness into the asset.

The builder also has a ready examination. The Lee Perry-Smith scan is a head with a known surface, so fitting the project's head to renders of it and measuring the remaining distance in millimetres is the benchmark above run in-house, and heads sampled from GNM or ICT-FaceKit give test faces of both sexes that depict nobody (inference). What a few hundred shape numbers capture is proportion to roughly a millimetre; they do not capture moles, scars, hair or skin detail, and likeness in practice leans on hairline, brows and tone. A face photograph processed into a face model is likely to count as biometric or sensitive data in several jurisdictions (the researchers' general knowledge; no legal source was opened). The low-risk design does all detection and fitting on the device, never uploads or stores the picture, stores only the parameters, asks the user to confirm that the picture is of themselves or used with permission, and treats sending the picture to a hosted model as a transfer that needs explicit consent. This needs a lawyer's review before launch.

Three things about the order deserve a sentence each. Stages 2 to 5 should land close together, because the consistency findings predict that real eyes over even skin, or real skin under drawn brows, reads worse than the present drawn face. Stage 4 is conditional on stage 0 and stage 3 showing that the donor adds what the evidence predicts, which is also the moment the rule is actually lifted. And the grey comparison of stage 0 can reverse stages 3 and 4: if the form, not the surface, proves the larger gap, calibration against a real head comes first, as the shape-before-material result demands.

The measurements are a deliverable in their own right. The sources contain no millisecond for a skin shader, an eye shader, strand hair in three.js, a morph count or a diffusion pass in a browser, no transcode time, no memory limit for a tab, and no perceptual comparison of a procedural face with a scanned one. The harness the sources support uses Chrome with `trackTimestamp` and the developer flag for unquantised per-pass time, checked against a throughput test because timestamp deltas alone mislead; frame-time percentiles over a fixed camera path on iPhones, where the timing feature cannot be assumed; first-frame time recorded apart from steady state, because a large hand-written fragment function is a compile cost before it is a frame cost; and a blind "photograph or render?" test on strangers who give reasons, bearing in mind that untrained observers called photographs correctly 92 % of the time and renders only 60 % ([Holmes, Banks and Farid 2016](https://farid.berkeley.edu/downloads/publications/tap16.pdf), extract; the percentages from a [secondary source](https://ufo3d.com/photorealistic-rendering/)).

Four decisions are Samuel's. The rule: the recommendation is to lift it for one donor's relief and one permissive shape set, keep it for identity, and let stage 0 and the stage 3 comparison confirm that the lift is worth making. The screen curve, which changes the whole game's picture. Whether the photograph that serves as the gate enters the repository, since the plan judges every stage against it. And the female head's upgrade path, a commissioned capture with a release or a purchased scan with written permission for web delivery, should the shared relief not hold on her.

## Conclusion

The research narrows what the project's rule actually costs to two named things, the irregular relief of real skin and the trained shapes of a moving face, and finds both under licences that allow a public commercial web game, with one hard limit: measured skin exists openly for one man and for no woman. It also turns the question of adopting a scanned head around. The scan world has surfaces and no parameters; the project has parameters and no surface history. An avatar builder, a female variant and a population of faces all need the parameters, so the head that ships should stay the one written in numbers, and the scan should serve as donor, as control and as examiner, never as the face. What the rule protected, that every identity in the game is the project's own, survives the lift intact.

The second shift is from argument to experiment. Whether light or asset holds the larger share of a photograph, whether structured procedural skin is enough, whether a man's relief reads on a woman, what any of it costs on a phone: the literature answers none of these, and each is one render or one timing away once the scan stands in the project's renderer beside the project's own head. Four outside facts would change the plan: the blocked primaries read directly, above all the scan's original archive, the FLAME and Epic licence texts and a vendor's written terms; a verified scan of a woman under CC BY or CC0; three.js merging its diffusion pass or gaining an eye node; and the release of a relightable universal avatar prior with weights, a commercial licence and consenting subjects, which is the one development that would reopen the learned route.
