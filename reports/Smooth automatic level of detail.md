# Two automatic rules retire hand-written levels

**Yes, for anything that is a surface, and one companion rule covers what is not.** The algorithm is the cluster hierarchy with an error cut, the level-of-detail core of Unreal Engine's Nanite ("virtualized geometry"): from nothing but an asset's full mesh it builds every coarser version itself, and each frame it draws, patch by patch, the coarsest version whose error stays under a set number of pixels, so **one number for the whole engine replaces every asset's levels and breakpoints**. Measured in this session on the game's own boulder, with the open-source simplifier a browser worker would call, the hierarchy built in **0.4 s** on one thread (ten million triangles: **25 s**), left **no crack in 177 test views**, and at two pixels of error drew **2 to 7 times fewer triangles** than today's ladder for about the same error; a ten-million-triangle ball 400 px across was drawn with 55,574 triangles. It leaves three gaps, each with a known completion. It destroys tree crowns (an oak's crown at a tenth of its triangles covered a third to a quarter of what it did), where thinning the sprigs in a fixed random order and enlarging the survivors kept the cover within 1 % down to 4 % of the sprigs. Its changes are hidden, not smoothed: the published systems keep the error under a pixel and lean on a temporal filter, and the engine's own way of moving vertices, carried into the hierarchy, cut the largest jump by 46 to 63 % at two and four pixels of error, not to nothing. And it ends at one patch of about a hundred triangles, not at a pixel; a short tail of halvings, the engine's dot and the land's colour finish it. Open code covers the whole build (meshoptimizer, MIT) and, since October 2026, the whole method on the engine's exact stack (three-virtual-geometry, five weeks old, measured by its author only in Chrome). **The one number nobody has is the frame time of such a cut in Safari on an iPhone**: nothing was drawn in this session, and Safari's WebGPU lacks the 64-bit atomics that desktop engines use to draw pixel-sized triangles cheaply. The recommendation: adopt the hierarchy for rocks, bodies and made things and the thinning rule for crowns, each driven by one engine-wide number in pixels that the governor may raise, after a short measurement on Samuel's own devices that decides how much of it a phone can carry.

**How to read the evidence.** This session's network rules blocked most primary pages: neither of Brian Karis's slide decks, none of Epic's documentation and no Pixar or other paper could be opened, and the shared search budget ran out early. Four marks therefore travel with the claims below. *First-hand*: measured or read in this session (the engine's code, two probes on the game's own assets, the three-virtual-geometry package). *Read directly*: the source itself was opened, which worked for files hosted on GitHub (meshoptimizer and its `clusterlod.h`, NVIDIA's builder, the posts of the meshoptimizer and Bevy authors, Godot, three.js, WebKit, the WebGPU proposals). *Search extract*: known only through the search tool's digest of a page, its wording and numbers unchecked against the page. *Recalled*: from the researcher's memory of the source, unverified. Everything attributed to Epic, Karis, Pixar or SpeedTree is a search extract unless marked recalled. The recommendation rests on the first two kinds; the blocked pages weaken the history and Epic's figures, not the decision.

## One error in pixels replaces every level, for anything that is a surface

The method is best known as Nanite, which Epic presented in 2021, but its level-of-detail core is older and public: NVIDIA's open builder lists its descent from a pyramid of triangulations (1989) through "Batched Multi Triangulation" (2005) to the Nanite talk ([nv_cluster_lod_builder README](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/main/README.md), read directly). What Unreal adds around that core, a software rasteriser, a visibility buffer and page streaming, serves the drawing of pixel-sized triangles. The level-of-detail algorithm itself ends when the list of patches to draw is known: Nanite sends patches with large triangles to the ordinary hardware rasteriser, and independent renderers reuse the same hierarchy with rasterisation of their own ([CuRast, arXiv 2604.21749](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.21749); [jglrxavpok, "Recreating Nanite"](https://jglrxavpok.github.io/2024/05/13/recreating-nanite-mesh-shader-time.html); search extracts). That separation is what puts the method within reach of a browser.

### The build needs only the full mesh

**Cut the mesh into clusters**, patches of at most 128 neighbouring triangles. Then repeat one loop until a single cluster is left: gather neighbouring clusters into groups (8 to a group in this session's probe, 16 by default in meshoptimizer's builder), merge each group, **simplify it to half its triangles while every vertex it shares with another group stays locked**, and cut the result into clusters again ([clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/demo/clusterlod.h), read directly). Each cluster keeps two pairs of numbers: its own error, which is how far its simplified patch lies from the surface it came from, in the asset's own units, with a bounding sphere; and the same pair for its parent, the simplified group that replaces it. Two details carry the design. A group's locked border is the same line one level finer and one level coarser, so neighbouring groups can stand at different levels and still meet exactly. And the groups are chosen afresh at every level, so a border locked at one level lies inside a group at the next and is simplified there ([nv_cluster_lod_builder README](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/main/README.md), read directly); without that regrouping, in Karis's account, the locked seams collect so many triangles that a level can no longer halve ([Karis et al., "Nanite: A Deep Dive", SIGGRAPH 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf), search extract). The asset contributes nothing but triangles that share their corners. All levels together hold twice the triangles of the full mesh: 221,042 for the boulder's 110,592 ([probes note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/probes_on_the_games_own_assets.md), first-hand).

### Every patch decides alone each frame, and the surface still closes

Each frame the error of every cluster is projected to pixels by the arithmetic the engine already uses for size on screen (its `px = size × pxK / distance / uLodInv`, with the cluster's error in place of the asset's size), and **a cluster is drawn when its own error is at or under the threshold and its parent's is over it** ([clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/demo/clusterlod.h), read directly). The builder forces errors never to shrink toward the coarse end, so along any chain from finest to coarsest exactly one version passes; all clusters of a group hold the same numbers, so they decide alike without knowing of each other; and because the test needs no neighbour and no order, a compute pass can run it for all clusters at once ([nv_cluster_lod_builder README](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/main/README.md), read directly). The cut that results can run through the middle of one thing: fine on the side near the camera, coarse on the far side, closed along the line between. The probe checked exactly that on the game's own meshes: in 177 views each of the boulder, of a million-triangle ball and of the bell tent, **not one edge was left open**, and every parent's error and sphere contained its children's ([probes note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/probes_on_the_games_own_assets.md), first-hand).

### Triangles follow the screen, not the asset

Epic states the consequence as a law: whatever the source holds, the number of triangles Nanite tries to draw is proportional to the number of pixels ([Epic: working with Nanite-enabled content](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content?lang=zh-CN), search extract of the Chinese edition in the researcher's translation). The probe saw the same on a displaced ball of 10,002,828 triangles: 400 px across it is drawn with **55,574 triangles at one pixel of error**, 22,656 at two and 9,638 at four; at 2,000 px, far larger than the screen, it is 704,156, of which 171,755 lie in view. Against the ladder on the engine's own boulder (full mesh: 110,592 triangles) the cut wins on triangles and on error at once.

| 400 px | 12,288; off by 0.56 / 4.41 px | 13,210; off by 0.14 / 1.10 px | 6,340; off by 0.24 / 1.49 px |
| 128 px | 3,072; off by 0.45 / 2.86 px | 1,716; off by 0.19 / 1.02 px | 428; off by 0.44 / 2.11 px |
| 32 px | 192; off by 0.42 / 1.95 px | 106; off by 0.28 / 0.86 px | 52; off by 0.47 / 1.51 px |

*Triangles drawn, and how far the full mesh's vertices lie from them (root mean square / the most, in pixels) at the sizes where a level of the ladder takes over. First-hand: [probes note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/probes_on_the_games_own_assets.md).*

Cut at one pixel, the hierarchy spends about the ladder's triangles at 400 px and a little over half below, for a quarter to two thirds of its mean error and a worst error near one pixel where the ladder's is 2 to 4.4. Cut at two pixels, it matches the ladder's mean error with **2 to 7 times fewer triangles**. Its steps are one group at a time, about 500 triangles becoming 250, where the ladder steps by 9 times at its first change and 4 times at the others. The build that buys this took 0.42 s on one thread for the boulder, less than the recipe needs to make the mesh (0.24 to 0.51 s), 2.6 s for a million triangles and 25 s for ten million; the boulder's whole hierarchy is 3.08 MB where the heap holds 2.21 MB for the full mesh alone. These are measurements with stated limits: one core of a cloud machine under Node, one boulder shape, shape error only, and nothing drawn.

### The changes are hidden, not smoothed

**No published cluster system moves or fades anything when a patch changes level.** Nanite switches cluster by cluster, at once; Karis had explored the smooth alternative, vertices sliding by distance as in Progressive Buffers (2005), and dropped it because it "assumes everything in a LOD band is loaded" ([Karis, "The Journey to Nanite", HPG 2022](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf), search extract). The switch is meant to be invisible on two conditions, which meshoptimizer's author puts in one sentence: a coarser cluster replaces a finer one "only if the resulting visual error is under 1 pixel (and the resulting switch is hidden by TAA or other temporal filters)" ([Kapoulkine, 2025](https://zeux.io/2025/09/30/billions-of-triangles-in-minutes/), read directly). Experience supports the bargain: what the eye catches is many triangles changing in the same frame, and a game that changed a few at a time saw "no popping (geometric or lighting) ... even when people deliberately looked for it and moved cameras around very slowly" ([Forsyth, "Comparison of VIPM Methods"](https://tomforsyth1000.github.io/papers/gem_vipm_webversion.html), read directly). It is not a guarantee. The builder's own author notes that in recent games "you might have noticed geometry popping in cutscenes now and again" ([Kapoulkine, 2026](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md), read directly), and shading gives way before shape does: a renderer whose positions were "near-seamless" across cuts still showed normals jumping until normals were weighed in the simplifier's error ([JMS55, virtual geometry in Bevy 0.15](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-11-14-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-15/), read directly).

Desktop engines reach one pixel by drawing their smallest triangles in a compute pass instead of the GPU's own rasteriser, about three times faster for pixel-sized triangles by the figure usually quoted for Nanite ([CuRast, arXiv 2604.21749](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.21749), search extract), and that pass needs 64-bit atomic writes, which WebGPU does not have. Two facts soften this for TheGame. One pixel of *error* is not one pixel of *triangle*: by the probe's counts the boulder at 400 px and one pixel of error is drawn with triangles about six pixels along an edge, the ten-million ball with triangles about three (my arithmetic, taking half the triangles as facing the camera and the picture as a disc). And the engine already owns the second half of the bargain, a temporal pass, plus something the published systems lack: it moves vertices.

## Three gaps the hierarchy leaves, and what closes each

The hierarchy answers for connected surfaces drawn at about one pixel of error. The brief asks for three things more: any asset, no visible change, down to a pixel. The table sets out where each reaches past the algorithm; the paragraphs after it take the gaps in the order of their weight.

| The brief's words | Where the hierarchy stops short | What completes it | Standing of the evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Any asset | Crowns, leaves, grass: crowds of small separate parts | Thin the parts in a fixed random order and enlarge the survivors so the cover stays | Probe, first-hand (a model of cover, not a picture); literature by search extract |
| Any asset | Made things: unshared corners, thin and loose parts | Weld by position first; a floor under the error from the outline that was lost; loose parts built apart | Tent probe, first-hand; remedies read in the package, not tried |
| Any asset | Things that bend: creatures, wind | Errors and spheres hold for the rest shape only; bounds must be generous | Untested here |
| Fully smooth | Above about one pixel of error a change shows | Slide each vertex to its place on the coarser patch before the change, or stipple between two cuts under the temporal pass | Probe, first-hand: the slide closes and about halves the jump at two to four pixels of error; no published precedent |
| Fully smooth | Shading can jump though the shape does not | Weigh normals and colours in the simplifier's error | Read directly; the probes measured shape only |
| Down to a pixel | It ends at one cluster and tests every instance | Keep halving under the last cluster, then the dot, then the land's colour; accept a far thing's whole level from its distance alone | Probe and package, first-hand; the engine has the last two already |

**Crowns are the gap that matters most, and the simplifier fails on them by construction.** It can remove a vertex only by merging it along an edge; a sprig of two crossed cards shares no edge with any other sprig, so the only move left is to shrink it away, and the error it reports is a small distance while the eye sees a hole. Epic names the class ("aggregate geometry ... breaks Nanite's level of detail and occlusion culling techniques"), saw Fortnite's trees lose their canopies in the distance when it brought them to Nanite, added an area-restoring option that its API reference now labels a "legacy foliage technique", and since Unreal Engine 5.7 (November 2025) replaces it with pixel-sized voxels: a feature still marked experimental, its shading reported unfinished in 5.8 ([Epic: working with Nanite-enabled content](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content); [Epic: Nanite in Fortnite Chapter 4](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/bringing-nanite-to-fortnite-battle-royale-in-chapter-4); [Epic API: ENaniteShapePreservation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/ENaniteShapePreservation); [Epic: Nanite Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage); [Unreal forums](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/nanite-foliage-and-twosidedsign/2835970); all search extracts). The probe reproduced the failure on the game's holm oak: at a tenth of its triangles the crown covers **34 % of what it did from the side and 25 % from above**, and the simplifier ate the wood first, leaving 194 of 8,244 wood triangles at half the tree's count ([probes note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/probes_on_the_games_own_assets.md), first-hand).

The completion is about twenty years old and half-built in the engine already. Cook, Halstead, Planck and Ryu (Pixar, SIGGRAPH 2007) draw only a first share of the elements in a fixed random order and enlarge the survivors so the total area stays; SpeedTree's leaves do the same in real time ("fewer and bigger leaf instances"), as does the far grass of Ghost of Tsushima ([Pixar: Stochastic Simplification of Aggregate Detail](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/StochasticSimplification); [SpeedTree runtime manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/speedtree-runtime-sdk/manual/level-of-detail.html); [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027214); search extracts, and the paper's own formulae are recalled, unverified). On the oak, thinning the 3,300 sprigs in a fixed random order and enlarging each survivor **about its tip** held the cover at 99 to 101 % down to 4 % of the sprigs and at 98 to 102 % at 1 %. The enlargement the measurement found (1.44, 1.98, 2.92, 4.52 times) is nearly what keeping the area asks (1.41, 2.00, 3.16, 5.00), so the rule needs no table; enlarged about the foot by the area's factor, the crown swells by a third, because the sprigs grow out of it. The rule has no levels at all: one sprig comes or goes at a time, which is what the engine's `partGrown` already does between two hand-written levels. meshoptimizer's builder also carries the hierarchy's own patch for foliage, restoring lost area by dilating the open borders of whatever triangles survive (`simplify_dilate_borders`, the idea behind the option Epic now calls legacy), which its author describes as "most useful for foliage, should not be enabled everywhere" ([Kapoulkine, 2026](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md); [clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/demo/clusterlod.h); read directly); the probe tried the thinning rule only. The thinning rule's limits come with it. "Cover" is a model that takes 45 % of a card as filled and looks from two directions, not a rendered picture; the stone pine, with fewer and larger sprigs, came out less cleanly (113 % and 87 % at a tenth); and the outline grows as the sprigs get few (106 % at 4 %, 122 % at 1 %). Wood is two things: trunk and limbs are surfaces, to be simplified on a budget of their own (the probe did not try the wood alone), and twigs are a crowd, to be thinned and thickened the way Pixar thins hair ([Pixar: 500 Million Hairs](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/500MillionHairs/paper.pdf), search extract).

**Above one pixel of error something has to hide the change, and nobody has published how to do it inside a cluster hierarchy.** Two ways are known outside it. A slide moves every vertex of the finer mesh to its place on the coarser one before the change, costs no extra triangles, and is what terrain engines ship and what the engine's solids do today ([Strugar, CDLOD](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/blob/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf), read directly; [engine note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/the_engine_today.md), first-hand). A stipple draws both versions in a band, each on a complementary share of the pixels, and leaves the grain to the temporal filter; three-virtual-geometry switches it on by itself above 1.25 px of error by its type files, above 2.5 px by its guide ([package note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/three_virtual_geometry_read_first_hand.md), first-hand; [its guide](https://github.com/elad12390/three-virtual-geometry/blob/main/docs/guide/how-it-works.md), read directly). A look through the meshoptimizer, Bevy and Godot repositories and their authors' posts found no slide between cluster levels, though only two web searches ran before the budget ended, so the absence is weaker than it looks. The structure does not forbid one. Borders are locked and a group changes as a whole, which leaves a single condition, worked out in the research notes and stated by no source: a group must not begin to slide before every finer group that feeds it has changed, and that holds when each group's error stands above its children's by at least the width of the slide's window (1.25 times in the probe). The probe built exactly that on the boulder and walked up to it in 412 steps: **no cracks** (two clusters placed a shared vertex within 3 × 10⁻⁸ of the boulder's size), 11 % more triangles at 400 px, and the largest change in one step fell from 1.09 to 0.93 px at one pixel of error, from **2.72 to 1.47 px at two** and from **6.75 to 2.51 px at four**. It does not fall to nothing because the target is the nearest point of the simplified surface; the exact target is the simplifier's own record of which vertex was folded into which, and meshoptimizer returns only the new triangles ([probes note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/probes_on_the_games_own_assets.md), first-hand).

**The hierarchy stops at one cluster and counts every thing.** Nanite covers the rest with machinery outside it: tiny baked pictures for far instances (known here from one forum post and from recall only), a cap of 16 million instances, and a cost that grows with the count of things ([Epic: Nanite virtualized geometry](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-US/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine); [Karis et al., "Nanite: A Deep Dive"](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf); search extracts). Here the engine is ahead of the algorithm: what is under a pixel is not drawn, solids end as a dot, a sunk stand's colour passes to the land, and the tiers look at cells before things ([engine note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/the_engine_today.md), first-hand). Two small pieces close the seam. Keep halving under the last cluster: the probe went from 106 triangles to 52, 26, 12 and 6, and three-virtual-geometry has the same under the name `tailSimplify`. And accept or refuse a far thing's whole level from its distance alone, as that library does for anything of four clusters or fewer, so that the hundred thousand and more small things in view never reach a test per cluster ([package note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/three_virtual_geometry_read_first_hand.md), first-hand). For crowns the end needs repair rather than addition: today's far body covers 58 to 83 % of what the crown covers and its speck 29 to 63 %, where the thinning rule kept the cover down to 1 % of the sprigs, the smallest share the probe tried.

**Made things expose the simplifier's two blind spots.** Each of the bell tent's 1,396 faces owns its corners, and the simplifier can do nothing until they are welded by position, to 1,066 vertices; meshoptimizer 1.3's "permissive" mode, which lets collapses cross hard edges when normals are weighed in the error, is the newer route for flat-faced things ([meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/README.md), read directly). The canvas then halves at an error of 0.0007 of the tent's size. The eight guy ropes and eight pegs, though, vanish in single steps at a reported error of about 0.8 px when the tent is 170 px across, while their ends then lie 37 px from anything drawn; and with the error taken as that true distance instead, the ropes hold the whole tent at 688 triangles from 400 px down to 8 px, because they share their groups with the canvas ([probes note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/probes_on_the_games_own_assets.md), first-hand). Two remedies are on the table: a floor under the error from how far the simplified shape's box retreats, so that a version which lost its outline is drawn only once that loss is under the threshold (three-virtual-geometry's `extentShrink`), and loose parts built as hierarchies of their own.

## Open code covers the whole build; nobody has measured Safari on a phone

| Code | What it is | State in November 2026 | Its use here |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| meshoptimizer 1.3, MIT | Simplifier and cluster builders; `clusterlod.h` is the whole build loop in one C++ header | Mature; 1.3 of 25 September 2026; the simplifier inside Godot | Take it: it is the build |
| three-virtual-geometry 0.1.3, MIT | The whole method for three.js's WebGPU renderer with TSL compute passes | First published 8 October 2026; one author; static meshes only; no Safari or iPhone figure | Read it and measure with its live demo; do not depend on it |
| Scthe/nanite-webgpu, MIT | Browser demo: hierarchy built in the page, compute culling, a rasteriser on 32-bit atomics | 2024; Chrome only; no frame rates published | Shows what a 32-bit compute rasteriser costs in artefacts |
| Bevy "meshlets" | The most complete open system on wgpu, WebGPU's native relative | Experimental; native only, since it needs 64-bit atomics | The record of what goes wrong in a builder |
| NVIDIA nv_cluster_lod_builder, vk_lod_clusters | NVIDIA's own builder, and a Vulkan sample that uses `clusterlod.h` | Current; mesh shaders or a compute rasteriser | The clearest written account of the rules |
| Godot 4 mesh LOD | Whole-object levels from the same simplifier, one threshold of one pixel | Shipping | The recipe for the fallback |

**meshoptimizer is the one maintained open toolkit that holds every building block.** Since version 1.0 (December 2025) it ships the whole loop as a single C++ header, and version 1.3 is current and MIT-licensed ([meshoptimizer releases](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/releases), read directly). Natively it processes a scene of 1.6 billion triangles in about 2.5 to 3.75 minutes on 16 cores ([Kapoulkine, 2025](https://zeux.io/2025/09/30/billions-of-triangles-in-minutes/); [Kapoulkine, 2026](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/master/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md); read directly), roughly half a million source triangles a second for each thread by the notes' arithmetic. Its npm package exposes the simplifier and the cluster builders to JavaScript through WebAssembly, but **neither the grouping function nor `clusterlod.h`** ([meshopt_clusterizer.js](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/js/meshopt_clusterizer.js), read directly). The probe shows that is no obstacle: with the stock package and a greedy grouping of a few dozen lines it built 267,000 to 400,000 triangles a second on one thread, the same order as the native figure ([probes note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/probes_on_the_games_own_assets.md), first-hand). Compiling `clusterlod.h` to a private WebAssembly module would add its fallbacks for stubborn meshes; no published build of that kind was found. Bevy's public history is the record of what a builder gets wrong first: lock only the vertices shared between groups, never every open border; group eight or more clusters, not four; and give a group that will not simplify a defined fate ([JMS55, virtual geometry in Bevy 0.15](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-11-14-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-15/), read directly).

**three-virtual-geometry is the method on the engine's exact stack**: three.js's WebGPU renderer, TSL compute passes, storage buffers, indirect draws, meshoptimizer for the build, and builds cached in the browser. Its author reports 6.5 to 9.0 million triangles drawn a frame at 160 to 230 frames a second at 1080p on a MacBook Pro with an M4 Pro in Chrome 154, with hardware rasterisation only and "at most 0.6 ms of CPU time per frame". Nothing here confirms those figures. The package was read, not run; it was first published on 8 October 2026 by a single author, has three stars, takes static meshes only, and names Safari 26 as supported without publishing a measurement in Safari or on any phone ([package note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/three_virtual_geometry_read_first_hand.md), first-hand; [its repository](https://github.com/elad12390/three-virtual-geometry)). Its value is twofold. It proves the whole method fits inside three.js's public interface, and its design answers in code most of the questions this report raises: a fixed 384 corners a cluster, a fast path for far instances, the tail, the error floor, the pairing of leaf pieces. It cannot be dropped in, because TheGame's solids carry bones, wind, snow, their own 16-byte vertex and their own shading models, and are placed by compute passes of the engine's own.

**Safari's WebGPU has what selection needs and lacks what pixel-sized triangles need.** Compute passes, storage buffers and single indirect draws are core WebGPU and on by default in Safari 26 on macOS and iOS, and three.js 0.187 issues `drawIndirect` and `drawIndexedIndirect` directly, several from one buffer if asked ([gpuweb implementation status](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/Implementation-Status); [three.js WebGPUBackend.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/renderers/webgpu/WebGPUBackend.js); read directly). Three things are missing everywhere as a standard: multi-draw indirect (an experiment in Chrome only, by a search extract; the working group's investigation is closed as not planned), mesh shaders (set aside until after version 1) and 64-bit atomics (a proposal text exists; no browser is known to ship it) ([gpuweb issue 4349](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/issues/4349); [gpuweb issue 3015](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/issues/3015); [atomic-64 proposal](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/blob/main/proposals/atomic-64-min-max.md); read directly). So a cut can be chosen entirely on the GPU and drawn in a handful of calls, but through the hardware rasteriser and under a budget of triangles. The one open browser demo that rasterises in compute on 32-bit atomics squeezes depth into 16 bits and reports that this "produces tons of artifacts" ([Scthe/nanite-webgpu](https://github.com/Scthe/nanite-webgpu), read directly).

**What small triangles cost on Apple's GPUs is not published.** Apple's own talk, as the notes quote it, says only that every triangle of a pass is shaded and binned into a "Tiled Vertex Buffer" that "may cause a Partial Render if full" ([Apple, WWDC20 session 10602](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10602/)), which suggests that the quantity to hold down on these GPUs is triangles submitted a frame, more than pixels covered. Two data points exist for Macs and none for phones: the library author's figures above, and the engine's own measurement of 13 November 2026, 1.5 million triangles in 5.4 ms of GPU time on an M1, in WebKit under Playwright, while walking among a million things ([engine README](../TheGame/src/detail/README.md), first-hand). A Nanite-like system built natively for phones does exist, and it scales its threshold by device ([Tencent NanoMesh, SIGGRAPH 2024](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Cao-NanoMesh/AdavanceRealtimeRendering_NanoMesh0810.pdf), search extract; its threshold formula as a third party transcribed it in [Scthe/nanite-webgpu](https://github.com/Scthe/nanite-webgpu), read directly).

## The engine already owns the drawing half; the work is the build, the cut and the thinning

The engine already has most of what the method needs at run time. It measures everything by size on screen in pixels, through one lens constant and one dial that the governor turns. It draws through one heap, with lists packed by compute passes and draws counted on the GPU. Its draw by "class of size" reads fixed-length runs of corners from the heap, close kin to three-virtual-geometry's fixed 384 corners a cluster, and its packing per thing is that library's instance pass. Its solids' vertex already stores the place on the next coarser mesh. It has a temporal pass, workers that make meshes off the page's thread, and a store that keeps them between starts. Its plants already hand over sprig by sprig, and its far end, the dot and the land's colour, exists ([engine note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/the_engine_today.md); [package note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/three_virtual_geometry_read_first_hand.md); first-hand). What is written per asset or per kind today is four lists: the ladder's eight pixel sizes, each kind's pixel sizes, each tree's tables per level, and each made thing's faces per level. What is missing is a builder that needs none of them.

**The recommendation is to adopt both rules and to end both in what the engine already has.** Everything that is a surface (rocks, the stand's bodies, tents, buildings, trunks and limbs) gets a cluster hierarchy, built from its full mesh in the recipes' workers with meshoptimizer. Everything that is a crowd of small parts (sprigs now, grass later) gets a fixed order and the enlargement rule. Both end in the dot and the land's colour. Two engine-wide numbers in pixels take the place of the four lists: the error allowed on a surface, which becomes the governor's dial, and the size a sprig may shrink to before sprigs start to go. The reasons are these. It is the only method found that meets the brief for a thing larger than the screen: a whole-object level draws all ten million triangles when the camera stands at the asset, where the cut drew 704,156, with 171,755 in view. It beat the ladder on the game's own boulder, both in error for the triangles spent and in the size of its steps. Its build fits a worker and an MIT library. And the engine's drawing path already has its shape.

Three alternatives deserve a hearing. *Levels from the recipe* are the engine's present way for solids and the principle of film renderers, which dice every surface into pieces about half a pixel across for the current view ([Cook, Carpenter, Catmull, "The Reyes Image Rendering Architecture"](https://my.eng.utah.edu/~cs5610/handouts/reyes.pdf), search extract). They are cheap from the coarse end and can filter noise properly, but every generator must honour the contract, levels made independently do not correspond to each other, and a shape that is no function over a sphere, a tent, a building or a tree, has no sampling rate to turn down ([engine note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/the_engine_today.md), first-hand). They keep a place as an optional shortcut at a first start, described below. *Whole-object levels made automatically*, as Godot ships them with the same simplifier and a threshold of one pixel ([Godot importer_mesh.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/scene/resources/3d/importer_mesh.cpp), read directly), need nothing new in the drawer but hold one level for a whole thing. They are the fallback, and they come free with the same build, since every level of the hierarchy is a complete mesh. *A stand-in for the far field* is either a baked picture, about 11 MB a kind at the engine's own quality ([engine README](../TheGame/src/detail/README.md), first-hand), or voxels, which are experimental even at Epic.

**First, measure; the result decides the shape of everything after.** Open three-virtual-geometry's [live demo](https://elad12390.github.io/three-virtual-geometry/live), which its README links and which was not opened in this session, in Safari on an Apple-silicon Mac and on an iPhone: it is the quickest way to learn whether Safari draws a cluster cut at 60 frames a second at all, and with how many triangles. Then run a spike in the engine itself: the probe's builder (`probes/dag.mjs`) moved into the recipes' worker for the boulders, their cut drawn through the drawer, and the GPU's time read with `?gputime` in Safari on both devices, against the ladder on the same stand. The spike should compare two ways to draw a cluster: fixed runs of 384 corners looked up in the heap, as the drawer's class of size does today, and corners written by a compute pass and drawn indexed, as three-virtual-geometry does. The engine has measured that a vertex looked up by its corner is worked out six times, and Bevy found the indexed form 10 to 20 % faster and padded fixed-size draws slow ([engine README](../TheGame/src/detail/README.md), first-hand; [JMS55, virtual geometry in Bevy 0.14](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-06-09-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-14/), read directly); against that, the probe's clusters are 95 % full on average (my arithmetic from its counts), so padding wastes little. The size of a cluster is a third thing to vary: 128 triangles follows from Unreal's software rasteriser, and an engine that draws through the hardware rasteriser is not bound to it (the notes' inference). The gate is the engine's own: 60 frames a second on the iPhone, at an error that looks right.

**Then solids and made things move onto the hierarchy.** The builder replaces `assets/kit/ladder.js` and every `detail.levels[].faces`; made things are welded first; each cluster carries its error and sphere and its parent's. The tier's packing pass keeps choosing per thing and accepts a far thing's whole level from its distance. Only things large on screen go on to a second pass that tests their clusters: Bevy's renderer, which dispatched a thread for every cluster of every instance, found that pass its bottleneck and ran into a cap of 2²⁵ clusters ([JMS55, virtual geometry in Bevy 0.15](https://jms55.github.io/posts/2024-11-14-virtual-geometry-bevy-0-15/), read directly). Normals are weighed in the error, the error gets its floor from lost outline, and the hierarchy is built with errors growing 1.25 times a level, so that vertices can slide wherever the threshold stands above one pixel. The top of every kind's hierarchy is always held and the rest streams in for the kinds near, as the heap's pools do today.

**Crowns can start at once, because nothing in them waits for the gate.** Each shape's sprigs go into one list in a fixed order, spread evenly through the crown rather than plainly random so that every share is a fair picture of it ([Pixar: Stochastic Pruning](https://graphics.pixar.com/library/StochasticPruning/paper.pdf), search extract). By the notes' proposal, the share drawn falls with size on screen so that a surviving sprig keeps about the same size in pixels; each survivor is enlarged about its tip by one over the square root of the share, as the probe found; and the wood keeps a budget of its own. That retires `levels: [{ P, most }]`, the `grow` tables and the plants' lists of pixel sizes. **Third comes the far end**: the tail under one cluster, the last sprigs in place of the solid far body, and the land's colour computed from the same cover and colour as the stand it replaces; the one forest renderer found that claims to run "without popping" likewise ends its far trees as a shader on the terrain ([Bruneton and Neyret, 2012](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v31i2pp373-382), search extract). **Last comes the ten-million asset**: streaming by groups of clusters, and a build that fits a phone's memory. What is measured for it is the build (25 s on one thread) and the cut (55,574 triangles at 400 px); what is not is holding its 278 MB and building it on a phone. It is the least proven part and needs the first step in place. If the gate fails on the iPhone, phones draw whole levels of the same hierarchy through today's drawer: still no level written by any asset, at the price of detail that cannot vary inside one thing.

One consequence needs a decision before the first step. The hierarchy is built from the fine end, so nothing of a kind can be drawn from it until its full mesh has been made on the device: about 0.7 to 0.9 s a kind for a 110,000-triangle boulder on one thread, by the probe's times for mesh and build together, or eleven to fifteen minutes of one thread for a thousand kinds, the scale the stress stand tests (my arithmetic). Today the always-held coarse levels come straight from the recipe on a coarse grid and never need the full mesh. three-virtual-geometry's author reports "a few minutes" to build 13 rock scans in the browser on a first visit ([its caching guide](https://github.com/elad12390/three-virtual-geometry/blob/main/docs/guide/caching.md), read directly). Builds are kept between starts, so the cost falls on a device's first meeting with a kind; what a kind looks like until then is Samuel's to decide.

## What could still go wrong, and seven decisions that are Samuel's

**The largest risk is the unmeasured one.** No frame of a cluster cut was drawn in this session, the only published figures are one author's on an M4 Pro in Chrome, from a library five weeks old that may change or stop, and no source measures small triangles on an iPhone's GPU. The engine has little slack for a surprise: its own README records the pixel ratio falling to 0.75 in Safari while walking among the stand's materials, and the plots not yet holding the gate ([engine README](../TheGame/src/detail/README.md), first-hand). The first step exists to turn this risk into a number, and the whole-level fallback exists in case the number is bad.

**The probes are narrow by design.** One boulder shape, one oak, one pine, one tent; shape error only, with no normals, shading, texture seams or wind; a greedy grouping written for the probe; build times from one cloud core under Node, where a phone will be slower and several workers faster. The engine itself was read in its code, its two largest files in part, not measured running. The simplifier's error is an estimate, not a bound: asked for one pixel, the worst vertex stayed within 0.86 to 1.10 px at the sizes tabled above, but over the whole walk the worst was 1.2 px at a target of one pixel, 3.0 at two and 9.1 at four ([probe output](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/probes/out/boulder-moving.txt), first-hand). A threshold above one pixel therefore needs a safety factor or a measured check per level, and weighing normals in the error is likely to raise the triangle counts above the probe's.

**Smoothness above one pixel is new ground.** Sliding vertices inside a cluster hierarchy has no published precedent. The probe shows that it closes and that it helps, with jumps of up to 1.5 px left at a threshold of two pixels; the exact version needs the simplifier's record of its collapses, which means patching meshoptimizer or rebuilding that record.

**Memory and the first start grow before they shrink.** The hierarchy is 1.4 times the full mesh. Ten million triangles make 278 MB, more than the 128 MiB a single storage binding holds by default, and the probe built them under Node with the memory ceiling raised to 12 GB, without recording what it used. No source says how much a Safari tab on an iPhone may hold in GPU buffers before it is closed.

**Hard meshes and bending things are untested here.** Flat-faced buildings with a seam at every edge, thin parts, and anything on bones or in wind have only the tent's evidence, and the tent's is mixed. If the stock package stalls on them, the remedy is a private WebAssembly build of `clusterlod.h`, which nobody has published. In Unreal, bone-driven foliage shipped as experimental in 5.7 ([Unreal Engine 5.7 release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-7-release-notes), search extract), and three-virtual-geometry takes static meshes only.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question more than it answers it. Levels turn out not to be a property of assets at all: under both rules an asset says what it is at full detail, and the screen says how much of it to draw. The line that matters does not run between rocks, trees and tents but between a surface and a crowd of parts, and every asset in the game is one, the other, or a sum of both: a tree is wood plus sprigs, a tent is canvas plus ropes. The engine had already chosen the right measure, the right drawing path and the right far end. What it lacked was a builder that asks nothing of the asset.

The request for *fully smooth* reaches past the state of the art. The published systems hide their steps under a pixel, with rasterisers Safari cannot run, so on this platform the engine's habit of moving vertices stops being a refinement and becomes the piece that would let a phone run at two pixels of error without showing it. That piece is unpublished and half-proven by one probe, and it is worth owning. Three outside events would change the plan: 64-bit atomics reaching Safari's WebGPU, which would make a compute rasteriser possible; Epic's voxels leaving experimental, which would give crowns a tested far end; and a Safari measurement of three-virtual-geometry by anyone. Until then, the next fact to get is the one that only Samuel's own devices can give.
