# Four demands outrun every published method

**No.** No published method does all four, and the most mature one, Unreal Engine's Nanite, read here from its authors' own slides and from Epic's documentation up to the 5.9 preview, does none of them as stated. It reached each kind of asset through a mechanism of its own over nine releases and still has a person mark foliage mesh by mesh; it swaps clusters at once and trusts an estimated error under one pixel plus a temporal filter to hide the swap, while admitting pops from late streaming and from its far stand-ins; it claims no quality for crowds of small parts; and its published 4.5 ms were measured at about 1400p upsampled to 4K, with a compute rasteriser that Safari cannot run and a hard cap of 16 million instances where the stress world places 898 million. What the originals and the measurements on the game's own assets support instead is a method of six parts: one builder that is told nothing, tries several ways of making each group of triangles coarser (folding edges, thinning a crowd and enlarging what stays, a hull) and keeps the one that loses least; one measure of loss that is a difference between pictures, because every published builder's error is a length and a length cannot weigh "moved" against "gone"; one rule for what to draw, in the pixels actually drawn; one way to change, in which every vertex and every part knows its place on the coarser stand-in, so that steps, the zoom, a late arrival and the governor's dial all move the picture without a jump; a far stand-in that never waits, with the stress world's rule of keeping nothing per thing; and a drawing path shaped by WebKit's source and Apple's tile-based GPUs. **Proven so far, in scripts and not in the game:** the cut closes and follows the screen up to ten million triangles, and a builder told nothing read nearer to full detail than today's hand-written levels in 16 of 21 rows on average and in 20 by outline, holding an oak's outline at 91 to 104 % where today's runs from 113 % down to 42 %. **Unproven is most of what the last two demands rest on:** nothing was drawn by the game or on a GPU, nothing was measured in motion, nobody has published a measurement of a cluster hierarchy in Safari, "higher than today" fails at a few sizes, a thinned crown still differs from the full one by 14 to 24 % in a small patch, and thin loose parts and anything on bones have no shown answer. The first things to measure are therefore numbers on Samuel's own devices in Safari that need none of the new method, then the picture measure against his eye, then a recorded walk and zoom over today's levels.

**How to read the evidence.** Unmarked statements below were *read directly*: the source itself was opened in this session, which this time includes both of Brian Karis's Nanite decks page by page (155 and 148 pages, slides and speaker notes), Epic's documentation and release notes from 5.0 to the 5.9 preview, the open builders and the three.js library in their source at named commits, about forty papers and slide decks on crowds of small parts, WebKit's source and Apple's developer pages. *First-hand* marks what was measured or read in the game's own repository: a script run on the game's boulder, tent, oak, pine and shrub, and the zoom and the stress world read in the code. The first-hand notes carry their own limits, which are kept here: nothing was drawn by the game or on a GPU, and every result is a still. *Search extract* marks what is known only from a search engine's digest of a page, and *recalled* what comes from a researcher's memory. Three bodies of evidence stay at those two levels and are marked where used: Epic's blog posts on Fortnite (the host refuses plain requests), Pixar's 2007 paper and its memos (the host was refused), and Unreal's source code (licence-gated). *Estimate* marks a researcher's own reckoning from other people's figures. One figure is left out because two of this round's notes read the same Epic page differently (the size of one mesh with and without Nanite).

## No published method meets any of the four demands as stated

The four demands are: any asset of any kind at full detail, with nothing told about it (1); no visible change from the nearest view to 12 km, walking or zooming (2); a better picture than today's hand-written levels (3); and the gates of 60 frames a second, 8 GB and a 4K canvas, in the stress world too (4). The systems that come nearest descend from one idea, the cluster hierarchy: a mesh is cut into clusters of about a hundred triangles, groups of neighbouring clusters are simplified to half again and again, and each frame a cluster is drawn when its own error is under a threshold in pixels and its coarser replacement's is not. The table sets four of them beside the demands. Each cell is sourced in the paragraphs under it, and every frame rate is its author's own.

Any asset, nothing told | No level is authored, but a person still sets Preserve Area or Voxelize, an edge-length factor and tangents per mesh and a maximum vertex offset per material; each kind of asset has its own code path | One loop told nothing, for surfaces; seams, attribute weights, border dilation, fold preservation and regularising are the caller's choice; NVIDIA ships overrides per mesh name | Chooses by itself among folded triangles, pieces thinned in pairs and a voxel hull, by comparing errors; its own jungle demo sets pruning, hulls, draw distance and a 3 px threshold by hand | Six fixed ratios for a whole object; opaque and alpha-tested meshes whose materials leave the geometry alone |

### Generic over the shape of a surface, not over what a thing is

Karis set himself, for surfaces, the requirement Samuel sets now: "Support importing meshes authored anywhere", with "No assumptions about the mesh's uniformity, or anything about it being closed or having thickness" ([Deck p.8](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=8); [HPG p.59](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=59)). The 2021 system met it for one kind of thing: "Focused on rigid geometry first: >90% of the geometry", with "Translucent or masked materials" and "Non-rigid deformation, skeletal animation, etc" unsupported, and the admission "Not great with aggregates: Many tiny things becoming a porous volume / Grass, leaves, hair" ([Deck p.146](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=146)). **Every kind since has arrived through a mechanism of its own**: a programmable rasteriser for masked, two-sided and vertex-animated materials in 5.1, spline deformation in 5.3, run-time tessellation in 5.4, skinning in 5.5, assemblies and voxels for foliage in 5.7, and in the 5.9 preview a split of all instances into a "simple" and a "complex" culling class. In November 2026 tessellation, baked displacement and the whole of Nanite Foliage are still marked Experimental, vertex-animated foliage is "Beta", morph targets are unsupported, and translucency exists only behind a console variable that is off by default ([Epic: Nanite Virtualized Geometry](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine); [5.7 release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-7-release-notes?application_version=5.7); [5.9 preview release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-9-release-notes); [Epic: Nanite Technical Details](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)). What Epic removed is the level authored by hand, not the word about the asset. A person still sets Preserve Area ("This setting should be enabled on all foliage meshes and nothing else") or Voxelize per mesh, an edge-length factor per mesh that will be deformed, explicit tangents per asset and a maximum vertex offset per material ([Epic: Nanite Technical Details](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details); [Epic: Working with Nanite-Enabled Content](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)). Karis's own lesson, in the 2022 keynote, is a slide titled "The unification trap", told after Epic's "Geometry 2.0" project ("One geometry unified solution / Over constrained! / On hold"). His plan reads "Do not try and solve 'Everything Everywhere All at Once'", and the talk ends "Nanite approaches the dream but does not completely fulfill it" ([HPG p.68](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=68), [p.69](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=69), [p.84](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=84), [p.141](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=141)).

The open builder comes nearer to "told nothing" and stops at the same line. meshoptimizer's `clusterlod.h`, 962 lines and unchanged since the 1.3 release, is one loop that never learns what kind of mesh it holds: cluster, group, lock the vertices shared between groups, simplify each group to half in a mode that lets collapses cross hard edges, fall back to a cruder simplifier when the target is missed, and declare a group finished when less than 15 % could be removed ([clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L799-L869)). The kind still enters through five choices its caller makes: which seams to protect, the weight of each attribute, whether to dilate open borders ("most useful for foliage, should not be enabled everywhere"), whether to preserve folds, and whether to regularise for meshes that deform ([clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L15-L74)). NVIDIA's maintained sample, built on a lightly modified copy of that header, says so in one sentence: "The simplification settings are global, which is not always enough: foliage wants different weights than a solid surface". It ships a file of overrides matched to mesh names, sets the weight of texture coordinates to 0.5 by default and to 16 for one scene, and switches dilation on wherever a material is two-sided, "as a proxy for 'foliage'" in the words of the header's author ([vk_lod_clusters: scene processing](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/vk_lod_clusters/blob/04e5b44f0d2f6376a2edf60702165e38c44f972c/docs/scene_processing.md?plain=1#L6-L33); [its override for one scene](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/vk_lod_clusters/blob/04e5b44f0d2f6376a2edf60702165e38c44f972c/scene_overrides/zorah_textured_public.v1.cfg); [Kapoulkine, 2026](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/a9dc7b0dba6489288f84ba532dfcb37fb416cad9/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md?plain=1#L120)).

Two published rules do choose by themselves, and both have the same form. three-virtual-geometry, the five-week-old library on the game's exact stack, builds up to three candidates (the collapsed triangles; the loose pieces thinned in pairs with the kept piece widened; and, for the coarse end, a hull extracted from a voxel grid), gives each an error as a length in the asset's own units and keeps the smallest. Nothing asks what the asset is ([buildVirtualMesh.ts](https://github.com/elad12390/three-virtual-geometry/blob/6c1ccdb63c237e07a8a2e30ec88beecc0dc795be/src/core/preprocess/buildVirtualMesh.ts#L237-L239); [aggregate.ts](https://github.com/elad12390/three-virtual-geometry/blob/6c1ccdb63c237e07a8a2e30ec88beecc0dc795be/src/core/preprocess/aggregate.ts#L103-L110); [its guide](https://github.com/elad12390/three-virtual-geometry/blob/6c1ccdb63c237e07a8a2e30ec88beecc0dc795be/docs/guide/how-it-works.md?plain=1#L18-L27)). Epic's builder decides between triangles and voxels cluster by cluster the same way: "it will switch to voxels if doing so would result in lower error than using triangles" ([Epic: Nanite Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)). Neither is generic in use. Epic runs its rule only on meshes a person has set to Voxelize. The library's own jungle demo builds its textured trees with `{ prune: true, voxelLods: false }`, because "voxel stand-ins would lose the leaf textures", gives them a draw distance of 250 m and starts the scene at a threshold of three pixels ([forestScene.ts](https://github.com/elad12390/three-virtual-geometry/blob/6c1ccdb63c237e07a8a2e30ec88beecc0dc795be/examples/demo/forestScene.ts#L279-L291)). **The decisions still made by hand are exactly those where a length does not capture what is lost**: area, the content of a texture, shading across a crease. Roblox, which must take whatever its users make, reports the same frontier in 2026: a simplifier that "keeps disconnected parts disconnected" with "a penalty for area loss", and stand-ins beyond triangles listed as "research directions, not shipping features" ([Roblox, SIGGRAPH 2026](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2026/content/SMAK_SIG26_SLIM-Scaling_User-Generated_3D_Worlds_on_Roblox_8_Aug_2026.pdf)).

### "Invisible" stands on two legs, and nothing moves

One slide of the 2021 deck, "Seamless LOD", carries the whole argument: "Binary choice either parent or child / Won't this visibly pop? / Need smooth transitions? Geomorphing / Cross fading / If < 1 pixel error, they are imperceptibly different / Temporal AA sees any difference as aliasing". The notes under it decline both smooth alternatives on cost, not on looks ("That would be expensive at render time or require significant additional data or both"), and name the condition: "TAA is built to blend subpixel differences over time. It does our work for us so long as the error is subpixel" ([Deck p.67](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=67)). **Both legs are weaker than the word "imperceptible" suggests.** The error is an estimate that Karis calls a "Complete heuristic hack" with "no theoretical foundation", the fruit of "probably a man year", which "definitely isn't perfect and can fail although it's rare to be noticable" ([Deck p.55](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=55), [p.56](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=56), [p.57](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=57), [p.58](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=58)). In general it needs triangles the size of a pixel ("Pixel sized features need pixel sized triangles to represent them without visible error"), which is why Nanite's compute rasteriser exists ([Deck p.79](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=79)). And whether the filter would be enough, "Popping? TAA enough to hide?", is on Karis's list of unknowns that "luckily worked out" ([HPG p.132](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=132)). Neither deck contains a measurement of how visible a switch is, a statement about a threshold above one pixel other than a default of two for shadows, or a statement about switching without a temporal filter ([decks note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/nanite_decks_read_directly.md)).

The authors name where a change does show. The baked stand-ins for tiny instances: "switching to it can sometimes be noticed as a pop" ([Deck p.97](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=97)); Epic made building them optional and off by default in 5.1 ([5.1 release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5.1-release-notes?application_version=5.1)). Detail that arrives late: Epic's settings offer a minimum of always-resident data because it "can mitigate streaming pop-in issues", and warn that with too small a pool "streaming never settles even for a static view" ([Epic: Nanite Technical Details](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)). Geometry thinner than a pixel, which flickers under the temporal filter until a separate detection pass relaxes it ([Epic: Thin Geometry Detection](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/thin-geometry-detection-with-temporal-super-resolution)). The author of the open builder, who is himself "still not sure" that all engines should embrace clustered detail, adds the plain observation: "if you've played recent games you might have noticed geometry popping in cutscenes now and again" ([Kapoulkine, 2026](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/a9dc7b0dba6489288f84ba532dfcb37fb416cad9/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md?plain=1#L160)). For foliage he names a pop that follows from the method itself. Once every leaf is a single triangle, every cluster reaches the same error at the same distance, and "many clusters will flip to a coarser LOD at once, causing visible pop - and, eventually, complete erosion", an act of "coordinated omission" ([meshoptimizer commit 7d8eca5](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/commit/7d8eca58); [Kapoulkine, 2026](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/a9dc7b0dba6489288f84ba532dfcb37fb416cad9/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md?plain=1#L114)).

Two published mechanisms exist for a change above one pixel: one in a library five weeks old, the other from 1998 and outside cluster systems. three-virtual-geometry draws both levels inside a band and lets a noise pattern pick one per pixel. Its guide is candid about the result: at one pixel "changes of detail are hard to see", at two to three "Detail changes start to show while moving", and without a temporal filter a cluster in transition "looks like a fine, even stipple" ([its settings guide](https://github.com/elad12390/three-virtual-geometry/blob/6c1ccdb63c237e07a8a2e30ec88beecc0dc795be/docs/guide/settings.md?plain=1#L11-L57)). Hoppe's geomorph moves each vertex of the finer mesh out of its place on the coarser one, so that no face appears at more than zero area, and his 1998 results are the strongest published support for the engine's own habit: "Although the model may at times have a projected geometric error of a few pixels, results indicate that geomorphs make this error nearly imperceptible" ([Hoppe, 1998](https://hhoppe.com/svdlod.pdf)). Hoppe's meshes change vertex by vertex, which Karis rejects for instanced geometry: "making decisions per vertex is excessive", and the per-instance state "kills instancing and is simply too much memory" ([HPG p.49](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=49), [p.50](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=50)). **No source read combines a cluster hierarchy with a geomorph.** Batched Multi-Triangulation, the 2005 paper Nanite builds on, mentions geomorphing once, as other authors' technique, and its sister paper relies on "very small pixel thresholds ... without the need to resort to costly geomorphing features" ([Cignoni et al., 2005](https://vcg.isti.cnr.it/Publications/2005/CGGMPS05/BatchedMT_Vis05.pdf); [Cignoni et al., 2004](https://vcg.isti.cnr.it/Publications/2004/CGGMPS04/vbdam_sig04.pdf)). Karis designed a morphing hierarchy before Nanite and never built it ("the majority of what I'm telling you about only existed in my head"); he notes that its main flaw, needing a whole band of detail loaded, falls to "a clamp to the geomorph" ([HPG p.21](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=21), [p.29](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=29), [p.47](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=47)).

The zoom sharpens all of this. The engine's one camera runs from 1.7 m to 12 km on a single value that the wheel moves without a step, so on a quick scroll every thing in view passes through all its levels within a second or two, and zooming in asks for the fine detail of things that were far a moment ago, before any of it is built ([zoom and stress-world note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/the_zoom_and_the_stress_world_today.md), first-hand). A part that arrives late is by definition more than the threshold off, or it would not have been asked for. A system whose only device is a swap under a pixel has nothing for that case, and it is the pop-in Epic names. The engine's present rule is the opposite, "Nothing of it changes at once": a solid's vertices move to their places on the next coarser mesh, sprigs come and go one after another, and a mesh that comes late "grows in over a second and a half" ([engine README](../TheGame/src/detail/README.md), first-hand). The only swap left today is the made things': the tent and the fire pit change whole from one frame to the next.

### Higher quality is claimed for the near view, not for crowds

Epic's claim of quality is specific: "Loss of quality is rare or non-existent, especially with LOD transitions", and "move the camera close enough and Nanite will draw the original source triangles that were imported" ([Epic: Nanite Virtualized Geometry](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine)). The finest level is the asset itself and the steps between are small; that is the sense in which it stands above a hand-made chain. It is not made for crowds of small parts: "This type of geometry breaks Nanite's level of detail and occlusion culling techniques", in the same words in the 5.0 and the 5.9 documentation ([Epic: Working with Nanite-Enabled Content](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)). For the voxels meant to mend it, the only statement of quality is "imperceptible to the eye because of their size on screen", with no error figure and no test image. The released engine picks one normal at random per pixel; the filtered shading "was already in use in the Witcher 4 demo" and is promised for "a future release" on a page last updated in June 2026 ([Epic: Nanite Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)). NVIDIA lists for its own sample: "The vegetation will appear to fade out a bit quickly, especially the grass. ... We do not use any techniques that preserve volume during decimation" ([vk_lod_clusters: scenes](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/vk_lod_clusters/blob/04e5b44f0d2f6376a2edf60702165e38c44f972c/docs/scenes.md?plain=1#L55-L61)). Both cluster builders patch foliage by pushing out the open borders of what survives, so that each coarser level has "fewer, bigger, leaves" (Kapoulkine's words for Unreal's version): the change of style Samuel names, built in. Of his own version Kapoulkine warns that "the shape of trees far away may deform a bit" and that on card-based trees it "may result in odd looking branches from far away if they were baked into the texture"; the game's trees are cards ([Kapoulkine, 2026](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/a9dc7b0dba6489288f84ba532dfcb37fb416cad9/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md?plain=1#L118-L126)). The one measured comparison found sets plain thinning well below a stand-in fitted to pictures: with nine tenths of a Disney Moana plant's leaves removed and the rest enlarged, the picture reaches **14.49 dB** against the reference, where quads optimised against pictures reach **23.89 dB** with a twenty-fifth of the triangles ([Hasselgren et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03989)). No source publishes a picture measurement of a cluster hierarchy against hand-made levels. "Near-seamless" and "mostly looks fine" are their authors' eyes on their own test scenes ([builders' note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/open_builders_read_directly.md)).

### No published frame time is from Safari, and none is at the stress world's scale

The one frame Epic published in full drew 25,041,711 triangles in 199,420 clusters, culled and rasterised in "~2.5ms" with "~2ms" more for materials, at an "Average ~2496x1404 upsampled to 4k" ([Deck p.109](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=109), [p.110](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=110)). The deck does not name the hardware on those pages; Epic's documentation for 5.5 gives the same figures for a PlayStation 5 ([Epic: Nanite, 5.5 version](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine?application_version=5.5)). Of the 191,514 clusters visible in the main pass, 184,828 went through the compute rasteriser, which is "3x faster than hardware on average" and rests on a 64-bit atomic write ("Without that we wouldn't be able to do fast software rasterization") ([Deck p.81](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=81), [p.84](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=84)). Karis's hindsight slide adds that "Nanite scales with **output** resolution pixels" ([HPG p.140](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=140)). By a search extract of Epic's own post, the one shipped game in the record, Fortnite, ran at "an average internal resolution of 55%-60% of 4k" ([Epic tech blog](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/tech-blog/lumen-brings-real-time-global-illumination-to-fortnite-battle-royale-chapter-4), search extract). **None of Epic's published figures is for a picture drawn at 4K.**

Safari has none of the three things that desktop designs lean on. Its WebGPU, shipped in Safari 26.0 on 15 September 2025, has compute passes, storage buffers, 32-bit atomics and single indirect draws; neither any released version nor WebKit's main branch has a call for many draws at once, mesh shaders or a 64-bit atomic ([WebKit: Safari 26.0](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/); [WebKit: GPUFeatureName.idl](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebCore/Modules/WebGPU/GPUFeatureName.idl)). The standard gained an optional 64-bit atomic minimum and maximum in September 2026, but Apple's own tables allow it only from the M2 on macOS and from the A17 Pro on iPhones: it can never exist on an M1 ([WebGPU specification source](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/blob/main/spec/index.bs); [Apple: Metal Feature Set Tables](https://developer.apple.com/metal/Metal-Feature-Set-Tables.pdf)). three.js 0.187 ships a compute rasteriser that makes do with two 32-bit words, at the price of 16 bits of depth, no alpha test and no motion output, so it serves neither leaf cards nor a temporal pass ([three.js: VisibilityBufferDrawingPipeline](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/VisibilityBufferDrawingPipeline.html)).

Scale is the second mismatch. Nanite's scene is "hard-locked to 16 million instances", each with a stored transform ("10M * float4x3 = 457MB"), and Karis's hindsight list says "Instance transforms don't LOD" ([Epic: Nanite Virtualized Geometry](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine); [Deck p.96](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=96); [HPG p.140](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/slides22/Journey_to_Nanite.pdf#page=140)). By the deck's arithmetic the stress world's 898 million placed things would need about 40 GB of transforms alone (my arithmetic). The stress world holds them within the gates because it keeps nothing per thing: a plot is a row of numbers, its million a lattice "worked out on the GPU for the cells a tier looks at", and "no mover has a state to keep" ([zoom and stress-world note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/the_zoom_and_the_stress_world_today.md), first-hand). For its nine million movers, each a mesh on sixteen bones, the published record is thin. Nanite has skinned meshes since 5.5, puts every skinned mesh and every instance that "moved this frame" on the slower of its two culling paths, and does not support morph targets; three-virtual-geometry takes "Static meshes" only ([Epic: Nanite Virtualized Geometry](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine); [Epic: Nanite Technical Details](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details); [three-virtual-geometry README](https://github.com/elad12390/three-virtual-geometry/blob/6c1ccdb63c237e07a8a2e30ec88beecc0dc795be/README.md?plain=1#L145-L149)).

Measurements on Apple's GPUs exist, all by the authors of the software and none of a cluster hierarchy in Safari. three-virtual-geometry: 9.0 million triangles plus 2.1 million for shadows at 103 frames a second at 4K on an M4 Pro in Chrome 154 ([its performance guide](https://elad12390.github.io/three-virtual-geometry/guide/performance)). three.js 0.187's own meshlet pipeline in a user's forest, with leaves and grass drawn outside it: "30-35 fps on base M1 Mac at 1080p and 40-50 fps on an iPhone 16 Pro" ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/forest-environment-built-with-three-js-and-webgpu/96126)). A closed WebGPU engine with meshlets and temporal upscaling on an M1 Pro at 3456 × 2234: 32 frames a second at full resolution and 47 at 60 % inside ([three.js forum: Shade](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/shade-webgpu-graphics/66969/158)). One paper ran the same WebGPU load in both browsers on one Mac and found Safari 8 % slower on the whole frame and about 30 % slower in a compute stage ([Han et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03207)). Natively, Tencent draws Nanite-like clusters on phones through the hardware rasteriser, avoiding a software one for "lack of atomic64 support", at about 3 ms of GPU time on a high-end phone and about 20 ms on one five years old ([Tencent, SIGGRAPH 2024](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2024/content/Cao-NanoMesh/AdavanceRealtimeRendering_NanoMesh0810.pdf)). So a cluster cut through the hardware rasteriser is shown on a phone's GPU natively and in Chrome on a Mac. In Safari it is shown by nobody.

## Six parts the sources and the measurements point to

If nobody has the whole, the originals and the game's own measurements still say what it has to be made of. The six parts below are named by what each must do. The builder and the measure serve the first and third demands, the way to change serves the second, and the cut rule, the far stand-in and the drawing path serve the fourth.

### A builder that tries several ways on every group and keeps the least loss

The form is the one the two automatic rules above share: make candidates, measure each alike, keep the best. The sources supply three ways of making a group coarser, and the game's second probe ran all three under one builder that was given only positions, triangles and how much light each triangle stops (all of it for wood, stone and canvas; 45 % for a leaf card) ([one-stick note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/one_stick_for_any_asset.md), first-hand).

Folding edges is the cluster loop itself, and the open builders' lessons are settled enough to copy: weld identical vertices first; lock only the vertices shared between groups, never the mesh's own open border; group eight clusters or more, afresh at every level; let collapses cross attribute seams when the error allows; give a stuck group a defined end ([meshoptimizer README](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/README.md?plain=1#L567); [JMS55 on Bevy 0.15](https://github.com/JMS55/jms55.github.io/blob/32f2e46ba1c9726829423efc3b142197320f99ef/content/posts/2024_11_14_virtual_geometry_bevy_0_15/index.md?plain=1#L650-L662); [clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L366-L416)). Thinning a crowd and enlarging what stays is about twenty years old and ships: a fixed order made once, the first share drawn, each survivor's area multiplied by one over that share. Pixar's 2007 paper could not be opened, so its own formulae stay recalled; four later papers that were read restate the rule, one of them with its formulae (a leaf's sides times √(1/λ) for a kept share λ, colours pulled towards the mean by √λ), and MachineGames documents it for the hair of a game shipped at 60 Hz: "we use the common practice of randomizing strands order. Then we can take only a section of strands buffer, scale thickness of each strand accordingly and hair shape will still be the same" ([Hasselgren et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03989); [MachineGames, SIGGRAPH 2025](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2025/content/Strand%20Hair%20in%20IJGC%20-%20Final%20Slides%20%28Post-Conference%29.pdf)). A stand-in for a group is what the literature turns to where thinning runs out: a hull from a voxel grid, voxel bricks with a distribution of normals, three crossed impostor planes with dithered opacity, or a single primitive placed at the group's weighted centre with the group's spread and an opacity equal to the area of the parts over the area of the stand-in ([Epic: Nanite Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage); [van Antwerpen et al., HPG 2026](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/2026/_assets/slides/Dietger_slides.pdf); [Kerbl et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12080); [Zhou et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03761)). Of these, only depth-tested triangles, solid or cut out, are shown to run fast on Apple's hardware; a million sorted and blended splats cost 38.5 ms a frame on an iPhone 15 Pro Max in Safari ([Han et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03207)).

The probe's builder found an asset's loose pieces by itself (triangles that share no corner with the rest) and called sixteen or more like pieces a crowd. **Nobody told it what it had, and it chose a different mix for each asset.** It folded the boulder at every size. It folded the wood and thinned the leaves of the oak and the pine down to about 80 px and took a hull round the crown from 34 px down. It thinned the mastic and gave it a see-through hull at 16 px. It folded the tent, then thinned its ropes and pegs and made those that stayed one and a half to two times as thick ([one-stick note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/one_stick_for_any_asset.md), first-hand). Two ways are missing, and the numbers show where. Between about 30 and 50 px a crown is too far for sprigs enlarged five times and too near for one hull, which is the place of a stand-in for each group of sprigs; the one tree generator whose code was read stops enlarging its leaves at five times ([Kuth et al., sample code](https://github.com/Bloodwyn/gptree)). And thin loose parts have no way that beats simply keeping them. The crowd rule itself is a rule of thumb that put the oak's trunk among its twigs. The literature warns against the alternative of sorting an asset once into surface and volume: the method that does so "tends to produce bloated, over transparent results", through "misclassification" among other causes ([Zhou et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03761), on Loubet and Neyret, whose own paper was not read).

What a far crowd must keep to look like the near crowd made small is spread over a dozen sources and stated together by none: the stand-in must stay inside the bound of what it replaces and turn any surplus of area into opacity; it should sit at the group's weighted centre; colours should be pulled towards the mean; the spread of normals should be carried, not one normal; shadows must see the same thinned crowd; and not every group may change at once ([crowds note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/crowds_of_small_parts_read_directly.md)). No source gives a rule for the point about which a survivor is enlarged, or for keeping a leaf at its true size while its card grows. The first probe found by measurement that enlarging about the tip, back into the crown, held an oak's cover within 1 % down to 4 % of its sprigs ([first-round probes](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/probes_on_the_games_own_assets.md), first-hand). For an asset of repeated parts, Epic's assemblies are the precedent: one hierarchy that points at the clusters of shared part meshes cut the largest demo tree from 3.5 GB to about 29 MB, and at the coarse end the parts are merged so that the whole still ends in one cluster. Epic's forests of trees with "between one and ten million polygons each" exist through it: "Without them, there wouldn't be enough disk space or streaming memory to render a large-world forest" ([Epic: Nanite Assemblies](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-assemblies); [Epic: Nanite Foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)). Epic recovers that structure from authored content with editor tools. A recipe that built the tree from parts has it for nothing (the notes' inference).

### A measure that can weigh "moved" against "gone" against "see-through"

This is the part no builder has, and its absence is why the kind of an asset still has to be said. Every published builder gives a candidate its error as a length. meshoptimizer's is the largest collapse error of the step, which does not see loss of cover; its author says so in prose: "the collapses of edges that simplify leaves lead to loss of visible area, which leads to loss of coverage: a simplified mesh covers fewer pixels far away" ([Kapoulkine, 2026](https://github.com/zeux/zeux.github.io/blob/a9dc7b0dba6489288f84ba532dfcb37fb416cad9/_posts/2026-09-30-billions-of-triangles-redux.md?plain=1#L116)). The partial repairs each turn one kind of loss into a length of its own: the radius of a removed piece, on whole meshes only; the retreat of the bounding box, so that "a version that lost its silhouette is only drawn once that loss is below the pixel threshold"; the distance between two merged pieces; two cells of a voxel grid ([simplifier.cpp](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/src/simplifier.cpp#L1955-L2002); [buildVirtualMesh.ts](https://github.com/elad12390/three-virtual-geometry/blob/6c1ccdb63c237e07a8a2e30ec88beecc0dc795be/src/core/preprocess/buildVirtualMesh.ts#L603-L628); [aggregate.ts](https://github.com/elad12390/three-virtual-geometry/blob/6c1ccdb63c237e07a8a2e30ec88beecc0dc795be/src/core/preprocess/aggregate.ts#L178-L183)). Each has a blind side, and **none of these builders measures cover or compares pictures**. The idea is in the literature all the same. Karis lists it as an open question on his own error slide, "Render results and use image based perceptual?", beside the wish "Want collapses to optimize for same metric as returned pixel error" ([Deck p.57](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=57)). An ancestor of the method set the scale of its error by pictures: "the smallest value ... leading to no image difference in a fixed number of random views" ([Cignoni et al., 2004](https://vcg.isti.cnr.it/Publications/2004/CGGMPS04/vbdam_sig04.pdf)). And the method that beat plain thinning by nine decibels fits each stand-in by "visual loss of rendered images", at a price of thousands of optimisation steps per asset (10,000 at 2048 pixels a side in its example), which no worker can pay for every group ([Hasselgren et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03989)). One obstacle Karis names does not apply here: his estimate is "an impossible task because at this stage we don't know what material will be applied to this mesh" ([Deck p.55](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=55)), and recipes that make mesh and material together do know it at build time (the notes' inference).

The game's second probe tried exactly that measure and called it the stick. The full thing and a coarser one are drawn from thirteen directions at the size on screen the coarser one would be used at and compared patch by patch, four pixels a side: how far the outline is off, how much a patch differs in what it shows of each stuff on average, and at its worst. Today's hand-written levels are read on the same stick, which is what turns "higher than today" into a number ([one-stick note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/one_stick_for_any_asset.md), first-hand). Its limits are stated with it, and they are large. It sees shape and cover in two stuffs: no colour within a stuff, no painting, no light, no wind. The sum by which it chooses (the outline's error, plus the average, plus a quarter of the worst) is the researcher's choice, and nothing calibrated it. It was wrong three times in ways that only its pictures showed: seeing only cover, it made the wood thicker; with patches of four pixels it misread things of a few pixels; and it passed a hull that turned the pine's umbrella into a block. And it is slow: nine comparisons of the oak took about five minutes in the script, where a boulder's whole hierarchy builds in 0.4 s. **Two things about it are therefore not shown: that it agrees with the eye, and how its reading becomes the one number per group that the cut needs.** The second has a hard condition from the sources: "the angular error, or whatever metric is compared to a threshold, must never decrease with each level of decimation", and a group's sphere must contain the spheres of what it replaces ([nv_cluster_lod_builder README](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/nv_cluster_lod_builder/blob/e27f05a8cb3d4537f0fd4dd40f6f28d0fcd8fb7b/README.md?plain=1#L193-L216)). The probe built no hierarchy on the stick.

### One rule for what to draw, in the pixels that are drawn

The rule is the part of the earlier research that the originals confirm without change: "Render: `ParentError > threshold && ClusterError <= threshold`", with every cluster of a group holding the same error and sphere ("Same input => same output") and errors forced never to shrink toward the coarse end, so that each cluster decides alone and the surface still closes ([Deck p.63](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=63), [p.66](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=66), [p.70](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=70)). On the game's own meshes it left no open edge in 177 views each of the boulder, a million-triangle ball and the tent, and it drew a ten-million-triangle ball 400 px across with 55,574 triangles at one pixel of error ([first-round probes](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/probes_on_the_games_own_assets.md), first-hand). Three details matter for the stress world. A hierarchy for rejecting clusters that are too fine is "An optimization. Not a requirement for Nanite" ([Deck p.73](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=73)). A far thing needs no test per cluster: every level of the hierarchy is a complete mesh, so NVIDIA's sample can draw far instances as one whole level, and three-virtual-geometry emits up to four clusters straight from its pass over instances ([vk_lod_clusters](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/vk_lod_clusters/blob/04e5b44f0d2f6376a2edf60702165e38c44f972c/docs/raster_discrete_lod.md); [cut.ts](https://github.com/elad12390/three-virtual-geometry/blob/6c1ccdb63c237e07a8a2e30ec88beecc0dc795be/src/core/runtime/cut.ts#L136-L215)). And the number 128 belongs to Unreal's rasteriser, not to the method; three.js uses 64 ([Deck p.50](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=50); [three.js: MeshletDrawingPipeline](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshletDrawingPipeline.html)).

For a crowd the same threshold applies once its error is defined the way the systems read define it for every other stand-in: the size on screen of the region it summarises. For a share of an ordered list that is the spacing of the survivors, which with the area repaid equals the size of a survivor, so holding it at a set number of pixels thins by the square of the distance (the notes' derivation; "granularity" in the splat hierarchy of Kerbl and others and Epic's "near pixel-sized" voxels are numbers of the same kind) ([crowds note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/crowds_of_small_parts_read_directly.md); [Kerbl et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12080)). The thresholds in use, from one pixel to fifteen, are engineering choices; no source read gives a perceptual measure for crowds. Which pixel is meant decides the triangle count by the square. The engine measures size in the page's pixels today, so on a screen of two pixels to each of them a level's error is twice as many of the screen's ([first-round probes](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/probes_on_the_games_own_assets.md), first-hand). three-virtual-geometry does the same by default ("On a 2x display, device pixels would request ~4x the triangles for detail nobody can see"), and Epic keeps a console variable for whether Nanite's detail follows the upscaler's screen percentage ([VirtualGeometry.ts](https://github.com/elad12390/three-virtual-geometry/blob/6c1ccdb63c237e07a8a2e30ec88beecc0dc795be/src/core/runtime/VirtualGeometry.ts#L179-L183); [Epic: console variables](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-console-variables-reference)).

### One way to change: every vertex and every part knows where it goes

This is the part the engine already owns as a rule and the published cluster systems lack. Four things move detail: the walker's steps, the zoom, the arrival of a part that was not built yet, and the governor's dial. A swap answers the first two only while the error stays under a pixel, and the other two not at all. A stored place on the coarser stand-in answers all four with one device: a change by size is a move along it, a late part grows in along it over time, and a turn of the dial is a small move everywhere instead of a wave of swaps.

What is published supports each half separately. Hoppe's geomorph guarantees that every vertex and attribute changes continuously and that the mesh equals the coarse one at one end and the fine one at the other; his run-time version starts a new vertex at its sibling's position and moves it over a second, the same construction as the engine's late mesh ([Hoppe, 1996](https://hhoppe.com/pm.pdf); [Hoppe, 1998](https://hhoppe.com/svdlod.pdf)). The cluster builders already carry the knob the construction needs, a factor "to separate transitions more" between a group's error and its children's: 1.0 by default in the header, 1.5 in NVIDIA's sample ([clusterlod.h](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/f0a684f11ead064e176775c9b796f82a55910e92/demo/clusterlod.h#L835-L836); [vk_lod_clusters: scene.hpp](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/vk_lod_clusters/blob/04e5b44f0d2f6376a2edf60702165e38c44f972c/src/scene.hpp#L44-L110)). The first-round probe put the two together on the boulder. With each group's error at least 1.25 times its children's, a group finishes its move before the next begins; two clusters placed a shared vertex within 3 × 10⁻⁸ of the boulder's size, so no crack opened; and the largest change in one step of a walk fell from 2.72 to 1.47 px at two pixels of error and from 6.75 to 2.51 px at four, for 11 % more triangles ([first-round probes](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/probes_on_the_games_own_assets.md), first-hand). **It halved the jump; it did not remove it**, because the target was the nearest point of the simplified surface. The exact target is the simplifier's record of which vertex was folded into which, and meshoptimizer returns only the new triangles. Karis's objection to the whole idea is cost, "geomorphing is both data and computationally costly", together with keeping the finer data loaded for as long as it moves ([Deck p.42](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=42)). Both costs are real, and neither was measured here.

For a crowd the same idea is the shrink at the cut: a part that leaves shrinks to nothing at a point, and a part that stands in for a group starts at the group's state. The sample code of a 2025 paper on generating trees on the GPU scales the last leaf of each small group between nothing and its full size, with the comment "always keep at least one child", and a hair renderer of 2026 adds a random offset per bundle that "smooths LOD transitions", so that not every group changes at once ([Kuth et al., sample code](https://github.com/Bloodwyn/gptree); [Lipp et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04230)). The engine does this today between two hand-written levels, one sprig at a time ([zoom and stress-world note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/the_zoom_and_the_stress_world_today.md), first-hand).

One fact about this engine makes the geometric route the only one for part of its world. Its temporal pass keeps little of the picture where things move by themselves: least of all for a mover, and for a crown less the further it swings in the wind, so that such pixels are "as sharp as one frame, not smeared" ([engine README](../TheGame/src/detail/README.md), read for this report). Nanite's second leg, the filter that blends what is left, is therefore weakest here on exactly the nine million movers and on crowns in wind (my inference). A stipple between two levels, which needs the filter to become a fade, would show there as grain.

### A far stand-in that never waits, and nothing kept per thing

A cluster hierarchy is built from the fine end: nothing of a kind can be drawn from it until its full mesh has been made on the device (0.24 to 0.51 s for a boulder of 110,592 triangles, then 0.42 s for its hierarchy, on one thread of a cloud machine) ([first-round probes](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/probes_on_the_games_own_assets.md), first-hand). The stress world has eleven thousand kinds: eight thousand shapes of 50,000 to 200,000 triangles, two thousand plants of leaf cards and a thousand creatures. Only the kinds near the camera can be built, as their fine levels are today, in pools of 8, 48, 320, 768 and 2,048 slots ([zoom and stress-world note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/the_zoom_and_the_stress_world_today.md), first-hand). Every published system keeps the coarse end always in memory for the same reason, and streams by whole groups: Nanite's "First page contains top level(s) of DAG / Always resident" and "Full group needed before a cluster in the group can be rendered", NVIDIA's lowest-detail group of every mesh loaded for good ([Deck p.123](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=123), [p.124](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf#page=124); [vk_lod_clusters: streaming](https://github.com/nvpro-samples/vk_lod_clusters/blob/04e5b44f0d2f6376a2edf60702165e38c44f972c/docs/streaming.md)). The stress world's own coarse levels come from the recipe at a coarse setting, 29 KB a kind, and that is the stand-in a new method can keep. The step from it to the hierarchy is then a late arrival like any other and must grow in, which needs the coarsest groups' vertices to know their places on a mesh they were not simplified from. **Nothing shows that step yet.**

Memory then follows the screen instead of the kind. Today a near kind holds its whole full mesh (2 to 6 MB) or none of it. By groups, a near thing holds what its size on screen asks for, 13,210 of the boulder's 110,592 triangles at 400 px, against a whole hierarchy that is 1.4 times the full mesh (the zoom note's inference from the first probes). An asset of ten million triangles is 278 MB as a whole hierarchy at the solids' vertex size. That is more than the 128 MiB a storage binding holds unless more is asked for when the device is made; Safari since 26.2 grants an iPhone 256 MiB to 1 GiB on request, and three.js asks only for what the application passes ([first-round probes](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/probes_on_the_games_own_assets.md), first-hand; [WebKit: HardwareCapabilities.mm](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebGPU/WebGPU/HardwareCapabilities.mm); [three.js: WebGPUBackend.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/renderers/webgpu/WebGPUBackend.js)). Holding such an asset and building it on a phone are both unmeasured. On the Mac the 8 GB gate is far off, with 578 MB held on the GPU with the stress stand on 13 November 2026; on an iPhone the wall is much nearer, and Apple's own WebGPU talk warns of "getting your program terminated due to memory pressure" on iOS ([engine README](../TheGame/src/detail/README.md), read for this report; [Apple, WWDC25 session 236](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/236/)).

Per thing, the stress world's measured cost is the bound to respect. Drawing every one of a million cost "about 9 ns a thing however few its triangles", and of the million only 135,000 to 180,000 are a pixel or more at once ([engine README](../TheGame/src/detail/README.md), first-hand). Of those, few are larger than a few dozen pixels. A far thing has to stay one test and a handful of triangles; only what is large on screen can go on to a test per group. At the far end the engine already draws nothing that is under a pixel and lets the land wear a sunk stand's colour ([engine note](../research_notes/Smooth%20automatic%20level%20of%20detail/the_engine_today.md), first-hand). The probe's last hulls keep 91 to 97 % of a crown's outline at 18 and 7 px, where today's far body and speck keep 42 to 72 %; the hand-over from the last stand-in to the land's colour was not measured ([one-stick note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/one_stick_for_any_asset.md), first-hand). For movers, a group's sphere and its error are measured at rest and both move on bones. Nothing was tried on a skeleton in either round.

### A drawing path shaped by WebKit's source and Apple's tile-based GPUs

What decides cost in Safari is in WebKit's source, not in its release notes. An indirect draw whose pipeline has no vertex buffers goes straight to Metal. With vertex buffers, WebKit first runs an internal draw that clamps the arguments, adds a memory barrier, and for an indexed draw runs a vertex shader once per index to check it. On a Mac below Apple's eighth GPU family, which includes the M1, a render pass is split after 512 barriers, with WebKit's own log line "WebGPU: Splitting render pass on ARM64 - severe performance penalty" ([WebKit: RenderPassEncoder.mm](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebGPU/WebGPU/RenderPassEncoder.mm); [WebKit: HardwareCapabilities.mm](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebGPU/WebGPU/HardwareCapabilities.mm)). Both open cluster drawers put placeholder vertex buffers on their indirect draws. Whether three.js binds them was not traced, and nobody has reported running either in Safari, so a poor result from their demos there would not condemn the method; whether the game's own drawer binds a vertex buffer in WebKit's sense was not traced either ([Safari note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/safari_and_apple_gpu_gate.md)). Faults in exactly this path were fixed only weeks ago: a "severe frame rate drop" with indirect draws in render bundles in Safari 27.2 of 26 October 2026, a "GPU hang" on repeated indirect draws from one buffer in the 27.3 beta, and, so far only in a Technology Preview, "WebGPU content permanently dropping to a fraction of the display refresh rate after a few slow frames" ([Safari 27.2 release notes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/safari-release-notes/safari-27_2-release-notes); [Safari 27.3 beta release notes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/safari-release-notes/safari-27_3-release-notes); [Safari Technology Preview 254](https://webkit.org/blog/18420/release-notes-for-safari-technology-preview-254/)). If that last fault is in the Safari Samuel runs, a governor that reacts after slow frames is not enough: the frame must not go over in the first place.

Apple's GPUs set the budget in triangles submitted, not triangles seen. For a whole render pass, in Apple's words, "the GPU will split the viewport into a list of tiles, shade all of the vertices and, finally, bin the transformed primitives" before any pixel is shaded, into a buffer that "may cause a Partial Render if full". Opaque pixels are then shaded once whatever the order, while "Alpha tested foliage" is a second class of fragment, for which draw order matters ([Apple, WWDC20 session 10602](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10602/); [session 10632](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10632/)). Apple publishes no triangle rate for any chip; its one line on the subject is "Render fewer very small triangles, especially if you're applying MSAA as well" ([Apple: Reducing shader bottlenecks](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/reducing-shader-bottlenecks)). The consequences for a design are read off, not measured: opaque clusters first and alpha-cut ones after, roughly front to back; no depth pre-pass; little handed on by the vertex stage; compute passes in which no thread waits for another, since two independent reports saw waiting scans and sorts collapse on Apple silicon; and few command buffers, where three.js submits one for every compute call and every pass ([three.js forum: Shade](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/shade-webgpu-graphics/66969/158); [Han et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03207); [three.js: WebGPUBackend.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/src/renderers/webgpu/WebGPUBackend.js)). Vertex runs have to be counted too. A cluster drawn corner by corner runs the vertex stage three times a triangle (three.js 0.187: always 192 runs for a cluster of at most 64 triangles), where an indexed one runs it 0.6 to 1 times, and the engine has met that difference in its own drawer: "A vertex looked up by its corner is worked out six times" ([three.js: MeshletDrawingPipeline source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r187/examples/jsm/pipelines/MeshletDrawingPipeline.js); [engine README](../TheGame/src/detail/README.md), first-hand). For crowns, Epic's advice for its own renderer is "Use geometry instead of masked cards", because "Masked-out pixels cost nearly as much as drawn pixels" ([Epic: Working with Nanite-Enabled Content](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-naniteenabled-content)). Which of the two is cheaper on an M1 is unmeasured.

Epic's way to a 4K picture is to draw fewer pixels inside and build the screen's picture over frames; its upscaler is documented at 1080p in for 4K out, "reducing GPU frame time by half" ([Epic: Temporal Super Resolution](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/temporal-super-resolution-in-unreal-engine)). The engine already does the same: the canvas stays at the screen's own pixels (2880 by 1558 in Samuel's window; 8.3 million on a true 4K screen), the world is drawn inside at a ratio that is the governor's last dial, and the picture is built from frames over time ([intent 0008](../Intents/0008-webgpu.md); [engine README](../TheGame/src/detail/README.md), read for this report). What that leaves room for on the weakest Mac is known only as one researcher's reckoning from other people's figures: **a base M1 at a true 4K canvas affords roughly 2 to 3.5 million submitted triangles a frame over all passes, with the world drawn inside at about 1080p to 1300p**; an M4 Pro about ten million, which is close to measured in Chrome; a recent Pro iPhone 1.5 to 2.5 million at its own smaller canvas while cool ([Safari note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/safari_and_apple_gpu_gate.md), estimate). The game draws 1.5 to 2.4 million in the stress world today ([engine README](../TheGame/src/detail/README.md), first-hand). If the estimate holds, a better picture has to come from the same triangles spent better, not from more of them.

The spread counts triangles, not pixels, and enlarged cards and see-through hulls cost pixels. And there is one shape of each plant, one boulder, one tent, and nothing on bones.

The figure 184,828 is the count of clusters rasterised in software, not of hierarchy nodes visited, which is 39,274. One write-up's version of the rule, with "at or under" on the parent's side, is wrong by the slide. And Karis came to clusters before his voxel experiments, not after them ([decks note](../research_notes/Generic%20automatic%20detail%20of%20higher%20quality/nanite_decks_read_directly.md)).

What the second round overtakes is the first report's recommendation. It proposed two rules chosen by kind of asset, the hierarchy for rocks, bodies and made things and thinning for crowns, and concluded that every asset is a surface, a crowd, or a sum of both. That sorting is what every published system still does by hand, and the second probe shows that the line does not hold even across five assets: the tent's ropes are neither, the far crown wants a hull, and the oak's wood wants folding while its leaves want thinning. The sorting has to move out of the asset's file and into the builder, group by group, by measurement.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the genericity Samuel asks for can live. No mechanism has it: the cluster hierarchy is generic over the shape of a surface, thinning over crowds of like parts, a hull over what is far, and the most experienced team in the field spent five years adding mechanisms one beside the other while its lead warned against seeking one. What can be generic is the measure. Once unlike losses sit on one scale, a new kind of asset costs at most a new way of making coarser inside the engine and never a word in the asset's file. As a builder's judge that measure is unpublished; its written precedents are a question on Karis's slide, a calibration in a 2004 paper and offline methods that fit one asset at a time against pictures. It is therefore the piece most worth owning and the piece with the least outside evidence to lean on. The engine starts ahead of the published state in the two places where the published systems admit their visible faults: it already forbids anything to change at once, where they pop on late arrival, and it already keeps nothing per thing, where they cap their instances. It also knows at build time what Epic's builder cannot, the material and the parts.

Samuel should expect the four demands to be met in a fixed order of certainty. "Nothing pumps" is nearest: it is shown in stills and needs no new drawing path. "Nothing jumps" is half shown, on one boulder, and needs the simplifier's record of its collapses. "Nothing differs" is the furthest for crowns: at the triangle budget reckoned for an M1, a far crown made of fewer and larger sprigs differs from the near one in grain whoever thins it, and the stand-ins that would carry a group's true leaf size and spread of normals are experimental even at Epic. And the gates decide all three, on a platform where nobody has measured this kind of drawing. Four outside events would change the picture: any measurement of a cluster hierarchy in Safari; a reading of Pixar's 2007 paper, the one place left to look for rules on the pivot and the transition that no later source states; Epic's voxels leaving experimental with their filtered shading; and Safari gaining the 64-bit atomic that the standard now allows, which would still leave the M1 out.
